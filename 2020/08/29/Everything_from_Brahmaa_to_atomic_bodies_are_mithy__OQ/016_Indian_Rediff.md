<div>

On a separate note, sticking to this thread's premise, one thing I have heard from Sw. Paramarthananda in his BG lectures is that Shrutis are to be considered one more means of knowledge. Just as we have 5 senses, we have to consider Shritus as a sixth one.  

</div>

<div>

  

</div>

<div>

To continue with this analogy, however much you try, you cannot describe the fragrance of a rose to someone with no olfactory senses. You can try as many analogies as you wish, but the fundamental fragrance of a rose requires a nose to smell it.  

</div>

<div>

  

</div>

<div>

In a similar manner, without Shrutis (and intellect as a crutch - similar to a pole vaulter's need for a pole), it is impossible to understand/realise/see what is Brahman. This translates to me as - using solely the intellect to try an attain Brahman is like trying to use the sense of touch to describe the fragrance of a rose.  

</div>

<div>

  

</div>

<div>

Put another way, (IMHO) you HAVE to take the Shrutis at face value. Once you do so, you can use your intellect and the knowledge of the Shrutis to attain/realise Brahman. At that point, my understanding is that the Shrutis will be logical and make complete sense.  

</div>

<div>

  

</div>

<div>

Please correct any misconceptions I may have written.

</div>
