Dear Sreenivasa et all,



Gaudapada kArikA 2.38 translates as ‘Once the truth has been realized…’ dRRiShTvA doesn’t mean ‘seen’ in a literal sense. Reality cannot be perceived in any sense because it is non-dual. There has to be an observer and an observed for empirical ‘seeing’.



There is no pramANa to prove that shAstra is a pramANa – you have to have faith until such time as you recognize the truth for yourself. vivekachUDAmaNi 25 says that “A firm conviction, based upon the intellectual understanding that the teachings of the scriptures and of one’s Master are true – this is called by the sages the faith which leads to the realization of the Reality.”



You cannot ‘experience’ Brahman; you can only realize the truth of what the scriptures tell you. Shankara says this in many places; e.g. his bhAShya to Taittiriya, brahmAnandavallI, anuvAka 8: “And therefore (tat cha) knowledge alone (viyAmAtram hi) is prescribed (upadishyate) as the means (sAdhanam) of attaining Brahman (AtmA).”



He goes on to say: “You don’t need to ‘reach’ or ‘gain’ Brahman – you just need to ‘get it’.” “The word sa\~NkramaNa (sa\~NkramaNa shabda) is figuratively used (upacharyate), and it is indeed not possible to use (na hi upadyate) in the other primary or literal meaning (anyathA) of gaining or reaching (sa\~NkramaNam) as AtmA is all-pervasive (sarvagatasya AtmanaH), and as (cha) there is no second object available (vastu antara abhAvAt) to reach or enter, and also as one cannot (na cha) gain or reach (sa\~NkramaNam) oneself (svAtmana eva).”
