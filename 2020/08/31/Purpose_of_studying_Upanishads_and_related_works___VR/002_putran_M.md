Namaskaram Ekam-ji,

  

Related thoughts:

  

I think of the pressure cooker. Too many leaks and it doesn't cook, no matter the fire.

  

“What is the significance of the Gita? It is what you find by repeating the word ten times. It is then reversed into ‘tagi’, which means a person who has renounced everything for God. And the lesson of the Gita is: ‘O man, renounce everything and seek God alone.’ Whether a man is a monk or a householder, he has to shake off all attachment from his mind"……..Sri Ramakrishna ([The Gospel of Sri Ramakrishna](http://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/gospel/gospel.htm))

  

When we don't have a higher level of shraddha and mumukshutvam, the intellectual conviction "I am not really a buffalo; I am always a man" does not translate to finding peace in the knowledge of being Man. We will find ourselves indulging buffalo-hood even while talking about being Man (and even if feeling intellectually capable of grasping Manhood).

  

Just a lot of work at the basic levels. We cannot skip. Reading scriptures as a sadhana can help in the process, if we can do that singularly, find happiness in that study, instead of running the mind in politics, sports etc.; we are helping shut the leaks in the cooker. Vasanas will keep pulling us back to the world of the buffalo. Understanding shastra intricately, systematically, comprehensively and integrally, correctly, and aligning our intellect or mind completely with the knowledge is not easy. We tend to be here, there and everywhere in our lives and in our thoughts. For such people, getting the gist of the shastra to the extent possible - the message is simple, reading 100 times is not going to show something else - then working on our lives through other sadhana (like in SRK quote), maturing and becoming receptive to higher truths, so that we are ready to understand deeper and merge deeper - that may be the better approach. The hard work for chitta-shuddhi, ekagratha, viveka, vairagya etc needs to come in somewhere.
