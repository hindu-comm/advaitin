That is excellent, yes. But one must be careful not to equate this with progress in Self-knowledge. The two are related in that someone who is ‘disturbed’ will be unable to pay attention to the teacher and assimilate the knowledge. But it is still possible to be ‘enlightened’ and yet not be immune to such problems. Habitual responses to situations may still occur though knowing that they are not appropriate.



I have just looked back at the original question and the last part of this was: “if I am illiterate and deaf, do i get Moksham? How?” I don’t think anyone has actually answered this. I would venture to suggest that one doesn’t. Ability to attend to a teacher and/or read the scriptures on one’s own and exercise reason and discrimination are all pretty essential. Advaita would say that a person is born into such a body-mind as a result of past karma and would need to gain sufficient puNya to be born into a more suitable body in some future life.

Swami Paramarthanandaji gave a beautiful yardstick to measure the progress in spiritual life. He termed it as an acronym- FIR Decrease

 F — stands for frequency of emotional disturbances.

 I — means intensity of emotional disturbances. The intensity is high when it is at the level of mind, speech and body. The intensity is medium when it is at the level of mind and speech only. And the intensity is low, when it is at the level of mind only. Thus the emotional disturbance can be of highest, medium or low.

 R — refers to the recovery period. The time that is taken for coming to normalcy.

 And spiritual growth is reduction in all these three — Frequency, Intensity and Recovery period — with regards to emotional disturbances gradually coming down.
