Guru’s grace is nothing but Guru’s teaching only.

  

Vivekachoodamani states in no uncertain terms the role of ज्ञानम् and the role of कर्म

  

पठन्तु शास्त्राणि यजन्तु देवान् कुर्वन्तु कर्माणि भजन्तु देवता: ।

आत्मैक्यबोधेन विना विमुक्ति: न सिद्ध्यति ब्रह्मशतान्तरेपि ।। 6

  

One may study various shastras, perform yagnas for devas, perform karmas and upasanas etc, but if all that doesn’t culminate in Self Knowledge, one cannot get Moksha even in hundreds of lifetimes of Brahmaji. (Note that one lifetime of Brahmaji is311,040,000,000,000 human years.)

  

चित्तस्य शुद्धये कर्म न तु वस्तूपलभ्यते।

वस्तुसिद्घिर्विचारेण न किंचित् कर्मकोटिभि।। 11

  

Karma Yoga is required for preparing and purifying the mind, but it is not meant for gaining the knowledge of reality i.e Atmajnanam. Atmajnanam is obtained only by Vedanta Vichara, which is Atma Vichara. The knowledge of Reality can never be attained by (even crores of) karmas.

  

The fundamental reason why ज्ञानम् is said to be the only means can be appreciated if we understand our search for the house keys when the keys are in our pocket. The search will end only when I KNOW that the keys are already in my pocket. As long as I don’t KNOW that, search will fruitlessly continue for ever in all the wrong places, never to find what I am looking for. The situation here is very similar. The seeking will end only with KNOWLEDGE that I am already what I have been seeking.

  

When someone (including animals) are said to gain मोक्ष: in Puranic stories etc. without apparently having gone through Shravanam etc in that lifetime needs to be interpreted properly in keeping with the Shruti. If there is an apparent contradiction between Smriti and Shruti, Shruti should be given weightage. There are multiple possibilities for interpreting the Smriti (or Puranic story) without falsifying it and also not violating the Shruti rule that Moksha is only gained by Jnanam

  

- the person in question had done enough Shravanam in earlier janmas but due to some Prarabdha, they required another janma to finish the process. In the next janma, they required only a slight uncovering for the knowledge to take place. Even Shruti gives the example of Vamadeva who gained jnanam in his mother’s womb.

  

- or the person who is said to have attained moksha attained it indirectly by first gaining chitta shuddhi, that must then have been followed by some Vedanta Vichara.

  

In any case, such stories are to be treated as exceptions to the rule, and not the rule. It is in general a safer strategy to not consider oneself as an exception and hence it is better and safer to follow the rule meant for the non exceptional cases.
