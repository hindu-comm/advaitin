Almost all the responses in this thread so far, seem to state the following.  

  

A\) A Sadhaka's realization is different from the long preparation for it.  

  

**----------------**  

*A better way to state this would be: There are multiple paths to purifying the mind which can all be termed as Karma Yoga. There is only one path to Moksha, which is Jnana Yoga. Karma Yoga is incomplete without Jnana Yoga. Jnana Yoga is impossible without Karma Yoga.*  

**-----------------**  

  

B\) Preparation can be done in several ways (e.g. karma marga, bhakti marga ), and  

C\) Ultimately Realization happens only when the needed knowledge (Jnana) is acquired from (vedic) scriptures and the associated Shastra's.  

  

**----------------**  

*We should not forget to mention the role of the Guru in (C). You could restate (C) as: Self Knowledge takes place when a qualified student approaches a qualified Guru and does Vedanta Shravanam with commitment.*

**----------------**  

  

I fully understand and appreciate points A and B. But, to me it is hard to believe that C is always TRUE.  

  

Let me explain myself.  

  

I make a hypothesis here - "**Realization that we are all Brahman is a universal possibility, Vedic scriptures are one way to facilitate that possibility**". If this Hypothesis is true, it should be possible to trigger Realization in a number of other ways .  

  

**----------------**  

*If you understand the problem, if you diagnose the problem properly, there cannot be a number of other ways. In fact, there is only one way. The language of the teaching may differ. But the content cannot differ. There needs to be a Guru who needs to show the student that he is indeed the Whole, that the Jivatma seeker is not different from Paramatma, the whole. Just as you need to know that the keys you are searching for are in your pocket and someone needs to tell you that: in Chinese, or Japanese or whatever language you understand.  *

*  *

*Any teaching that shows the oneness of Jiva-Atma and Paramatma is Advaita, irrespective of the language it is taught in. But the core concepts for the communication need to be there without which there can be no communication. The teaching tradition of Vedanta is timeless and has all these ideas and Prakriyas (methodology) for doing the communication by carefully avoiding the accompanying pitfalls given that the topic is extremely subtle.**  ***

***----------------***

***  ***

  

I believe many of the Rishis who authored Upanishads, Bhashya's and their derivative works such as Vivekachudamani were also humans like us. Their intense seeking and spiritual discipline lead to "intuitions"
through which they were able to come up with such powerful insights and recorded them as various works. Many times, they extended works of their predecessors, with own insights gained through these intuitions.  

  

**----------------**  

*Rishis did not author the Upanisads. The Vedas are Apaurusheya (not of human origin) as per the tradition. The Rishis discovered the mantras, they did not author them. All those who came later and contributed to the Vedanta tradition in the form of Prakarana Granthas, Bhashyas, Tikas, etc. belonged to some Guru-Shishya parampara, They held their Guru and Parampara in the highest regard for they knew very well that without their Guru/Parampara they could not have gained the knowledge...and that was not just lip service to their Guru. They really meant it.*  

**----------------**

  

**Now**, if a Sadhaka has the same level of intensity and discipline, they should be able to go through realization even if they have not had the opportunity to study vedic scriptures or even if they pursue totally different path such as a scientist studying the unified nature of creation or a Zen practices. Thus, It is the intensity of seeking that facilitates the realization and not the knowledge itself. Knowledge makes this intensity possible.

  

**----------------**  

*Intensity and Discipline are explicitly included in the qualifications required for Vedanta study (called Sadhana Chatushtaya Sampatti) that need to be gained through Karma Yoga. However, the intense seeker of Moksha needs to be converted into an intense seeker for Self Knowledge. Otherwise, the intense seeking will continue aimlessly with no end in sight. A Guru is very much needed to point out that the seeking for Moksha needs to change into seeking for Knowledge.*

*  *

*As to whether one can stumble on this knowledge by oneself with just an intense seeking combined with discipline: one can stumble upon objects in the world by oneself. Marie Curie discovered Radium by serendipity. She was an intensely focused scientist. Unfortunately, in the same manner, one cannot discover the Brahma-Atma Aikyam for the simple reason that one cannot stumble upon oneself! One cannot objectify oneself! Hence one just doesn't have the necessary means to know oneself properly without the help of a Guru and without the help of Vedanta Shastram. It is like a blind person intensely trying to find the way out of a forest by merely groping around aimlessly.* *If we see cases wherein someone gained the Jnanam without Shravanam in a particular birth, our only answer is that he or she must have done some Shravanam in some earlier birth, or else it cannot be explained.*  

**----------------**  

  

With that logic, shouldn't we be giving higher importance to intensity of seeking and discipline in order to gain realization and use the knowledge from scriptures and their derivative works as an **optional means** **to accelerate** the preparation where needed?  

  

**----------------**  

**Knowledge is not optional.* The Realization is Knowledge and nothing else. Knowledge is the only solution as the problem is ignorance. Ignorance is removed by Knowledge and Knowledge alone. If you make Knowledge optional, then the seeking will continue endlessly for eternity. Knowledge doesn't accelerate preparation...it in fact it ends all the need for preparation, it ends the seeking, it ends the seeker. Preparation comes first. Knowledge comes later.  *

***-----------------**  *

*regards.*
