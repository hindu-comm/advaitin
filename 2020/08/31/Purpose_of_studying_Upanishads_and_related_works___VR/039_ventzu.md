Ram Chandran

  

I’m afraid you are incorrect about Ramanamaharishi. He has always said he was enlightened at the time of his death experience as a boy in Madurai, and that he had not read any sruti at that stage. His later reading of scriptures simply accorded with his experience. David Godman has meticulously collected all of the anecdotes recorded by devotees from their discussions with him:

  

[https://www.davidgodman.org/bhagavans-self-realisation/](https://www.davidgodman.org/bhagavans-self-realisation/)

  

Whether you agree with him or not, Ramanamaharishi - whilst he had the greatest respect for vedanta - was unambiguous that much (even any) reading of scriptures was unnecessary. From his Guru Vachaka Kovai:

141: All the jnana scriptures that teach the way to redemption proclaim in unison that restraining and stilling the mind is the best means for liberation. This is also emphasised by Jnanis. If, after a certain amount of study, one knows this to be the inner purport of the scriptures, one should then direct one’s whole effort towards that practice. What is the use of continuously studying more and more scriptures without doing this

  

Ramakrishna thought along similar lines:

“Sacred books only point the way to God. Once you have known the way, what is the use of books?”

“Only two kinds of people can attain self-knowledge: those who are not encumbered at all with learning, that is to say whose minds are not over-crowded with thoughts borrowed from others; and those who, after studying all the scriptures and sciences, have come to realise that they know nothing.”

  

Swartz, who you point to as a reference, has either not done his research, or is making this up to fit his pre-conceived notions of ’traditional’ advaita.

  

This also answers the initial question as to how can a deaf and illiterate person realise? Both Ramanamaharishi and Ramakrishna had unambiguous answers to that question.
