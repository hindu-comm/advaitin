Namaste All - Is it correct to say something is correct from a limited finite dual standpoint? Gaudapada for example will probably burst out in laughter!

  

From the standpoint of one's Unlimited Self-Awareness there is no duality such as birth or rebirth, known nor unknown etc. As such it is fictitious to state there is somebody who is born, dies and will be reborn or whether he got it or did not got the it, as acquired knowledge is adventitious to one's Swarupa.  

  

Even from the finite perspective of drishti-srishti-vada which make compromises for those yet to comprehend non-causality, nothing can be declared of transcendental notions as 'Unlimited', 'Infinite', 'Anadi'.. except to say all delimiters as birth, death and other dual perspectives are included as expedient concepts which are ultimately superfluous, non binding, non tainting. But one must be mindful that these are imaginary constructs described in the MandukyaKarika as follows:   

  

****16.** First of all, He imagines the individual self and then He imagines various objects, external and internal. As is a man’s knowledge, so is his memory of it.  **

**  **

******19.** The Self is imagined as countless objects like prana, mind etc... This is the illusion of the luminous One by which It itself seems to be deluded, as it were**  **

  

****82.** Owing to the Self fondness for any object whatsoever, He appears to be veiled and is **automatically** unveiled every moment by repeated reflection on its own nature.**

**  **

This Self-existing Awareness is the only One to finish Its own game of pretense and dissolve the veil of ignorance (i.e. snap out of the dream-trance) ultimately awakening from all states (including the tri-states of the mind) established in Turyia, its own sublime transcendental nature (i.e. Nidhidhyasana).  

  

No imaginary character including the idea of a separate limited self, which actually don't exist (except in imagination) can perform this feat. This is the reasoning behind the statement in the Katha Upanishads roughly paraphrased as "Not by effort, learning, debate, practices is it realized but to whom the Self chooses".
