Namaste,

One can understand where this is coming from.

  

Yes, the study of Vedic scriptures does structurally exclude a majority of the human race, but the study of the truth expounded by them is certainly available to many more.

  

For example, only the other day I came across Portuguese translations of many vedantic texts being studied in South America. So yes, if the knowledge of language, or restrictions on Veda adhyayana are barriers to realisation, it is possible to circumvent them to a certain extent.

  

However, whether studied in Latin or in Sanskrit, the truth is still the same. When we say Vedas are the only means to know Brahman, what is meant is that they are the original source for the knowledge. If the vedas were not there, Brahman cannot be known. How that knowledge revealed in the vedas is conveyed to individual candidates can vary, but the source of the knowledge must be acknowledged, at least in gratitude if nothing else.

  

The second point about the role of the mind in liberation and the role of study requires some elaboration. It was said - "Having understood that mind has no (or very limited) role when one is realized or ready for realization" - how did one arrive at such a conclusion?

  

The Vedas, which are the original source through we which we know Brahman say, मनसैवानुद्रष्टव्यं/मनसैवेदमाप्तव्यं /दृश्यते त्वग्र्यया बुद्ध्या सूक्ष्मया सूक्ष्मदर्शिभिः etc. So contrary to the view that the mind has no or very limited role in realisation, shruti pramANa says that it is by the mind (मनसा) and mind alone (एव) that Brahman can be known. And as it is only the knowledge of Brahman that is the means to realisation, it follows that the mind is indispensable for realisation.  

  

So yes, the general point that once the knowledge of Brahman is attained there is no need for anything else, including further scriptural study, the role of study (ie shravaNam) in attaining that knowledge of Brahman cannot be overlooked.

  

For a rare few, shravaNam itself is sufficient, but for most it will require further mananam and nidhidhyAsanam. That can be done with or without the aid of texts. For example one can use the help of manana grantha-s like advaita siddhi or nidhidhyAsana grantha-s like aShTAvakra gItA, or one can do these by oneself. The idea is that the truth revealed in scripture is imbibed and transformed into one's own living reality. This will require the eradication of doubts (manana) and the removal of habitual tendencies that act as obstacles to realisation (nidhidhyAsanam). Where the manana texts help is in voicing several doubts that may arise in the seeker and by systematically resolving them. nidhidhyAsana texts are subtler - no new information is revealed in them, but as they repeatedly point to ones own nature, merely hearing / studying them is nidhidhyAsanam, one is abiding in that nature during the study.

  

The mind must necessarily be present and active in each of shravaNam, mananam and nidhidhyAsanam, because without the mind as the locus, where will the vritti that can remove ignorance arise?

  

Which path one takes after shravaNam - independent cogitation / meditation or further study will vary depending on the individual's situation, but the role of the guru is still indispensable here because they can assess where the student is and prescribe the appropriate course.

  

Regards

Venkatraghavan
