> **"If the Vedas were not there, Brahman cannot be known".**

What other means is there to know Brahman capable of leading to moksha? The abovestatement was not made out of jingoism, to claim vedic supremacy - it was made because it is a fact. To explain, the Brahman that is revealed in the vedas, is attributeless consciousness - and that consciousness is the basis for all pramANa-s. The scope of all pramANa-s therefore is objective,and not Brahman, which is the universal subject. Even the shruti, which is also a superimposition upon Brahman, does not reveal Brahman directly, because such a Brahman would be objective, it goes through a sequence of steps to indirectly point towards It and hopes that the student gets it. That being the case, whatever is revealed as "this" by any other means of knowledge, including the shruti, is not Brahman. What is pointed to by the shruti through the mahAvAkya is what is Brahman. Therefore, without the shruti first revealing the tatpadArtha, the true svarUpa of the tat and tvam padArtha, and their aikya in the mahAvAkya, the knowledge of Brahman that is capable of leading to moksha cannot be gained.

  

> 2. I am not sure if Mind and Manas are the same. When i said "Mind",
> it is the intellectual and analytical aspects of me which find (egoic)
> satisfaction in gathering more information, reading more books and
> continuously connecting dots between different scriptures.
>
>  May be they translate into the *buddhi and ahankara.* Whereas Manas
> and Chitta are the faculties that also drop off at the time of
> realization. (मनो बुद्ध्यहंकारचित्तानि नाहम्. *mano buddhi ahankara
> chittani naaham* )

That is different, you are referring to vidyAnanda and abhimAna above. vidyAnanda, or the joy that one derives from knowledge and study is also a form of objective happiness, and therefore must be overcome in the quest of Brahman. The ahamkAra that you are also referring to above is used in the sense of abhimAna or pride (in this case, in one's scriptural knowledge), which also must be given up. However, that ahamkAra is not the ahamkAra that is a component of the mind (manah, buddhi, chittah, ahamkAra).That refers to chidachidgranthi or the intimate identification of consciousness with the inert on account of ignorance, and is one of the components of the mind. The buddhi is the analytical faculty of the mind, which is another component of the mind. Both ahamkAra (chidachidgranthi) and buddhi are different from vidyAnanda and ahamkAra (abhimAna).

  

While vidyAnanda and abhimAna must be overcome (along with the attachment to otherobjective pleasures) on the path to moksha, the mind consisting of its four components are necessary requirements.

  

Even the ego is required, because it is in the waking state when the ego is present, that one uses the ego to identify Brahman through the process of jahadajahallakshaNA. Buddhi is also required for that process to differentiate between the inert (acid amsha) and the conscious (cid amsha) of ego or cidacidgranthi. That is why, one cannot gain knowledge in deep sleep or samAdhi, because the ahamkAra, buddhi, manah and chitta are absent then.

  

To clarify, no component of the mind is dropped off (ie ceases to exist empirically) after realisation - they continue to exist empirically along with the physical body and when the physical body drops dead, they get resolved as well. When shankarAchArya said manobuddhyahamkAra chittAni nAham, he was saying that He, the consciousness, is different from the manah, buddhi, chitta and ahamkAra. If they didn't continue to exist (again we are referring to empirical existence) and function, AchArya would not have been able to speak the words of the nirvANa ShaTkam.
