Ekamji,

  

You make the following two statements:

  

**\> "everyone in this group, including me have taken the Vedic path for realization."**

**  **

**\> "i have been very committed to studying advaitic scriptures as part of my journey. i will probably contiue to do that for a  **

**\> long time to come."**  

  

I have a fairly straightforward question:

  

Do you have a Guru whom you have approached to study the scriptures you refer to? There are only two possible answers:  

  

1) Yes. Then my next question is why don't you take your dilemma(s) to your Guru? It's as simple as that.  

OR  

2) No. Then you do have a significant problem by not having a Guru you can approach. That would not be the Vedic path. Remember that most Upanisads are dialogues between a student and Guru. sa gurum eva abhigacchet says Mundaka. Lets not cite

exceptions in this case where someone realized without a Guru, for those exceptions probably did not have such dilemmas

being exceptions!  

  

In case you fall in \#1, which I hope you do, your Guru would probably know you by now (and vice versa), and if you really  

have Shraddha and Bhakti towards your Guru, then the answers you get from your Guru will either satisfy you immediately, and even if they don't, you will take the answer as your Guru's Vakyam, a statement that you may not yet understand, but you give

it the benefit of doubt with the idea that you will understand it in due course of time. In either case, you will not have a dilemma

to discuss.

  

The problem with discussing such questions (and you have raised not one but multiple questions) on a list with 15 people, all willing to help with all good intentions, is that you will get 16 views! There will invariably be someone who may also agree with whatever you are saying, which may reinforce possibly wrong ideas you have. And even if someone on the list is indeed saying all the right things as per traditional Vedanta teachings (which several people already have), because of your lack of Shraddha in the unknown person, you may overlook and/or reject what he has been saying because it doesn't agree with your notions, or sounds too uncomfortable or foreign to accept. The same is not true of a Guru's vakyam because one's Shraddha in the Guru will help one wiggle out of one's comfort zone over time, even if one may not initially be able to accept or swallow what the Guru says.  

  

Just as for a medical issue: say for a liver problem or skin problem, it is better to not discuss that on an online forum, or do a google search to try and self medicate, the best advice would be: go to a doctor. A family doctor who you have a relationship with often can provide the right medical advice knowing you and your background. So also in spiritual matters, the Guru is your spiritual doctor,

and someone you can run to when you have a spiritual fever!  

  

आचार्यात् पादमादत्ते पादं शिष्यः स्वमेधया ।

पादं सब्रह्मचारिभ्यः पादं कालक्रमेण च ॥

  

A quarter is learnt from the Guru, another quarter is grasped by the students own pondering,

the third quarter is learnt from discussions with fellow students, and the last quarter becomes clear over time.

  

Note that what this group is doing (I hope) is just the third quarter. It cannot be and should not be a substitute

for the first quarter. And ideally the third quarter should be done only after the first two quarters have been done

or again there will be enormous confusion. There is no substitute for proper Shravanam with a Guru with whom  

you have a committed and formal Guru Shishya relationship.  

  

regards,

--Satyan
