> Yes, there is also the etymological expression: bhasanaat bhasma which
> means 'it is called bhasma because it is splendrous', Jnana is
> self-luminous. Narayanashrama who lived in the 13th Century CE has
> commented on the Atharvashira Upanishad where the mantra occurs:
> agniriti bhasma, vaasyuriti bhasma, etc. He says: Lord Shiva applies
> the bhasma on his whole body signifying that the whole world that he
> has created, made of the five elements, is his vibhuti, splendor.
> The Pashupata vratam is what is prescribed there and Shiva observes
> that vrata even though he does not need it but for the sake of the
> aspirants, who will be released from bondage by observing the vrata.
> The bhasma dharana vidhi is stated in the Kaalaagnirudropanishat, the
> commentator says.
