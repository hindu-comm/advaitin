Have not been following the entire discussion in detail but just want to point out that this is incorrect.

There is no destruction or transformation of anything at the physical level with the removal of ignorance.

The antahkaranam that harbors the ignorance alone gets the liberating knowledge and it is the ignorance alone that gets destroyed. The prarabdha that is in play will maintain its momentum until exhausted and until then the sthula sukshmakaranashareera bundle will continue.

Just as in the case of the 10th man - no physical change is necessary, nor possible, in the 10th man once his ignorance of his own self identity is removed.

There is no “jnani” or “ajnani” IN the body mind complex - the jnani or ajnani IS the body mind complex - both ignorance and it’s removal are both vyavaharika - the paramarthika Self the Atman has no real association with any body mind complex.

Quote. ([http://ramana-talk-mailer.appspot.com/read?post_name=Talk&index=513](http://ramana-talk-mailer.appspot.com/read?post_name=Talk&index=513))

  

The kramamukti school objects to the idea of sadyomukti (immediate  
liberation) because the Jnani is supposed to lose body-consciousness  
at the same time that ignorance is dispelled but he continues to live in  
the body. They ask, “How does the body function without the mind?”  
The answer is somewhat elaborate:  
Knowledge (jnana) is not incompatible with ignorance (ajnana) because the  
Self in purity is found to remain along with ignorance-seed (ajnana beeja)  
in sleep. But the incompatibility arises only in the waking and dream states.  
Ajnana has two aspects: avarana (veiling) and vikshepa (multiplicity). Of  
these, avarana (veiling) denotes the veil hiding the Truth. That prevails in  
sleep. Multiplicity (vikshepa) is activity in different times. This gives rise  
to diversity and prevails in waking and dream states (jagrat and svapna).  
If the veil, i.e., avarana is lifted, the Truth is perceived. It is lifted for a  
Jnani and**so hiskaranasarira (causal body) ceases to exist**. Vikshepa alone  
continues for him. Even so, it is not the same for a Jnani as it is for an ajnani.  
The ajnani has all kinds of vasanas, i.e., kartrtva (doership) and bhoktrtva  
(enjoyership), whereas the Jnani has ceased to be doer (karta). Thus only  
one kind of vasana obtains for him. That too is very weak and does not  
overpower him, because he is always aware of the Sat-Chit-Ananda nature  
of the Self. The tenuous bhoktrtva vasana is the only remnant of the mind  
left in the Jnani and he therefore appears to be living in the body.  
This explanation when applied to the mantra amounts to this:**A Jnani****  ** **has hiskaranasarira destroyed;**the sthula sarira (gross body) has  
no effect on him and is for all practical purposes destroyed too. The  
sukshma sarira (subtle body) alone remains. It is otherwise called  
ativahika sarira. It is this which is held by all persons after the  
physical body is given up. And with this they traverse to other lokas  
until another suitable physical body is taken. The Jnani is supposed  
to move in Brahmaloka with this sukshma sarira. Then that is also  
dissolved and he passes to final Liberation.  
**The whole explanation is meant only for the onlooker. The Jnani  
himself will never raise such questions. He knows by his experience  
that he is not bound by any kind of limitations.**  
  
D.: What is the ‘final emancipation’ according to the foregoing  
explanation?  
M.: The ativahika or the sukshma sarira corresponds to the pure light  
which one experiences just after sleep and before the rise of the ego.  
It is Cosmic Consciousness. That is only the Light reflected from  
the Heart. When the reflection ceases and abides as the Original  
Light in the Heart it is final emancipation.

*D.:*In the explanation given yesterday, it is said that the**removal of*avarana*results in the annihilation of the*karanasarira***. That is clear. But how is the gross body considered to fall off too?  
  
*M.:*The*vasanas*are of two kinds:*bandha hetu*(causing bondage) and*bhoga hetu*(only giving enjoyment). The*Jnani*has transcended the ego and therefore all the causes of bondage are inoperative.*Bandha hetu*is thus at an end and*prarabdha*(past*karma*) remains as*bhoga vasana*(to give enjoyment) only. The physical body is inert. It has a beginning and an end. It is full of impurities. It is perishable. It is a product of five elements. It is full of parts. It can be seen by the naked eye. It is finite (Parischinna). It is changing. So, it cannot be self-existent, self-luminous, and Satchidananda.

First of all my reverence and devotion to Ramana Maharshi is immense and his writings have been an indispensible part of my mananam.

I shall present my perspective on some basic precepts here based on the traditional teaching.

  

Karanashareera is the primordial seed avidya that provides the nidus for individuality..

It is thekaranashareera that is the cause for the sukshma and sthula shareerams andIt is what persists in the deep sleep or sushupti when both the sukshma and sthoola shareera are folded.

  

Were it not for thekaranashareera there would be no one to wake up from deep sleep.

And what is true at the individual level in the mini-pralaya of sushupti is equally true at the Cosmic level of mahapralaya - during the COsmic dissolution. It iskaranashareera of the trillions of jivas that persists in the long dormancy of the Cosmic night of Brahmaji and at the beginning of a new manifestation of Srshti eachkaranashareera then picks up from where its karmic load left and gives rise to the sukshma sthoola shareera bundles that hitherto lay dormant. Without their basis causalkaranashareera neither sukshma nor sthoola shareera can ever independently exist.

  

With the removal of ignorance - all 3 shareeras - sthula sukshma andkarana- are sublated as they are understood as mithya, and are hence as good as destroyed. There is neither a necessity nor a possibility of any of the 3 to be physically transformed/ destroyed/ altered. A jnani may feel thirst at a physical level, and he will of course use his hands and mouth to consume water. Similarly his mind will entertain compassion if his eyes witness someone suffering - be it a human or a animal. He will continue to go through the 3 avasthas - wake dream and deep sleep. But the difference is the ajnani takes all of this duality to be real - the jnani remains established in the knowledge that all "this" is appearance that is dependent on me the Atma who am pure consciousness. Ahankara for a ajnani is forever aham different from the idam the prapancha, ahankara for a jnani is forever idam and included in the rest of the mithya prapancha- as Sureshwaracharya puts it beautifully it - the ahankara - is a kanchukam a garb he wears to interact with the world.

As Sakshi he is the substratum for ALL the bodies -karanasukshma and sthoola - in the Cosmos stretched across ALL space and ALL time - this puny body mind complex that he is seemingly associated with for the residual of ITS prarabdha until it drops in a minuscule period of residual time is rendered irrelevant.

Hence there is no contradiction here between Moskha and seeming continuity of mithya vyavahara, nor is there any esoteric metaphysical or mystical aspect to this “process”. Awaiting such an “event” is in fact a obstacle for a seeker, oddly convenient, as he can postpone his own freedom pending its “arrival”. A Moksha that “comes” will “go”. Moksha is ever our true swaroopam - there is neither coming and hence alone no going of what is ever intrinsic.
