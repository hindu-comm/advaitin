An essay written from a *Vyavaharika* standpoint to be critiqued:

  

"You can know everything except your own Self. The knowing Self cannot be known because to know something means to reduce it to an object. The very process of knowledge depends on duality. But I cannot know my self because I cannot reduce my self to an object. Self is pure subjectivity and this can never be made objective. Hence, the predicament: that which knows all cannot know itself; that which is the source of all knowledge remains unknowable.

  

Yet Atma-Gyna is being peddled by gurus in the marketplace as 'Self-knowledge' or 'Know thyself '. But how can one know thy Self ? You can know everything perceived under the sun other than that which is perceiving - your own Self. The ever fresh witnessing Self will always remain unknowable.  

  

If knowledge of the Self is impossible, then the whole spiritual search becomes nonsense, an absurdity, a spiritual circus which Jiddu Krishnamurti pointed out. So there must be some deeper meaning to the word 'Atma-Gyan' uttered by the Upanishadic Rishis which probably got distorted and lost with the passage of time.  

  

If there is only one way of knowing – objective knowledge – then Self can never be known. So how on earth can this perceiving subject, untainted by objects, be known or even said to exist?  

  

To understand this the illustration Vedanta give the example of a lamp to point out a subtle dimension of knowing where the knowing is not reduced to an object. If a lamp is lit in a dark room, everything in the room is lighted or known through the light of the lamp. Everything else the chairs, furniture and features in the room are known through the light. But through what is the light itself known?  

  

The light is self-illuminating: it knows itself by its own light, just by its presence it reveals others and it reveals itself also. But these two revelations are different. When the chair is known through the light, the chair is an object. The light falls on it and if the light disappears the chair cannot be known. The knowledge of the chair depends on the light but the knowledge of the light itself doesn’t depend on the chair. If everything is removed the light will still be shining. There will be nothing to reveal but it will go on revealing, illuminating itself.The revelation of the light is self-revelation.

  

Similar is the case with the ever present Self. Everything is known through it's light but It is not known by anything else – it is a self-revealing. So whatsoever is known in an objective manner cannot be the Self. So how can this self be known again? The naked perceiving Self is a self-evident, self-obvious, self-revealing, self-luminous. It knows itself by its own light. Experientially, the pure luminous Self- existing wakefulness can be glimpsed as the elusive lightning flashes (as mentioned in Kena Upanishad) between two events, two states, two perceptions and two thoughts.

  

Nagarjuna, the great Indian dialectic thinker, has refuted every apparent object as empty of intrinsic self-nature and hence illusory, but he remained mute on the silent observing Self else his Mahayana Buddhist philosophy would have collapsed. Gaudapada who was quite acquainted with Nagarjuna's philosophy had seen through this limitation and viewed his Madhyamaka treatise as untenable. Gaudapada's argument was ”If the whole world is illusory and everything can be refuted, who knows this, who sees this, who is this negator? How can that be refuted – this default Self who denies everything?”  

  

This Self-existing knowing is indubitable because it is evident as Self existence - I exist. No proof is needed, no argument is needed. It stands on its own, illuminating the transitory world appearance not only during waking and dream but as the light of Self-Awareness shining peacefully by Itself during sleep when objects are absent."
