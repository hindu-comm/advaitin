Dilip,



There is no possibility of ‘teaching through silence’. I think I may have posted this before but for the record here it is again:  
  

A guru \*cannot\* teach through silence. Enlightenment equates to self-knowledge and a teacher uses words to convey the pointers that bring this about. A teacher \*might\* respond to a question with silence only in the specific instance where any answer might mislead.



In the book 'Dialogues with Swami Dayananda', published by Sri Gangadhareswar Trust, 2000 (2nd ed). On page 61, he says:



"Knowledge cannot take place without a pramANa, a valid means of knowledge. There is no other way. A medium of communication is necessary and you must have the discipline, the saMskAra of that language, because the communication is in the form of words. The means of knowledge is in the form of words. And that alone culminates into knowledge.



"It is said, Lord dakShiNAmUrti conveyed by silence. He taught by mudrA, a sign made by position of fingers. mudrA here stands for language. In reality, He taught through words. If He was silent, all our upaniShads would have been in the form of blank pages! In fact silence is good for two reasons: either it is inconvenient to answer and therefore you do not answer or sometimes whichever way you answer is a problem, and so you do not answer. You become mauni, silent."



Notwithstanding Ramana's unquestioned abilities and achievements, he was not a sampradAya teacher and some of the things that he says are not in accord with traditional teaching as per Shankara. You cannot therefore quote him to substantiate this idea.



(I posted an article about the symbolism of the chin mudrA at [https://www.advaita-vision.org/chin-mudra/](https://www.advaita-vision.org/chin-mudra/).)

I don’t agree with the argument that Ramana Maharshi was not a Gnani. We cannot make any assertive statement about a Gnani unless we are Gnani ourselves. Until then we see him in the light of our mind which is imperfect.



But that is not the main point. If you feel elevated in the presence of a person, know that he is your Guru for that occasion. This was the stand taken by Nisargadatta Maharaj. **Ramamna Maharshi often remained silent and yet he answered many questions of the seekers in his silence.** A perfect example of - गुरोस्तु मौनम व्याख्यानं शिष्यास्ते छिन्न संशय:.



Each Gnani manifests with unique expressions. We have to learn the skill of testing and identifying one.
