Namaste

We also have the well-known example mentioned in the brihadAraNyaka upaniShad of a sage bAdhva communicating/teaching a student calked bAShkali without outwardly uttering words.

  

There are four levels of speech viz., vaikharI (vocalized), madhyamA (internal like in mental chatter), pashyantI (subtle and insight-based), and parA (equated to the source of even eternal vedic words). These levels of speech are accepted in the vedic tradition as in the rigvedic Asya vAmIya sUktam. But the last two are said to be accessible only to yogis and rishis.

  

While the straightforward method is of course to teach by appropriate use of words so that the shabda pramANa is operated, we cannot rule out exceptional cases where teaching occurs without outer i.e., audible vocalization at the vaikharI level i.e., at one of the other levels. Presumably the presence of the Guru gives a degree of quietness to the mind enabling such unusual methods of teaching.

  

But such exceptions as Sri Ramana Maharshi only serve to prove the rule that teaching through unfolding Shastra from a sAmpradAyavit is the standard method. Besides Sri Ramana Maharshi too taught and composed plenty of material. Many sAmpradAyavit acharyas like Swami Paramarthananda ji (a prominent disciple of Pujya Swamiji) often take Sri Ramana Maharshi's works like upadeshasAram and saddarshanam and unfold the same shAstra tAtparya. This indicates that the vision of Sri Ramana is fully in accordance with the Advaita sAmpradAya. This also shows that the acharya who teaches should be a sAmpradAyavit. Otherwise even the Brahma sUtras will yield only dvaita in the hands of vishiShTAdvaitin teachers for instance.

  

Even in Sri Ramana Maharshi's case, it is usually said that he \*taught\* through silence. Quite clearly it was not merely silence - the key operative word is \*taught\* - not so much the silence.
