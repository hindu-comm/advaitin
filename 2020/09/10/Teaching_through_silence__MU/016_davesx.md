While we wait for the scholar's translation of the verse, here's a commentary arising from this verse:

  

Verse 29 of Kena Upanishad gives the illustration of a timeless lightning flash of Gnan vanishing instantly from view of the mind (Indra) and senses (devas). However this flash of illumination has to be repeated many times until the Light of Self-Knowing becomes stable and steady. This permanent illumination comes only after constant and frequent reflection, thinking and contempletion (manana); hence the futility of Yogis to achieve it as Patanjali exhorts yogic practitioners to suppress thought in his famous aphorism 'yoga chitta vritti nirodha'.  

  

Even the first single flash of apperception is true Gnana, as one will never be mistaken again for there will be now be an apprehension of the true nature of things. However the instantaneous moment has already passed away due to attention lapsing in the trance of subconscious habit patterns ingrained since time immemorial. Hence it is recommended that the student of Gyana repeatedly reflect on removing the obstructions and refresh the clarity of Self to the point where it wavers no more and becomes a settled realization.  

  

As such, one must keep the insight activated no matter what may be the task at hand --eating, working, talking. This demands intermittent remindfullness throughout the day --not merely sitting to contemplate for a fraction of the waking period. This flash is an absence of thought and concepts which functions to divide the One Reality and for an infinitismal moment there is an apperception of the undivided infinite Brahman where all duality disappears.  

  

In summary, the first flash is repeated by associating it at every moment henceforth -- "This is Brahman, that is Brahman -- all this is Brahman -- until Brahman is seen in all its finite expressions: the cosmos in a grain of sand, heaven in a flower, eternity in a second -- to borrow from Blake".
