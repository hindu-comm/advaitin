There are three foundational texts recommended for studying Gyana (a) BrihadAranyaka Upanishad, (b) Panchadasi (c) Ashtavakra Gita.

  

Bhagavad Gita is an interpretation of Brihad Upanishad but geared towards the general public. Hence Sri Krishna expounds a mix of teachings customized for different levels of minds but can lead to confusion if not studied systematically. in the early chapters Bhavagan Krishna introduces religion, dualistic worship, mysticism and yoga as preliminary preparation for the more advanced stage of Gyana, declaring "Through Buddhi you reach me". Chapter 6 deals with meditation, it states "Yoga is for purification". This means it is not for truth but discipline. Throughout the text, it is clear one should not rely on Yoga, but rather on Buddhi (discrimination between real and unreal). It states after Dhyana yoga, while stilling and steadying the mind, one should progress into Vijnana yoga i.e. inquiry into world and then finally into Jnana yoga.  

  

Mandukya Upanishad is the pinnacle reached by human thought and reason. This text cannot be understood by reading alone: the commentary by Sankara or the Karika by Gaudapada are essential to comprehend this text. Mandukya takes for granted that one has read the Gita and other foundational works. It’s outstanding feature is new-- Avastatraya. When one has thoroughly understood Mandukya, it will be virtually impossible to defeat such a one in philosophical argument.
