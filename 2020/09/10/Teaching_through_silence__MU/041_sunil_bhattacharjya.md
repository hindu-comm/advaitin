Dear friends,

Nirvikalapa Samadhi is difficult and there is no doubt in that, but there is surely no artificiality in that. Some Brahmins of the time of Lord Buddha, had dificulty in understanding Advaita and they approached Lord Buddha to help them understand as to how a seeker can become one with the Brahman. Lord Buddha explained that to them in the easiest possible way ever and also taught them the meditation to achieve Paramprajnata -Samadhi, which is the same as the Nirvikalpa Samadhi. Lord Buddha said that he was an avatara of Lord Ram and we can see that what Lord Buddha taught is the same as what Lord Ram taught Hanumanji. Lord Ram told Hanumanji about the need to become Manah-shunya and Svabhava-shunya or Ahamkara-shunya. The great Bodhisattva and Bhumeeshvara Nagarjuna also said from the roof-top that Mahayana is not Nihilism, because Manah-shunya is only becoming adept at Amanana or cutting off attention to the aniccha or anitya or fleeting things of the phenomenal world. With the requisite Svadhyaya followed by Manana and dhyana on the Nirguna Brahmana (i.e., on the Avyaya state of Lord Krishna) and Amanana on the worldly things, as well as with the grace of the Guru and/or with the grace of Jagadguru Lord Krishna, eventually one is bound to achieve the Advaita aim. However Lord Krishna said that, as concentrating on the Nirguna Brahman is difficult, one can have the easier way to take his help by surrendering to him with devotion.

  

Adi Shankaracharya had advised his followers to follow the Guru-pranipata, Svashyaya, Manana and Nididhyarasa route, shown by Brahmarshi Yajnavalkya, who was a direct disciple of Surya-Nataya. Here if I may add, Hanumanji was also a direct disciple of Surya-Narayana.  

  

Sadaji and other scholars of the group may also add whatever, according to them, is needed to be added here, and I shall be thankful.
