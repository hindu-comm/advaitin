Namaste,

  

The anugraha of the Atma referred to in the kaTha mantra 1.2.20 / muNDaka mantra 3.2.3 is not something different from the sAdhaka's effort of abheda anusandhAna, or the focussed practice of identification with Brahman, to the exclusion of anything else.

  

It is not the case that a person who is striving for AtmajnAna fails to attain it because of the lack of Brahman's grace. Why? Because the Atma / Brahman is ever attained. As shankarAchArya says in the muNDAka bhAShya,यमेव परमात्मानमेव एषः विद्वान् वृणुते प्राप्तुमिच्छति, तेन वरणेन एष पर आत्मा लभ्यः, नान्येन साधनान्तरेण, नित्यलब्धस्वभावत्वात्।

  

Anandagiri's TIkA saysपरमात्माऽस्मीत्यभेदानुसंधानं वरणम्। तेन वरणेनैष आत्मा लभ्यो भवति। बहिर्मुखेन तु शतशोऽपि श्रवणादौ क्रियमाणे न लभ्यते। अतः परमात्माऽस्मीत्यभेदानुसंधानं परमात्मभजनं पुरस्कृत्यैव श्रवणादि सम्पादनीयमिति भावः। अथवाऽयमेव परमात्मानं वृणुते तेन परमात्मना मुमुक्षुरूपव्यवस्थितेन वरणेनाभेदानुसंधानलक्षणेन प्रार्थनेन कृत्वा लभ्यः परमात्मैव मुमुक्षुरूपव्यवस्थित इत्यभेदानुसंधानेनैव लभ्यो न कर्मणेत्यर्थथः।

  

Anandagiri AchArya comments on the word varaNam in the bhAShya to mean the anusandhAnam "I am the paramAtma". Interestingly he goes on to provide an alternative explanation. He says the paramAtma being none other than the mumukshu, when the mumukshu practices abheda anusandhAna, he "attains" the paramAtma, ie himself. Thus attaining Brahman is the same as identifying with Brahman.  

  

Thus, this differentiation between Atma jnAna and brahmajnAna as two different realisations, with the latter only occurring because of a grace that is different from the mumukshu identifying with Brahman, spoken of in the email below, is not supported in Vedanta.

  

Regards,

Venkatraghavan
