Thanks for the above passages. The words of Anandagiri Acharya // अथवाऽयमेव परमात्मानं वृणुते तेन परमात्मना मुमुक्षुरूपव्यवस्थितेन // (Brahman is itself the aspirant longing/endeavoring to realize Brahman) so nicely elucidate the famous Brihadaranyaka 1.4.10 passage‘ब्रह्म वा इदमग्र आसीत्तदात्मानमेवावेदहं ब्रह्मास्मीति, तस्मात्तत्सर्वमभवत्’\[Even before realization, it was Brahman alone. It (Brahman) realized its own self as 'I am Brahman' and thereby shed its notion of finitude.'  The Upanishad itself so clearly says that it is Brahman that puts in the necessary effort to realize itself as Brahman. This has been summarized in this famous passage by Advaitins: 'ब्रह्मैवस्वाविद्ययासंसरतीव, स्वविद्यया मुच्यत इव' \[Brahman alone, owing to its own avidya, is as though bound and due to vidya of itself, is as though released.\] That is why in Advaita the realization is not of the order: 'I was bound till now and henceforth I am liberated.' On the other hand, the realization of the liberated man is articulated by Shankara thus:

  

 पूर्वसिद्धकर्तृत्वभोक्तृत्वविपरीतं हि त्रिष्वपि कालेष्वकर्तृत्वाभोक्तृत्वस्वरूपं ब्रह्माहमस्मि,नेतः पूर्वमपि कर्ता भोक्ता वा अहमासम्, नेदानीम्, नापि भविष्यत्काले — इति ब्रह्मविदवगच्छति ; एवमेव च मोक्ष उपपद्यते ; ४.१.२३. इति ।

  

\[Contrary to the previous thinking that I am doer-enjoyer,*in all the three periods of time I am Brahman that is neither doer nor enjoyer*. Before, I was not doer-enjoyer, nor now, nor even in the future will I be doer-enjoyer - such is the realization of a Knower.\] BSB 4.1.23.
