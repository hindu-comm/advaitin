Conventionally sat stands for existence, and asat stands for non-existence. Chandogya Up. the statement refers to asat as non-existence.

Mithyaa is defined as 'sat asat vilakshanam', as it is neither sat nor asat.

  

Most of the criticisms of Advaita by dvaitins and vishishtaadvaintins rest on the fact that they consider that there are only two choices - If it is not sat it has to be asat and if it is not asat it has to be sat and from their point, there is no third category.

  

In Advaita Siddhi Shree Madhusudhana Saraswati provides five definitions of mithyaa.

Asat is defined as no locus for existence as in vandhyaa putraH.

  

In Vivekachudamani - Shankara defines maayaa as

  

sannapyasanna ubhayaatimikaano

saangapuyasanga ubhayatimikaano

bhinnapyabhinna ubhayaatmikaano

mahat bhuutaa anirvacaneeya ruupa.

  

One cannot say it is sat, nor one can say it is asat, nor it is sat and asat, etc., it is a wonder and inexplicable.
