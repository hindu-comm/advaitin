Now my response to my learned friend Sundarji.

  

Somewhere I did mention that Advaita and Yoga are not mutually exclusive and introversion or Dyana yoga is a foundational subset but merely as a preparation to rid the mind of distractions so attention is finely tuned to listen and contemplate the teachings of Vedanta.

  

You sir have taken this a bit further but I'll throw in a few pointers.  

  

Manas or mind, as I may define it, is a bundle of thoughts, beliefs and ideas implanted in memory; it is forever restless and in movement constantly projecting these ideas to mentally construct a meaningful world of diversity for its own exploits.  

  

We talk about a silent and clear mind but this is not strictly correct. Only when mind pauses is there Silence that can awaken Pure Consciousness, but for untrained minds this infinitesimal gap closes up immediately due to ingrained habitual thought patterns and vasanas. We are forever stringing up beads of ideas to create and preserve the fire band of an imaginary 'I-thought' or 'self-identity'. This trance of selfhood can only be broken by resting repeatedly in the gap of no-mind.  

  

Shrimad Bhagavatam (23.1) focuses on another gap; that which is between two states:

  

“AT THE POINT OF PASSING FROM SLEEP TO WAKING, ONE SHOULD TRY TO REALISE THE BRAHMAN.”.  

  

Whoever understands this will understand Vedanta. Before the waking state appears there was no I-thought in sleep. At the elusive junction between sleep and waking, as between two thoughts, the nature of ParaAtman reveals Itself in a flash.
