Namaste Davesirjue

  

"When the world is an expression of Brahman, there is no need for seclusion and to reject the world."

  

You may want to check out the gItA 13.10 commentary of Sri Shankara.  

  

The value of vivikta-desha-sevitvaM (lit. resorting to solitary spots) is extolled in the gItA for \*GYAna yoga\*.Sri Shankara says - 'resorting to solitary spots' like temples, forests, rivers is conducive to self-knowledge.

  

Besides, Shankara bhagavatpAda considers a lifestyle of outer renunciation, i.e., sannyAsa (monastic) lifestyle, a \*sine qua non\* for paripakva GYAnam.

  

Also while Brahman is no doubt 'everything', one cannot jump the gun and operationalize that fact and ignore the need to make tough choices in lifestyle as recommended by Sri Shankara, until that GYAnam is attained. In his gItA bhAShya, he comments on 'aratir-jana-saMsadi' (lit. rejection of or aversion to unrefined, unregenerate people) and talks of being very selective and choosing only the company of mature and refined individuals who are a help on the path of GYAnam, effectively an endorsement of traditional sannyAsa.

  

In other words, the view of Sri Shankara bhagavatpAda is that, with some clauses, the need for seclusion and rejection of the world is the norm for GYAnam.

  

The caveat is that a life of karma yoga is necessary to get the necessary mental preparedness before adopting the sannyAsa lifestyle mentioned above.
