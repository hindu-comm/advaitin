Namaste Venkatraghavan ji

Thank you for that lucid post.

  

You pointed out that the dvaita interpretation of katha 1.2.23 as  
"ParamAtman .... is attained only by him \*whom the Self chooses\*." is different from the traditional Advaita sense.

  

I recollect a conversation with a Madhva friend who suggested that 'even if you do all the shravana, manana, nidhidhyAsanam, still, GYAnam will elude you unless there is the "Grace of Narayana".

  

In other words, the dvaita perspective is that Ishvara is rendered jobless in the advaitic interpretation, if SMN is regarded as complete and sufficient for GYAnam, since the Lord's intercession is ostensibly rendered superfluous in the advaita GYAna mArga.

  

I suggested that for advaitins, Ishvara or the Self's 'Grace' operates \*all the way\* in the journey and not just as an addendum.

  

As VC says manuShyatvaM, mumuxutvaM and mahApuruSha saMshrayaH are all daivaanugrahahetukaM - a human birth, a desire for liberation and the refuge of a mahAtmA (who teaches GYAnam) are all due to Ishvara's Grace alone. The Grace which make everything the way it is.

  

It's not as if the advaitins view the spiritual journey as some kind of mechanistic trick or technique like operating a slot machine and yanking the levers of shravanam, mananam and nidhidhyaasanam. On the contrary, it is an intelligent process involving the Intelligent Being, Ishvara. It's just that we don't view Ishvara as a whimsical tyrant who chooses to dispense grace on his inscrutable whim. Because that (dispensing Grace as per his whimsical choices) would amount to vaiShamya-nairGhRNya doSha - the defect of partiality and cruelty.

  

Om
