Namaste Raghav ji,

Precisely. The entire principle of cause and effect is sustained by Ishvara anugraha, as the taittirIya upaniShad says - भीषास्माद्वातः पवते भीषोदेति सूर्यः.

  

As we admit, the very advaita vAsana is a result of Ishvara anugraha.

  

Thus for every step of the way - from the initial impulse to proceed down the path of advaita due to these advaita vAsanA-s, to performing actions in a desireless manner, to attaining the purity and clarify of mind as a result, to accruing sufficient puNyam from many lives to get a meeting with a guru, to getting the opportunity for shravaNam, mananam and nidhidhyAsanam, to overcoming obstacles in the path to jnAna, to attaining jnAna - Ishvara's anugraha is necessary.

  

However, this anugraha, as you rightly pointed out is not a result of Ishvara's personal favouritism or likes and dislikes - it is universally, eternally available. What each person receives is entirely proportional to the individual's effort.

  

Thus, moksha is not something up to Ishvara's discretion. Anyone who attains jnAna will be a mukta.

  

The way I see it, the misunderstanding of the words यम्‌ and एषः in the kaTha mantra is the common reason behind two things that we are observing in the list -

  

1\) In this thread, it has introduced IshvarAnugraha as another requirement to moksha, causing a dichotomy to emerge between jnAna and Ishvara's anugraha.

  

2\) In another thread, it has manifested as a dismissal of sannyAsa and yoga on the grounds that it is ignoring the world, because the significance of the words "he who wants the Atma alone" has been missed.

  

Regards,

Venkatraghavan
