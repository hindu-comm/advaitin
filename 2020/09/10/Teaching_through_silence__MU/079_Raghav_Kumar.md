You may be unwittingly missing an important point or two in your rejection of the sannyAsa or nivRtti lifestyle (we can conflate these two for this discussion) which is mandated in the Advaita tradition.

  

Sri Krishna says that sannyAsastu mahAbAho duhkham Aptum ayogataH implying that outer renunciation through sannyAsa lifestyle is not possible without prior karma yoga. You are reiterating this idea which you seem committed to (of maintaining grandchildren and the like, as you approvingly quoted from the Mahabharata.) So no outer renunciation is possible for such a person. Next the other type , the GYAnI , sees brahman as everything, so no renunciation for him either. This is what you imply.

  

This binary thinking is incorrect as you would appreciate if you examine why Shankara so clearly mandates sannyAsa lifestyle (outer renunciation) for many who are in a third category of being not jnAnIs but not in the need of a pravRtti lifestyle of actively engaging in the pursuit of wealth and object-based happiness. For such \*mature\* jiGYAsu-s, outer renunciation is clearly enjoined and these people are not in the category of being jobless or incapable of earning a livelihood or procreating etc.

  

Also, even if we assume that the Buddha prematurely left off things which he still considered real at that point, rather thoughtlessly walking off, as you suggest - have you considered why, he came back and approved monkhood for thousands who went to him for guidance. Even to his own son , Rahula, he does not tell him to "mentally renounce" and continue to live in the grand Licchavi palace in Lumbini itself and support grandchildren etc. - he instead gave his son monastic vows.

  

A moot point is that the human tendency to hold on to wealth and the avenues for security and pleasure from sense objects and people, may make the mind rationalize it's attachments as being compatible with Advaita - since 'everything is brahman, so what is there to renounce' etc.

  

That is why vividiShA sannyAsa, i.e., a lifestyle of outer renunciation even prior to jnAnam and for the sake of paripakva jnAnam, is mandated in the Advaita tradition.
