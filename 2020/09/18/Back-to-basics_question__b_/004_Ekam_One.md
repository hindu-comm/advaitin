Thank you for your response and also the comments. No special reason to not mention my own understanding. Just wanted to hear positions of different people, before this thread turns into a "debate" around one particular hypothesis.  

  

Now that you asked, here are my views. (I am in no way claiming that these are correct).  

  

**Main Question:**

**Why should one aspire for work towards enlightenment?  **

  

We should NOT aspire for nor work towards a goal called Enlightenment. Once we start seeing beyond the mundane, a process of transformation sets in within us.  It goes on by itself. Some times it picks up speed faster. Some times it slows down or stops. Our job is to let this process of transformation happen with trust and restful patience. All Guru's, Scriptures, Yogas help based on our characteristics (vasanas?) . We should always focused on this moment and this day and enjoy the transformation as it happens.  

  

In spite of all the scriptures i have been reading for last 10+ years, Gurus i am following with some amount of Shraddha, it is hard for me to believe that Punarjanma is a thing. I tend to think that the vasana's are the result of chemical compositions in us, which are result of many random events and circumstances. And these chemical compositions, by the way, are a play of Brahman. However, No need to decipher them too hard. When we are progressing, these disturbances go down. (F-I-R)  

  

**Auxiliary questions**  

**a) When all is the Brahman, and when the individuality is not really real, whats the point of realizing?**

**  **

Actually, there is no point in realizing. We just have to let the process of spiritual transformation take its course in us because it cleanses us of the impurities and disturbances in ourselves and let us experience the Brahman in its purest form. Again, this is not a goal to strive for. but Trust in the process and Restful patience is the key.
Guru's, Scriptures and Yoga's are aids in this process, to facilitate the right conditions for transformation.  

  

**b) is it possible that this desire for Moksha, another trick of ego, to feel special and be different from the "normal" people ? - Sort of the "itch to be at the edge of Gaussian curve"**

  

In stead of facilitating this transformation with Trust and Restful patience, if we want to speed up the process or get into a race, then it is indeed another "desire" and creates a strong disturbance in us. The language of disturbance could be different but it is a disturbance still. Some may even end up making this pursuit of Moksha into another dogmatic religion at a personal level.
