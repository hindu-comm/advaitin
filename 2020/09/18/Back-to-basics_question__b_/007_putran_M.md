Namaskaram Ekam-ji,

  

Without "enlightenment"/Jnana, the jiva is in a state of ignorance, wrongly identifying Self with body-mind-world. As a consequence the jiva is searching for its true nature in the wrong things, bouncing between pleasure and pain, desire and fear. So, if you are searching for something but working to find it in wrong things because yourvery idea of self is messed up, then ideally you should work to find the right thing you are searching for - which is primarily resolving your self-knowledge. That may involve other preliminary work preparing your body-mind.

  

Ignorance is the cause for your running here and there; Knowledge is the solution - so the jiva should aspire to work towards Jnana. Attaining Jnana liberates this jiva from ignorance and hence from the running-around.

  

What is the point of realizing it is rope and not snake? It was always a rope only. What does it matter that theperson realizes the truth? Only that person jumping up and down in panic can tell. Perhaps he may think that is not a good situation and want liberation from it; perhaps he may think it is his natural condition to be in this chaos.

  

The last question on "desire for Moksha" (mumukshutvam) being "trick of ego". See, if you suffer and realize it as suffering, then you wonder "Am I really bound like this? How can I find freedom from this wretchedness?" Not everyone thinks in this manner; but we have examples in the shastras of mumukshuswho were desperate for liberation and sought the guru. The common advaitin is happy at home watching a Criket match on tv.

  

With regard to your understanding of punarjanma, etc. We get such knowledge from shastras, at least as the orthodox traditions interpret them. So it is not regarded as a point for logical determination, although the traditional person uses logic to support what is *known from shastra*.

  

  

thollmelukaalkizhu
