Namaste,

  

Ideally, we should not be affected by what is materialy good or bad. We should observe the good and bad deeds of others as well as the proper and improper sayings or writings of others**;** and we don't have to praise or criticize anyone. Thai is what a saint does, and he is happy and contented.  

  

However, the ordinary mortals like me are far from bring ideal. Many a times, my knowledge and experience goads me to put forward my views. Probably the emotions get better of me in such occasions. For example, when I came to know that Shri Madhvacharya criticised Adi Shankaracharya very badly and even called the latter a demon incarnated, albeit posthumously**;** I felt very sad and I checked if the former was right. Then I found that Shri Madhvacharya was utterly wrong, and not well read. Further, he tried to apply his knowledge of Paninian Sanskrit to pre-Paninian texts, without discretion. Whenever I get a chance I tell people that Shri Madhavacharya's criticisms of Adi Shankaracharya
are utterly wrong. Similarly, when I find people criticising Lord Buddha as a nihilist and non-Hindu, I do get upset and try to tell people that Lord Buddha gave the highest Vedantic teachings.

  

Many a times, people give quick statements that Sankhya is Dualist philosophy, ignoring that there is both dualist and non-dualist Sankhya. Similarly there is both the dualist and non-dualist interpretation of Patanjali's Yogasutra. In one of these two, there is the need for Ishvara-pranipata. When RamaKrishna Paramhamsha wanted to get Advaita initiation, his favorite deity Mother Kali told him that she would like him to get initiated to advaita, but during the Vedantic meditation he always saw mother Kali before his eyes. Then his advaita guru helped him out of that situation.  

  

Now coming to the queries of Ekamji, kindly permit me to tell him that as long as we are attached to the ever-changing creations of the Prakriti, we are going through the cycle of birth and death, and there will be the continuity of the life of the pleasures and pains. Many of the great mahatmas of the past had let us know how to get ourselves out of this rut of the life-cycle of birth and death, within Prakriti, through the knowledge of Advaita and Sadhana. In this forum we are sharing our views, but for a novice it may always be better to contact an advaitin guru and learn Advaita at his feet and read some beginner's texts like the Tattvabodha by the great teacher, Adi Shankaracharya.

  

Regards  

Sunil K. Bhattacharjya
