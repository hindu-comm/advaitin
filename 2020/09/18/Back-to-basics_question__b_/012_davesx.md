Yes Putranji - That is the classical view of mainstream Hinduism.  

  

At the moment of demise of the nāma-rūpa, one's consciousness starts the process of internalizing and sinks into the subtle layers of mind eventually shedding everything and falls into deep timeless sleep in what is called the the causal realm.  

  

But how one dies make a difference, because after the death of the nāma-rūpa, although consciousness remains, it can be awake—conscious of itself—or not. Generally, after the death of thenāma-rūpa,
consciousness is passive, it is not conscious of itself but rather objective. As such consciousness awakens in a new body-mind apparatus with no direct recollection or memory of the previous, excepts seeds of potentiality.

  

Both views are complementary; what is dead is past and gone and future is not yet. For this one can only interrogate authentic Shastras.
