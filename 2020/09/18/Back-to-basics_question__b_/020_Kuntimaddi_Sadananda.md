Thanks for your kind mail. Most of these questions really get answered if one listens to the Vedanta systematically at the feet of a sampradaya teacher.

  

A simple answer is all chemicals are just inert. As per Vedanta, they come under the grossest of the five elements of the creation. Gross elements are formed from subtle elements by the process of panceekaranam - division and recombination process.

  

Brahman, the infinite is indicated by the words - sat - chit - ananda, existence-consciousness-happiness, or limitless. In the gross elements, Brahma expresses itself only as existence or only existence gets reflected by the gross elements - All the chemicals that were pointed out only expressing the existence aspect of Brahman. In the subtle elements like mind, not only the existence but consciousness is also expressed or reflected - no ananda. For ananda also gets reflected, the mind has to be quiet and unagitated. Then ananda also gets reflected.

These are experienced in the waking, dream, and deep sleep states.

  

It is similar to white light - with its seven components. An object looks red because only the red wavelength is reflected while the rest of the wavelengths are absorbed. Similarly, an object looks blue since all other colors except blue are absorbed. That is the law of reflection.

  

Is the mind different from the Brain which is nothing but some chemicals? Look at cognitive sciences and you will get all sorts of answers. Vedanta considers the mind as subtle material and gets affected indirectly by the type of gross food that we eat. Hence Krishna talks about satvic, rajasic and tamasic foods in the 18th Ch. of Geeta.

  

Most of these aspects are discussed in my talks 'Introduction of Vedanta', and 'Advanced Vedanta' talks series on you-tube. There are also talks on 'Geeta Navaneetam', 'Essence of Geeta, and the Application of Geeta in Daily life.

  

Do not look for a guru. Guru will come when the mind is ready. Keep the mind purified by karma, bhakti, and Upasana and many Satsang youtube talks are available. You will discover your guru whom you feel intensely that you have learned from him and feel grateful for the teaching.

  

Gurudev Swami Chinmayanandaji used to say - Do not go in search of a guru, Guru shall come. A flower does not go in search of a bee. The bee shall come when the flower is ready with full blossom - That is the law of nature.

  

My 2c.
