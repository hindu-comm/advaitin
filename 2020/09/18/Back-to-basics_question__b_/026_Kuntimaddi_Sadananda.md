PraNAms to all

  

Once someone asked Swami Tejomayanandaji - if he believes in rebirth. Swamiji replied - I do not believe in this birth itself and what to say about rebirth.

  

na jaayate mRiyatevaa kadaachit - Never it is born nor dies.

  

The Geetopadesha starts because Arjuna was said - vishaada - that he is going to kill the great people like Bheeshma and his teacher, Drona, etc. Krishna's first statement was -panditaaH - the wise people do not cry for those who are dead and for those who are going to die - gataa suuna gaataasumscha - na anusochanti panditaaH. He then declares - there was never a time I was not there nor you or all these kings that are assembled here to fight, not will three be a time we will be absent in the future. The chemicals of course undergo continuous transformations, while mass appears to be conserved.

  

Interestingly Krishna makes absolute law of conservation in the sloka 2-16 - naa sato vidyatee bhaavo naabhaavo vidyate sataH - that which exists can never cease to exists and that which is non-existent can never come into existence- and He adds that those who understand this are wise people - panditaaH.

  

Those who are interested can see one view about the consciousness-mind and brain. One can find a lot more articles in Cognitive Sciences. For those who are interested - I have done 'Critical Analysis of Vedanta Paribhasha' which Dennis Waite has edited and put it on his website.

  

Yes, ultimately what you believe in counts. Instead of belief, Indian Philosophy calls it as 'faith' - which is somewhat similar to 'Hypothesis' in Science. Until it is self-proved it will remain as 'working hypothesis' for investigation.

  

Hari Om!

Sadananda
