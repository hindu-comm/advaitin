Hari OM

  

I found Swami Paramanada's words immensely beneficial and insightful and sharing few.  
He was the youngest disciple of Swami Vivekananda, initiated into Sanyasa at the age of eighteen,

and despite decades since the words have been written or uttered, have a freshness to them and keep illumining.

  

\* "People sometimes imagine that the Vedanta (principles) are all and that when one has learned them and  

  can talk glibly about them, nothing further is necessary. This is as great a mistake as to suppose that looking

 at a well-spread table is all that is needed to satisfy hunger. Unless we not only eat the foot, but also digest and

 assimilate it so that it becomes part of our bodies, nourishing and preserving them, all the food in the world would

be of little use to us.

\[..so too..\]Vedanta should become part of our daily lives; .. we put them into practice and live by them every moment

of our existence. When we can thus assimilate them, they will nourish and sustain our spiritual nature, just as food

preserves our physical frame"

----------------------------------------------------------------------

  

\* If I am intensely hungry, I am not interested in seeking opinions and views of others about the food around, but keen to eat the food.

Conversely, if I am seeking out opinions and views of others about food around etc., it is proof enough I am not that hungry!

When one is intensely "immersed" while driving searching directions, unconsciously volume of radio is turned down and talking stops.

What to talk- no, less-talk about highest sadhana might be!

  

May be in a little rajasic way, but one that left deep impression onme, is book by brilliant physicist Richard Feynman:

"What do you care what others think?"

  

\* Attachment to one's identity is very, very strong. Almost amusing, that during my first year of Engineering itself I kept saying I am engineer!.

Equally amusing, that long after I retire, and when no activity of engineering done then, and yet to claim oneself as an engineer!

Or whatever profession!

  

One joke of Swami Bodhatmanandaji: At the end of a Vedanta talk, while exiting, one person accidentally steps on the toes of another.

Even though apologized, that person was called names! And offender got hurt and upset and went to Swamiji for guidance.

Swamiji replied:"Entire hour of the talk, I have been telling you are Sat-Chit-Ananda, but you did not take it as real.

But when other person told you once, that you are fool or whatever, you are taking that as real!".Not really a joke, but my daily experience.
