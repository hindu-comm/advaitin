Yes.

The deduction of dark matter from some observations such as certain gravitational effects that otherwise cannot be explained, does indeed fit the template of arthApatti.

  

The arthApatti will hold unless a flaw is shown. One flaw could be that there is some other factor which comes to light after many years which explains it. Until then this arthApatti is held to be true.

  

In the stock example - fat devadatta does not eat during daytime - ergo, he must be eating at night.

  

A respected acharya narrates a joke that there was one Kartar Singh, a great wrestler from Punjab, who sat on a 40 day "fast" during daytime to register some protest, but he did not loose any weight. The arthApatti that he was eating at night, was indeed true in his case. When the said wrestler was confronted about his dishonesty, he innocently claimed in his defence that he abstained from "bhojanam" (like Dal, Roti, vegetables, etc) but he only ate the sacred sweet halwa "khaDA" prasad from the temple. (A rich dish made by cooking semolina with equal parts of ghee, sugar and dry fruits.) Since this was merely "prasadam" from the temple, it did not count as "bhojanam"!

  

In all such arthApatti, a flaw or implicit wrong assumption, may render it incorrect, in the future. Until then it is held to be valid.
