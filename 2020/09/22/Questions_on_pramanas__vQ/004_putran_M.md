Namaskaram to all,

  

Thanks for the clarifications. I understand as follows:

  

In Arthapatti

  

1\. The event or object (A) in question is not in our perceived world (of pratyaksha pramana, hence beyond anumana or upamana as well).

  

2\. The existence and presumed activity of this object (A) will explain something (B) in our perceived world.

  

3\. There is some basis (C) in our perceived world that makes us assume that this hypothetical object or event (A) impacts the world as we expect and therefore causes (B) to happen.

  

4\. There is no other explanation for (B) that we can ascertain based on our known or perceived world. (This non-apprehension or anupalabdhi presently demonstrates the non-existence of other explanations *in our knowledge-world,* subject to correction if we find another explanation later.)

  

5\. By 1 to 4, we can postulate existence of A to explain B. Thus arthapatti is applicable in this case.

  

6\. It is possible that we could be applying arthapatti wrongly if in fact there is some flaw or other hidden factors skewing our knowledge of A, B or C. The pramana or method of knowledge is not wrong; just we did not know enough of the situation to know when or how to use arthapatti.

  

  

1, 2, 4, 5, 6 are straight-forward.

  

With 3, there is the question whether there are any restrictions, conditions or requirements about what constitutes proper basis (C) for assuming that (A) explains (B).

  

Dark matter is supposed to be no different than the matter we can see and experiment with. In our perceived world, we know of matter (a) causing an effect (b). We want to explain a parallel Effect (B) in our perceived world, so based on knowledge of a -\> b and similarity or congruence of b to B, we postulate the existence of Dark Matter (A) to explain B.

  

In this case, the basis (C) is the perception-based knowledge of a -\> b and b ~ B which allows us to postulate A such that A ~ a and A -\> B.

  

Does arthapatti require C in some strong sense like above? Do we have to demonstrate existence of a, b, B, a-\>b and b~B before postulating A with corresponding relations?

  

Let me give a different example:

  

Suppose in the Aryan Migration theory, someone postulates migration by *small bands* of 'aryan men/warriors' as the only way to explain why there is no archaeological evidence of the migration, since they reason that a large-scale migration would leave archaeological evidence. So within that postulation of small bands is the assumption that such a migration by small bands could escape archaeological investigation. The postulation does explain the lack of archaeological evidence. But is this valid application of arthapatti, or do they have to demonstrate known cases of migration by small bands (of the nature they predict with these 'aryans') somewhere in recorded history for which people have searched for and found no archaeological evidence? Is some such perceptible basis (C) necessary (and objectively demonstrable or recorded) for arthapatti, or can we go with our intuition, mathematics, theory and "sum of experience" to make these postulations?
