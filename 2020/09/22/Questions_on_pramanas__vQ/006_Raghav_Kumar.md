Two points to be made here.  

First is one small feedback.

When a hetu smoke (b) is perceived, that implies there is fire (a).

  

We therefore write b =\> a

      b implies a

Please note that we write, b implies a, despite fire 'a' being the causal factor for smoke 'b'. We don't say a =\> b or

fire =\> smoke. Fire implies smoke is incorrect.

Because there can be a smokeless fire as in a gas stove.

So we have to say

smoke (b) --\> fire (a)

(I am using -\> or alternatively =\> for implication)

  

I think you may have accidentally reversed the terms a and b in your last post. You wrote-

"Dark matter is supposed to be no different than the matter we can see and experiment with. In our perceived world, we know of matter (a) causing the (\*observable\*) effect (b). We want to explain a parallel Effect (B) in our perceived world, so based on knowledge of a -\> b...."

  

My corrective note on the above para -

We should rather say our knowledge in our perceived World is that:

b -\> a

(effect is observed/perceived) ---\> (cause must exist)

  

In the dark matter matter, we have :

(Gravitational changes observed) ---\> (some form of matter must be there in the intervening space)

  

The arthApatti kicks in because by anupalabdhi, we say (that no regular matter is detected), then by arthApatti, we assert there must be some other form of matter, because otherwise those gravitational changes are impossible - anyathA anupapattih.

  

Second point is about the C inference you mentioned in step 3. You are right that the arthApatti pramANa has sometimes been called "two step inference" for that very reason viz., a regular anumAna of the 'C' type you mentioned, is implicitly assumed to already hold good, even before applying arthApatti.

  

In the stock examples of "pIno devadattaH divA na bhunkte", the implicit \*anumAna\* 'C' would be that

Whenever a man is observed to grow fat or maintain weight (b), he must be eating food (a).

So there is b--\>a

This C has to be strong and defect-free for subsequent arthApatti to be possible based on using C as a building block.

  

Then in arthApatti, the \*absence\* of daytime eating is observed despite the man growing fat (\*anupalabdhi\* used) and the conclusion (through arthApatti) is reached that nighttime eating is going on w.r.t the man.
