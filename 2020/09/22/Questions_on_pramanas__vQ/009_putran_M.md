Consider 3 cases.

  

1\. We see smoke in the forest today. We see fire tomorrow. 
Perception: Smoke -\> Fire.

  

2\. We see smoke and fire together today. Perception: Smoke+ Fire.

  

3\. We see Fire today and Smoke tomorrow. Perception: Fire -\> Smoke.

  

  

In perception 1, fire follows smoke. Even if I perceive this sequence in several instances, it will not tell me that fire precedes smoke, fire causes smoke, or even that fire is concurrent with smoke. So the perception smoke -\> fire does not lead to vyapti smoke =\> fire, in either the concurrent or preceding sense. It only tells us fire follows smoke which may mislead us to think "fire =\> (preceding) smoke".

  

Both 2 and 3 affirm fire (either concurrently or as preceding) the very moment smoke is perceived. These are the perceptions (fire + smoke or fire then smoke) that can allow us to know vyapti in the sense intended. (We will also need perception of fire and no smoke to get fuller knowledge and understand the other direction is false.)

  

Now typically Fire -\> Smoke is an easier sequence to discuss vyapti with a lay person, in terms of causality. That is why I never thought much of using that in my descriptions of the problem.

  

More technically, there is the question: Can we perceive two objects simultaneously? I think Indian philosophies differ in this and am not sure which side Vedanta falls on. If we take the position that we can only perceive sequentially, then to know smoke =\> fire, we have to have the perception fire-\>smoke whenever our timeframe is extended; thus observing fire -\> smoke (and no contradiction) is necessary to ascertain that wherever smoke is, fire also/already is.

  

If we can observe two things simultaneously, then we need perception of fire+smoke (and for fuller knowledge that converse is false, fire without smoke). That becomes perceptual basis for vyapti: wherever smoke, there also fire.



> > My understanding is that the hetu-sAdhya sambandha is not
> > necessarily correlated with a kArya-kAraNa sambandha.

Yes, that is fine. There is a logical ordering we can put between A and B based on the vyapti relation. We can say A -\> B if A pervades B or A contains B or A causes B. (Maybe others think this ordering should be thought in reverse; I take the direction from causality examples where sadhya "causes" hetu.)

  

And the point is that this ordering should be confirmedby our perception. Inference is built upon this. If we did not have direct perception *of the order* in existence, then our inference will be a postulation at best. One cannot simply perceive "smoke then fire" and infer "fire precedes or is concurrent with smoke".
