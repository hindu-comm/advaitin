Namaskaram,

  

I thought more aboutthis.

  

Sometimes, we see fire and smoke together: F+S.

  

Sometimes, we see fire without smoke. ("F-S")

  

Both of these are valid perceptions.

  

But do we ever see smoke and not fire? ("S-F")

  

Yes.

  

Anumana however is the pramana that informs us that when we see S, there is F+S even if F is not visible.

  

This is because there is vyapti: F pervades S.

  

How do we know vyapti? It is to be deduced from the perceived world.

  

The three instances in our perceived world are F-S, F+S and S-F. Why should F-S and F+S be used to negate the implications of perception S-F (that is, that smoke can also occur without fire)?

  

One way to explain this dichotomy is as follows.

  

Suppose in our perceived world, we have found that whenever a thorough investigation is conducted, S-F has always been shown as being F+S though F was not visible. For example, we find F-\>S (we found the cause of smoke as a preceding fire) or we 'felt' the smoke and found it to be hot, hence there is "fire in it". Therefore in our (thoroughly investigated) perceived world, we only have F+S or F-S and this is our basis for deducing vyapti.

  

From here, if we see what we think is smoke (without otherwise thorough investigation), we can conclude that there is fire also, subject to correction if our initial perception of smoke is false.

  

(In this example, smoke is by definition tied to fire (through F-\>S and by its heat property). So S and F should represent other things where they are not necessarily connected by definition or causality etc. See Raghav-ji's example on X, Y, Z)

  

There is however the important point of how the perceived world is *thoroughly investigated* so that every instance of S-F is shown really to be F+S.

  

Why so? Because up front, S-F is as valid as F-S and F+S. We cannot just see S-F and conclude ontologically (?) F+S in every case.

  

This inner knowledge via other types of pratyaksha is key to our knowing vyapti. The yogi calls something alaukika pratyaksha. In our case, the perception F-\>S (F causes S/F precedes S) or perception of F in S (from definitional properties like temperature) are such special knowledge in the *perceived* world that form the basis for vyapti.
