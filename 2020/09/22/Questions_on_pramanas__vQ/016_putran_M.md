Also regarding the following from the subsequent mail:

  

Quote

  

Suppose in our perceived world, we have found that whenever a thorough investigation is conducted, S-F has always been shown as being F+S though F was not visible. For example, we find F-\>S (we found the cause of smoke as a preceding fire) or we 'felt' the smoke and found it to be hot, hence there is "fire in it". Therefore in our (thoroughly investigated) perceived world, we only have F+S or F-S and this is our basis for deducing vyapti.

  

From here, if we see what we think is smoke (without otherwise thorough investigation), we can conclude that there is fire also, subject to correction if our initial perception of smoke is false.

  

There is however the important point of how the perceived world is*thoroughly investigated*so that every instance of S-F is shown really to be F+S.

  

Why so? Because up front, S-F is as valid as F-S and F+S. We cannot just see S-F and conclude ontologically (?) F+S in every case.

  

Unquote

  

Here the perceptual basis for vyapti is the perceived world of thoroughly investigated instances. We find that only F+S and F-S are true and S-F false in this domain. This becomes the ground for our vyapti-*hypothesis* that in the world that lies beyond our ability to thoroughly investigate, there also S-F is always false. This does bring up the question whether anumana is really pramana for the "real/full" world or is it only pramana in the context of our perceptual basis? Do we have any means of knowing the validity of vyapti in a total-world sense?

  

That is where the notion of alaukika pratyaksha, or for us, other pratyaksha based understandings that inherently affirm vyapti come in. Certain senses like visual sense may not inform us of vyapti. We see what we think is smoke and use a limited comparison to say "This is smoke". But if we find it cold to the touch, it is not smoke because by its very definition, to be smoke means F-\>S is true and "Hot/warm" is true (i.e. fire pervades it.) So, to know S is to know S-F is false.

  

I think there is room for further discussion on this point.
