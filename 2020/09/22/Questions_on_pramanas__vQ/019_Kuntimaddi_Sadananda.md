PraNAms

  

About pramaana -There are various operational definitions.

  

1\. pramaa karanam pramaanam - means by which knowledge is gained

  

2\. ythaartham pramaanam - That which reveals the nature of the object as it is.

  

3\. Vedanta Paribhasha defines it as - anadigata abhaadita arthavishaka jnaanatvam pramaanam - anadigata that which cannot be gained by any other means, abhaadita that which cannot be negated, arthavishaka - that which has meaningful - that means of knowledge is pramaanam.

  

Perceptions are further divided into external (involving senses) and internal (not involving senses) perceptions.

  

That which can be negated later by a better pramaana as in the case of rope/snake - since snake knowledge was bhaaditam - it was originally felt as pramaa becomes now bhramaa (delusion) when one can see clearly the object using the light (better means).

  

In all the pramaanas - there is triputi or triad involving pramaataa, prameyam and pramaana - knower, known and means of knowledge.

  

The same way the world is taken is real through pratyaksha pramaana but upon deeper inquiry, if one discovers that unreality of the perceived world, then it becomes bhramaa. In that respect, Vedanta becomes a pramaana using which if the seeker recognizes that the world is not real but only apparent.

  

The self that 'I am' being the subject, and/or Braham, being infinite are aprameyam are not objects of knowledge.

  

upamaana, arthapatti, anupalabdi are considered as part of anumaana pramaana by several other darshanikaas. What are requirements for anumaana pramaana are exhaustively discussed in the Vedanta Paribhaasha.

  

Hari Om!
