Namaskaram Ram-ji,

  

If cancer did not specially seek out smokers, then the proportion of cancer patients among smokers should not be much higher than the proportion in the full population. That is another stat logic. For example: If 0.2% of the whole population got cancer and 2% of smokers got cancer, that would be statistically significant most likely,i.e by their math theory,very low chance that such a discrepancy happens randomly. On that basis, one may postulate that smoking does something bad in thebody that iscausally related to cancer. However there must be a system that is followed consistently that interprets the data-analysis and conditions the other pramanas within itsboundaries. What is "much higher", "very low", "stat significant" etc. that we can say "if the chance is *this* low, we accept the postulation by the rules of our system that smoking does cause cancer and take furtherdecisions on that basis."

  

Two parties with different systems will come to different understandings. In your example, one sees causality and the other argues correlation without a special causal connection. They both argued according to their system and its rules of interpretation. Many Vedantins do not think ita big deal that we should know the world according to the system of pramanas in our traditions. We are comfortable jumping from science-perspectives to history-methodology to advaita, but the fact is other schools of thought may be operating under different assumptions, using different pramanas, grounding themselves in different bases (like perceptual data vs intuition). We waste time engaging a world on its terms. Being ambivalent about ourselves or behaving like chameleons when we should be definitive about our system of knowledge can result in inner confusion and arguably form impediments to our assimilation of Vedanta as well. In that sense, I think it a good exercise to learn these things and apply them more diligently in life and thought.
