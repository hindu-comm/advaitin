> What happened in the story is that Narada "understood what Maya is" as
> a consequence of his experiences. He obtained knowledge of Maya. So
> the question is whether any pramana (means of knowledge) came into
> play to his gaining the knowledge. If not, is there some other
> subtlety to his understanding maya that would differ from other
> knowledge for which the pramanas apply?

  

  

Namaskaram Sunil-ji,

  

Here is my thought. Narada always saw Krishna in all and all in Krishna. He did not understand Ignorance of Krishna, like one who sees light sees not darkness. Is it even possible? Poor Narada did not realize the state of the regular jiva for whom the condition is exact opposite and who is quite skeptical of liberation from body-consciousness and bondage. But Narada had heard of maya and ajnana and seen the rest of us acting mad, that a world can be experienced as if separate and devoid of Krishna, just like the ajnani has heard of Jnana, nirguna BRAHMAN, aham brahmasmi, etc. He wanted to know Maya like we 'want' to realize Brahman.

  

Well Krishna raised the veil as it were and Narada was plunged in darkness. The world as if without Krishna appeared real and He became an afterthought. Narada went through the circle and then Krishna woke him up. Darkness vanished again, and he understood maya. It is Anirvachaneeya. He cannot affirm it as real for Krishna alone is, and yet having experienced Ajnana and bhrama, he cannot negate it as absolutely unreal. The jiva engulfed in ajnana but yet knowing of Krishna through shastra pramana must affirm Maya as the intrinsic Shakti of Krishna to project and veil Himself, thus playing His lila as jiva-Ishvara-jagat.

  

Now for your question. What is the pramana for knowing maya? It is Shastra pramana that tells us "Hey you are under the spell of Maya." Otherwise we will not know it at all: the world of duality will remain our only 'reality'. One who is in darkness must be told by the shastra that this is darkness and you have to see light. So the first pramana is shastra. Does it mean we understand Maya from shastra? No. Like Narada we have to wake up to Brahman first. When we realize ever present Light, then we will also understand "Oh that was apparent darkness, the veiled state of ignorance of (the true nature of) Self. It never was real and yet it cannot be negated absolutely."

  

For intellectual knowledge or tarka, perhaps we can argue that maya can be postulated (arthapatti) as the Power of Brahman, given our experience of duality and the Reality of non-dual Brahman as revealed in shastra. When we have to explain duality, we are already in vyavaharika standpoint and our positing of Maya in Brahman understands Him as Ishvara in some higher sense.
