Namaskaram Davex-ji,

>   
> Apart from the pointing out instructions of the Shastras, I'd add the
> faculty of Buddhi to discriminate and interrogate the real from the
> unreal. Seeing through and relinquishment of the illusion of
> separation.  
>   
> Recognizing again and again that all objects are mithya dressed up in
> disguises, imputed by the conditioned mind-imagination while appearing
> as if separate, distinct and discrete.  
> When actually there are no independent objects as all are one
> interdependent or interconnected whole, one supreme Brahman, one life,
> one humanity to be lived in the spirit of brotherhood and
> corporation.  
>   

  

I think this may be called mananam, using our buddhi to immerse in the knowledge gained from sravanam (or from buddhi-analysis if you see it as simple truth if not for the mind's recalcitrant reverting to dualistic mode). One point that may come up is that a certain fitness of the buddhi/mind is needed to be able to do mananam and further niddhidhyasanam in their proper unwavering sense, which is why karma yoga, bhakti yoga etc are prerequisites for most people. I engage in mananam of advaita sometimes and sometimes the mind whips me back to slave-mode.
