Namaskaram Ram-ji,

  

You have presented how the scientist arrives at certain 'knowledge' within their system, but you have not given its 'clear distinction' from Vedanta. We do not know whether the Vedantin will accept or reject (based on application of his own pramanas) the scientist's deductions from experiments and statistical or other analysis. For example, does their notion of experiment fallunder the pramana of perception, anumana, etc.? To the extent we accept their conclusions, we have to know that they are also grounded in our own system of knowledge. If you are willing to take Tylenol with shraddha that it will alleviate headache, then we want that shraddha to be grounded logically in valid pramanas. We cannot go to tylenol as soon as headache happens and also arguethere are multiple factors, it is confusing etc. NO. You know something and are acting upon knowledge; you are not gambling on tylenol. And if you are gambling, there is a basis for that; it is not a random trial. The vedantin has to articulate his decisions based on his knowledge and he has to know how he used **his** pramanas to arrive at that knowledge. (We need to know how to apply Vedantic concepts in real-world examples and in dealing with 'knowledge'/tarka thrown at us by purva-pakshins in science, history, etc.)

  

In any case, the focus should not be on my example taken out of the context/way it is presented. Yes it is a bit simplistic (which I acknowledge in the Notes) but it is meant to illustrate something about arthapatti vs anumana,not become its own subject for scrutiny. If someone wants to illustrate the slope of a straight line by citing an example of a man running at 4 mi/hr, we don't jump onto the fact that no one can run constantly at the constant speed. Etc. If thereal-world example confuses others, they can pay attention to the point being made and provide alternate vedantic examples that they think fit better. I started this thread with Dark-matter, Aryan migration, hanuman; but when Raghav-ji directed to fire-smoke, I went along with that in subsequent mails. We *can* have discussion on purva-paksha theories to understand the nature of our pramanas; but my posts here are meant to first understand our own pramanas even where I cited examples from science etc. or gave thoughts on how their systems work.

  

In this particular topic of artha-patti vs anumana, there is a standard example in Vedanta. A = You do a yagnya C= After death, the jiva goes to heaven as karma-phala for yagnya. B= the yagnya results in an apurva or mark in the sukshma-sharira that later fructifies as the karma-phala (going to heaven). Again, B (or A-\>B) is not an object of perception, so it cannot become inferred either. Yet to explain the karma done now and the karma-phala happening much later, we must postulate that some sort of record/mark is impressed in the sukshma sharira when the yagnya is performed. So A-\>B is known through arthapatti.

  

Problem here is that C and A-\>C are themselves based on shraddha in shastra pramana. A modern vedantic audience may not really appreciate this stuff for all the unwillingness they generally have about karma-yoga in orthodox sense. So if we only cite such examples to highlight subtle points on pramanas, likely people will just skim over and not take seriously. So better to initially go with examples in "real-world" that people are impressed with.
