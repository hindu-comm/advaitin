Namaskar:

The way that I understand Anumana is to come to a conclusion based on the gathered facts. For example seeing the smoke, we can infer that there could be a fire. But this is just to be taken as our best guess and to check whether there was really fire needs additional facts.  

In the case of Arthapatt, we come to a conclusion also based on the gathered facts but using sound logical reasoning. One of the classical examples used to illustrate the distinction between Arthapatti and Anumana is the following:  

Statement 1. Based on the fact that Devadatta is growing fat and he does not eat during day, we can conclude that Devadatta should be eating during night. This conclusion is based on the logical reasoning that it is impossible for someone to grow fat without eating and hence Devadatta has to eat and if it is not during day then it has to be at night.

Statement 2. Suppose if we use the fact that Devadatta is fat to conclude that Devadatta should be eating lots of food comes from Anumana which is our best guess.

If we carefully look at both these statements, we will see that Statement 1 is a logical conclusion where as Statement 2 is a pure guess because we do not know all the facts about Devadatta who might have some medical problem and need not necessarily because of over eating!

Scientific Investigation starts with Anumana (guess work) based on data. For example, by observing that larger percent of smokers in comparison to nonsmokers have cancer and hence cigarette could be a potential reason for cancer. Then they try to evaluate the hypothesis “smoking can potentially cause cancer” and collect scientific data with appropriate procedures. Based on that data, they will be to make the more precise conclusion. Whether there is a CAUSAL relationship or not can be determined statistically and precisely and there are no such two schools of thought giving two different interpretations!

Everything what we have stated above is only considered Vyavaharika Sathya. All data and data collections come from sensual perceptions and the Paramarthika level Sathya is beyond our senses!

With my warm regards,

Ram Chandran
