Yes, Anandalahari was most likely not by Adi Shankaracharya. It was by Abhinava Shankara of the 8th century AD. I understand that some people believe that it was by one Kashmiri scholar-king (probably king Pravarasena I or Pravarasena II), who wrote it and Shri Abhinava Shankara added more verses to it. The Bhagavad Gita Bhasya was also written by this great scholar Abhinava Shankara. Abhinava Shankara was also a genius and Jivanmukta like Adi Shankara.  

  

Adi Shankara did not write any bhasya on the Bhagavad Gita as he was advised not to, but to write the Lalita Trishati Bhasya. It was Adi Shankara who visited Kashmir and at that time King Nara II was reigning there. Adi Shankara went to the Jyestheshvara temple of Lord Shiva, on a hill, about 100 KM away from Srinagar. Later on the hill as well as the temple became known as the Shankarachaya temple. A Murti of Adi Shankaracharya is behind the deity there. About one hundred years after Adi Shankara's visit, there was one king Gopaditya in Kashmir, and he improved that temple and made a couple of brahmin villages close to the temple. I understand that devotees from the Kanchi Kamakoti math visit that place, around the time of the Shankara Jayanti.
