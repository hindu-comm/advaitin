Namaste ji

  

The debate about dates of Shankara will probably be difficult to conclude.

  

I understand that the Japanese scholar Hajime Nakamura's in-depth two volume "History of Early Vedanta" and also Sengaku Mayeda's introduction to his translation of the upadeSasAhasrI have clearly asserted on the strength of several references that the bhagavad-gItA bhAShya was indeed authored by the one who authored the Brahma sutra bhAshya (who by definition is called Shankara Bhagavatpada). So it's authentic.

  

I was told by several acharyas in Uttarkashi that this debate about dates was also visited upon by several respected dashanAmI sannyAsis of northern India. The consensus was finally well-articulated by Acharya Mahamandaleshvara Sri Swami Kashikananda ji who is regarded as a colossus amongst scholars. The dates of 788-820 AD was decisively accepted as correct by all the authoritative scholarly sannyAsIs of the dashanAmi traditions.

  

A small minority offer different dates, such as the well-known dvaita scholar BNK Sarma of Madhva persuasion, a well-known critic of Advaita; he has chosen a different date for Sri Shankara. A couple of recent western 'Indologists' also support this theory. Sri Kanchi Matha has, in recent times, preferred this date, on the basis of informal dating of a change in the river in Kaladi. Despite this, their unconditional endorsement of the gItA bhAShya is welcome and this indicates that the early date is only a preference pending other coherent explanations.

  

Thus,

1. Many modern scholars like Nakamura 2. The consensus of dashanAmI sannyasIs of northern India

3. The revered Sringeri Acharyas

  

have all validated the gItA bhAShya as authored by Sri Shankara, the sUtrakAra.  

The dates offered by the above three authorities on the subject are all in the eight century CE. (750 CE, 788 CE etc., is kind of slight variation observed. But not anywhere near 500 BCE)
