Ekam - praNAms

  

Anirvacaneeyam means it is indeterminate. Take the example of rings, bangles, and necklaces made of Gold. Are they real or not? The real being defined as that which remains the same or the changeless entity during changes. In this relative example, scripture (Chandogya Up.) says they are only naamkevaste or name-sake different - vaacharambhanam vikaaraH - They are really Gold only but in different forms. Hence Goudapada says -utility, transactability, perceptibility, expendability are not the criteria to establish the reality of an object. The only criteria is eternity. That which remains the same in all three periods of time - trikaala abhaaditam satyam. There was a discussion of the sloka that said - adouantecha yat naasti, vartamaanepi tattadhaa ' That which is not there before and not there afterwords is REALLY not there in the present also. Ring may become a bangle and bangle may become a necklace - but what remains the same is Gold in this relative example. One cannot say a ring does not exist and at the same time one cannot say a ring really exists as what exists is only gold in a different form. Hence the truth of Ring is anirvacaneeyam - or indeterminate. It exists temporarily for transactional purposes - but the cost of the ring really depends on the cost of the Gold it has, right.

  

If A+B becomes C+D - these changes are valid only if the substantive that supports the changes (matter is conserved). Hence Krishna makes absolute law of conservation - that which exists cannot cease to exist and that which is non-existent cannot come into existence - naasato vidyate bhaavo naabhaavo vidyate sataH - 2-16.

  

Hence the whole world is called anirvacaneeyam - since it is continuous changes - implying by the law of conservation there must be something that supports the changing world - that is SAT - pure existence and that alone is really real. The experienced world is like experiencing the rings and bangles etc.
