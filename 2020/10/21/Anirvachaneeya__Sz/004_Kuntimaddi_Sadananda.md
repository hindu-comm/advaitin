Sunilji - PraNAms

  

Adi Shankara defines anirvachaneeyam aspect of maayaa in the Vivekachudamani - as

  

sannapyasanna ubhayaatmikaano

binnaapyabhinnaa ubhayaatmikaano

saagapyasangaa ubhayaatmikaano

mahat bhuutaa anirvacaneeya ruupa\|

  

one cannot say it exists, nor one can say it does not exists nor one can say it as both (sat and asat), one cannot say it is different (from Brahman) nor one can say it is same as Brahman nor both, one cannot say it has parts, nor one can say it does not have parts nor both - incredible indeed - it is of inexpressible.

  

Apply to rings and bangles - one cannot say ring exists nor one can say it does nor it both, one cannot say it is different from Gold nor one can say it is same as gold, nor both, one cannot say it is part of gold, nor the opposite nor both - incredible indeed it is of inexpressible nature!

  

Hence maayaa is defined as sat asat vilakshanam - different from both sat and asat - hence it is mithyaa.

  

'yaamaa saa maaya' or aghaTita ghaThanaa paTeeyasii maayaa - that which makes impossible (apparently) possible - is maayaa.

  

And then in the next sloka of Vivekchudamani, Shankara defines it as parameswara shakti -avyakta naamnee paramesha shakti.. yayaa jagat sarvam idam prasuuyate.

  

Hari Om!

Sadananda
