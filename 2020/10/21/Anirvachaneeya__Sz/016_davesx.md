Some inspirational writings during the solitude of this quarantine period to be read and forgotten clearing space for the everchanging new...

  

The Buddha also said that enlightened ones are *nirasa*: “wishless, desireless, without expectation.” This doesn’t mean inaction or disengagement from the world as some Buddhist believe but rather in the spirit of Karma yoga outlined in Bhagavad Gita*:* “Your right is to the work, never to the fruits. Be neither motivated by the fruits of action, nor inclined to give up action.” Do what is needed, but without attachment to the outcome.

Non-attachment to results is generally misunderstood as worldly renunciation. In response to the ongoing pandemic and ecological devastation of nature, we are called upon to do the very best we can, *not knowing* what the consequences will be, *not knowing* if our efforts will bear fruit and make any difference whatsoever.

This is important to cultivate the humility of *"not knowing*” the raison d'etre of this mystery of life, without beginning or end. No one knows how this Mahamaya arose and what will be the outcome and if some claimant says he knows he is conning himself and others. Our task is not to clear up this mystery; it’s to make the mystery clear. The path isn’t about coming to understand everything but opening up to a sacredness of this infinite mystery that we access not by grasping it but in being taken by this enigma. “You are not Atlas carrying the world on your shoulder,” Vandana Shiva once said. “It is good to remember that the planet is carrying you.”

We don’t know what is possible, we don’t know what will work, but we do our best in earnest. We don’t know if what we do is important, but we know that it’s important for us to do it. Have we already passed ecological tipping points and civilization as we know it is doomed? We don’t know—and that’s okay. Of course we would like our efforts to bear fruit, yet ultimately they are our gift, our offering to mother earth, *gratis*, and gifts should be made without any expectation of return.
