It is my understanding that Bhagavan Ramanuja never preached hatred against Shiva or any other gods. They consider Shiva as the great bhakta of Shree Rama and Sri Rama as the great bhakta of Lord Shiva.They however insists that only Lord Vishnu or His avataras can grant Moksha – as Krishna says – daveema eshaa guna mayi mama maayaa duratyayaa, maamevaye prapadyante maayaam etam taranti te\| This maayaa of mine is of divine origin and therefore difficult to cross over with self-effort. Only by surrendering to Me one can cross this ocean of my maayaa – says Krishna – hence the charama sloka -sarva dharmaan parityagya.. in the 18^(th) ch. before Krishna concludes Geetopadesa

There are several stories of the history of Ramanuja. As per these stories, there were attempts to kill Ramanuja first by this Advaita teacher who apparently became later Ramanuja's disciple and also by a fanatic King who conspired to kill Ramanuja. His cousin (forgot his name) who was also Ramanuja's disciple saves him. It is the latter fanatic Vishishtaadvaitins that propagated hatred to counter the pro-Shaivites. Ramanuja was of 11th Century. Later the Vishishtadvaita split into two groups - vadahalai and tengalia - the first one follows the marjaalanyaaya (kitten philosophy) and the second the markataka nyaaya (baby monkey philosophy). The first one says once bhakta is surrendered (sharanaagari) to the Lord, it becomes His responsibility to take care of His disciple just as it is the mother cat taking the responsibility of its kittens. On the other hand the Tengalai (southern) group believes that one has to hold on to the Lord’s feet all the time just like the baby monkey holding to the mother monkey while the mother jumps from on branch to the other. There are of course other differences in terms of the role of Mother Lakshmi in terms of jeeva’s solvation. 

In the 13th Century, some movement was made to develop Hari-Hara - a combined Lord Shiva and Vishny deity. Poet Tikkana of Telugu Bharatam starts his composition offering salutations to Harihara.
