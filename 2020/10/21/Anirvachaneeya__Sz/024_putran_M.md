Namaskaram Suresh-ji,

  

Some thoughts based on my understanding.  

  

Hindus are united by "Ekam Sat. Viprah bahudha vadhanti". We may disagree on what is Sat, but we can also agree that each path and perspective of that Sat may correspond to the jiva according to its adrishta or particularities of its upadhis. Our comprehension of and means of absorption in that Sat is contingent on our own inner state, samskaras and ajnana. So a Hindu (typically) does not have an issue with another person thinking of Sat in a different way, not because it is not his business but because he realizes that each jiva has its own svadharma accordingto its spiritual existence that is most suitable at this stage of its journey. If we think our end-idea of Sat is correct, at worst we will say "let the other jiva follow his path and way of worship properly thatwill help him mature at this stage. Eventually he will mature to a stage where he will naturally realize Ishvara as we know Him and seek Him in this manner." Etc.

  

In Visishtadvaita, you said that only Brahman is identified in all names and forms. The issue is with regard to the status of distinct reality afforded to Brahman identified in one name-form vs another vs the Brahman that is the "soul" of them all.

  

In Advaita, it is the same Brahman that in the context of upadhis is identified as if jiva; but this contextual identification is mithya. You do it in vyavahara but in Knowledge, it is entirely superfluous.

  

Suppose a child sees a sun in different windows and thinks it sees many distinct suns. After it knows there is only one sun seen as if many, there is no question of distinguishing the window-sun from the real Sun except as appearance,no question of window-sun needing to relate in any realmanner to the real Sun except "aham brahmasmi". After knowledge, we talk about the Sun or its *appearance* as if window-sun; but we negate any real difference.

  

But what about the window? Is it not real? Sure the dvaitin can get that spin in the analogy. But for Advaita, the purpose of the analogy is to convey the non-duality ayam atma Brahma, after which any indulgence of jiva-jagat would be in the as-if sense only.

  

The dream analogy is also useful for this. In VA, Brahman is the nimitta and upadhana karana for the universe/body. In Advaita, that "universe/body" belongs in the dream/appearance, inside of which of course Brahman may be known the dreamer who projects Himself as universe/body. But knowledge precludes any reality to the "universe/body" vs the Dreamer: it is Brahman identified as if king, beggar, universe, body. Thus the "universe/body" identification is like the king in the dream, only Brahman appearing as-if. The king is not "really" there; then what IS? Brahman alone is the Reality; all else is mithya. That "window" has no separate reality except within the vyavaharika or dream context wherein we see as if multiple window-suns or as-if a universe/body having soul within.
