Namaste Sureshji,

  

With all regards the most of the advaitins, who firstly are not aware that the Mahabharata, whether the Gita press edition or the Kumbhakonam or other editions, (other that the critical edition, which has been critically distorted), say that Lord Krishna spoke 620 verses in the Bhagavad gita and the presently available Bhagavad Gita has only 575 verses of Lord krishna. Should we say that all versions of the Mahabharata, which have lasted till today (other than the critical edition are wrong) are distorted and have no authenticity, because they state that Lord Krishna spoke 620 verses in the Bhagavad Gita?  

  

Secondly, most of the advaitins are not aware as to how many bhashyas Adi Shankara had written. The general tendency has been to believe that Adi Shankara wrote all the Shankara bhashyas, forgetting that aii the pontiffs of the Shankara mathas have also been called Shankaracharyas. For example, there are two bhashyas on the Kena upanishad. Are the both Bhashyas on Kenopanishad written by Adi Shankara?. There is a Yoga bhashya of Shankaracharya. Was that written by Adi Shankara? All the mathas have no time to address these issues. If on the basis of what an ancient Shankara Vijaya says, someone claims that Adi Shankara wrote only so many bhashyas, then there will be an uproar. One scholar from Maharashtra found that from the style of writing seen in the Shankara bhashya of the Bhagavad Gita, it could not have been written by Adi Shankara.  

  

Now, though nobody speaks on this issue, the silence of everybody on this issue means whoever wrote the first commentary on the Bhagavad gita, dropped the 45 verses, but I am unable to accuse Adi Shankaracharjya committing such a great mischief. My belief is that Gaudapadacharya used the Ajativada verse from the Bhagavad Gita, in his Mandukya karika. and that is why he or Govinda Bhagavadpada stopped Adi Shankara from writing the Shankara bhashya on the Bhagavad gita, and asked him to write a commentary on the Lalita Trishati. If someone has claims that according to one of the Shankara Vijayas, Adi Shankara had written only "X" number bhashyas, and if that is so, then there will be an additional point to think that the Shankara bhashya on the Bhagavad Gita was probably not written by Adi Shankara. Interested scholars may look at this. I have nothing more to say on this issue.  

  

Coming to the word "Mithya" it can be used for "anitya" things, but then why do we have to be so particular that we must call anitya things as Mithya. Why then Lord Krishna had to appear as an avatara for these mithya things. So we have to be careful before using words like mithya. A JIvanmukta or a Bodhisattva (i.e Buddhist Jivanmukta) can say that whole world is mithya, but I will prefer to say that the world is anitya (which Lord Buddha called Aniccha).  

  

Jai Shri Krishna
