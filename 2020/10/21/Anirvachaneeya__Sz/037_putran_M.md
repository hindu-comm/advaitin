Namaskaram Raman-ji,

  

It depends on how our tradition understands the Bhagavatha purana. I would defer to others to explain this.

  

Suppose we do take it as ithihasa. Then we know that Gajendra nearing death was able to remember the Lord and self-surrender to Him who rescued him from the makara. However the back-story is that both Gajendra and the makara were cursed in their previous lives to have such experience in these animal bodies. So their shariras are not ordinary results of their own karma; but have been specially brought into existence by sages' curse in order to have this final experience (Gajendra surrendering to the Lord as the final option, and makara being liberated by the Lord's discus). So such examples typically are exceptions and not a rule that animals also can naturally seek moksha or think Ishvara.

  

In other words, we cannot cite these examples to make a necessary case for inference for animals in general since the full information shows them as special cases.

  

I understand message of Gajendra Moksham for the human is how the Lord responds to true self-surrender by the jiva no matter his physical state etc.
