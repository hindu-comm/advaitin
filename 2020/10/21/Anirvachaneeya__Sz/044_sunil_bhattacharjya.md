Namaste Sadaji,

  

I read with interest your Ode to your parents. Parents and particularly mother is very very dear to all. I read that after performing the last rites of his mother, Adi Shankara broke down and spent the whole night crying in a Vishnu temple in Kaladi. It is said that once a brahmin lady looked at Bhagavan Mahavir from a distance and broke down. When his disciple told him about the crying lady, who was taken away. he replied that she was her mother. That is also how we know that Bhagavan Mahavir was from a brahmin family but was adopted by a king. There is still a custom that mothers are not allowed to see their Sanyashi sons.  

  

For me too the parents are very dear, but in our particular case our father had less time for us and we were wholly brought up by our mother. Father was a Post and Telegraph Master (combined) and though he had some assistants, he could not leave his office for 24 hours. Later on, towards the second half of his career, when he went to the head office and retired as the Head Postmaster, he took to gardening and used to get up at 5 am and do the gardening work till it is time to take his meals and leave for the office, and in the evening hours after his office he was dead tired, after continuously working for 13 hours from 5 am to 6 pm. in fact, he was more like a sanyashi. In his young age he left home to become a sanyashi in the Ramakrishna order, but his friends and close relatives succeeded in getting him back to a house holder's life.  

  

In my elder brother's case, after he had the B.tech degree from IIT, Kharagpur, he wanted to take up a job, but my mother insisted on him to do further studies. He did M.Tech, and then he got an ICI Scholarship for his PhD in the UK. In my own case I was hoping to become a college teacher, but my mother insisted that I study further. I was about to join IIT Kharagpur for M.Tech, and at that time I got an offer from the Atomic Energy Establishment in Mumbai (later on it became known as the Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC). I joined the latter and later on I went to Industry. I owe a lot to my grandmother also as she encouraged me to read the Bhagavata Purana to her in my Childhood, and I practically read most of the puranas in Childhood, albeit in Bengali translation. I dedicated my book on "The Original Bhagavad Gita of 745 verses, Complete with the Rare V+erses", to my parents.  

  

After my retirement, that past experience pulled me towards the Bhagavad Gita and ancient Indian history. Most Sanatani Hindus are not aware that maintaining proper updated historical record in the Vedas was a must, and after Vedavyasa separated the four vedas leaving the rest of the Puranas and the Epics as constituting the fifth Veda, the burden of
maintaining of the historical records fell on the Fifth Veda texts. It is one of the pancha-lakshanas of the Fifth Veda.  

  

As regards the Bhagavad Gita, Adi Shankara wrote the bhashyas on the Sanatsujatiya and Vishnu Sahasranama, from the Mahabharata, and it was natural that he would have written a bhashya on the Bhagavad Gita**;**
but he was dissuaded from that by Gaudapadacharya, ostensibly for the reason that he already took the Ajatavada verse from the Bhagavad gita and used in his Mandukya Karika. Adi Shankaracharya was told to write the bhashya on the Lalita Trishati than on the Bhagavad Gita. So it stands out that the innocent act of Gaudapadacharya, had created problems for the later day readers of the Bhagavad Gita, as Bhatta Bhaskara, by his heinous act produced a Bhagavad gita containing 575 verses of Lord Krishna, twhere there were originally 620 verses by Lord Krishna.

  

Jai Shri Krishna

Sunilk B
