Sureshji - PraNAm

I refrained from further discussion only because thequestions you are posing are not new and have been asked by vishishtaadvaitins and dvaitins.

Please rest assured that Advaita stands on scriptural support and also more logical than vishishtaadvaita or dvaita. Your statement below is not true.

‘ajAtavAda is true with respect to Brahman. VedAntins from all traditions accept that.But it is also a fact that all names/forms arise, remain and return back to Brahman.’

I explained before but did not want to go into details.

1. Braham being infinite cannot exclude anything. Hence there are no jeeva, jagat and Iswara divisions inside Brahman. Therefore, your second statement is misleading. The jeevas and jagat are in a subtle form in Iswara which Vishishtaadvaita identifies as Sagana Brahman. Creation is from subtle to gross only. From the Advaita point at Brahman referrnce, there is no creation also - that is also the essence of Ajaati Vaada.

2. There is no nirguna Brahman Vishistaadvaita.

Infinite is unqualified by definition. Even the word infinite is only a negation of finite entities as Brahman. na vaak gaccahti na manaH – yato vaacho nivertante apraapya manasaa saha. Jeevas are eternal in adviata only at vyaavahaarika level. No jeevas or jagat in Brahman. Hence creation is mithyaa from the point of Advaita. Not true from Vishistadvaita and dvaita.

3. Since it is mityaa, the Ajaati vaada applies and the discussion only from the point of the jeevas who are in pursuit of the truth – Hence it is within vyavahaarika level. Even the scriptures are also at that level. Hence what Goudpaada says the apparent plurality is not reality but it is swabhaava of the Iswara level – He himself became many –tat aikshataa - bahusyaam prajaayeyeti.

4. God-realization or Brahan realization is also at vyaavahaarika level only. From the point of Paaramaarthika there is no question of realization as there are no jeeva-jagat-Iswara triad.

Please recognize that in Advaita – we have three levels of reality – 1. Paaramaarthika satyam 2. Vyaavahaarika satyam and 3. Praatibhaasika satyam. Goudapaada is addressing ajaati vaada from the point of Paaramaarthia Satyam after his elaborate analysis of the dream and waking states in this vaitatya and advaita prakaranas.

I will stop with this. My suggestion to you is to have first the understanding of the Advaita from Advaita point and not from the vishishtadvaita point. Most of your quotes are from that reference. Hence I did not want to go into a refutation of those. I had many discussions in the past on this list, from the point of Vishishtaadvaita, dvaita and even from Buddhisam. Those where collected and put in three volumes in Journey Beyond books.

Hari Om!
