Namaste,

  

This question has been asked and answered in this thread itself, but the significance may have been missed.

  

One can say the answer doesn't convince, but that is different from saying that the question has gone unanswered.

  

Anyway the answer is given again, in detail - The nirguNa Atma in its true nature is not a doer at all, however the same Atma because of the presence of the mind, has an aupAdhika kartRtva, a superimposed doership, because of which there is the appearance of action.

  

Just like the redness of the flower next to the transparent sphaTika crystal, makes the crystal appear red, the doership of the mind is superimposed on the self and makes the self appear to be a doer. When the mind upAdhi is present, the self is (apparently) the doer, without the mind upAdhi, the self is not the doer. There is no svAbhAvika kartRtva in the Atma.

  

The same idea has been addressed in the takshAdhikaraNa of the brahmasUtra. As shankarAchArya says in the bhAShya:

  

तक्षा हि विशिष्टेषु तक्षणादिव्यापारेष्वपेक्ष्यैव प्रतिनियतानि करणानि वास्यादीनि कर्ता भवति, स्वशरीरेण तु अकर्तैव ; एवमयमात्मा सर्वव्यापारेष्वपेक्ष्यैव मनआदीनि करणानि कर्ता भवति, स्वात्मना तु अकर्तैवेति ।

  

Even the BhagavatgItA has mentioned this several times, in verses like गुणाः गुणेषु वर्तन्ते, नैव किंचित् करोमि etc.

  

Now, you may have a different interpretation to the gItA verses or the brahma sUtra, perhaps based on vishiShTAdvaita, but there is no necessity for the advaitin to explain his philosophy on the basis of a different interpretation of the texts. Just because one has a different interpretation of the verses, it does not invalidate the advaitin's answer.

  

The problem that you had raised - that an advaita interpretation invalidates dharma and encourages an "anything goes" mentality - is not at all true. shankarAchArya has refuted this in several places in the bhAShya. In fact the very entry into vedAnta is on the basis of possessing sAdhana chatuShTaya sampatti, which can only be gained when one has led a dhArmic life and has performed karmayoga and upAsana yoga for a lengthy period of time.

  

So those desiring jnAna must necessarily have led a dhArmic life. And those that are jnAni-s, lead a dhArmic life as an example to the rest of society, as well as performing them for society's well being. It is for this very reason that Advaita AchArya-s perform the nitya pUja-s - they do it as loka samgraha.

  

However, you had said that that is a contradiction in terms - who is performing the action, you had asked. The answer is that no one is really performing the action, but there is the appearance of action. The performance of action by the mind and senses does not affect the changeless substratum of all actions, nirguNa Brahman - यत्र यदध्यासः तत्कृतेन दोषेण गुणेन वा अणुमात्रेणापि स न संबध्यते ।

  

Therefore, in summary, there is no real contradiction between an unchanging substratum over which there is the apparent drama of samsAra. The substratum is the reality, apparent actions do not negate its unchanging nature, nor does considering actions to be apparent, condone or provide a license for adhArmic behaviour.
