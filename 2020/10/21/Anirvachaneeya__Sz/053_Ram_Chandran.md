Namaskar Sri Suresh:

All of us are guided by what we believe in and develop our own thought framework to accept what we believe. We have used many structural frameworks also known us model in life routinely. For example, in our high school times, the commonly used models was V = U + a x t where V = final velocity, U = initial velocity, a = rate of acceleration and t is the elapsed time. We are quite familiar model and during car purchases, youngsters would like to have cars with a high acceleration rate. A model comes with a set of parameters and also with a list of assumptions while making use of the model.  

In mathematics models, when we can determine uniquely our point of interest, then we call such a model as deterministic model. A simple of rule of thumb in linear mathematical equations, an unknown variable will be deterministic if we have same number of independent equations with the same number of variables. In statistical models or also known as stochastic models, the equation takes account of omissions by adding an error term. A simple statistical model can be written as Y = a + b X + e where a and b are parameters and e represents error due to omission of other information. We do come across situations where we have one equation with 2 unknowns variables then such variables become non determinable. In Vedanta, such a situation is labelled as Anirvachaneeya or indefinable!

The world, mAyA, etc., falls into this category where we can’t determine until we know the Brahman. In the case of deterministic models, mathematically we can get unique resolutions without anybody disputing! In the case of stochastic models, large amount of data are used to estimate the unknown parameters to predict the values and statisticians will state by increasing the sample size, the error can be reduced. This was my professional job and I used to teach graduate level students to educate them how to develop and estimate forecasting models. We all can be happy to know that Vedantic concepts follow the mathematics rules and always remind our limitations and warn us not to overextend our limits!

Vedanta and spiritual questions become more difficult to answer and our saints and sages have provided convenient frameworks to understand and appreciate those structures. Any structure comes with assumptions based on beliefs and we all have varied beliefs and understandings of what those sages have said. Unfortunately, unlike the models that described, we don’t have data to validate in support of one structure over others.
The saints and sages of the Upanishads and our Acharyas including that of Sankara, Ramanuja and Madhav also have their own framework of thoughts to understand God or the Brahman. These structures got the unique identity and that explains the differences between advaita, vishistadwaita and dwaita. It should be pointed out that even these template models have many variations based on the assumptions, parameters and the established relationship. I am describing these thoughts so that we can try to distinguish between different ways of looking at the unknown the Brahman!

We need to be much more careful to reject anyone structures and we are better off to leave them open and accept that we will know the answer to our questions when we know the Brahman. The often quoted passage from Kenopanishad provides the subtlest way to describe the Brahman. According to Kenopanishad, the Brahman or the Consciousness is the Eye behind the eyes, the Ear behind the ears, Mind behind the mind, Speech behind speech, Vital Life behind life. The eyes can’t see it; the ears cannot hear it; it is what makes the ears to hear. The eyes cannot see it; it is what makes the eyes to see. You cannot speak about it; it is what makes you to speak. The mind cannot imagine it; it is what makes the mind to think. It is different from what all we know; yet it is not known either. Those who feel they know Him know Him not. Those who say that anything amenable to the senses is not the Brahman are the one who know Him. If and when the innermost witness of all cognitions, whether sensation, perception or thought, then only it is known. One who knows thus becomes the Brahman. We need not feel bad that they do not seem to answer our fundamental question because the answer and the Brahamn are the subtlest of the Truth!

Coming back to your sincere attempts of unification of the proposed structures, I am of the opinion, we are better off to leave them as they are presented which are meant for specific audience with specific beliefs and understanding. There are no easy ways to change someone’s beliefs and even we have difficulty to change our own beliefs and how can expect others to do the same!

Many Vedantic statements are tautological because they are not false and at the same time they do not answer our question. For example, if I say that the post office is next to the shopping mall and also the shopping mall is next to the post office, you still will not know the location of either of them. When I say that we can visualize the Brahman through the world even though the world is not real! You may say that the we can visualize the Brahman through the world and the world and the Brahman both are real. Who is right, you or me will only be known if we know the Brahman!!

What I have written is to illustrate our difficulties and may not necessarily a complete answer and it is only exhibits my limitations and ignorance!!

With my warm regards,

Ram Chandran
