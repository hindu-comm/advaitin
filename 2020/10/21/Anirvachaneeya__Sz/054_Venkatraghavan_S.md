Namaste Sri Suresh ji,

It is consciousness in conjunction with one mind that is teaching, and consciousness in conjunction with another mind that is engaged in sAdhana. It is mano-vishiShTa chaitanya. The mano-vishiShTa-chaitanya is the ahamkAra or ego - that entity, in its vishiShTa rUpa, is mithyA.

  

On what basis are you saying the Atma willingly using the instrument of mind and body rules out the superimposition of the mind and the body? In advaita we willingly use the rope snake to illustrate the mithyAtva of the world, that does not make the rope-snake real, does it?  

  

Also which Atma is willingly using the instrument of mind and body? It cannot be nirguNa brahman, because that is not a doer at all, so talk of willing, or using, etc does not apply to nirguNa chaitanyam. All talk of action is only possible when there is some upAdhi associated with the consciousness. Therefore, the one that is "willingly using" the upAdhi, is the one upon whom the upAdhi has already been superimposed. So how can that rule out superimposition?

  

> I agree that Atman is unaffected and asanga. But in the embodied state
> its association with the body/prakriti cannot be denied.

It is not being denied. We are saying that that association is mithyA.



> The body/mind cannot act by themselves and so the example of
> flower/crystal is not convincing enough. Sri Shankara makes this clear
> in the following sutra.
>
> कर्ता शास्त्रार्थवत्त्वात्॥ ३३ ॥

We are not saying that the body and the mind act by themselves. The takshAdhikaraNa that I quoted earlier is the one that immediately follows the kartRadhikaraNa containing the above sUtra quoted by you. The purpose of kartRadhikaraNa is to refute the sAnkhya philosopher who holds that while puruSha has bhoktRtvamit is the pradhAna that has kartRtvam. The purpose of the kartRadhikaraNa is to say that as the locus of kartRtvam and bhoktRtvam have to be same, therefore, if the sAnkhya philospher holds that consciousness has bhoktRtvam, it necessarily follows that consciousness has kartRtvam also. This leads to the doubt whether such a kartRtvam is intrinsically an attribute of consciousness or not?

  

To answer such a doubt, comes the takshAdhikaraNam that I quoted, wherein shankarAchArya says, just like it is possible for a carpenter to be a doer (perform carpentry) when he has his tools, while not being a doer (ie not perform carpentry) in the absence of his tools at home, consciousness too, when associated with the mind is the doer, and in the absence of the mind is not the doer.

  

Therefore, the purpose of the takshAdhikaraNam is to answer the doubt raised by kartRtvAdhikaraNam by establishing that kartRtvam is not an intrinsic attribute of consciousness. The kartRtvam that is intrinsically located in the mind (svAbhAvika kartRtvam) is superimposed on consciousness, by the process of sopAdhika adhyAsa - just like the redness of the flower is superimposed on the crystal because of the flower upAdhi, the doership of the mind is superimposed on consciousness by the mind upAdhi.
