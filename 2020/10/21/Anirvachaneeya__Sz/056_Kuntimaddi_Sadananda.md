Sir, it is not mityaa vaada – it is satyam or vyaavahaarika satyam. You sound like dvaitin who calls Advaita as maaya vaada.

  

Satyam means the truth. The world we see is transactionally true. No Advaitin will deny it. When Goudapaada was writing kaarika on Mandukya, obviously he is writing for some one to read -the writer and the reader both are transactionally true -

But from the truth (nityatvam point) they are not absolutely real.

  

Ajaati only refers from the absolute point. I mentioned already that from Brahman point, there could not be a creation as infinite cannot undergo a transformation. Infinite cannot have parts also. If one sees the creation then it is not really real and since he sees it, for the seer, it appears to be real. We experience this every day when we go to the dream state. The dream is real for the dreamer while he is the dream. Nature provides dream experience to everyone to recognize that experienceabilty is not a basis for absolute reality. Extensive analysis of shell-silver or snake-rope has been used to explain for those who see, they seems to be real until one uses a better pramana to know theun

  

As per Advaita vyavahaara satyam or transactional reality is mithyaa, not ultimately as it is- sat asat vilakshanam. Sat being defined that which is eternal - and asat means no locus for existence as in vandhyaaputraH.

I gave an example of rings and bangles - they are mithyaa. The existence of rings and bangles are not mithyaa vaada. Accepting their transactional reality does not make Hinduism go down the drain. People can still use the rings and bangles without getting confused. They do preserve them since they are made of gold. Nothing will happen to Hinduism when if people consider the rings and bangles are only namesake and they are precious since they are made of gold.

  

Please wakeup. Advaitin does not say the world does not exist. Ajaati vaada clearly defines the perceptibility, transactability, utility, name-ability are not the criteria for the absolute reality. It is nityatvam that defines the absolute reality. Hence Goudapaada goes into detailed analysis of dream state.

I have provided the scriptural statement - vaachaarambhanam vikaaraH - what is satyam is gold only that does not undergo a transformation in appearing as rings and bangles. This is nothing to do with Hinduism (a word coined by others) - Vedanta is the science of the absolute – it includes the analysis of both the subject, the knower and the object, the knowable entities.

Shankara clearly says – dRisyatvaat – the world is perceptually experienced – hence it is only apparent reality.

The arguments between vishishtaadvaitins and dvaitins and advaitins have been going on for centuries. There is nothing new.

Since you are very much concerned about the Hinduism and Advaita contributing to confusion (in your understanding), I suggest this may not be the right form for you before you get thoroughly confused.
