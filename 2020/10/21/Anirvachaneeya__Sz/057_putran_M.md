Hindus are united by "Ekam Sat. Viprah bahudha vadhanti". We may disagree on what is Sat, but we can also agree that each path and perspective of that Sat may correspond to the jiva according to its adrishta or particularities of its upadhis. Our comprehension of and means of absorption in that Sat is contingent on our own inner state, samskaras and ajnana. So a Hindu (typically) does not have an issue with another person thinking of Sat in a different way, not because it is not his business but because he realizes that each jiva has its own svadharma accordingto its spiritual existence that is most suitable at this stage of its journey. If we think our end-idea of Sat is correct, at worst we will say "let the other jiva follow his path and way of worship properly thatwill help him mature at this stage. Eventually he will mature to a stage where he will naturally realize Ishvara as we know Him and seek Him in this manner." Etc.

Hinduism is the modern reference for what we call the Sanatana Dharma.

  

The Sanatana Dharma is the Foundation of all valid Religion.

  

A Religion has an Outer form as practiced by the people, and may have both

  

a\) A Valid Inner form of the Sanatana Dharma, an inner form that corresponds to the Sva-Dharma of some jiva that will allow that jiva to progress towards moksha. This inner form corresponding to the sanatana dharma may however have limitations in its scope to guiding the jiva all the way to moksha; and a jiva abiding by this Dharma may eventually have to transition from it.

  

b\) Invalid elements thatare man-made and not corresponding to the svadharma for any jiva. Such accretions and/or deletions result in practices that contradict the sanatana dharma. The classic example is "My dharma is to convert all humans to follow my Religion." This is fundamentally invalid because "My Religion" will have a valid inner form that corresponds to some jivas but not all.

The Pramana for knowing Dharma is the Veda; hence the Valid inner forms of the sanatana dharma are to be known by recourse to the Veda.***Hinduism in a restricted sense refers to the family of Religions that explicitly use the Veda for Pramana. In a more general sense, Hinduism refers to the Sanatana Dharma that is manifest in the valid Inner forms/elements of all Religions.***

  

So, a tribal may not use the Veda as Pramana; but the tribal may still be a Hindu and a follower of Hinduism in so far that her Religion can be validated through recourse to the Veda, as having a valid inner form of the sanatana dharma and perhaps invalid outer elements that are more or less superfluous.

  

As an example, suppose the dowry system is not part of the sanatana dharma. However a person thinks that to take dowry is a religious act. This would be accretion to the Dharma that is more or less removable without affecting the inner form. So the person may still be identified as a Hindu. On the other hand, ... \[Give examples of accretions/deletions that would warrant saying the Religion can no longer be called Hinduism: though it will have inner elements that are valid, they are so intertwined with the outer invalid elements that the Religion is best not identified as Hinduism.\]

  

We can say perhaps, in some cases the invalid Outer form dominates the valid inner elements to such extent that an inner form cannot be separately accessed. It is no longer Hinduism. When the outer invalid elements are subsumed by a pervading valid inner form that can be separately accessed, it is still Hinduism.

A Religion's validity for a jiva is contingent on the jiva's own adrishta. In other words, the sanatana dharma as reflected in the jiva is its own sva-dharma; and as reflected in the jiva's consciousness of the world is the Path of Action and Seeking that will allow this jiva to fulfill its svadharma, clear its adrishta and move optimally towards moksha... It is not yours or mine but the ground of all Religion and all existence that we refer to as Dharma, and that is why it is satya and sanatana.
