Namaskaram Suresh-ji,

  

I may not have a problem with your view if you do not subscribe to Veda apaurusheya and seek to use it as pramana nevertheless on account of the logic on Ishvara. But a non-Hindu need not subscribe to your Veda-derived logic that there is such a duty to return back or that selfless service should be seen as service to Ishvara. He may be a charvaka/scientist or someone with a view that animals and other things are God's gift for human use and pleasure,where God is different from your idea. You have to figure out whether his "religion" is within the Hindu fold and why? Or why is it not? I tried to explain the broader problem in my post.

  

Anyway, as Ram-ji as said, the topic of "How to unite Hindus"tempts people like me to go off on tangent. I will leave it here; others can perhaps engage it keeping closer to the main topic.

  

As far as the purusha suktam and varied worship of Ishvara, that is already an accepted part of Advaita tradition. Advaitins don't have quarrelwith other Hindu faiths. My post also argues why not. I am not sure if Visishtadvaitinshave a problem with such viewpoint of Hinduism; if they do, then you have to broaden their vision rather than worrying about the flaws of anirvachaniya: because whether we deem the Ishvara-jiva-jagat triad as mithya or not, we don't believe the Visishtadvaitin is going to hell. It is progress for both ofus if we follow our dharma. We are not going to be the problem for Hindu unity, going forward.

  

thollmelukaalkizhu
