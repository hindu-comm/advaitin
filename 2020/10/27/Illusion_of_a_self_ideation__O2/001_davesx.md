In case someone like our resident quantum physicist Sundarji is wondering where the below post fits in, it's in response to Verse 6, Chapter II of Mandukhya, which came up elsewhere.  

  

*6. If a thing is non—existent both in the beginning and in the end, it is necessarily non—existent in the present. The objects that we see are really like illusions; still they are regarded as real.*   

  

At that time I suppose neither the movie projector nor computer was invented so a declaration was made without getting down to the modern day explanation on how this could be like an illusion.  

  

The sense of being an individual self was not there in the beginning (even at birth i.e. prior to formation of a conceptual self at ~ 3 years) and will not be there in the end; so how can it be said to exist in the present? It is the mind -- the programs downloaded and running in memory that projects a self-identity and a manifold world.  

  

From the standpoint of thinking (vyavahara satya or duality) it appears to be real but from the position that transcends thought (Paramartha or non-duality), the one implied in this verse, there is no individual self or jiva that was actually born, existing or will die (except simulated by memory and imagination).
