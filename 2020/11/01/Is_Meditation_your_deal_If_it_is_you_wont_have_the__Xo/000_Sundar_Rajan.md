Here I was, watching TV late evening and saw this Ad for a prescription drug (I think it was Latuda) flash by on my TV screen and didn't pay much attention to it and took it in stride. I also assumed such Ad's are common everywhere. Guess what, I found out, believe it or not , US is ONLY one of 2 countries permitting prescription drugs to be advertised on television directly to consumers. New Zealand is the other. *Ask your doctor if Latuda (or similar) is right for you* is a catch phrase Americans and New Zealanders hear three times every half hour, especially if they listen to the late night news. That doesn’t happen anywhere else. There you go. Something you assumed is universal is ONLY available in couple of countries!  

  

How is this relevant to our topic in hand?  

  

Is Meditation the path for you. Are you cut out for Meditation?  

  

I was thinking about it the other day and then it struck me (also confirmed by a quote from Acharyal you will see later).  

  

What is that? If you are cut out for Meditation, you won't have the ARJUNA QUESTION?  

  

What question? You ask?  

  

Remember the one from the Sixth chapter for the Gita. After listening to Lord Krishna's exposition on the detailed process of Meditation in the 6th Chapter, Arjuna says "That's all cool, Boss. However, I don't see Meditation happening for me. *My mind is more uncontrollable than Trump's Twitter account*".  

  

OK. Maybe not quite those words. Here is Arjuna's question:

*cancalam hi manah krsna pramathi balavad drdham tasyaham nigraham manye  *

*vayor iva su-duskaram*  

For the mind is restless, turbulent, obstinate and very strong, O Krsna, and t*o subdue it is, it seems to me, more difficult than controlling the wind.* (BG 6.34)  

  

After reading this, it struck me. Even if you’re not an expert but an intermediate in the path of meditation, you WON'T HAVE this question. Your reaction will be: Yes, I hear Arjuna, can somewhat relate to his question but I *really don’t have that problem*.  

  

As one cut out for Meditation you won't have a problem *getting* Meditation, your journey may not be all smooth sailing . You may have a rough journey somedays like like a bullock cart on a bumpy road, on other days like a self driven Tesla on the Autobahn. On some days, you get deep tranquility and even taste bliss; On other days you may have a bad hair day (as the ladies say) when everything goes wrong and your meditation may be patchy. Nevertheless you certainly won’t have the Arjuna question.  

  

So, while the majority of folks do have the problem related by Arjuna, some certainly are oblivious to it. Similar to the Prescription Ad's
permitted only in a few countries (quoted from the Opening paragraph) :-)  

  

Acharyal (Jagadguru Sri Abhinava Vidyatirtha Mahaswamigal) explains this beautifully in almost a matter of fact way:  

  

"People say that thoughts keep coming to their minds. They can hardly conceive of a situation where the mind is quiet. However, this surprises Me. As I see it, the mind should be quiet and just rest on the Self, unless a thought is needed for any task. When the necessity ends, the mind should revert to the state of tranquility. Even when the task is at hand, active thinking is generally not needed all the time. For instance, when reading a book, the mind ought to become quiet when a page is being turned."  

  

Such was the tranquil nature of His mind even when it was not absorbed in meditation.  

  

(Source: Edifying Parables, Sri Vidyatheertha Foundation)  

  

Of course, some explanation is in order. Acharyal's state of tranquility depicted is the state of a Supreme Yogi, focused on the self. But *even at a intermediate* level, when the mind enters the plane of ekagratha
during meditation and establishes itself in the *ekagratha bhoomi*, (to use Patanjali's phrase), the practitioner should be able to observe the mind doesn't have many thoughts and stays content during the rest of the day.  

  

This has major implications for Vedantic Shrvana, Manana and Nidhidhyasana , which we will take up in a separate post.
