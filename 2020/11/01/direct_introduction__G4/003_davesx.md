If we take this a step further, it is seen that the Self-Consciousness that I am does not remain with Itself (as the 'I am') but has a tendency or habit of resorting to gross objects like the body or subtle like the mind, imagining I am 'this' or 'that'.  

  

By clinging or identifying with its own expressions Consciousness forgets Itself and gets lost in delusion, suffering the consequence of the birth and death of objects.  

  

**"*The Self is imagined as endless objects like prana, mind etc. This is the illusion of the luminous One by which It itself is deluded, as it were****"*. Chapter 2 Verse 19 Mandukhya  

  

In deep sleep, nature has given us a free sample of the *Peace* of Consciousness, in the absence of objects including the individual identity. So even thought objects can be utilized as reminders of the Self, the Self does not depend on objects and can exist on its own. Even the subject (the thought 'I am') cannot stand by itself and is automatically deconstructed when objects are released revealing the undefined, boundless, unborn Self.  

  

Therefore it is suggested that while waking Advaitin practitioners make intentional effort to intermittently break this automatic trance, the chain of samsara, the habit of stringing up repetitive ideas believing I am 'this' or 'that' and remember to rest in the serene clear default Awareness -- the nature of Consciousness (Awareness aware of Itself).  

  

Established in the Self, objects sparkle as momentary wonderful display of Itself. As such everything is included in Consciousness and IS Consciousness. Only in Sthita Pragnya can one realize "Sarvam khalvidam brahma" -- All this is Brahman.
