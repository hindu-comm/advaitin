Yes, It has to meet the clear reason. One has to accept only with the open eyes. Swami Vivekananda did not accept Ramakrishna Paramhamsa at first sight and he waited until he was prima facie convinced. Even in accepting the existence of the Sushumna nadi, he was skeptic and was trying to carry out some experiments in theJadavpur Medical University, in collaboration with the Medical scientists there. I admire Swami Vivekananda's investigative spirit. It seems to be missing in most of the scholars of today, particularly in the field of spiritual and historical matters. Even in the Ramakrishna order, which I belong too, not many people know that the Bhagavad Gita, the Upanishad of the Mahabharata, which Vedavyasa himself called Karshna Veda, had originally had 620 verses spoken by Lord Krishna. I have found that only Keshava Kashmiri Bhatta, of the Bhedabheda school, claiming that the Original Bhagavad Gita had 745 verses (which includes 620 verses of Lord Krishna). One particular scholar, with immoral or vicious mindset, removed as many as 45 verses of Lord Krishna from the Bhagavad Gita, some 1200 years ago, just because the understanding of those verses were beyond him.  

  

Secondly, one earlier mail brought in the topic of levels in in spiritual progress / attainment. Vedanta is the "ANTA"or the "Final
Conclusive Part" of the Veda and that is why it has been considered, from time immemorial, to be the highest level. The pure Dvaita as well as the other modified Dvaita-propositions, such as the Vishishtadvaita, are really yet to at the various rungs of the lower level, as they are yet to accept the Vedantic Mahavakyas, without any reservation.  

  

Jai Shri Krishna

Sunil K. Bhattacharjya
