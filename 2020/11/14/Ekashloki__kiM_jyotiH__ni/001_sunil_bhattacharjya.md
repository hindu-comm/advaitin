Thank you Subbuji for this Ekasloki. You have rightly said about the limitations of language, used in spiritual matters.

  

For this very reason, Buddhism seems to be very intriguing to many of us. In Buddhism, there is not much talk about Atma and Jivatma, but there is a word "Tathagata-garbha". "Tatha-gata" means "Thus Gone" and this generally refers to Lord Gautama Buddha, who attained "Parinrvana", as he became "Adehamukta", i.e., gone beyond the cycle of birth and death, and became one with the "Adi-Buddha" or the "Param-Brahma". "Adehamukta", to my knowledge, is the term used by the Buddhists for "Videhamukta". The Zen Buddhists say " If you see the Buddha kill him", which means Gautama Buddha can never be seen again,  

  

"Tathagata-garbha" thus means the Buddha in the "Embryo-state" or it means the "Buddha in the Making", It is the Jivatma, which when sheds the veil of Ajnana, i.e., when attains the Jnana, attains the Buddha-hood.  

  

My 2 cents

Jai Shri Krishna
