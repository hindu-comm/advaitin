Namaste,

  

There are many slokas in SrimadbhAgavatam that explicitly supports Advaita. Similarly there are many more that support Dvaita also. This is similar to the aikya and bheda vAkyas in the Vedas. This can be reconciled only by accepting the reality of the jagath as the body of Brahman which is the VishishtAdvaitic view. The existence of Brahman as the inner Self (antaryAmi) of every entity in jagat and in the atman/jiva also is clearly taught in the AntaryAmi brAhmaNa which no serious VedAntin can reject as mithya. (Atmano antaro yamAtmA na veda yasya AtmA shareeram).

  

Jiva and jada are both indicators or linga for Brahman who is indwelling in them. If we reject the world as mithya then it amounts to rejecting dharma and that makes it impossible to know or attain Brahman. If Advaitins want to reject it later after attainment of paramArtha so be it. But as long as the jiva is embodied, jagat and body must be accepted as dependent reality which acts as an indicator and instrument / means to attain the indwelling Brahman. Only then we will be able to walk our talk.

  

The word "ते नमः"in this verse clearly supports bheda (dvaita) and complete surrender (V.Advaita). It should be realized that Brahman is not just in Advaitins. It is in Dvaitins , V.Advaitin's and in every being and non-being also. If the world/body is rejected as unreal then this vision to realize the all- pervading Brahman becomes impossible.

  

Regards,  
Suresh
