Dear Sri Sunil-ji and respected Advaitins,

  

I thought of writing back again to make my point clear.

After many years of my interaction with scholars in all the three schools of vedAnta

and continuous manana within me it is getting clearer day by day that Advaita, V.Advaita

and Dvaita are not mutually opposed to each but are complimentary to each other.

  

The teachings from the entire corpus of the Vedic scriptures cannot be strictly compartmentalized

into these individual views as truth infinite and can come in many different forms.

There is a need therefore for sincere Hindus/Truth seekers to study all of them and develop

an integral outlook for our own benefit and to protect sanAtana dharma as a whole.

  

Adviata and Dvaita are not opposed to each other at all. Infact many feel that Dvaita is

another and even a better way of presenting Advaita. Yes, few advaitins, that I know are with

me on this 🙂

  

Let me restate what Isaid earlier with regard to Dvaitin's teaching of Atma+atat+tvam_asi:

  

"***\<So if I am an advaitin, I would take dvaitin's interpretation of atat tvamasi as the elimination***

***of identification of Brahman associated with a particular name and form of Swetaketu.\>"***

***  ***

***Here Dvaita is trying to teach that the sky in the pot(Swetaketu) is not the sky******outside it.***

***It also says "tvam tadheeno asi" (you are dependent on that).***

***  ***

***Now Advaitin can use the snake-rope analogy to resolve this. Snake (pot-sky/Swetaketu)***

***is not the rope and is dependent on the rope.***

***Also, Dependence for existence = material cause****which leads to essential oneness.***

***  ***

***Also, jiva/jagat is accepted as a pratibimba of Brahman which is the Bimba or the original.***  

***So, this goes well with the Bhagavata Sloka 8.3.14 explained by Subbu-ji in this thread.***

  

******

***Also "Atma + atat + tvamasi" can be understood as Atma/Brahman is not something that,***

***but YOU. If this is not Advaita, what else is?***

***  ***

***Traditional Dvaitin's may not accept this, but this is a point that cannot be denied.***

***  ***

***Now let is come to Advaita:***

***  ***

***Advaita means "that which is opposed to dvaita". So the definition is based on Dvaita***

***only 🙂****without which it becomes difficult to give it any name.***

***  ***

***There is a need to move from duality to non-dual Brahman. Dualistic jagat cannot be***

***denied outrightly as all****duality/difference****in name/forms is cognized in consciousness only.***

***As long as one is embodied, there****is going to be duality and there is need to accept***

***one's body/speech as real at least as a pratibimba****which is a lower form of reality anyway.***

***But still it is needed to attain the non-dual****Brahman. So instead of denying jagat and***

***claiming that "I am Brahman" while still embodied, there is need to realize Brahman***

***within oneself and in all.***

***  ***

  

Now let us come to V.Advaita:

  

Here it is taught as "tat + tvam + asi" which is similar to Advaita.

Brahman is accepted as the inner Self of all jivas supporting jiva/jagat as body.

  

The body is also kind of reflection of the Self as it has opposite characteristics.

It is also a dependent reality on Brahman who is the inner Self.

  

So the non-duality of Brahman established as the ultimate Self or ParamAtma

who is the source of all existence/consciousness/bliss is accepted and this is the

ultimate teachingin Advaita also.

  

Anityatva of jagat:

Even though jagat is accepted as real in Dvaita and V.Advaita it is accepted

as anitya also.

  

So this points to the nityatva of atman. Location of this atman (self) in the all

pervading universal Self/Brahman is the essence of all sAdhana.

  

Traditional Vedantins in all the three schools when they do Achamanam, they

sip water and chant "AchutAya namah/svAha, AnantAya namah/svAha, and

GovindAya namah/svAha". That shows that the ultimate Brahman is the inner Self

of all.

  

This sarvAtmatva of Brahman is where all the diverse traditions converge.

Instead of denying any reality to jagat, there is a great need to recognize

Brahman in all name/forms.

  

So in the light of the Bhagavata sloka 8.3.14, there is no harm for Advaitin's if the

reality of jagat is accepted as pratibimba (as in Dvaita) or as body (in V.Advaita).

  

All these paths lead to same indwelling Brahman and whether it is saguna/nirguna,

one needs to discover within oneself only through tapasya as per the Vedic dictum

"tapasA Brahma vijigyAsasva"

  

My sincere pranAms for putting up with my ramblings and my only aim is to

unite Hindus from all traditions and this is possible only when the entire jagat

is looked upon as a linga or pointer to that supreme Brahman.

  

VedAntins from all the three tradition need to get united instead of fighting

each other over apparent doctrinal differences.

  

Namaste

Suresh
