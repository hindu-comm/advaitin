> Probabilistic knowledge could be understood as follows.
>
>   
>
> When we say a coin has 50% probability of coming up heads, it implies
> we have the pratyaxa pramANa knowledge that if we roll the die N
> times, we will observe with our eyes that \*nearly\* N/2 heads will
> come up. ( The use of the word 'nearly' does not retract from any
> datum being pratyaxa pramANa based, as in "the distance between the
> Mumbai and Delhi is 'nearly' 1420km" - that is pratyaxa). So the
> pratyaxa pramA is that 'a coin will be observed to turn up heads about
> half the time without any particular pattern in the series of heads
> and tails.' This very pratyaxa pramA is expressed in an alternative
> way by saying "the probability is half." - it's just a restatement of
> a large body of pratyaxa data.
>
>   
>
> In the case of weather predictions too, extensive pratyaxa data is
> analysed to form statements of the kind
>
> " El nino temperature warming in South america occurred on 10
> occasions \*and\* in 9 out of those 10 years, the monsoon weakened in
> India.' We capture this extensive body of data in a brief and concise
> way by saying 'the probability of a weak monsoon in some year is 90%
> since El nino is present'.
>
> So when the chain of causation is unknown (as in nonlinear phenomena)
> or even unknowable (as in the micro world of quanta) between certain
> variables, we still don't discard the observed perceptual data
> correlations between putative causes and their possible effects and
> instead merely restate the well documented pratyaxa data (like weather
> data documented over several decades) in a brief way by using
> probabilistic language.
>
>   
>
> To summarize - probabilistic statements and predictions are only large
> amounts of pratyaxa data restated. The restatement is necessitated
> because of lack of knowledge of all the factors that determine an
> effect, due to which we have strong correlations between certain
> possible causes and effects. So we choose to not discard the data, and
> also not form an imperfect anumAna or imperfect arthApatti and merely
> \*restate\* the large body of correlated pratyaxa data in a single
> statement using the 'probability' word. So that we can carry our
> umbrellas when rain is 'likely'.
