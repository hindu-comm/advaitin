Namaste Sri Prashant-ji,

  

A pramana is a means by which right-knowledge (prama) is obtained. Right-knowledge is that which corresponds to truth and it is free from doubt as against wrong-knowledge, which fails to correspond to truth, and doubtful-knowledge, which is ridden with doubt. Freedom from doubt is the mark of right-knowledge.

  

The knowledge that is obtained from analyses of data such as from regression analysis and forecasting techniques, etc., are probabilistic knowledge; they are not free from doubt and they cannot hence be classified as prama nor the means by which such knowledge is obtained as pramana. They work on the principle of *fit to best explanation*.

  

In the modern world, there is no such thing as pramana. Pratyaksha does not qualify to be a pramana because the knowledge obtained from it does not fit the definition of knowledge as held by the Western tradition (and the Contemporary tradition which follows the Western tradition) which is that*knowledge = justified true belief*. The only thing that would qualify to be called 'knowledge' would be inferential knowledge but the kind of inferential knowledge conferred by the Indian (Nyaya) inferential syllogism has been discounted by the argument that the vyapti can never be established until all cases - past, present and future - have been observed. This is an old Charvaka argument re-introduced in the modern world by David Hume and the argument has not been countered by any Indian scholar so far. It is sad but true. So, both pratyaksha and anumana stand discounted in the modern world. As regards Sruti, I need not say anything considering that the modern world thinks that the Vedas were composed around 2000 BC.

  

Warm regards,
