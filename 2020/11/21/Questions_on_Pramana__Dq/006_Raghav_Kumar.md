> A pramana is a means by which right-knowledge (prama) is obtained.
> Right-knowledge is that which corresponds to truth and it is free from
> doubt as against wrong-knowledge, which fails to correspond to truth,
> and doubtful-knowledge, which is ridden with doubt. Freedom from doubt
> is the mark of right-knowledge.

> The knowledge that is obtained from analyses of data such as from
> regression analysis and forecasting techniques, etc., are
> probabilistic knowledge; they are not free from doubt and they cannot
> hence be classified as prama nor the means by which such knowledge is
> obtained as pramana. They work on the principle of *fit to best
> explanation*.
>

> Namaste Chittaranjan ji
>
> Vyapti GYAnam is \*not\* required to fulfill the requirement (which
> you suggested) that all cases past present and future have been
> observed. Infallibility is not a prerequisite for perceptual or
> inferential knowledge to be labelled pramA. It is sufficient if it is
> not in contradiction with all other knowledge gathered \*up to\* that
> point.
>
>   
>
> Any scientific theory such as classical physics is regarded as valid
> knowledge (pramA) \*until\* it is revised or \*subsumed\* in another
> claim like quantum mechanics. This process of constant revision of
> inferential knowledge is present in \*both\* modern science as well as
> in traditional Indian epistemology. The non-absolute nature of any
> knowledge in vyavahaara is a given, even in traditional vyapti GYAnam.
> Charvaka misrepresents the process of gaining inferential knowledge.
>
>   
>
> We also therefore have the Brahma sUtra tarkasya apratiShThAnAd-vA
> which reiterates what was well-known viz., the non-finality of
> inferential knowledge ( and this was held as true within vyavahaara as
> well and not only for brahma tattva nirUpaNam).
>
>   
>
>
