A few thoughts on your questions:

  

All of science is an attempt to convert vast amounts of individual pratyaksha observations into generalized laukika shabdas that can be verified by future pratyaksha. Mathematical formulas in physics or equations of chemical reactions etc are also shabdas that have taken into account all past pratyaksha (experimental evidence). Of course, they can be understood only by those who have the samskAras to understand those shabdas. We study about Kepler's laws of planetary motion, Newton's law of Gravitation or Einstein's General and Special theories of relativity etc. They all were based on observations (pratyaksha) but then what we study are shabadas distilled by the scientific community. So eventually a lot of science comes to us in the form of laukia shabda pramaNa only. It is not sublated by any other pramaNA at the vyAvahArika level and we clearly see the utility of it at vyAvahArika level and therefore it is an important part of how we understand and interact with the vyAvahArika world. If any of these laukika shabdas are contradicted by any new pratyaksha experiments, they are subject to revision and whoever discovers the revision may also get a Nobel prize for it. As an example, the Ultraviolet catastrophe observations led Einstein to propose the particle nature (quanta) for light when light was considered purely as a wave at that time (for which he got the Nobel). The discovery by Michaelson and Morley that the speed of light is constant in all frames of reference led Einstein to the conclusion that time is relative. He then proposed the special and general theories of Relativity revising the shabdas of science that came from Newton (Einstein did not get a Nobel for that though). And the pratyaksha that a single-electron-a- a-time-beam interferes with itself in the double slit experiment led to the development of the mind numbing laukika shabdas of quantum physics which demolishes all ideas of well defined minute golf ball like particles in the subatomic world. And the models of quantum physics are of great utility in vyAvahArika world inspite of being very probabilistic and highly un-common-sensical.

  

To put in it brief, all sciences take into account experiments (pratyaksha), and come with with models (laukika shabda) to make useful predictions (about future pratyaksha). The shabdas of science HAVE to be subservient to pratyaksha.

  

Of late, computers have become extremely good at coming up with complex models automatically based on machine learning (ML). ML is about a machine building the model based on learning from a data set set used to train the machine (which is based on pratyaksha). The model the machine builds itself is a form of shabda represented in some machine language that humans in general may not have the samskAras to read and understand, but the machine does! The output of the model are shabdas using which we come to know as its prediction....and it is up to us to make use of those shabdas or not based on our Sharaddha in the model. Time will certainly tell us if the knowledge that the shabdas give us are pramA (right knowledge) or bhramA (wrong knowledge)! Just like any of the sciences, the shabdas of such machines may be considered as pramA
until it is sublated by a more powerful pramANa such as pratyaksha. We often lose shraddha in the weather prediction models when we see it make wrong predictions. On the other hand, there are some really good models that people have also come to rely on. If this was not the case, AI wouldn't have such widespread acceptance and people would not be speaking to Siri or Alexa to the extent that they do. Until it fails (i.e is sublated), AI model based predictions and responses would be treated as pramA by those who trust it. If/when it fails, it would get categorized as bhramA only to get further revised by further training of the machine. So the process appears to be similar to how science works...assume relative truth until there is a need to revise.

  

Lastly, some things in vyAvaharikA are inherently probabilistic. If you ask what is the chance that I will get 1 if I roll a 6 sided dice, the answer is of course "you have a 1/6th chance of getting the number 1". The fact that the sentence has a probability associated doesn't undermine its relative truth. Hence, it can be taken as valid laukika shabda. So also, it goes with the uncertainty of position and velocity of subatomic particles. Quantum Physics can only speak in terms of probabilities as the uncertainty is inherent in nature at the subatomic level. Inspite of the uncertainty, humans can still build well behaved nuclear reactors and generate power.
