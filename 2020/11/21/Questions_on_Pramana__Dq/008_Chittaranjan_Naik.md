Namaste Sri Raghav-ji,

  

  

*"Vyapti GYAnam is \*not\* required to fulfill the requirement (which you suggested) that all cases past present and future have been observed. Infallibility is not a prerequisite for perceptual or inferential knowledge to be labelled pramA. It is sufficient if it is not in contradiction with all other knowledge gathered \*up to\* that point." *  

  

If that should be the case, could you please explain why the Nyayayikas propose alaukika pratyaksha (extra-ordinary perception) as the means to the establish the vyapti?

  

Also, Advaita which does not accept the alaukika pratyaksha of Nyaya, says that the invariable concomitance (vyapti) may be known either during the first or second or any of the successive perceptions of the relation (please see Vedanta Paribhasa). If it would have been sufficient merely that the knowledge is not in contradiction with other knowledge up to that point, it would have been superfluous to speak of knowledge of vyapti or invariable concomitance as occurring during some specific perception of the relation.

  

In the Indian Vedic tradition, knowledge of vyapti is knowledge of the invariable concomitance as prevailing for all time up to the rise of brahma jnana whereupon the entirety of vyavaharika realm is sublated.
It is not knowledge that is merely uncontradicted up to that point in time as you suggest.

  

  

*"Any scientific theory such as classical physics is regarded as valid knowledge (pramA) \*until\* it is revised or \*subsumed\* in another claim like quantum mechanics. This process of constant revision of inferential knowledge is present in \*both\* modern science as well as in traditional Indian epistemology.*

* *  

This is true for scientific knowledge which is positivist and not for right-knowledge or prama as it is held in the Indian tradition.

  

  

*"The non-absolute nature of any knowledge in vyavahaara is a given, even in traditional vyapti GYAnam."*

  

The knowledge of vyapti once it is had would be sublated only on the rise of brahma-jnana and not within the domain of vyaharika sathya.

  

  

*"So it's not clear how you assert that modern science does not accept pratyaxa as a means of knowledge." *  

  

It is not merely I that assert it; scientists and modern philosophers are themselves are not able to explain how we may be able to claim that a representation produced by the brain at the end of a long causal chain corresponds with the actual object in the world. The theory of perception espoused by science leads to representationalism and representationalism cannot show how the world we see is the world as it really is.

  

  

Regards,

Chittaranjan
