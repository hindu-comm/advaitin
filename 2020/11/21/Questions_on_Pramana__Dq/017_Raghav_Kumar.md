Namaste Chittaranjan ji

Firstly, it's a relief to learn you recovered from the virus and are well. Hope you continue to share your knowledge through your works.

  

Regarding your idea that "any vyApti GYAnam (which is held to be pramA) is necessarily not sublatable for all time except by Brahma GYAnam" - it does not accord with traditional vedanta pramANa understanding of vedanta paribhAShA etc., as I understand it.

  

Regarding perception - Any perception as it arises is regarded by default as (valid) pramA unless there is bAdha by other prama-s, in which case the perception stands sublated as prAtibhAsika. This default pramA status accorded to a given perception is at the heart of vedAntic epistemology of svataH-prAmANyavAda.

  

Similarly for anumAna, too, svataH-prAmANyavAda implies that, a given syllogism, if prima facie not found to have a defective (vyabhicArI) hetu etc., it is by default granted pramA status.

  

Otherwise, (if we go along with what you said) then we can never grant pramA status to \*any\* given vyApti since - who knows if the invariable concomittance will invariably continue in all future instances of the linga/hetu. This way, we can never get any pramA whatsoever from anumAna. If i am misrepresenting what you are saying regarding the absoluteness of vyApti GYAnam within vyavahara (for it to be called pramA), kindly point out.

  

Any vyApti is first accepted as pramA (unless there are obvious defects). And then there is continuous distillation of the body of knowledge obtained by vyApti GYAnam which may very well lead to revisions and discarding of some of the older anumAna-s based on new viparIta dRShTanta-s or some hetvAbhAsa-s which may have come to light. This process of progress/modification in inferential knowledge known to humanity (even before the final sublation by Brahma GYAnam),is very well accepted in traditional vedantic epistemology too and is hinted at in sutras 2.2.11 तर्काप्रतिष्ठानादप्यन्यथानुमेयमिति चेदेवमप्यविमोक्षप्रसङ्गः etc. That the conclusions of tarka on a given topic are non-final and therefore Brahma GYAnam is not arrivable purely through tarka is unexceptionable. But, in vyavahaara, tarka need not be final in order to be conferred the status of pramA.

  

Regarded the idea of vyApti GYAnam insight, it can certainly arise any time, be it upon the first, second or later instance of perception of hetu and sAdhya. The concomittance is noticed/grasped (by the first discoverer) through spontaneous insight and is based on sharpness of intellect. The others learn it by being taught. That depends on medhA, intelligence etc. That's not surprising.
