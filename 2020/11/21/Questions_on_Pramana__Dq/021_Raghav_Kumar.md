"Take the case of the invariable relation between smoke and fire. When we apprehend the relation between smoke and fire, do we grasp a relationship that exists intrinsically between smoke and fire or do we grasp a relationship that is merely bestowed upon smoke and fire by the mind? "

  

Namaste Chittaranjan ji

  

The general context -

  

A few instances of concomittance are initially observed by pratyaxa in the kitchen, in the yajna shala etc., and after observing of one, two or many instances of concomittance, the flash of insight of invariable concomittance arises and the vyApti GYAnam takes root in the mind. With such a prepared mind, whenever a subsequent instance of smoke is perceived, fire is inferred based on the already appreciated/asserted vyApti present in one's mind. The arising of vyApti GYAnam (the leap of faith so to speak, from simple concomittance to invariable concomittance) can occur anytime after observing at least one instance of simple concomittance. This conclusion about invariable concomittance (i.c.) is arrived at by our mind alone. (This is the usual process as I would put it).

  

Now I understand you to be asking whether, this leap of faith to conclude i.c. is based on observing any intrinsic relationship between the hetu or sAdhya, \*or\* is it based on our own mind's suo motu assertion.

  

My understanding is that both are possible. In cases where there is a more comprehensive understanding of what the hetu is and how it occurs, then i.c. is based on grasping some intrinsic relation. \*But\*, this is not always the case.

  

Take the case of two events X and Y which are observed to occur successively, and which have a common cause called Z. (Z causes both X and Y). Then, even if someone is totally unaware of Z and how and if Z causes both X and Y, still, he can infer the existence of X by the observed hetu of Y, and assert i.c. between Y and X. Although he does not notice or grasp any clear relationship between X and Y.
