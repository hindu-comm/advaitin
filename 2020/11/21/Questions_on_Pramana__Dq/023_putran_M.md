Namaskaram Chitta-ji, Raghav-ji, others,

  

The issue I am reading is this:

  

Does “vyavaharika satya” refer to a universal “Ishvara standpoint” of what is manifest existence, or is it a variable notion that corresponds to each jiva’s standpoint of observation and thereby knowledge?

  

In the former case, it makes sense to say knowledge is prama only where it reveals the reality of Ishvara’s manifestation. If what is deemed as satya by jivas in their vyavahara is not vyavaharika satya, then that “knowledge” of the jiva never was, is, will be ‘valid’ and will eventually find correction assuming or according to a principle that all objects and their relations (whatever is vyavaharika satya) are indeed ‘knowable’ through right application of pramanas. (Not sure of the extent of this principle as per tradition.)

  

If vyavaharika satya is itself relative to the individual standpoints of observation, corresponding to and not independent of the body-mind of the observer, then it makes better sense that the validity of knowledge is also relative to the observer. The knowledge of snake is prama so long as it is not contradicted by subsequent knowledge of rope.

  

The other possible assertion here is that even though vyavaharika satya is Ishvara’s standpoint of things, still prama is an individualized notion. If you “know” snake, then it is prama to you even if it is rope in fact.
