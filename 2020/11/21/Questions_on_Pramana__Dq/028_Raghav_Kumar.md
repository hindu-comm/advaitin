Namaste Chittaranjan ji

My use of the words, leap of faith was only a figure of speech to indicate that it (vyApti GYAnam) is regarded in vedanta to be fundamental enough to not warrant questions such as how does it arise or what makes it arise. We could use better alternative words like insight etc. - the mystery is that after observing one or more instances of simple concomittance, the vyApti GYAnam postulating invariable concomittance arises.

  

Regarding whether pramA is infallible within vyavahAra etc. -

There is the possibility of progressive depth in vyavahaarika GYAnam whereby, even in the realm of pramA which deals with Ishvara sRShTi, there is indefinite improvement or refinement possible. The earlier approximate GYAnam need not be dismissed as bhrama, since the imprecision is known and factored in to the perception.

  

For example - the status of imprecise or approximate knowledge such as "the distance between a point in Mumbai to a point in Delhi is 1420km" need not be labelled not-pramA.  

  

And regarding vyAvhArika Prama being sublated even prior to Brahma GYAnam - we do accept the capacity of (tUla) avidya to create objects of perceptions like a rope-snake for us which are provisionally held to be pramA but are sublated as rope. Therefore, a vyavahaarika pramA can well be sublated within vyavahAra itself, much before the ultimate sublation of all vyavahAra by brahma GYAnam.

  

So if we ask, what is the status of a perception for the initial few hours, which some hours later is understood as bhrama? As I understand it, avidyA actually gives rise to a bhrAnti-viShaya (like the snake) and so from the individual's own perspective, it has to be taken as pramA but later he sublates it by samyak GYAnam upon which it gets relegated to bhrama status.
