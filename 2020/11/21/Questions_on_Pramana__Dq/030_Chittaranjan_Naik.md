Namaste Sri Raghav-ji,

  

*"My use of the words, leap of faith was only a figure of speech to indicate that it (vyApti GYAnam) is regarded in vedanta to be fundamental enough to not warrant questions such as how does it arise or what makes it arise."  *

  

That is not correct. On the contrary, it is in the Vedic tradition alone that the question "*What is that confers validity to all our knowledge?"* is asked and is pursued right up to its logical conclusion. This is what the *svatah-pramana verses paratah-pramana debate* is all about.

  

  

*We could use better alternative words like insight etc. - the mystery is that after observing one or more instances of simple concomittance, the vyApti GYAnam postulating invariable concomittance arises.*  

  

I agree that it is a mystery for the common man but the insight arises due to an internal spark which may be broadly termed as anuvyavasaya or self-cognition wherein the nature of the padarthas such as samanya and vishesha, etc., reveal themselves.

  

  

*Regarding whether pramA is infallible within vyavahAra etc. -*

*There is the possibility of progressive depth in vyavahaarika GYAnam whereby, even in the realm of pramA which deals with Ishvara sRShTi, there is indefinite improvement or refinement possible. The earlier approximate GYAnam need not be dismissed as bhrama, since the imprecision is known and factored in to the perception.*

  

Whether prama is infallible within vyavahara is determined by the definition of prama that prevails within the realm of vyvavaharika sathya and this definition (as it comes from Pramana-Shastra and which is accepted by Vedanta) is that prama is right-knowledge which is free from doubt. There are two components to the definition: (i) it must be right-knowledge, i.e., it must correspond to reality as it manifests in vyavahara, and (ii) the knowledge must be free from doubt. So, even though it is held in Vedanta that every instance of knowledge is valid at the time of its cognition, that cognition may be invalidated by a subsequent knowledge that either sublates it or presents some contradiction whereby the previous cognition becomes subject to doubt. So, yes, there are a great many things about which our knowledge of objects is progressively acquired but such knowledge which is still riddled with doubt cannot be termed prama as per the definition of prama.

  

Also, if knowledge riddled with doubt should be called prama, it would undermine the entire debate between svatah-pramana and paratah-pramana because the Nyayayika raies the occasion where there is presence of doubt to challenge the svatah-pramanya postulate of the Mimamsaka / Vedantin. If knowledge with doubt should be called prama, there would be no svatah-pramana verses paratah-pramana debate at all. :-)

  

  

*For example - the status of imprecise or approximate knowledge such as "the distance between a point in Mumbai to a point in Delhi is 1420km" need not be labelled not-pramA.*

  

The question is not what it is that may be termed prama or aprama in our tradition but that the notion of pramana has been rejected in the modern world and has been replaced by what may be called "Inference to best explanation". Astonishing as it may seem, the knowledge that "the distance between between a point in Mumbai to a point in Delhi is 1420 km" may be regarded as not apodictic (i.e., not prama) until the distance has been observed in the future too. There is no place for apriori convictions in this matter, it has to be decided a posteriori and that can be done only after it has been observed at all points in time. Please note that after the advent of David Hume, the notion of causality, causal connections and other phenomena that appear to display persistence all stand on tenuous foundations.

  

  

*And regarding vyAvhArika Prama being sublated even prior to Brahma GYAnam - we do accept the capacity of (tUla) avidya to create objects of perceptions like a rope-snake for us which are provisionally held to be pramA but are sublated as rope. Therefore, a vyavahaarika pramA can well be sublated within vyavahAra itself, much before the ultimate sublation of all vyavahAra by brahma GYAnam.*  

  

*So if we ask, what is the status of a perception for the initial few hours, which some hours later is understood as bhrama? As I understand it, avidyA actually gives rise to a bhrAnti-viShaya (like the snake) and so from the individual's own perspective, it has to be taken as pramA but later he sublates it by samyak GYAnam upon which it gets relegated to bhrama status.*  

  

Yes, knowledge in the form perceptual experiences may be sublated by the rise of subsequent knowledge. But please note that the knowledge of words and their meanings do not get sublated during such sublations. So, even though knowledge of snake may get sublated upon the subsequent rise of knowledge revealing that the object in reality is a rope and not a snake, the knowledge of the words 'rope', 'snake', and their meanings do not undergo sublation. Indeed, it is the persistence of words and their meanings that lend morphological structure to our various experiences. The connection between words like 'smoke' and 'fire' and the objects they denote do not change within vyavahara. This is an inviolable principle of Mimamsa / Vedanta; for if words were to change their meanings and become chimerical, dharma itself would be subverted. So, as long as the objects denoted by the words 'smoke' and 'fire' remain constant, we may seek to know the relation that abides between the objects denoted by these two words; and once this knowledge is obtained and has been validated, it may be termed as prama and such knowledge does not get sublated by the rise of any subsequent knowledge.
