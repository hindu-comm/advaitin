> Sure, both are vyavahaarika pramA for those particular observers -

>
> If two people from \*different frames of reference\* see an object
> different in size, and we call each of their perceptions pramA for
> each of them respectively, there is no issue. And if they now argue
> about who is right, that is called the process of distillation of
> pramA-s. Over a period of time, there is either reconciliation or
> elimination of some putative prramA-s and the distilled pramA-s get
> commmunicated by shabda pramANa etc. The frame of reference (speeds of
> the observers in this case) will provide the upAdhi which qualifies
> each of the pramA-s. I don't see any problem here. The statements
> would be "the distance between X and Y as seen from a frame of
> reference moving with V1 velocity, is 1420km." And "with that velocity
> V2 w.r..t V1 is 1000km". So there is some mathematical reconciliation
> which is arrived at to reconcile these relative-velocity-dependent
> distance. Nothing problematic here, surely.
