Namaste Sri Raghav-ji,

  

  

*:) Really? But the arising of doubt about everything in vyavahAra does indeed occur. To say that no \*doubts\* should ever be 'raisable' anytime in the future too, for a perception or inference to be granted pramA status, is an impossible requirement.*  

  
Yes, a veridical perception grants indubitable knowledge about the object (of that perception) and it will not invite further doubts (for that percipient) about that knowledge. This is the position of the Vedic tradition. If you don't agree with it, let us agree to disagree.

  

  

*So you agree that it is indeed pramA from an Indian traditional perspective?*

  

I have never had any problem with prama as defined from the Indian perspective. The issue that had i pointed to in my response to Sri Prashant Parikh's post was that the pramanas by which the Indian Vedic tradition claims to obtain knowledge have been discounted in the modern world and that Indian scholars have sadly not responded to this situation.

  

  

*But only the western empiricist perspective has no equivalent pramA concept? Because of the representational nature of sense data which renders all perception subjective and so western science has a problem? Is that what you are saying?*

  

Yes.

  

  

Thank you for the discussion. Let us stop at this point.
