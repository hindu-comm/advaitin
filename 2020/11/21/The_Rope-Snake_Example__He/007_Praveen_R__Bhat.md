> In the Rope-Snake example, Avidya to me seems more a nimitta karana
> and not an upadana karana because the material for the Snake has
> already been provided by the Rope.

First of all, pls understand the nimitta-kAraNa without this dependency on upAdAna-kAraNa. Just because something is an upAdAna-kAraNa, it doesn't mean that it cannot be a nimitta-kAraNa. Hence the example of svapna to understand brahman as both abhinna-nimittopAdAna-kAraNa or the spider in Mundakopanishad. Secondly, rope is \*not\* the upAdAna-kAraNa of the snake. There is no rope found in any snake, even imaginary. avidyA is the material, which is subtle, which manifests as the mind and that is the subtle snake too bringing in a subjectively real snake for the perceiver.

  

Kindly do not understand avidyA through the translation ignorance, as in English, there is either knowledge or no knowledge, the word for the latter being ignorance. Sanskrit has no such limitations in the prefix/ upasarga 'a'. It can be in sixmeanings, only one being absence. Here, it means an existententity (with mithyA/ vyAvahArika reality) which is opposed to vidyA. It is a bhAvapadArtha.



> Since the Snake is only an appearance, Avidya also can only be a
> vivarta nimitta Karana -

This has no basis, the error can be seen in thinking that an emotion such as krodha can be present without the mind changing!



> a cause which brings about an appearance change and not any real
> change. How can Avidya be parinami upadana karana because the term
> parinami suggests real change which as we have seen is not possible in
> this case?

We have not seen it to be not possible. The logic is incorrectly used with an erroneous assumption to start with. To say that avidyA doesn't undergo change, you should be able to cognize avidyA and see the change. Has it been seen as not happening? Definitely not! I suspect that the technical words used have not been understood well. I suggest to look into examples of vivarta and pariNAmi kAraNas given in shAstras and see if you are using them the same way or whether you feel the examples do not appear to be inline with your understanding. If so, pls understand those examples well and then extend them to the context.
