Namaste,

I have enjoyed the many well-informed posts that I have started setting in my inbox in the last few months. This is my first post here. My background in brief. I have been reading and listening to works by Pujya Swami Dayananda Saraswathi (AVG) and several of his disciples including Swami Paramarthananda in particular over the past 20 years. I have seen many interesting posts on Mithyatvam of the world and thought I would add my views. Please let me know your thoughts.

Pujya Swamiji has written and spoken many times on “Bringing Ishwara into your life” and “Relating to Ishwara”. He has said – “All that is here is God”. “We don’t say there is one God. We say there is only God”. “Ishwara is the maker and the material for the world” “Ishwara is the Universal order of Dharma” and “Ishwara is the Karma Phala datta”. From a Vedantic perspective, Ishwara is taken as Maya Sahitha Brahman.

Now, in the statement "Brahma Satyam Jagan Mithya" the word “Mithya” is taken as neither real (Brahman alone is real because everything else is dependent on it) nor unreal (as in the horns on a man). Since Jagat which is just name and form is dependent on Brahman it is said to be Mithya (just like ring is just name and form and is dependent on gold). But in every name and form of Jagat there is Brahman just like in every ring there is gold. So, one cannot ignore any name and form, say a tree, because there is Brahman in it. This is the Vyavaaharika view of the world. With this view, which is not world-denying, one can actively engage in this world and perform one’s duties (Karma Yoga) and purify one’s mind to get it ready for Gnanam. This is the Pravritti marga. This is why Pujya Swamiji talked about relating to Ishwara as we saw earlier.

If we see every name and form of Jagat as Brahman alone (similar to seeing ring as Gold alone) then Jagat disappears (just as ring disappears) and only Brahman remains. This is the Paramaarthika view. Still, when a Jnani functions he still has to engage at the Vyavahaarika level i.e. with the Jagat with name and form. Adi Sankara debated with followers of various Astika and Nastika schools to reestablish Vedanta. He did not ignore them as Mithya. He travelled the length and breadth of India at great hardship and his efforts were definitely not half-hearted. Krishna helped establish Dharma by helping Pandavas. Even though the Jnanis were free not to act the fact that they did commit notable acts in this Jagat suggests that the Jagat should not be ignored as just Mithya.
