Namaskaram Karthik-ji,

  

That the world is mithya is not a sentimental stance; it is stated as a statement of fact. It is similar to people speaking of impermanence of body; doesn't mean at once we are to ignore life since it is anitya. But if someone looks at the events of life with a certain detachment because of the impermanence, we cannot find fault at their pointing to the impermanence - because it is fact. Perhaps we can argue why we should still engage in life, why there is relative meaning that matters, or why there is implicit hypocrisy in their argument vs their actual life. Etc.

  

Not everyone will respond to that fact in the same way, at least not intellectually. So we should take care not to attack the principle of "mithyatva" in the name of attacking how people respond to it in a 'wrong' way.

  

As you point out, advaita acharyas as well as Sri Krishna point out the pravritti marga, of dharma, karma yoga, bhakti yoga, etc. for most of us who are fixated on our body-identity. I don't know who you are addressing to here but generally the forum is not contradicting what you are saying.

  

With regard to how an advaitin internalizes or "see" Ishvara, it can vary according to how he practices bhakti yoga as well as how he does mananam of advaita tattva. It will be an ongoing process of his sadhana because the boundary between i and He is mithya.
