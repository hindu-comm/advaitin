0.  

  
For the ordinary person, there is a constant 'meditation' that is the foundation of our usual living: it is the "I am this body-mind," that underlies all the "I am walking, I am talking, I am meditating". The Shastra says this is superimposition on that actionless Atman that is our Reality.  
  
This identification of Self as body-mind is a product of Ignorance. Suspending the mind in sleep or 'meditation' or through distractions will not remove this Ajnana. Only through the Realization/Knowledge of Self. When the Self is realized, the everpresent true Meditation is revealed, effortless and real. The jnani is ever in this "meditation" no matter the state of affairs of the body-mind.  
  
It is like a Man who is thinking "I am Buffalo". "I want better hay!" "Meditation will help me find it!!" Surely meditation will help him (possibly find better hay), but his mixup will remain as it is. The shift from  
Buffalo to Man has to happen. Once the realization happens, "Buffalo" is gone once and for all (whether vasanas linger a bit or not, the fundamental turn in awareness is absolute.)  
  
Initially meditation is part of upasana (alongside and/or in conjunction with karmayoga, bhaktiyoga, etc.) for the purpose of making the mind fit and capable and introspective. But at some later stage, the seeker must receive Knowledge from Shastra (through Guru etc.) which becomes the object of meditation. Here, meditation is like the Hands driving the Spear of received Knowledge at the apparently impregnable Fort of Ignorance. Our sages confirm that the Spear will pierce through, ignorance vanish, Self/Knowledge is realized.  
  
One other point: Why should I want to realize I am man? I just want to meditate and be the happier buffalo. Well and good, but happiness based on this ignorance is also unreal. A couple of thrashings of the right kind, and the buffalo's castle will crash.    
  
  
1.  
  
See, if your goal is good music, you may be excited to discover a piano. But if your goal is carnatic music, it may not be a big deal to get a piano; a veena is more appropriate. If someone speaks of carnatic music and its expressions of gamaka, and you are a skeptic to begin with that such is even possible because all you see are pianos, then either you have to trust and just move forward in the direction shown, or you can just as well stay with the piano and western music and think that is all.  
  
If you find involuntary-breath meditation useful for concentration and absorption, then take it up. But the goal is moksha and not sitting on your head. We should bother on Hinduism vs Buddhism only where it actually relates to Moksha or Veda and how they differ in such things, not so much on who has patent on what technique - especially for loukikas whose actual contemplation is getting advantage in their samsaric lives. It is with regard to the End-goal that we have to ask which is the best/complete path for that purpose; will the Buddhist methods send me in wasteful arduous directions because they are based on wrong premises, or can they complement the Vedic path? Gaudapada used Buddhist categories while establishing Vedanta, and Buddhism being an off-shoot of the Vedic religion has in it several concepts of Hinduism modified according to their viewpoints.  
  
In fact sadhakas as they deal with their body-minds may discover and use variations of methods according to their immediate needs; sometimes you need to be proactive and sometimes you turn to witness-mode. For all the discussions in our texts on the Atman being actionless, the detached witness (sakshi), etc., you think the only meditation taught is iccha-driven? I don't think so (can ask around).  
  
2.  
  
"I am meditating/watching on involuntary breath (or thoughts as they arise)" or "I am consciously deliberating breath/thought", both are upasana only taken up by some jiva to obtain chitta-shuddhi or chitta-ekagratha. Neither alters the condition of ajnana. The body-mind-world is part of Ishvara's prakriti and according to your karma, Ishvara will put it through the necessary trials and experiences. If you deserve it, he may give you a good/sought experience/awareness; if you deserve/require otherwise, he can create a whirlpool in your prakriti in spite of all meditation. Notions of meditation that set forth goals of stability, power, focus etc. in environment, body or mind are therefore akin to special forms of prayer to God (who is the  
karma-phala-data.) In our traditions therefore, the recommended path is of conscious meditation and mindfulness of the Reality that is Ishvara, as the Source of Creation (Prakriti) and the indwelling Consciousness (antaryami Purusha), of whom this body-mind-world is part and parcel. This is not about deliberated imagination; it is about being aligned to Truth: Ekam eva adviteeyam; tattvamasi - not just while "meditating" but always.

  

This Unitary Awareness of Ishvara is difficult for body-identified jivas, so there are several personalized approaches recommended that also have intrinsic validity as per tradition. For instance, the Swami Paramarthananda (like also Sri Ramakrishna) said in his lecture that Ishvara being omnipotent can give darshanam to the devotee seeking Him in a particular form. So our meditation on Ishvara through Ishta-devata is valid and can obtain darshanam, worldly wants, or visesha jnana of Him. But here is the grandeur of Hinduism. If that jiva cannot accept Ishta devata or (say) "conscious breathing", but can accept meditation on "involuntary breathing", that meditation is also prayer to the same all-pervading Ishvara who alone can grant the desired phala of mind-focus etc. It may be harder path and may require a turn somewhere later, but it may be presently suitable to that jiva in its state of ajnana. So, Hinduism can accept such paths as well knowing that it fulfills the needs in the journey of that jiva, except where its votaries build a fort of philosophy and theology that contradicts, maligns or seeks to "destroy" Veda/Vedic religion - in such case, of course we will "disapprove" of them in the right contexts.

3.  
  
When the body-mind is put under great stress, either by yourself under "meditation" and austerity, or by Ishvara (beyond your volition), where your present identifications of self and external holds of security have become either useless or liabilities to your survival in the situation, then naturally as you deal with such challenge, your knowledge of the body-mind's mechanics will become refined, you have the opportunity to learn and become aware/mindful of thoughts, trace where and how they arise, how mind impacts body and viseversa, be the sakshi, separate the gross world and the subtle and practice dwelling in one as manifest now, self vs not-self, etc. Yes you can become more mindful of the processes below the typical thinking level.  
  
But this is not about Hinduism or Buddhism. Most real sadhakas (walkers of the razor's edge) will gain such insights if Ishvara wills it so. And if Ishvara wills, ordinary folk can go through situations where they have to become thus mindful and self-aware. The question for all of them is whether such knowledge, insights, experience turns them into real mumukshus. Generally not; most will fall back into samsara happily as soon as the fire is gone. Their knowledge is like some insurance and their mindfulness is emergency medicine for crisis situation (and typically, crisis means ego-protection).  
  
Suppose as you claim, Hindu texts are not analyzing the mechanics of mind or discussing "mindfulness" (to the same extent); it is not because the saints were ignorant of such things. The focus is always on the Atman and not the details of prakriti. "I am Real, the unchanging, Immutable!" - that is the truth ever shining (with shastra as external pramana) amidst whatever illusions are thrown up by maya/prakriti.  
  
For example, we see a shop full of ornaments and are lost in the variety, not knowing what to choose or why one is different from other, pleased sometimes and dejected others.  
  
The shop has two parrots in a cage. One is Buddhist and the other Hindu.  
  
Buddhist parrot: "Look you are struggling because the shapes are like this and that. Shapes like this are followed by shapes like that. Be ever mindful of the arrangements as they are and your struggles will cease."  
  
Hindu parrot: "Sir/Madam, it is all Gold no matter the shape. Realize and become mindful of that reality and your struggles will cease."  
  
Customer: "Buddhist parrot is impressive, knows the shop inside-out, I can see and follow what it says. I cannot trust that Hindu parrot (who seems not as knowledgeable of the shapes) nor am I convinced that knowing all is Gold is proper solution to my shape-problem."  
  
Hindu Shop-keeper: "That is fine, sir. Hindu parrot is telling truth, but if you can't accept and follow its advice, follow the Buddhist parrot. Not an issue right now."  
  
That is the conviction and approach. All the details of body-mind and bondage are secondary in interest for the way to liberation is realization of Self (beyond body-mind). So, this becomes the overriding theme in Hinduism and the methods at large follow it. Whereas Buddhism is against such affirmation of Brahman if not committing to the outright negation of its reality beyond mental ideation; so its approach is different and there may therefore be greater focus and analysis on the details of how body-mind-world are working to create bondage.  
  
4.  
  

One place to see this methodology at work is how we (sadhakas/bhaktas) link every experience to Ishvara either as His granting our karma-phala, or His play, or as just His manifestation. This includes even our  
thoughts especially where they rise without our volition; even ordinary folk like me are working in this manner - not because it is some secret method but just because that is the natural bhakti process - to  
see the Gold behind all shapes.  
  
In Hinduism therefore, the Buddhist non-judgmental mindfulness of prakriti is inseparable from mindfulness of the underlying purusha tattva, thus realizing the manifest Reality as Ishvara.  
  
Everything is dependent on each other and interlinked because everything is Ishvara manifesting as if "every thing". The logical/intuitive reference point is "Ekam eva adviteeyam/tattvamasi" - which however  
until realization proper is received knowledgefrom shastra pramana.

5.  
  
One can also see this principle at play in jnana-approaches. I will mention something I like to follow. (Understand as upasana only; not something guru or sampradaya sanctioned for advanced sadhakas. Don't  
know all that.)  
  
Recall my poem on "Manifest Present" where I distinguished the Sense-world and the Mind-world. The sense-world denotes prakriti as it is right now, as possibly available for direct cognition by senses+mind.  
The mind-world refers to both the world of independent imagination ("Me as superman flying in the sky") and the intertwined world of perception+imagination ("seeing a mirage as water") based on superimpositions,  
interpretations, judgments, etc. on the sense-world. In the latter case, the mind is understood as possibly interfering and distorting our apprehension of reality.  
  
The Manifest Present refers to both these worlds as manifest now in Ishvara's or our consciousness. However the initial priority as if "real" is given to the sense-world.  
  
There are two essential parts/steps to the open-eyed contemplation.  
  
1. Stay with the sense-world, resist getting pulled into the mind-world. This means you move scene to scene, alert to the now but avoid making connections with and dragging along the past and future.  
  
2. Become aware of the Self as manifesting/pervading that sense-world in awareness. (Or let the awareness of Self pervade the awareness of sense-world). This means we shift from "(I am) seeing the sense-world"  
to "I am manifesting thus".  
  
In the way I practice this, the step 2 is the necessary consummation and culmination; I have to reach it after somewhat stabilizing in 1 and then the dhyana is to stay in that total awareness. The focus is not  
just mindfulness of things as they are as much as of the Self appearing thus. ("Hand up or hand down, you are the same", so the awareness of "I" pervades and absorbs all changes.)  
  
3. Later on and separately, we can also practice this connecting to Self even as thoughts arise in mind so that the thoughts get pulled into this Self-awareness - "I am manifesting as this (mind-world)." (In  
this practice we will naturally start checking every thought at as early a budding point as we can.)  
  
1. is similar to non-judgmental mindfulness of prakriti, whereas 2 (and 3) is of the Purusha within. But unlike the Bhakti approach, where the Self is typically seen the Personal Ishvara of the world in our  
consciousness, here in the jnana approach, the Self is nothing but the "I" of 'All'. We remove the typical limitations of body-mind and instead identify it with and as manifesting all that appears in  
consciousness.

6.  
  
A possible sequence in guiding our focus during this type of contemplation/meditation.  
  
  
world/prakriti (i am seeing/experiencing)  
-\> sense-world (as it is now)  
-\> (i am seeing) thus  
-\> I am the witnessing Consciousness/Sakshi/Purusha (of this) \[at a later point the 'this' will include the mind-world as well\]  
-\> I am manifesting thus \[dissolving (uniting) purusha-prakriti bheda in the universal I\]  
-\> I am (manifest) thus  
-\> I am (thus)  
-\> I am  
-\>\> I \[Realization of nondual Self/Fullness/Pure Consciousness/Awareness\]  
  
What I do primarily follows step 2, in trying to grasp the sense-world without being unduly dragged into nearby mind-worlds, and then in shifting awareness to somewhere between "I am manifesting thus" and  
"I am (thus)". (Can't express through words alone since the thought alignments may involve body-mind shifts; people have to try.) But I thought systematic sadhakas can also see a more refined step by step  
process like above. People inclined to this type of thing (and not having formal guru for guide) have to test and decide what works.  
  
Some more thoughts:  
  
As I said before, upasana has to be approached with humility knowing that Ishvara is its karma-phala-daata and He will open the way only when the jiva is ready for it. The attitude is flawed if it is "If i do  
this meditation, i will get this result." (In my thinking) This is more a prayer where we are showing our earnestness to align with scriptural teachings and seeking the Lord to show us the way. The Goal is  
Knowledge of Self, not some mind games to feel good in our stupidity.  
  
Typically, this type of contemplation/meditation (if I understand correctly) comes in at more advanced stages where the sadhaka has already attained high levels of chitta-shuddhi (purification/removal of  
mental obstructions), chitta-ekagratha (one-pointedness), has acquired basic qualifications (like viveka, vairagya, ..., shraddha, mumukshutvam (desire for liberation) etc.). Guru is said to be essential  
for guidance.  
  
For beginner sadhakas, it can reduce to temporary feel-good or avoid-feeling-bad mind-fitting imposed on bad foundations. We may soon find ourselves lost of purpose and direction beyond that.  
  
Even still, I think since we are involved in scriptural thinking, it is good to pursue such contemplative habits as a way of strengthening our shraddha and efforts in the religious path. Bhakti-approaches  
are more well known, but the jnana-approaches can also be appealing and motivating to the intellect - though they can more easily leave us stranded in no-man's land and not knowing it.

Quotes by Shankara in Aparokshaanubhuti (as quoted by Swami Budhananda in Meditation and its Preparation (RKM publication))  
  
Note how Shankara turns everything round to singular objective of realization of Brahman.  
  
1. The Atman that is absolute Existence and Knowledge cannot be realized without constant practice. So one seeking after knowledge should long meditate upon Brahman for the attainment of the desired goal.  
  
2. The control of the senses by means of the knowledge 'all is Brahman' is called yama and should be practiced again and again.  
  
3. The incessant flow of only one kind of thought relating to the unity of the individual self with Brahman such as 'This Atman is Brahman' and 'I am Brahman' to the exclusion of all other thoughts is called  
niyama, which verily is the supreme bliss and is regularly practiced by the wise.  
  
4. That serene state of the constitution in which Brahman is incessantly contemplated with unmixed bliss is known as asana and not others which destroy bliss.  
  
5. The siddha-asana is the Immutable which is the beginning of all beings and the reality behind the universe, that in which the perfected ever repose.  
  
6. Absorption in the all-pervading Brahman is known as the equipoise of the limbs. Mere straightening of the body like that of a dried up tree is no equipoise.  
  
7. Converting one's ordinary vision into one of knowledge, one should realize the whole world to be Brahman itself. This is the profoundest vision and not that which is directed to the nose.  
  
8. Or, the vision should be solely directed to that wherein ceases the distinction of seer, sight and the object of seeing. It need not be directed to the tip of the nose.  
  
9. The restraint of all modifications of the mind by regarding all mental states like the chitta as Brahman alone, is called pranayama. The negation of the universe in the outgoing breath (rechaka). The thought 'I am Brahman' is called the incoming breath (puraka). And the steadiness of that thought thereafter is the restrained breath (kumbhaka). This is the pranayama for the wise. While pressing of the nose is only for the ignorant.  
  
10. The merging of the consciousness in Brahman by realizing the Self in all objects is known as pratyahara and should be practiced by all seekers of liberation.  
  
11. Concentration in its highest sense is holding of consciousness by realizing Brahman wheresoever the mind goes.  
  
12. The condition, wherein there is only the uncontradictable thought 'I am Brahman' Itself, and there is no external hold, is denoted by the term dhyaana or meditation, which is productive of highest bliss.  
  
  
Swami Brahmananda (RKM) in The Eternal Companion

Verily, if a man can realize Him and have taste of divine bliss he will lose all taste for everything else.
