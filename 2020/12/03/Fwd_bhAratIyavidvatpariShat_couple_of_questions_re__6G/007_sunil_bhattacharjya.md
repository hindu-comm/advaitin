Dear Raghavji and Subbuji,  

  

Yes, I was trying to recall that. It is from the Niralamba Upanishad, which is an authentic upanishad. Similarly, there is also confusion about the source of "Drg Drsya Viveka" of Swami Bharati Tirtha. It appears that it was largely based on the Saraswati Upanishad.  

  

It seems that the scholars in the olden days, were not required to quote the sources, and that had very bad consequences / repercussions also. Shri Gaudapadacharya did not mention that in his Mandukya Karika, he quoted / used the "Ajatavada verse" from the "Original Bhagavad Gita" and that gave an opportunity to Bhaskara Bhatta (who later on changed his name to Nimbarka) in the 8th century CE, to drop 45 verses of Lord Krishna from the Bhagavad Gita. Very sadly, all the great scholars, including the great advaitins of all the Shankara Mathas, remained ignorant about the Original Bhagavad Gita of 745 verses. Worse still,they thought that Adi Shankara wrote the bhashya on the Bhagavad Gita of 700 verses, thereby indirectly implying that Adi Shankara mutilated / damaged the Bhagavad Gita.  

  

I salute Shri Keshava Kashmiri Bhatta, of the Nimbarka school of Bhedabheda doctrine, who was the spiritual guru of Chaitanya
Mahaprabhu, was the first scholar, who could gather up the moral courage, to admit about five hundred years ago, that the Original Bhagavad Gita had 745 verses. Of course, those who read the Original Mahabharata (and not the mutilated critical edition of the Mahabharata), knew all along that the sage Vedavysa stated in his Mahabharata that the Original Bhagavad Gita had 745 verses, which included 520 verses spoken by Lord Krishna. My heart goes to Sant Jnaneshwar, who as a teenager in the 13th century CE (i.e., about five hundred years after Bhaskara Bhatta, who damaged the Bhagavad Gita, by dropping 45 verses from that), wrote his Bhashya on the Bhagavad Gita of 700 verses. He passed away at the age of 21 years, and obviously had no time to read the Mahabharata, to realize that Vedavyasa stated that the original Bhagavad gita had 745 verses, and also that the manuscript of the Original Bhagavad Gita, was very much available in Varanasi, if not in Pune (Poona).

  

Regards,

Sunil KB
