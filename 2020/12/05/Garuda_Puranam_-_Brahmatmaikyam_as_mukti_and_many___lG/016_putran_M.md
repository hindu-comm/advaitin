Namaskaram Suresh-ji,

  

What is the point in making this statement?: "untilthat pAramArthika state \[knowledge\] is attained,it helps sAdhakas to look upon the entire universe as the manifestation of that supreme Ishwara/Brahman." We agree that Ishvara is vyavaharika-satya, where the jiva-sadhaka can and in general should look upon All as His manifestation and Him as the antaryami of All.

  

You are saying things advaitins also say as if it is something new to us, or you are disingenuously trying to act Hindu-friendly toward advaitins.

  

Now let us assume you are sincere in the above statement about paramarthika vs vyavaharika. Then (unless I am simplifying too much) advaitins do not object to your other statements before this, regarding body and atma, Supreme Self as antaryami, etc. - as vyavaharika satya or of Saguna Brahman. I don't see the problem then.

  

Come to your later sentence: "Whatever you see and touch is Brahman alone".This has to be understood carefully. "you see" and "you touch" belong to vyavaharika plane; so "you touch the tree" is correct in that plane.

  

1. "The king touches the tree." This makes perfect sense.

  

2. "The king really touches God." This would make sense provided God is understood in a manner corresponding to king and his touching. Whatever conception of God/Ishvara in dvaita schools that allows them to say things like "Whatever you touch is Brahman", that conception seeks to posit reality to king and his touching, worshipping etc. That automatically projects Brahman to Godwithin vyavaharika plane.

  

3. "The king in the dream touches the Dreamer." This is non-sense. There are two levels of reality, that of the dream wherein king and his touching are real and that of the dreamer wherein king and his touching are unreal. When we try to understand Paramarthika or advaita jnana from within the vyavaharika plane, through the ordinary mind and intellect, this is pretty much the limit for our conception and expression. Brahman is still grasped as Ishvara but in a higher sense: the "king", "touching", "tree" are the seeing/projecting/manifestation/appearance of Ishvara (Brahman+ intrinsic maya-shakti). Brahman (as Dreamer) is the real; the dream of "king", "dharma", "worshipping", "God" etc. is mithya.

  

Here also, Brahman is realized as the ALL and All is Brahman. But if we unravel this statement in terms of 'All', we land in 3.

  

4. Paramarthika (beyond words and expression): the final duality of Dreamer and dream is also resolved in advaita-jnana, as nirguna Brahman.

  

You want advaitins to dismiss 3 and 4 and instead agree with dvaitinsthat 1 and 2 suffice, so that 'Hindu unity' can be achieved. You are not uniting Hindus by seeking a singular view to reconcile established siddhantas that have for millennium+ defined definitely their differences. It does appear a fanatical agenda that is rather unnecessary and dangerous. I gave you my thoughts earlier on the basis of unity in Hinduism.
