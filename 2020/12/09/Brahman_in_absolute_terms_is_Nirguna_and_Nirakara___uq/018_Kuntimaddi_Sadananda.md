I must say that some of your statements are not really correct.

Both Vishishtaadvaita and Dvaita do subscribe Moksha that is beyond the cycle of births.

Samsaara is anaadi for all since ignorance is anaadi. In Vishishtaadvata, it is due to the ignorance of the nature of the Jeeva and Iswara and of the Jeeva's dependence on Iswara as He is the controller of the whole universe including the phenomenal forces. Hence the recognition and complete surrender – Sharanaagati – is the only means for Moksha that involves Videha Mukti. Shree Ramanuja does criticize the avidya as presented in Advaita that includes anyonya aashraya dosha, in his mahaapurva paksha of Sribhasya on Brahmasutra. But he has the same problem as he does subscribe to karma as the cause for the beginningless birth cycle. His Moksha involves – going to Vaikunta where Jeeva will be eternally serving the Iswara along with others with sattvic bodies. Hence duality or plurality still remains in Mokha, violating many scriptural stamens starting with dviteeeyaadyou bhayam bhavati or udaramantaram kurute aththasya bhayam bhaviti.

In Moksha, in Vishishaatdvaita, by proclamation or axiomatic statement, the Jeevaas are eternally and infinitely happy being in the service of the Lord – end of the arguments. 

About the Deep sleep states, one can say that the Lord has provided to the experience of the Adriatic state. A Jeeva experiences three things: 1. I exist (as one says that I slept very well), 2. I was ignorant of all the subject-object duality, expressed as I did not know anything when I was in sleep, including the space-time continuum indicating that I am beyond space and time too. Space -time comes with the objective mind. 3. I was happy. People take even sleeping pills to go to sleep. One can remain without food for a few days but not without sleep since the objective mind needs rest.

The mind is still there in a subtle form along with chidaabhaasa or reflection of consciousness in the deep sleep state. Brahman being infinite, remains as antaryamin, enlivening the Jeeva(s) all the time.

Krishna declares as 'aham vaiswaanaro bhutva praaninaam deham aashrayaH'. Life pulsates via the mind and chidaabhaasa. Hence a Janani sleeps as a Janani, and ajnaani sleeps as ajnaani – both will not have subject-object duality in the deep-sleep state.

Advaita provides a correct understanding of the state of experience of the Deep-Sleep, and the Nature also provides that experience indicating one can be really happy in the Adriatic state as there is no duality – this is also indicated by the Mandukya in the description as 'na kanachana kaamam kaayata and in matra 7 in the description of real I -as adviatam chaturtam manyante, sa aatmaa, sa vijneyaH – indicating that one has to inquire into the real nature of oneself.

Exhaustive analysis of the deep-sleep state can be found in the Sri Vidyaranyas Pachadasi, 11^(th) Ch.
