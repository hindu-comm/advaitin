Namaste Dave-ji

  

\<

May I suggest that these activities happen automatically without a doer by sheer momentum of karmic propensity for mind borne beings.The doer 'I' (ahamkar) jumps in thereafter and claims that I was thinking or talking etc. but during the activity itself it was not present.

\>

  

Who is saying the above? It cannot be just the body or even "ahankAra" as they are inert. When you write a book or debate with a dualist you are doing it consciously. When you are controlling your body/mind/speech as part of your sAdhana you are doing it consciously. So, the jiva who is using real and is definitely responsible for his actions. Yes, ahankAra also plays part here but when all actions are done for the good of the world and attributed to the VaishwAnara Atman, it gets sublated.

  

\<

Yes the body is dependent on countless factors so the idea that there is a separate independent substantial body without these non-body elements is a fiction. Ultimately the body is dependent on mind and Consciousness and as such the body is really and idea, justask the quantum physicist.  

\>

  

Body is that which is dependent, controlled and used by the Self to achieve whatever it wants to achieve. It is absolutely dependent on the Self, an integral part of the Self and derives its existence from the Self only.Even if the body is just an idea it has its origin/abode/destination in the Self. Even a quantum physicist has to breath, eat food and drink water and use his sense organs and mind, in order to make that statement. What is missing in your analysis is the universality of the VaishwAnara Atman that is actively working in every body/entity in the universe. From that point every activity is a yagya or sacrifice and the self is shining because of that principle. Every perception or action is a yagya in the fire of the Self. Every individual knowingly or unknowingly is engaged in this activity. There is a need to recognize this principle in every being/entity. Only then respect for each other and true Brahmatva or equanimity can arise.

  

There is an organic relationship that obtains between the universe and the essential Self and from this relationship the reality of dharma as duty arises. This aspect of Dharma as duty operating as the law of the universe is missing in the modern day non-traditional advaitins who want to look upon the body/universe as mere illusion. If Dharma is real then the reality of body/universe must be accepted. Truth and Dharma for one single whole.

  

Here is an extract from Swami Krishnananda's book "The teachings of the Bhagavad Gita":  

  

"Our organic relationship with things is the reason behind the duty that we owe to things, and this also is the reason why we need not expect any fruit from the duty that we perform in respect of anyone or anything. To expect a fruit is a mistake.*Mā phaleṣu kadācana, mā karmaphalahetur bhūr mā te saṅgo’stvakarmaṇi*(Gita 2.47): You have a duty, you have an obligation to do, but you have no right to expect a particular consequence or result or fruit to follow from what you do. This is a very difficult, pithy enunciation in the Bhagavadgita – that we have duties but we cannot expect any fruits from the duties that we perform.

This may look very odd and unpleasant to the selfish individual, but as I have tried to mention earlier, the law of the universe is not necessarily a pleasant dish that is served to the ego of man; it is a principle that operates, and it is neither pleasant nor unpleasant. Its reactions, under given conditions of personality, appear to be pleasant or otherwise. The duty that we owe to anyone or anything is the homage that we pay to the vastness of the atmosphere in which we are placed, and the grandeur of the relationship that is there between us and the whole of creation. There is a majesty ruling the whole cosmos; and it is this superabundance of magnificence, which is the law of the universe, that inexorably operates and impartially dispenses justice without any favour or disfavour in regard to any person or thing."

You can read more here:[https://www.swami-krishnananda.org/bhagavad/bhagavad_04.html](https://www.swami-krishnananda.org/bhagavad/bhagavad_04.html)
