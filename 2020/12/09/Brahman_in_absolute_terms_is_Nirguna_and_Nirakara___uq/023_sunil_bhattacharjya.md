Dear Sadanandaji,

  

The Dvaita and the Vishashtadvaita schools don't believe in giving up the ego of individuality and that is why their views are non-Vedantic. I wonder, as to how can we the followers of the Advaitavada, taught by the Great Adi Shankaracharya, equate their beliefs with that of the Advaita views, which is based on the Vedantic texts, just for accommodating Sureshji's appeal to do so. If the Dvaitns and Vishishtadvains think that their views lead to going beyond the cycle of Birth and Death, let them think so. It is their sweet will, and we have to leave them to their own judgement and we have neither to oppose them nor to certify certify them  

  

Initially there was only one Veda. Vedavyasa, towards the end of the Dwapara yuga, took out four portions to classify these into the four individual Vedas (i.e., Rig, Yajur, Sama and Atharva) and the remaining portion of the Original Veda became the Fifth Veda. When the Fifth Veda texts show that the Muktis like Saaroopya don't guarantee escape from the cycle of birth and death, how can we go against theVedic views? I have shown the case of Ravana as an example.  

  

Jai Shri Krishna  

Sunil K. Bhattacharjya
