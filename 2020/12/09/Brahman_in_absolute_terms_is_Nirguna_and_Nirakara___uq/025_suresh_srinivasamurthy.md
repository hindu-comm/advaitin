Dear Sri Sunil-ji,

  

\<The Dvaita and the Vishashtadvaita schools don't believe in giving up the ego of individuality and that is why their views are non-Vedantic. \>

  

All Vaishnava schools identify themselves as a dAsAs (servants) and Vishnu as the swAmy (Owner).There is also the teaching of NeechOpAsana - identifying the self as the lowest of the low. To have ego means to think one is independent and also to have false sense of ownership. But Vaishnavas consider themselves as absolutely dependent on the Vishnu who alone is independent. The jiva/jagat is considered as the body and the antaryAmi Brahman as the Soul-Self. The body always serves the Soul/Self and is ever dependent on the soul/Self which always remains as the owner/supporter/controller/protector of the body. So the swaroopa of the jiva and Brahman are clearly established in this analogy of body-soly which is explicitly taught in the antaryAmi brAhmaNa of BruhadAraNyaka Up.

  

Now please tell me how can such a upAsana of identifying oneself as a servant lead to egoism?

  

Regards,  
Suresh
