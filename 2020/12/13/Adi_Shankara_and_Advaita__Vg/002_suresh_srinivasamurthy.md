My thousand praNAms to Sri ChittA-ji,

  

I am completely in agreement with his words. The supreme Truth or the Self is singular (simple) and plural (complicated)at the same time. Infinite grains of rice along with the plants can potentially exist in one single grain of rice. Worldly opposites lose their meaning or come together or happily co-exist in Brahman, which is amruta and mruthyu at the same time. Vishnu and Shankara are one and the same.The mantra from Sri Rudram - "Om Namo Bhagavate rudrAya Vishnave mruthyur may pAhi" explicitly teaches that.

  

Brahman is both nirguna and saguna at the same time.Sri Shankara mentions this many times in his B.Sutra bhAshya (1-1-1). Here is one example:

  

तत्पुनर्ब्रह्मप्रसिद्धमप्रसिद्धंवा स्यात् ; यदिप्रसिद्धंन जिज्ञासितव्यम्। अथाप्रसिद्धंनैव शक्यं जिज्ञासितुमिति। उच्यते — अस्ति तावद्ब्रह्म नित्यशुद्धबुद्धमुक्तस्वभावं सर्वज्ञं सर्वशक्तिसमन्वितम्। ब्रह्मशब्दस्य हि व्युत्पाद्यमानस्य नित्यशुद्धत्वादयोऽर्थाः प्रतीयन्ते, बृंहतेर्धातोरर्थानुगमात्। सर्वस्यात्मत्वाच्च ब्रह्मास्तित्वप्रसिद्धिः।सर्वो ह्यात्मास्तित्वं प्रत्येति, न ‘नाहमस्मि’ इति। यदि हि नात्मास्तित्वप्रसिद्धिःस्यात् , सर्वो लोकः ‘नाहमस्मि’ इति प्रतीयात्। आत्मा च ब्रह्म। यदि तर्हि लोके ब्रह्म आत्मत्वेनप्रसिद्धमस्ति,ततो ज्ञातमेवेत्यजिज्ञास्यत्वं पुनरापन्नम् ; न ; तद्विशेषं प्रति विप्रतिपत्तेः। देहमात्रं चैतन्यविशिष्टमात्मेति प्राकृता जना लोकायतिकाश्च प्रतिपन्नाः। इन्द्रियाण्येव चेतनान्यात्मेत्यपरे। मन इत्यन्ये। विज्ञानमात्रं क्षणिकमित्येके। शून्यमित्यपरे। अस्ति देहादिव्यतिरिक्तः संसारी कर्ता भोक्तेत्यपरे। भोक्तैव केवलं न कर्तेत्येके। अस्ति तद्व्यतिरिक्त ईश्वरः सर्वज्ञः सर्वशक्तिरिति केचित्। आत्मा स भोक्तुरित्यपरे। एवं बहवो विप्रतिपन्ना युक्तिवाक्यतदाभाससमाश्रयाः सन्तः। तत्राविचार्य यत्किञ्चित्प्रतिपद्यमानो निःश्रेयसात्प्रतिहन्येत, अनर्थं चेयात्। तस्माद्ब्रह्मजिज्ञासोपन्यासमुखेन वेदान्तवाक्यमीमांसा तदविरोधितर्कोपकरणा निःश्रेयसप्रयोजना प्रस्तूयते ॥ १ ॥  

  

The wordसर्वस्यात्मत्वाच्च ब्रह्मास्तित्वप्रसिद्धिःis very significant and this is what I have been trying to highlight in all my posts. He is the Self of all which includes both jiva and jada. The non-externality of jagat is what is taught by Shankara and not it's total non-existence.He also explicitly teaches that Nirguna Brahman can take a divine form to bless the devotee according, which would always be the devata form pertaining to the veda mantra that the sAdhaka has been meditating.

  

The three realities of V.Advaita (Atma-anAtma-paramAtma) and the multiple realities with pancha bheda as in Dvaita can completely co-exist in the unknowable and unthinkable Advaitic nirguNa Brahman. From the point of the view of the universal Self everything is possible. The individual i/eye should align with the Universal I/EYE in order to correctly and experience the supreme truth that includes and transcends all.

  

What about the various philosophical refutations of each other's viewpoints by AchAryas of the three major vedAntic schools? They are refutations of "possible mis-understandings" that can arise due to faulty perceptions of all those viewpoints. All the many vedAntic views that are apparantly different are like different snAna ghattA's for the same ganga of Brahman.

  

My sincere prNAms to Sri ChittA-ji.

  

Namaste

Suresh
