Namaste:

This ghazal, Zindagi se Badi Saza Hi Nahi by Krishn Bihari 'Noor' from, Lucknow is philosophical. This is one of the poems that touches everyone who understands the subtle philosophy buried inside.

[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2ZWE-bVbveg](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2ZWE-bVbveg)

A simple translation is the following:

There's no punishment bigger than life

And I don't even know what my mistake is

I've been broken into so many pieces

That I don't have anything left in my share

If the truth goes up or down then it isn't the truth anymore

Paint me with silver or gold, But the mirror doesn't lie

There's no punishment bigger than life

Deeper Philosophical Meaning of this Poem:

The poet says that when I am in distress my mind makes me to believe that Life is a punishment. He implicitly points out the human weakness due to the oscillatory nature of our mind. When I am happy and content, my mind stays calm and makes me to recognize that life is a rewarding experience. The question, whether life is a punishment or reward is only my subjective assessment from what I believe. This belief is because of my jivatma (soul) getting attached to body, mind and intellect. But in reality soul is limitless and ever happy when it is free from the oscillations of the mind. When the soul gets its freedom from the perceived experiences through the senses that include eyes, ears, tongue and skin! My attachment to body, mind and intellect is responsible for me broken into many pieces and I have nothing left to share. I failed to realize that all I see, hear, touch or taste are transitory and receptacles to continuous changes all the time. Consequently I incorrectly, believe these changes as the Truth though this is not the Truth! When I divert my attention from sensual perceptions, my mind is able to reflect the Truth directly from inner soul. With that realization my mind can’t lie and it will truly reflect my true nature. My mind will become just like the mirror truly reflecting whatever is in its front!

Interestingly many Urudu poems contain some hidden philosophy through simple verses and force us to think deeper to correct our attitude toward life. Some of those poems were as good as the words of the sages of the Upanishads! There are many Urudu ghazals and Hindi bhajans have the power to touch our soul. This beautiful soul touching bhajan was originally from Kabir: Naam Japan Kyon Chhod Diya; Krodh na choda Joot na choda; Satya vachan kyo chod diya tune (Here the poet pleads why don’t we agree to forego Raga and Dwesha instead of praying the Divine and forgetting the importance of virtues such as being Truthful) Link: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UTP4T4PmU3Q](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UTP4T4PmU3Q)
