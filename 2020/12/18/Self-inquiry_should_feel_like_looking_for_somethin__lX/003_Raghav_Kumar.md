Namaste Dave ji

Thank you for your post.

  

Without technical words, one could say -

  

All knowledge takes place in the intellect alone. There is no dental knowledge or nasal knowledge etc. The adjective "intellectual" is superfluous before the word "knowledge" - it needlessly creates confusions in people. Traditional Advaita discountenances such innovations.

  

Knowledge necessarily involves the jIva who strives for and arrives at the knowledge that "I am ever-free, have never been bound etc.". Neither Brahman nor the mind seek to know.

  

you mentioned -  

"From the standpoint of*Jiva*, there can be \*no direct knowledge\* of*Atman*." And

"However Atman can only be intuited or \*indirectly inferred\* by the intellect as the source of perceptual objects."

  

-i would suggest that the articulation could be more aligned with the very tight and well-honed epistemology of vedanta when it comes to Atma-GYAnam.

  

All teachers/Acharyas who have clarity about Advaita Vedanta, would insist on the use of the word aparoxa-GYAnam, that is, "direct" knowledge of Atman and that does indeed take place in the intellect/buddhi - and it takes place \*for the jIva\* alone and not for brahman (it's superfluous) or any other inert entity like mind (knowledge obtains only in a conscious entity) etc. Also there is no inference (direct or otherwise) involved in this aparoxa Atma GYAnam as you suggested. It is by the laxyArtha. (meaning by implication) . The words of the mahavAkya effect a cognitive transformation (the jIva lands at 'i am brahman') and don't merely explain some object or concept.(I know a table, chair, english, science, brahman').

  

Thus, this (fact that Atma GYAnam takes place in the intellect) does not imply that there is any kind of objectification or conceptualization of formless Atman. Such objectification does indeed happen in the case of pratyaxa or 'direct' perception of sense objects (indriya pratyaxa) and ideas (sAxI pratyaxa).

  

The GYAna process w.r.t brahmAtma is very uniquely and clearly explained in vedAnta paribhAShA and other texts. Self-knowledge happens in the buddhi or intellect alone.

  

The modern innovation that "intellectual knowledge" or "Self-understanding" is something indirect or inadequate w.r.t. Atma-GYAnam, is an innocent mistake and is incompatible with vedAnta epistemology.

  

True, there are obstacles to GYAnam and there is, at a certain stage in a person's evolution, mere information (not knowledge) of the kind 'i am told that the Self is limitless and formless' etc. And there may be shraddhA.

  

And at a still later stage, a person may feel that he understands and yet sorrow persists. Such viparyaya-s are dealt with in definite ways \*without undermining\* the assertion of "Self-knowledge takes place in the mature intellect".

  

A short mail may not do justice to the topic. But to put it briefly,
vRtti vyApti (intellect assuming the form of the object) happens but phala vyApti (the notion that "I know this object") is absent or superfluous, in the case of Self-knowledge. Sri Sada ji has I think dealt with this topic - a favorite of his, if I recollect.

  

Om

Raghav
