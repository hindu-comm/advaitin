Namaste Raghavji,

  

An "intellectual understanding." like any set of words, is used in different ways by different people andin different contexts.

  

Many people studying Vedanta have the wrong impression that just by reading the ideas of the texts and/or hearing them, and then trying to understand their logic at a distance, merely as concepts, and then repeating them to themselves over and over again, trying to "practice" them, whatever that means -- that that is what sravana, manana, and nidhidhyasanamean.That is what I meant by merely intellectual understanding.

  

People think *that*kind of understanding is self-inquiry.

  

It is not. There is an act of *introspection* that the real understanding of the words require. It requires a diving into the experiential sense of I.  

  

Whether the direct knowing of the Self happens "in the (Vedantic) intellect" is a different question... If knowing can be said to "happen" at all (since "happenings" are themselves a category of maya and thus if ignorance), it happens at the *boundary*of the intellect. Whether one wants to say that when a mirror is reflecting nothing but white light it is still reflecting at all is a matter of definition.
