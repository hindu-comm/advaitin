Namaste Akhilesh ji

  

You wrote -

  

"There is an act of*introspection*that the real understanding of the words require. It requires a diving into the experiential sense of I."  

  

The "act of introspection", as you put it is well within mAyA or the realm of ignorance . That does not invalidate it's role in removing the veil of avidyA. Similarly for the process of knowing (pramANa-vyApAra) - it is in time but sublates time.

  

Again you wrote -

"Whether the direct knowing of the Self happens "in the (Vedantic) intellect" is a different question... If knowing can be said to "happen" at all (since "happenings" are themselves a category of maya and thus if ignorance), it happens at the*boundary*of the intellect. "

  

Knowing is most certainly a happening or even a process within time with a definite culmination. But again, that in no way militates against its efficacy in ending the illusion of time. A dream tiger is instrumental in someone waking up. One cannot say, "any process or entity within time, being of the realm of ignorance, cannot remove the ignorance of taking oneself to be an entity within time". That's not logically tenable.

  

You could call it "an act of introspection", "GYAna vRtti", "self-enquiry" etc etc - all are happenings in time which can dissolve the illusion of smallness.

  

It's a popular modern vedAntic misconception that any effort or process in time has no relevance to arriving at paramArtha satyam.

  

Overall, if you were to align your articulation to differentiate between vyavahaarika satyam and pAramArthika satyam, it would be free of a performative contradiction.

  

Om

Raghav
