Here is my understanding - one may call it 'Intellectual'.

  

Any understanding including Self-knowledge is only at the intellectual level.

  

Paroxa jnaanam or simply jnaanam word is used to designate understanding at objective level - like understanding Chemistry, Physics, etc.

  

The word Apaoxa jnaanam or Vijnaanam is used to recognize the subject of this knowledge is the 'Subject' itself which cannot be objectified. Hence Krishna discusses 'Jnaana Vijnaana yoga' in Ch. 7 to make a point that the subject of the discussion cannot be objectified.

  

However, one has to use the intellect to appreciate even this also. Intellect has to be used to recognize that which makes even the intellect to function. For that only the intellect has to be very subtle to recognize who is that 'I am' in the very notion that 'I am the intellect' statement.

Hence Kena Up. states as the mind of the mind or 'pratibhodha viditam' - it is revealed in every thought. Thinking is at the intellect level only.

  

The problem, for most of us, is that we understand but we understand it not - put in the words of JK. (his statement is actually 'it is not an understanding as understanding as thought, but it is an understanding as understanding as a fact'.)

  

Many of our problems rest in not purifying the mind fully, for the recognition (re-cognition) of the 'who I am' in the equation 'I am = this'; which cannot be avoided and is needed not be avoided as long as one is alive or intellect is there. In essence, it is needed for recognition of maaya as maayaa - ya maa saa maayaa, that which appears to be there but REALLY not there.

  

If one is pure as in Nachiketas, sharavana and manana are enough. But many madhyama adhikaaries like us, we need 'nidhidhyaasana' contemplation on the truth learned via shravana and manana to internalize the knowledge gained. Goudapada provides in his fourth ch. of his kaarika where are the obstacles for this knowledge and how to overcome them.

  

In essence, one has to use the intellect to understand the fact that is beyond the intellect and that which enlivens the intellect. The process involves subtle thinking. Hence the study of scriptures and discussion of the scriptures, contemplation on the truth revealed by the scriptures, meditation are all means for Nidhidhyaasana ONLY.

  

The Kena student states this clearly in his statement ' I understand it - not that I understand IT, but I understand it.

  

Hence Vedantic discussions are also meant for clarification of the nature of the goal.

  

Hari Om!
