Pranams,

  

Again, whether one says it is in the intellect or not ultimately is a semantic question of little ultimate significance. The argument for Self-knowledge being in the intellect is that all discrimination must necessarily happen there. The argument forit not being in the intellect is that the intellect operates by subject-object distinction, and thisknowledge is not of that character.

  

What *does* have significance is that question of nidhidhyasana that you put forth. People often mistake nidhidhyasana with obsessive study and meditation on the logic of ideas -- when in fact it is a *looking inward and away from thought*. That is a very different process.

  

As Ramana Maharshi puts it in Guru Vachaka Kovai,  
  

"After knowing that the purport at the heart of all scripture is that the mind should be subdued in order to gain Liberation, what is the use in continuously studying them? Who am I?"

  

and also

  

"Self, which shines within the five sheaths, should be attended to within the Heart. Instead of doing so, to enquire for It in the scriptures is only scriptural enquiry – how can it be Self-enquiry?"
