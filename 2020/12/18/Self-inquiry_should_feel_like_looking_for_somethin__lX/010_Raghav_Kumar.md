> Thats where you are mistaken. The knowledge of brahman takes place in
> the intellect but \*still there is no subject-object duality\* in that
> special modification of buddhi called akhaNDAkAra vRtti. I have
> already mentioned that this is explained by all the teachers of
> Advaita through the fact that the objectification by the subject is
> absent in the akhaNDAkAra vRtti because phala vyApti is absent. I
> request you to reference vedAnta-psribhAShA for details for this.
>
>   
>
> Ananda Giri says in Katha 2nd chapter towards the end-
>
> विषयकल्पनाशून्यया ब्रह्मास्मीत्यविषयतयैव ब्रह्मभावव्यञ्जिकया
> महावाक्योत्थयाबुद्धिवृत्त्याज्ञातुं शक्यत
>
> (Parsphrase - Brahman is known through a modification (vRtti) of the
> (matured) intellect arising out of the appreciation of the mahAvAkya;
> it is devoid of the subject-object duality and arises even without
> objectifying Brahman)
>
>   
>
> As Sri Shankara in effect says, in taittiriya bhAShya, the knower of
> brahman "attains everything" by being buddhi-vrittyArUDha (by a
> special modification of the intellect).
>
>   
>
> The issue is much more than semantics. Because, if the mature buddhi
> on hearing the mahAvAkya gives rise to brahman knowledge that "I am
> brahman and was never anything else", and it is found that in some
> case, the knowledge does not arise upon teaching the mahAvAkya, then
> we can infer presence of obstacles. The act of introspection you spoke
> of is to take care of one type of obstacle viz., vixepa or a
> distracted condition of mind. It will lead to a quiet and peaceful
> mind but does not by itself have the capacity to confer self-knowledge
> without the background of the holistic teaching of the sadguru. Any
> technique viewed as a stand-alone practice will at best lead to
> chitta-naiscalyam and at worst a kind of manolaya. But if there is
> sufficient holistic upadesha by the Guru, then it's fine.
>
>   
>
> Bhagavan has to be understood holistically rather than pigeonholed in
> to any specific technique. He emphasised different aspects of
> Self-enquiry to different aspirants. Sri Bhagavan always maintained
> that it takes a lot of effort to get to non-effort. As He told
> Kunjuswami, \*the three stages\* of sadhana – sravana, (hearing the
> Teaching), manana (continuously reflecting on it until it is fully
> digested) and nididhyasana (abiding in what one has digested) \*must
> be kept up\* until full Realization.
>
>   
>
> Bhagavan discouraged pedantry and polemics, but the same \*continued
> study of scripture\* is nidhidhyAsanam for one who has the appropriate
> background of prior shravanam, mananam and nidhidhyAsanam from a Guru.
> In fact, Bhagavan went further and said that the attitude of
> Self-enquiry can be maintained even in regular "worldly" activities
> (what to speak of continued Self-enquiry through a receptive study of
> the sacred words of vedAntic sages).
>
>   
>
> In another context, Bhagavan also commends the \*recitation\* of ribhu
> gita as nidhidhyAsanam (recitation and reading do involve thought.)
> Quite clearly, Bhagavan never meant to say that the mere presence of
> thought precludes nidhidhyAsanam.
