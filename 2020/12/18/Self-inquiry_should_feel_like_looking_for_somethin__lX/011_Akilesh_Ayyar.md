Namaste Raghavji,

  

*The knowledge of brahman takes place in the intellect but \*still there is no subject-object duality\* in that special modification of buddhi called akhaNDAkAra vRtti.*

  

As I said, this is a semantic distinction. That "special modification of buddhi" takes away the character of the buddhi that defines it as buddhi, namely, a subject-object distinction. It's something like saying "motionless motion," "spotless spot," "lightless sun," or some other such oxymoron. The character of buddhi is *discrimination*. Discrimination is separation, but Brahman has no parts.

  

I understand what you're saying about some Vedantic teachers, but from the broader viewpoint what those teachers are saying is *merely one view of something inherently not capturable in language*. Let's not confuse mere views for absolute truth.

  

*Because, if the mature buddhi on hearing the mahAvAkya gives rise to brahman knowledge that "I am brahman and was never anything else", and it is found that in some case, the knowledge does not arise upon teaching the mahAvAkya, then we can infer presence of obstacles. The act of introspection you spoke of is to take care of one type of obstacle viz., vixepa or a distracted condition of mind.*

*  *

No, not quite. A distracted condition of mind is an obstacle, all right... but even to understand the words of the scripture, one must introspect. Otherwise there is in fact no understanding. Quietude of mind is extremely helpful, but still cannot substitute for looking within. Introspection does in fact help quiet the mind, and quieting the mind helps introspection, but introspection is nevertheless required *even to understand the words.*

  

And frankly it is enough even without the words.

  

*It will lead to a quiet and peaceful mind but does not by itself have the capacity to confer self-knowledge without the background of the holistic teaching of the sadguru. Any technique viewed as a stand-alone practice will at best lead to chitta-naiscalyam and at worst a kind of manolaya. But if there is sufficient holistic upadesha by the Guru, then it's fine.*  

  

No. Properly understood, self-inquiry is not merely a kind of repetitive concentration meditation like simply chanting a mantra. It is itself an act ofdiscrimination... that very act of discrimination whose mere *record*is inthe Vedas, is in living form when the mind turns inward to attempt to gaze at the "I."

  

*Bhagavan discouraged pedantry and polemics, but the same \*continued study of scripture\* is nidhidhyAsanam for one who has the appropriate background of prior shravanam, mananam and nidhidhyAsanam from a Guru.*

  

Yes, but it is only to provide motivation tolook within. It is looking within, and not logical constructions with philosophical ideas, that counts. As he said, "These are all only words. It is enough that a person becomes antarmukhi (inward-bent). The sastras are not needed for an inward turned mind. They are meant for the rest."
