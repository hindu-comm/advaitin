This is a very interesting picture. Makaraayana means Sun's sojourn in Makara. Instead of worrying about the 'Raashi or the Sign' the direct meaning would be the **month of solar Makara** and the associated entry PuNyakAla. If this is what the Kanchimatham also means for the special Pooja, then it is remarkable and extremely satisfying. > > > > Speaking of the infinitesimal, our ancient Indian mathematicians
> > > > and astronomers played with it confidently long before Newton or

> > > > other Indian astronomers too used the infinitesimal happily.
> > > >
> > > > When x is zero, sin x is zero. So, is sin x/x illegal because
> > > > the numerator and denominator are both zero? That is where the
> > > > beauty of infinitesimal comes in. As x “tends to zero”, sin x/x
> > > > will “tend to” 1. This is but one small instance of the happy
> > > > frolicking of the infinitesimal in mathematics.
> > > >
> > > > After this infinitesimal pause, called “solstice” (sun standing
> > > > still) our days in the northern hemisphere will happily grow
> > > > longer. Will continue to grow long for six months.

> > > > figure itself. The formula can be derived from basic spherical
> > > > trigonometry. This formula is only approximate as it does not
