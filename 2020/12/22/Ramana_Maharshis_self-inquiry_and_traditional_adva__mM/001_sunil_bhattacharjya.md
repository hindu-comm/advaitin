Namaste Shri Akilesh Ayyarji,  

  

The Mahatmas like Shri Ramana Maharshi and Shri Ramakrishna tried to speak in a way, so that even the commoner would understand the Advaitic truths. In my own case I was greatly benefitted by reading how Lord Buddha taught the Advayavada to the Brahmins of his time.  

  

Lord Buddha basically told the inquisitive brahmins that once we recognize our mental impurities, and we try to get rid of these impurities, eventually the light will dawn on us that we, with the purified mind, will have nothing to do with the transient worldly things, thus we will be like blind to to these transient/Anitya/Aniccha things and these will appear as shunya (not-existing) to us. Unfortunately most of the qualified Hindu scholars started calling Buddha to be a Nihilist.  

  

A scholar, named Bhaskara Bhatta, in the 8th century CE, had even removed 45 verses from the Bhagavad Gita, including the the Ajatavada verse in the Original Bhagavad Gita, which said the same thing some 1,300 years before Lord Buddha, that "***what was not there in the beginning and would not be there at the end, are as if not existing in the Middle***". Thankfully Jagadvijayi Shri Keshava Kashmiri, of the same school, admitted in the preface to his Bhashya on the Bhagavad Gita, that the Original Bhagavad Gita did have 745 verses.  

  

Regards,

Sunil K. Bhattacharjya
