*"Where did Adi Shankara say the Nididhyasana to be atma-vichara?"*

*  *

That's the same question I'm asking....maybe one of the scholars here can answer?

  

For 2021 it will be more challenging to particpate and I'll be a bit more reclusive. The New Year message below encapsulates the quintessentials of Advaita and all I can ever say:  

  

**What is the aim of all sentient beings? It is the attainment of infinite happiness, a life free from confusion, ignorance and suffering. Now, we should discriminate and analyze if there is anything in this apparent world which can give us lasting happiness. From the ant up to Brahma the Creator, all are held captive within the field of change and relativity.  **

**  **

**Infinite happiness can only come from that which is immortal, non-changing, eternal. This which is the goal of everyone, this infinity is actually your own *Self*. And in order to realize this *Self* which is the basis of all existence, we don't have to seek, we don't have to search, we don't have to be a doer making efforts. It's already here ever-present but simply overlooked. Wherever you are, in whatever dimension of time and space, this *Self* is already here. Only, one has to rest our attention, our awareness deeply within our own Self and that is it! Having forgotten the source of delight, we are seeking for happiness outside our Self in the relative world. That *Self* is *being, aware* and *blissful*.  **

**  **

**As long as we don't enter into *That* which is infinite happiness, free from psychological suffering, we will remain in ignorance perpetually seeking to be free from suffering. There is no lasting happiness of significance in the relative world; the child is gone, the youth is gone, and the old man is still seeking to be fulfilled in the world. When one is established in his *Self*, then automatically there is freedom from suffering and bliss will be there as our nature.**  

  

**Now understand this clearly, **You don't have to seek for it.** That which is omnipresent doesn't have to be sought. It's already there, simply *be*; cease all activity and rest your awareness in it's own nature (swarupa) and bliss (ananda) is already there. Unless one gets established into this omnipresent bliss, contentment will never be realized in the circuit of Samsara. If it were to come, it would have already been revealed by now through so many avenues in this enchanting world. But clearly it has not and one keeps wandering, circling continuously in the physical and spiritual realms. Therefore, that which is our *Self* is your own *Being.*You don't have to look for it outside your Self. And, it is irrespective of any religious faiths or beliefs: Christians, Jews, Muslims, Buddhist or Hindus. This *Being* is Atma-Jnana, Self-Knowledge.  **

**  **

**All these brief moments of happiness which we experience in the world are sporadic manifestations of the One supreme *Being* which is your own *Self*. If one is aware of the *Self* fine, otherwise simply pause all doing, cease all becoming, rest and *be* in your natural state. Relax in your awareness so deeply and intensely that you are consumed b*y That* which is omnipresent and omniscient, expressing it's magnificence and wisdom in your daily life. Having known that *Self* one will be eternally contented while remaining in the world. It's not a matter of detaching yourselves from humanity or the world. Only by knowing and being It, is the plethora of manifestations in this world recognised as an expression of *That*.** ~Tat Wale Baba, Jnani of the Himalayas  

  

Shanti Om
