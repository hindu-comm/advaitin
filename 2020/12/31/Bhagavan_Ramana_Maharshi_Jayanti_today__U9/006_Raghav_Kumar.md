Namaste Raman ji

The word tapas in the context of the taittirIyopaniShad has been explained by Sri Shankara as

  
"तप इति ज्ञानमुच्यते,[‘यस्य ज्ञानमयं तपः’ (मु. उ. १। १। ८)](https://advaitasharada.sringeri.net/display/bhashya/Mundaka?page=1&id=MD_C01_S01_V08&hl=%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%20%E0%A4%9C%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%9E%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%A8%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%82%20%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%AA%E0%A4%83)"

  

(The word tapas refers to knowledge itself, i.e., it is the repeated effort to understand, a modification of knowledge itself.)

  

  

In the context of bhrigu and varuNa who is the teacher, the fact that there was "study" or "teaching" which was in stages, is clearly spelt out. The student starts with the request to be \*taught\* Brahman "adhiHi bhagavo brahmeti" and then there is successive clarification of doubts. Such an intelligent communication between teacher and student is a sine qua non for GYAnam.

  

For example, the Guru gives in a brief way the entities to be successively meditated upon as the manifestation of Brahman. "अन्नं प्राणं चक्षुः श्रोत्रम् मनो वाचम् इति । अन्नं शरीरं तदभ्यन्तरं च प्राणम् अत्तारम् अनन्तरमुपलब्धिसाधनानि चक्षुः श्रोत्रं मनो वाचम् इत्येतानि ब्रह्मोपलब्धौ द्वाराण्युक्तवान् "(Annam, prANa, sense organs, mind and speech....these are successively more and more subtle and are the portals whereby Brahman is available for contemplation.) Quite clearly, the Guru Varuna taught many things and we only get a concise summary of what transpired between them, in order to exalt the need to approach a teacher and study under him for clarity.

  

Any practices of say closing the eyes and being a witness, stopping
thoughts by an act of will or speculating on his Self until he "stumbles" upon the truth that Atman is Brahman, is insufficient and not in accordance with Advaita Vedanta. The \*cognitive input\* in the form of the mahAvAkya (or it's equivalent) must be \*already present\* for any 'practice' of meditation to culminate in self-knowledge. In such cases, the practice is valid and is called nidhidhyAsanam.

  

Please note that any statement which propounds the non-dual identity between the individual and the Whole, is regarded as equivalent to a mahAvAkya. For example, the statements of Sri Ramana Maharshi such as,
"Atma-samstithiH svAtmadarshanam, Isha darshanam svAtmarUpataH" is also in the mahAvAkya category when unfolded by a traditional teacher.

  

Any independent cogitation or even quietness of mind is a good start but not sufficient for avidya-nivRtti.(the removal of avidyA).

  

  

Om

Raghav
