I have no interest in this forum. I joined here temporarily because I saw a surprising message directed at the character of Yudhishthira and I wanted to set the record straight.

  

One Sri Chittaranjan Naik writes -

In the same Mahabharata in which the sublime message of the Bhagavad Gita is contained, there is also the episode wherein Yudhishtira asks his brothers to go and plunder the neighboring countries to obtain wealth for performing the Rajasuya Yajna. Balance?  

  

It is surprising that no member has actually asked Sri Naikji to show the relevant portions of the Mahabharata, either in Sanskrit or in English. I think the gentleman is referring to the following episode below from sabha parva (tralslated by Van Buitenen of BORI edition) -  

  

Chapter 23: //Vaiśampáyana said: The Pärtha (Arjuna) who had got the superb bow, the inexhaustible quivers, the chariot, the banner, and the hall said to Yudhisthira, “I have got bow, arms, arrows, allies, land, fame, and strength, whatever men desire and find hard to obtain. \*\*Now I think it is our task to swell our coffers–I shall levy tribute on all kings, great king!\*\* On a day, at an hour and under a star that are propitious, I shall set out to conquer the north, which is swayed by the God of Riches!" Yudhisthira the King Dharma listened to Dhanamjaya's words and replied in a voice that was resonant with love and gravity, “Depart, bull of the Bharatas, with the blessings of worthy priests, to the grief of our enemies and the joy of our friends! Certain shall be your victory, Pärtha, win the joys you desire!" Thus the Pärtha set out with a large army.....//  

  

This is far from a loot and plunder. This is a case of defeating other kings and levying a tribute on them. There is no attack or plundering from any civilians.

  

Did King Yudhisthira plunder for the sake of Rajasuya yagna? How did he get wealth for the sacrifice? What kind of a king was he? Sabha parva answers it in chapter 30

  

  

//Vaisampāyana said: Because of the King Dharma's protection, his adherence to the truth, and his subjugation of his foes, all the subjects were bent upon their own tasks; because of the correct collection of revenues and his law-abiding government, the monsoon rained abundantly and the countryside was fattened. All affairs prospered, especially cattle tending, husbandry, and trade: all this was the doing of the king. Neither from robbers or cheaters, nor from the king's favorites among themselves, did one hear a false word about the king. Neither drought nor floods, neither disease nor fires nor pestilence raged at all while Yudhisthira was steadfast in the Law, Kings came to do favors, to pay attendance, or to bring spontaneous offerings, and for no other purposes. \*\*The accumulation of lawful income grew so large that it could not be spent even in hundreds of years. Recognizing the size of his treasury and granary, King Kaunteya, lord of the earth, set his mind on sacrifice. All his friends said to him, severally and together, "My lord, it is time for the sacrifice.\*\* Do what is fitting for it!”//  

  

Hopefully, this sets the record straight.
