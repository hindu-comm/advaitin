Here are some References from the texts of Vedanta to support the fact that the kAraNa sharIra/jIva/ignorance are anAd (beginingless)i and that there is no first creation. Hope these pointers are helpful.

  

1\) **Text: Tatva Bodha 3.3**

  

**कारणशरीरं किम् ?**

**  **

**     अनिर्वाच्यानाद्यविद्यारूपं शरीरद्वयस्य कारणमात्रं**

**  **

**    सत्स्वरूपाज्ञानं निर्विकल्पकरूपं यदस्ति**

**  **

**    तत्कारणशरीरम् ।**

**  **

kAraNasharIraM kim ?  
  
anirvAchyAnAdyavidyArUpaM sharIradvayasya kAraNamAtraM  
  
satsvarUpAj~nAnaM nirvikalpakarUpaM yadasti  
  
tatkAraNasharIram \|

  

**अनिर्वाच्यानाद्यविद्यारूपं** = अनिर्वाच्य + **अनादि** + अविद्यारूपम्

That which is indefinable (as sat or asat), **beginningless**, of the nature of avidyA,

  

शरीरद्वयस्य कारणमात्रं

the cause of the other two bodies (namely sUkshma and sthUla sharIras)

  

सत्स्वरूपाज्ञानं

Ignorant of its own real nature,

  

निर्विकल्पकरूपं

Free from divisions/duality,

  

यदस्ति तत्कारणशरीरम् ।

is the causal body

  

2\) **Vichara Sagara Taranga 2, Topic 93**

Vasudeva Brahmendra Saraswati Svaminah says:

  

**वेदान्तसिद्धान्तस्त्वयम्**

vedAntasiddhAntastvayam

**The view of Vedanta Siddhanta is as follows:**

**  **

**जीव ईशो विशुद्धा चित् तथा जीवेशयोर्भिदा ।**

**अविद्या तच्चितोर्योगः षडस्माकमनादयः ॥**

jIva Isho vishuddhA chit tathA jIveshayorbhidA \|  
avidyA tachchitoryogaH ShaDasmAkamanAdayaH \|\|

  

**"jIva, Ishwara, Pure Consciousness and the Difference between jIva and Ishwara, Ignorance and its relation with consciousness, these six are beginingless according to us****”**

  

This is an oft quotedshloka in the paramparA, which appears in several commentaries,but I am not able to trace the source

  

3\) **Brahma Sutra Shankara Bhashyam 2.1.35**

Translation by Swami Vireshwarananda from[here](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/brahma-sutras/d/doc63662.html)

  

Brahma-Sutra 2.1.35:

  

**न कर्माविभागादिति चेत्, न, अनादित्वात् ॥ ३५ ॥**

  

na karmāvibhāgāditi cet, na, anāditvāt \|\| 35 \|\|

  

na—not; karmāvibhāgāt—for want of distinction in work; iti cet—if it be said; na—no; anāditvāt—because of (the world) being without a beginning.

  

35\. If it be said (that is) not (possible) for want of any distinction in work (before creation), (we say) no, because of (the world) being without a beginning.

  

Since before the first creation the individual soul cannot possibly have had a previous existence, whence comes the difference in the condition of beings in that first creation, unless the Lord has caused it out of His partiality? **This objection is answered by the Sutra, which says that creation is without a beginning and the question of first creation cannot arise.** **It is like a seed and its sprout. So the individual souls have always had a previous existence and done good or bad deeds in accordance with which their lot in a subsequent creation is ordained by the Lord.**

  

  

4)**Brahma-Sutra Shankara Bhashyam 2.1.36**

Translation by Swami Vireshwarananda from[here](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/brahma-sutras/d/doc63662.html)

> उपपद्यते चाप्युपलभ्यते च ॥ ३६ ॥
>
> upapadyate cāpyupalabhyate ca \|\| 36 \|\|

*upapadyate*—Is reasonable;*ca*—and;*api*—and;*upalabhyate*—is seen;*ca*—also.

36. **And (that the world is without a beginning) is reasonable and is also seen (from the scriptures).**

**Reason tells us that creation must be without a beginning. For if the world did not exist in a potential state in the form of[Samskaras](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/samskara#hinduism "show Samskaras definitions")(impressions), then an absolutely non-existing thing would be produced at creation. In that case even liberated souls might be reborn. Moreover people would be enjoying or suffering without having done anything to deserve it—an instance of an effect without a cause, which is absurd. It cannot be attributed to primeval ignorance, which, being one, requires the diversity of individual past work to produce varied results. The scriptures also posit the existence of the world in former cycles in texts like “The Lord devised the sun and moon as before” ([Rig-Veda](https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/rigveda#hinduism "show Rig-Veda definitions")10. 190. 3).**

So partiality and cruelty cannot be imputed to the Lord.
