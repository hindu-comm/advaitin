Dear Sada-ji,



Of course I agree entirely with what you say. In particular: “From the jnaani's understanding point - He has understood that I am not these shareeras; but now I am playing the role as this particular jeeva until BMI vesham or costume is there.In essence, the shareeras end only when the jnaani drops this BMI,and Jnaanam itself involves only cognitive understanding of 'aham brahmaasmi' and abiding in that knowledge while still playing the drama of life.”



This is essentially the topic of 4 – 5 months’ worth of discussions at Advaita Vision, with myself claiming that the world (and the jIva) do not disappear when that jIva attains enlightenment and the others arguing that they do. For the past couple of months, I have been summarizing all of this under quite a number of sub-topics and providing quotations from prasthAna traya and Shankara in support. So far, I am up to nearly 30,000 words! It is a BIG topic!



The problem is that I have been unable to find a quotation that EXPLICITLY supports this contention. Consequently it seems one has to rely on intelligent understanding of Advaita and lots of circumstantial quotes. On the other hand, one has quotations such as Brahmasutra bhAShya 2.1.23 to the effect that “mundane existence is an error arising from the non-recognition of the difference” with “body and senses being a creation of name and form called up by ignorance” which “does not exist in reality”. We know all this of course but there is no denying that the simplistic conclusion would be that all of this disappears when Self-knowledge dawns. So the question is: do you know of a quotation (shruti, BG, BSB, Shankara bhAShya or Sureshvara) that EXPLICITLY says that the world does NOT disappear on enlightenment?



An interesting subsidiary question, if there is no explicit quotation, would be ‘Why not?’. From my own point of view the answer is simply that ‘it is obvious if you understand Advaita’ but apparently not!



Best wishes,

Dennis
