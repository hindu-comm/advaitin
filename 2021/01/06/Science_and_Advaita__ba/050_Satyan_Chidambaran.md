Dennis-ji,

  

A few points in this regard (you may have already thought of these (or similar) points):

  

1\) I don’t recall any explicit quotes to this effect. The ones I recall “imply" the world is there without being explicit. Sada ji has pointed out some.

  

Another one that comes to mind is "tadviddhi praNipAtena…." (B.G 4.34) that says “Understand that (which is to be known) by prostrating, by asking proper questions, (and) by service. Those who are wise, who have the vision of truth, will teach you this knowledge”. Obviously, such an instruction cannot make sense unless the jNani is operating in the world.

  

2\) A similar one would be from Mundaka…”sa gurumeva abhigacchet samitpANih shrotriyam brahmanishtham” (Mun.Up 1.2.12). If the brahmanishtha jnAni was not there, how would the mumukshu approach him at all?

  

3\) Lastly, their “jIva’s world and jIva disappear on jnAnam” is a self-contradictory statement. If the pUrvapakshi’s view is self contradictory, then there is no need to invoke shruti pramANa support. Shruti is not needed to refute an illogical statement. Just the fallacy in the statement needs to be pointed out.

The fallacy is as follows. If a jnAni disappears on enlightenment, there will be no jnAni left to teach. If there is no jnAni teacher/Guru,

all Guru’s in the world will be ajnAnis! An ajnAni Guru can only teach ajnAnam. There cannot be any Guru paramparA either, or if there was a Guru paramparA, it would only be an ajnAni Guru paramparA, that can only teach ajnAnam. Then no mukushu will be able to gain jnAnam as all the Guru’s they can approach would all be ajnAnis. Then possibility of gaining jnAnam itself then doesn't arise. So the statement “jIva’s world and jIva disappear on jNanam” becomes fallacious as jnAnam will be impossible to gain.

  

Regards,

—Satyan
