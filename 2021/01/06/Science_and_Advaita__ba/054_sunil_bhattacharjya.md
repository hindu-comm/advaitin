Dear Dennisji,

  

Even in the Ajativada verse in the Original Bhagavad Gita, as well as in the Uddhava Gita, the Lord tells us that ***the world is as good as not there***. This does not imply that the world has vanished. We have to consider the worldly matters as impermanent and they deserve only that much attention as may be needed at any particular moment. One has to develop unmindfullness towards the impermanent worldly matters, and in other words, the worldly matters should not agitate the mind at all.  

  

Another way of looking at the worldly things can be that the Lord is everywhere, as Lord Krishna said "***Pratimaa Alpabuddhinaam, Sarvatra Samadarshinaam***". Didn't the Lord also say in the Bhagavad Gita that he is three parts Unmanifested and one part Manifested? For us the ordinary folks, this may be easier said than done, but may not be so for the Sthitaprajnas.  

  

In his teachings Lord Buddha also taught us that we should be Unmindful towards the worldly objects, such that these are as good as derecognized by the mind, and these should not cause any ripple in the mind. He never said that the world does not exist. Nagarjuna also told from the rooftop that Buddhism is not Nihilism.  

  

Jai Shri Krishna

Sunil K. Bhattacharjya
