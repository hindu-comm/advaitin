Namaskaram,

  

I want to know what the sampradayateachings are.

  

1\. Can dharma be known from the birth and family? How or how not, and when may this indirect learning of what is dharmalead astray?

2\. Can dharma be known from body? How or how not, and ...?

3\. Can dharma be known from mind? How or how not, and ...?

4\. Can dharma be known from shastra? How or how not, and ...?

5\. Can a jiva's visesha dharma change after birth due to conditions imposed on body, mind, world? How or how not, and ...?

6\. Are the answers different with regard to samanya dharma?

  

I understand that the formal understanding is that dharma is known from the Veda/shastra. It has to be clarified how this can be when it is an external reference.

  

Purvapakshin: "i" am born to this family with this body-mind. I have to learn my dharma by allowing the inherent knowledge to unravel and then realizing what it is,or by adapting it according to the environment and experience I have in this world. How can a shastra be the means of defining my dharma for this whole life, when there are so many variables both in my birth body-world conditions and in the subsequent life I experience?

  

My questions came from another writeup I had made elsewhere (which would have been a separate post if not for the 'doubts' above; note the bold-type sentence). You can read below for this context, but I would like replies primarily to the questions above.

  

  

Quote

  

... a few basic "charvaka" (materialist) belief-aspects at play :

  

1\. by reading/mapping the body-mind-world, you are known

2\. by ordering the body-world, you are defined. (in charvaka construct, body-\>mind-\>"self")

The weaker form of 2. would be,

2'. by superimposing an apparent order upon the body-mind-world, you are controlled.

  

.. So the charvaka thinks that to secure his self, he has to read, order and control the body-mind-world.  

  

It is not about what he can or cannot do right now or in future. In principle, 1 and 2' are possible. Assume the limits of your imagination. Charvaka belief is that 2 is also possible because to him the body-mind-world is source of "self".

  

The astika or religious person does not agree; ordering body-world at the gross level is not equivalent to defining dharma. His shraddha is that A. dharma is intrinsic to the jiva in this life (i.e. you have taken this birth with an aligned svadharma) and he should abide by his dharma no matter the conditions imposed on body-mind-world, and that B. atma (the real You) transcends the body-mind-world, is immutable, unchanging, constant.

  

So for the astika, liberation from the charvaka-construct is by Knowledge. Yes, we must fight for dharma, initially by seeking to counter the charvaka'sattempts to map, order or impose upon body-mind-world. However ultimately that fight cannot depend on our ability or success to counter at the gross level; maya can be ruthless to such fancies. **The fight has to be the natural response *thatstems from the inherent knowledge of dharma* (Ishvara's Order at the subtle level) - the fight is really to stay in this knowledge - and the way is karma yoga.** It will remain a fight so long as the self is also being identified with body-mind-world, because both you and the charvaka can act upon the latter and he may appear the more powerful in this plane.

  

To go beyond this plane, beyond the Charvaka's apparent reach, one has to realize the true Self. "Na hanyate hanyamane sharire" \["It is not killed when the body is killed"; so the knower is not perturbed by \[charvaka\] no matter how they come at body-mind-world.\]

  

Unquote
