Dear Putranji,

  

I am sure you have written well in your paper/article. However. as you wish to have our inputs I would like to share some thoughts on a few of
the areas you have touched:

  

These are  

**1. Can dharma be known from the birth and family? How or how not, and when may this indirect learning of what is dharmalead astray?**

We have to keep in mind that birth is not by accident. Our prarabdha karma decides our spiritual progress. For example, the Lord Krishna
tells us that if someone yogi died with his yogic sadhana incomplete, he would take birth in a family of yogis, so that he can continue his pursuit in the next life. Some believe that one can take birth in the family / lineage of his last birth, so that he would experience the consequences of his own contributions in his past birth, but Lord Buddha told one girl, that she was a princess in her last birth and she spurned a man of lower status, who loved her very much and that is why she was reborn in a very low social status, which is why she is unable to marry
the boy she wanted to in the present birth.

  

That said, we should also know that we are all born shudras and by sanskaras we can become become Dvijas etc. In Ramayana, it is said that in the Kali Yuga there won't be any restrictions on what lifestyles, the Shudras would have to pursue. **  **

***  ***

**4. Can dharma be known from shastra? How or how not, and ...?**

All Shastras are not equally intelligible to all. The Veda are not intelligible to all as the Veda itself says that it loves paroksha interpretation. The Lord told Bhishma that what he would teach would be considered as the Vedic teachings, and in addition the Lord's own teachings are there in the Mahabharata, for which the sage Vedavyasa calls the Mahabharata as the Karshna veda (or Krishna's veda). But after completing the Mahabharata, even Vedavyasa realized that the Mahabharata is not intelligible to all. Then he wrote the Bhagavatam, which would be intelligible to all. Because of not knowing what Vedavyasa said, Shri Madhvacharya said that nobody can understand the Bhagavatam without his commentary. To undo the damage of this action of Shri madhvacharya, Shri Shridhar Acharya's guru asked him to write a detailed commentary on the Bhagavatam.  

  

So for the Vedas and the Mahabharata some may need Guru's help to understand However, anybody should be able to read the Puranas, including Bhagavatam.  

**Charvaka**  

Charvakas may be Nastik, but we should not forget that they too have a niti-shastra, written by none other than the great Guru Brihaspati, which is of high moral standard.
