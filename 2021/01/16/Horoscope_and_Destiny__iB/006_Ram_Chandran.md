Prarabdha and Purushartha

Let me share some of my thoughts on the role of Prarabdha and Purushartha in our life path. Prarabdha is what we have (we have no control over who are our parents) and Purushartha is what we can do with whatever we already have. The twin forces - ‘fate’ (Prarabdha) and ‘freewill’(Purushartha) both play inevitable roles in our daily life. When we exercise our freewill, we instantaneously create our fate. For example, our specific body with specific problems or virtues is Prarabdha but how do we nurture our body and keep it healthy is Purushartha. The steps that we take through Purushartha has the potential to create our own destiny. This may explain the statement that our Character (developed through our self-efforts) becomes our destiny which determines how we are respected by others. In our daily routine we become the victims of both the fate and freewill! The wisdom that we gain help us to recognize that our self-efforts can’t guarantee to fully suppress the effects of our fate. Such an understanding will help us to accept the outcome without unnecessarily resisting. In a recent discourse, Swami Omkarananda foremely of Arsha Vidhya Gurukulam (currently has an Ashramam in Teni near Madurai) jokingly said that our height is our Prarabdha (fate) but our weight is the consequence of Purushartha (freewill)!

In both Bhagavad Gita and especially in Uddhava Gita, Lord Krishna explains that the ‘Divine’ just witnesses everything that happens because of ‘Karma Chakra,’ For the wheels of Karma, Bhagwan Krishna serves as the axis. The Karma Chakra can’t run without the axis but axis just helps the wheel to rotate without getting affected by the movement. Both the Pandavas and Kauravas in Mahabharat were responsible to control this Karma Chakra and Lord Krishna was the axis of this chakra. Axis is necessary for the Chakra but axis can’t stop the movement Without the axis, the chakra can’t move but the axis can’t be responsible for improper operation of the wheel!

Sage Ramakrishna Paramahamsa once said, “free will is limited like the movement of the calf tied by a strong rope to an iron post.” We including the Pandavas of Mahabharat had been tied by the rope of fate to the tree and couldn’t go beyond where the rope (destiny) can take us! The message of Uddhava Gita is quite subtle and tell us that though the Grace of God is always available, and even that Grace requires some self-efforts (Purushartha)!!
