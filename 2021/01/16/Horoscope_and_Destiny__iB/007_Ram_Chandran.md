Let me add the following which may be useful for understanding Prarabdha and Purushartha.

Relevance of Prarabdha and Purushartha for Real Life Situations

(Note from Ram Chandran: this article that I wrote for Chinmaya Mission is useful for all)



Prarabdha the Sanskrit word simply means what we already posess and Purushartha represents our self efforts to make our possessions for better utilizations. When we made the decision to live in Florida, our Prarabdha is hot weather with 90 degree temperature. But with Purushartha, we have airconditioner which allow us to bring the temperature down to 76 or 78 degrees! The question often arises in our mind that how our understanding of Prarabdha (fate) and Purushartha (self-efforts/freewill) can help us to lead our life with greater peace and happiness? I believe answer to this question has high potential to keep track of our life path and prevent us from pitfalls and come out of entangles. Also, we always look for a simple framework with very few parameters to help to sail us through the samsara sagaram! I believe every activity and/or situation can be easily understood through the framework of Prarabdha and Purushartha. Simply knowing that our Purushartha will likely influence how tomorrow’s Prarabdha can greatly help us in any context. Our knowledge of the interrelationship between over eating and stomach discomforts can prevent overindulging!



Let us begin with the analogy of a car journey. To run a car, both the left and right wheels are necessary. Also, the car will not move without either one of them. Similarly, our life journey requires both the Prarabdha and Pururshartha. To make the car journey comfortable we need to check the alignment and balancing of the wheels. Similarly, for the life journey how we align, balance and fine tune our understanding of Prarabdha and Purushartha are quite essential. The story of Smart King (as narrated by Pujya Gurudev) explained the importance of effective utilization of both Prarabdha and Purushartha, We should recognize that Prarabdha and Purushartha are divine resources provided by the Lord to manage our life.



Let us try to understand how someone got exposed to cigarette smoking. Starting with one cigarette and continued to smoke cigarettes will make the smoker addictive. To reverse from Smoking or drinking addictions, lots of self-efforts are necessary. Such reversions are not impossible, and we have evidence of many successful people who were able to abandon smoking. To be successful, the smoker needs helpful and supportive environment, doctors, friends and relatives. Smokers with favorable Prarabdha were able to abandon more easily than those with unfavorable Prarabdha. It should be pointed that with substantive Purushartha, it is possible to associate with good friends and avoid places congenial to smoking. Vedantic organizations such as Chinmaya Mission encourage their members to join the Satsangh groups to exchange view points to learn and improve the quality of life.



The natural law of “Cause and Effect” supports this discussion with the statement: "As you sow, so shall you reap." Whatever we put out in this universe is what will come back to us. If we want happiness, peace, love, friend-ship; then we should try our level best to engage in activities to keep us happy, peaceful, loving and to develop true friendship. The Brihadaranyaka Upanishad states that the actions of the human beings decide the outcome. The doer of good becomes good, the doer of evil becomes evil. One becomes virtuous by virtuous action and sinful by sinful action. As we desire so we become, as our will so will be the deed, and whatever deed we do, that alone we will reap! To grow spiritually, the first and foremost is that we need to take self-efforts to change. We have control over our life alone and when we change it can help us to change our behavior. Anything that goes wrong in our life only reflects our mistake.



Note: It may be worthwhile to bring the concept of Karma in the context of the present discussion. Karma is generally classified into Sanchita (accumulated total of past karmas), Prarabdha (part of Sanchita influencing now) and Kriyamana or Agami (which is the result from Purushartha that account for the future). The positive or negative level of influence of Prarabdha is unknown to us. Because of this reason that the management of karmas is much more complex. It is always worthwhile to conduct Purushartha with the focus to accumulate good Sanchita karma.
