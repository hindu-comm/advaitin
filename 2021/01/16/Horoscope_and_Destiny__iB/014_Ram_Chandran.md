The Law of Karma (Cause and Effect)

(Article published in Hindu Society of NC Newsletter)

Ram Chandran

The Doctrine of Karma rationalizes the role of the Jiva during the transition between the lives. The law of Karma rationalizes the purpose of the movement. Rebirth is dependent on moral behavior in a previous phase of existence and life on the universe is transient. In the Upanishads, it is no longer a question of rewards and punishments meted out by an external judge. The human beings become the architect of their own spiritual fortunes, no longer subject to chance or the will of an hypothetical God. The Brihadaranyaka Upanishad states that the actions of the human beings decide the outcome. The doer of good becomes good, the doer of evil becomes evil. One becomes virtuous by virtuous action and sinful by sinful action. As is his desire so is his will, as his will so is the deed, he does, and whatever deed he does, that he will reap. Ultimately, being  
Brahman, he goes to Brahman! The law of karma explains the entire phenomenon of life on the basic principle of cause and effect. The law of karma says that we are not only a product of the past but we are also the producers of the future. With reference to our past actions we are a product. However, we are solely responsible for producing our future. We are product and producer at the same time. When we pay our attention to our past, we believe that we became the hero, victim or a slave. But when we shift our attention to the future we become the architect, the artist and the master.

When we look back, we become a pessimist and if we look ahead, we can become an optimist. As a pessimist we see only the difficulty in every opportunity. As an optimist we are able to visualize the opportunity in every difficulty. We can apply the law of Karma to transform our negative attitude into positive attitude using the correct framework. We may be able to appreciate that our present difficulties are the creation of our past unintelligent actions. It is up to us to realize our potentials by seeking the opportunity to create a better future. The law makes us to be aware that we are the masters of our own destiny. This quotation of Mahatma Gandhi summarizes the cause and effects beautifully in simple words: “*Our beliefs become our thoughts; Our thoughts become our words; Our words become our actions; Our actions become our habits; Our habits become our values and Our values become our destiny***.”**  

As it is, we are bound by our own past. At the same time we are free to act as we will. Freedom and bondage seem to coexist in all of us. We are like the driver of a train. We can drive the train to wherever we please (free-will) but our movement is restricted by the tracks (destiny) laid down already. Both free-will and destiny play important roles in every action. Free-will is an independent act and destiny will depend on our past acts. The independent element is the Purushartha or self-effort. The dependent element is the Prarabdha or destiny. We are free to choose our action but every action is also subject to the influence of self-effort and destiny. We should recognize that the consequence of our action depends both on the self-effort and our destiny. Our destiny or fate is the result of our past actions. Our Prarabdha is the sum total of our past Purusharthas. If our self-efforts in the past have all been positive, our present destiny will be positive. If they have been negative, our destiny will be negative. Our destiny is proportional to our self-efforts.  
Destiny is comparable to the bank balance. Savings in the past will result in positive balance. Positive balances produce returns (interest) and enable us to accumulate our balances. Borrowers will have negative bank balances whereas savers will have positive bank balances. Our earning capacity is independent of the bank balance and we always have the option of changing the bank balances from negative to positive or vice-versa. The accounting principle used to manage our finances is equally applicable in managing our karma which is reflected through the law of karma. If we use Purushartha (self-effort) to our advantage we rise and if we abuse it, we fall. The law is infallible. It is important to know that our effort creates our destiny. We fail to recognize that God is witness of how we behave including our successes and failures. God is only the substratum of all activities. God has nothing to do with the mode of activity. God helps those who help themselves.

When we put in effort, we get what we fully deserve. But without efforts, without deserving we will never get what we want. There is a saying "First deserve and then desire." Vedanta says, "Deserve and do not desire." If we really deserve, objects reach us of their own accord.
There is no escape from the outcome of our past deeds – good and bad deeds produce good and bad outcomes. Once we entertain negative thoughts we will likely have negative outcomes and similarly positive thoughts and words will produce positive outcomes. It never happens that we richly deserve something and we are denied. This is a misconception. If we fail to achieve our objective remember that we do not deserve it. It only confirms that something has gone wrong with our effort. We do need to recognize the fact that “Karma will follow us whether we accept it or reject it and that is the Law!”
