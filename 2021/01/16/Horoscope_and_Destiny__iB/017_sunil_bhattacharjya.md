Dear friends,

  

On the contrary there is absolutely no proof that Shankara was born in the 9th century. Unscrupulous people are spreading a lie that Shankara was born in the 8th or 9th century, without any proof. There was one Vikramaditya after whom there was one Vikrama samvat, which started in 457 BCE, and Albiruni mentions about that. In the 14th year of the reign of that Vikramaditya , Adi Shankar as born in 509 BCE. One of the Gurus of the Tunga Shringeri math, declared in early 20th century that Adi Shankara was indeed born in the 14th year of the reign of Vikramadita. That Vikramaditya was the son of Adi Shankara's guru Govinda Bhagavadpada, whose pre-sanyasha name was Chandra Sharma.  

  

Further, there was one Pathak, who claimed that one Shankara born in the 8th century in Chidambaram, was the Adi Shankara and that was a ruse to be in line with the German Scholar, Max Muller's Aryan Invasion Theory (AIT), according to which the Aryans came to India in the 15th century BCE. Later on Max Muller, an honest man that he was, withdrew his theory, when the French Sanskrit scholar Prof. M.A.Troyer, challenged Max Mulller, on the basis of the Rajatarangini. Rajatarngini clearly shows that Kanishka was living in the 13th century BCE, and that is compatible only with the 509 BCE date of Adi Shankara.  

There are several proofs that Adi Shankara was born in 509 BCE. Even the Original Shringeri Math, in Kudali, on the bank of the confluence of the Tunga and the Bhadra river, has the guru-parampara, starting from the 5th century BCE, which has been published from Tirupati. The Tunga Shringeri math was established only in the 13th century CE, with the support of the Vijaya Nagara Kings, Hukka (Harihara) and Bukka (Bukkaraya), who were the disciples of Shri Vidyaranya Swami, who along with his brother,were educated in the Kanchipuram, under Swami Vidyateertha or Vidyashankara Teertha. The Tunga Shringeri math was
established much later than the other Shankara maths in Dwarka, Puri, Kanchi Kamakoti and Kudali, who have their Guru-Parampara from the 5th century BCE.  

  

Abhinava Shankara, who was born in the 8th century CE, was born in Chidambaram and it was he who disappeared in Kedarnath. It was this Shankaracharya, who wrote the Shankarabhashya on the Bhagavad Gita, and it was a pity that he did nort know that the Bhagavad gita had 745 verses, out of which 620 verses were spoken by Lord Krishna, as given in the Mahabharata. Prof. Belvalkar, who edited the Critical edition of the Bhishma Parva of the Mahabharata, dropped the Verse in which Vedavyasa said that the Bhagavad gita had 745 verses, as he could not believe that Sant Jnaneshwar was not aware of the Original Bhagavad ita of 745 verses.

  

Jai Shri Krshna

sunil.K Bhattacharjya
