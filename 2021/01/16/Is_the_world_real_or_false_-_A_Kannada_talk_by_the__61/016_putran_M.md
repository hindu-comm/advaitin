Namaskaram,

  

I am not sure if this is a standard topic of debate between schools in advaita. From what I read, the acharya is saying

  

1\. Brahma Satyam

2\. Jagan mithya

  

This is too standard an epithet in advaita to be finding fault with the acharya; can we say almost ridiculous to do so? Perhaps we don't like the translation but there too, translation of mithya can be (?) that which exists in ignorance and ceases in knowledge. We can perhaps look at it as a debate within established advaita schools (if that is the case) where the acharya is putting forth his sub-school's position; but we cannot act as if such a position is only taken up by ignoramuses or that the acharya is being careless with his words. His usage must be standard and we have to find its true meaning.

  

Even in Mandukya Up., we can find such phrases: Ma. 3 bhashya: "..non-duality stands established on the removal of the entire phenomenal world..."; Karika 1.18: ".. Duality ceases to exist after realization".

  

Should he have said ".. nama-rupa of Brahman exists after realization but other types of duality ceases"?? You can decide. We have to interpret and understand, can't be accusing acharyas for this.

  

thollmelukaalkizhu
