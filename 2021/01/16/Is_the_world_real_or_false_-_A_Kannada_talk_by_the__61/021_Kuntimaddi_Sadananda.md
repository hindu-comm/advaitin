Here it is - based on my understanding.

Brahmasutras are written to make a coherent meaning of the scriptures as the sutra itself says - tat tu samanvayaat.

It is a pourusheyam.

After addressing that, one has to inquire into brahman - athatho brahma ginjnaasa - Brahman is being defined using a tatashta lakshana- janmaadyasya yataH.

I had notes on the first five sutras of the first adhyaaya that was stored in the Yahoo.advaitin list, based on Swami Paramarthanandaji's talks. It was written close to 20 years ago, but now it is not available now since we moved the listserve to Google.

Here are some important facts, as I see it.

Brahman (meaning infiniteness - satyam - Jnaanam - anantam) cannot undergo a transformation (anantatvaat) - hence no creation can occur from that reference.

This very discussion and the Vedanta and Brahmasutra addressing the issues implies that creation appears to be there. Hence creation and if you want to call it 'manifestation' - these are just words - can be used to define the experiential plurality. Hence any experiential plurality cannot be a ‘reality’ as Goudapaada's ajaati vaada indicates.Shankara’s commentary on the kaarika says – the perceived plurality is mithyaa since it comes under ‘dRisyatvat’. Mithyaa is sat asat vilakshanam – neither real nor unreal – it is not real since it undergoes changes and it is not unreal since it is experienced.

Hence ‘creation or manifestation’ comes under mithyaa.

Since from Advaita point – Brahman cannot undergo a transformation – the apparent creation accounted by bringing ‘Iswara’ and with maayaa shakti based on the cumulative vaasanaas of all beings accumulated in the past live forming seed for creation.

Hence janmaadi involves janma-sthiti and laya – beginningless cycles.

Since Brahman being infinite – creation cannot be separate from Brahman also – if it is separate, then Brahman ceases to be Brahman (infiniteness). Hence one can say that Brahma is adhishTaanam for the whole creation that involves plurality – jeeva-jagat-Iswara - traid. Advaita points out the absolute truth – Brahman (not Iswara) as the fundamental truth – in the ‘tat tvam asi’ statement substantiated by ‘ ayam atmaa brahma’ and ‘aham brahmaasmi’.

Correct understanding of Advaita therefore involves:

1. Brahma satyam ( satyam is trikaala abhaaditam)

2. Jagan mithyaa (sat asat vilakshanam)

3. Jeevo braham eva na aparaH (tat tvam asi).

Shankara says ‘anena vedyam tat shaastram’, that because of which one understands the above three is the real science since it points out to the absolute reality unlike the other objective sciences which only deals with objectifiable entities that come under mithyaa.

Advaita is experienced by everyone during the deep-sleep state. However, understanding of that experience requires ‘knowledge’ for which ‘mind’ is required. Hence knowledge has to take place in the waking state.

By the by, dwaitins do not accept Brahman as the material cause and for them ‘tat tvam asi’ is split as ‘atat tvam asi’ due to sandhi. Shree Ramanuja accepts it but uses 'visheshana visheshaabhyaam samaanadhikaraNam' to explain it. I am sure you are familiar with all that.

Hope this helps.

Hari Om!
