Dear Friends - Now how did I get implicated in this fiasco :-)

  

I was simply responding to the English translation on what Sri Swamaji may or may not have said in Kannada, from the translator.  

  

What I was pointing out is the 'apparent world' (vyavahAra) is always in transformation continually renewing itself as a wonderful expression or display of fresh, novel phenomena.

  

Human sentiments form attachment in memory fixating on the passing parade and imposing a layer of name, permanence and independent identity on momentary objects which is purely fictional (or imaginary). Our inability to release one's hold on these transitional objects leads to the sorrowful state of PratibhAsa.

   

By reflecting again and again and instantaneously 'Seeing' this very clearly, the 'imaginary world' - which is simply our conceptual overlay imposed on objects - falls away effortlessly and one remains at peace embracing the 'apparent world', the unknowable mystery, 'as it is'.  

  

Seeing through this childish illusion of ours (and not that the apparent world is an illusion) is the first step of Gyana and the very foundation of Shankara's Advaita philosophy.  

  

I don't know how to express this with more clarity.... maybe someone else can?  

  

Om

Dave
