Dear Sri Sada-ji,

  

\<

'For Vishishtaadvaita there is no mithyaa'. For them sat and asat are only two and are mutually exclusive.

\>

  

Sat and Asat can take different meanings depending on the context. If Sat = atman/jiva/chit and asat = anAtma/jada/achit, Brahman alone is identified

through both in V.Advaita as it is the Self of both. Brahman alone is identified

through allnames and forms. Do you think Brahman/Self and all name/forms/body are

mutuallyexclusive?

  

Having said that, let me clarify that I do not like to look at reality with the lens of

Advaita or Dvaita or V.Advaita. All of these viewpoints come together when Brahman

is identified as the sarvAtma.

  

Here is my view:

Brahman = The universal/absolute Self (sarvAtma) is not (just) jada or jiva or Ishwara but everything!

In short Self = Vishwa = Everything.

  

Brahman is infinite and one but it also has place for all the countless finite entities including the mysterious zero which is mAya.The sacred syllable OM is one, but it also contains the letters A,U and M
(standing for Ishwara, Prakriti and Jiva) and also all the 33 aksharas standing for 33 devatas. All of these are rooted in the fourth unknowable and unthinkable turIya shabda.

  

You mentioned Vishwaroopi Brahman has devatas standing for dharma and rAkshasas for adharma and that is true. As He supports and sustains all kinds of beings, Brahman is called Dharma. If you perform Vaishwadeva kriya, there is Ahuti for Adharma also!

  

If your Advaita could be called as science, my advaita is an art of looking everything as one.The problem in your analysis is that there is no place for Ishwara / dharma which is avaidhika and so not reflecting Sri Shankara's true intent.

  

Namaste

Suresh
