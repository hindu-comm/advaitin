If we speak of jnani'sdrishti of jagat, ourquestion is posed within the context of the duality of seer-seeing-seen where jnani is the seer and jagat is the seen. The question makes sense for the ajnaniwho identifies the Self with certain namarupa and calls it ajnani, with another namarupa and calls it jnani, another namarupa and calls it seeing - so that "i see jnani and jnani sees world". The jnani "sees" the world - that is automatic to the question that splits Self as jnani and world - but the jnani knows that this world is apparent: it is subsumed as the Seeing of the Seer, the Self of him and world. So where the ajnaniknows (namarupa of) body/jagat, the jnani 'sees' body/jagat-namarupa and knows Brahman; everything is thus namarupa of Brahman, the only Self, the only Seer/Projector.

  

In Ignorance, "jnani sees world" is true statement because in ignorance, the Self is divided three-fold and our statement is contextual to this division. Jnana is that the Self is undivided, full, non-dual, so this statement “jnani sees world” is vikalpa in jnana. We cannot take this baggage to that standpoint.

  

So the standpoint of jnana is what Sada-ji said: ekam eva adviteeyam. It is paramarthika, turiyam; no place for “what does jnani see?” type questions.

  

The vyavaharika standpoint on the other hand is based on the seer-seeing-seen divide of the Self. The ajnani takes it to be real; the jnani knows it as mithya, for this duality is absent in the standpoint of jnana (of non-dual Self). BUT this is not the same as saying the jnani knows it as asat (absolutely unreal). Why not?

  

The “jnani” is a contextual reference of Self identified with certain body-mind namarupa. This is within vyavaharika where the seer-seen duality is pre-affirmed as not asat. It is experienced. The ajnani thinks his dualistic identification of selves (as ishvara, jivas, things, world) is sat. The jnani also sees the “same”. Pratyaksha reveals the same duality and he has the same vyavaharika knowledge. However it is filtered by the Knowledge of Self. Where the ajnani sees world and things, the Jnani sees the Self (appearing/projecting thus). Brahman is reflected in his consciousness as “Ishvara” - the Self+Maya who projects as if ishvara-jiva-jagat or seer-seeing-seen: thus he sees all in Self and the non-dual Self in and as All.

  

Ajnani: I see and know hand, head, shirt etc. and must realize that all that I see is namarupa of Bhaskar-ji.

Jnani: I see and know Bhaskar-ji.

  

What must be kept in mind is that this reference to jnani pertains to the ajnani who identifies Self with that body-mind. So the answer is description of the namarupa of jnani from the standpoint of ajnani; he is describing how jnana is reflected in that body-mind. The Upanishad itself goes beyond and tells us our original question is ill-posed:

  

“When there is something else, as it were, then one can see something, one can know something.” “But when to the knower of Brahman everything has become the Self, then what should one see and through what? What should one know and through what?” (Br Up)

  

This is akin to the paramarthika standpoint. The idea seems to be that realization is liberation, it is meaningless thereafter to impose individuality to “jnani” and ask “what does jnani see?” Better to ask: what do you think the jnani sees based on your superimposing jivatva to that body-mind?
