Unless one sees both the manifold and the unity simultaneously he is not a Jnani. Do not be misled by claimants, especially practitioners of yoga, who say Jnana is not to see the world or anything at all, while they hide out in a sleep trance of Nirvikalpa Samadhi.  

  

Jnana is to see everything yet simultaneously knowing it as One essence. It is like seeing table, shelf, chair, door distinctly yet at the same time knowing they are all wood. They are creative expressions (or ideas) carved from wood. Wooden shapes dismantled still remain fibers of wood.  

  

Similarly, all the dramatic performances have emanated from the Silence of our essential unborn Being and returns to this eternal presence of Silence; nothing ever actually happened to be exuberant about and nothing is ever lost to grieve about.

  

The world appearance is visible and seem to really exist but upon inquiry, it lacks an inherent or independent existence. Hence it is said to be Mithya or indeterminate because it is paradoxical, defying all views.
