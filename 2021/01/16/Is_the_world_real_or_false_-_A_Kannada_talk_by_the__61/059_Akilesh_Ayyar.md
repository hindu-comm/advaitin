Namaste,

  

I'm going to have to disagree about Ramana's jnani status -- he most certainly was a jnani. He mentions this story because one can most certainly operate in the worldin the very way of the sage in this story. The sage is making a point that living in the world is itself *not*living in the world for the jnani, because 'the world' is itself a concept that comes out of the implicit belief that the ego is real. To say that "the world continues to appear but is considered merely the Self" is one way of putting it & and is not in principle wrong; but another way of putting the same thing -- the deeper way, the more precise way*--* is that the very notion of appearance itself rests on the ego. In that silence in which there cannot be said to be an ego, there cannot be said to be a world.

  

And in fact there is *only*ever that silence in which there cannot be said to be an ego.
