PraNAms to all.

  

Looks like we are ready to give the certificates of who is a jnaani, who is yogi, who is a yogi-jnaani, etc.

  

I cannot know any body's mind and how it operates. I can only what I think.

  

Once a child asked Swami Tejomayanandaji - ' Can you read other people's minds?' Swamiji said ' No I cannot, but I can know what they are thinking". The child asked, ' If you cannot read other's mind, how can you know what he is thinking?'. Swamiji responded. 'It is very simple. If I love that person enough, he will tell me what is in his mind. That is how I will know.'.

  

The moral of this story is one can guess but not really know what the other person is thinking'.

  

'Ego' is required to operate in the word. Jnaani puts the vesham of 'ego' in order to transact in this world as long as BMI is three. When Shankara wrote the Bhaashya, he identified himself as a person and invokes the grace of Iswara for the successful completion of the text. Invocation can be offering prayers to the Lord or can be just a statement of fact or a combination of the two. Bhagavan Ramana uses the second for the text 'Sat Darshanam' text. Sat pratyayaH kinnu vihaaya santam.. sloka depicts the statement of fact. In the next sloka he invokes Lord Shiva ' mRityunjayam ..

  

The sthitaprajna lakshanas provided to evaluate oneself how far one is abiding in the knowledge. Any dependence on others than oneself for one's happiness shows that the mind is not mature enough to realize 'aham brahmaasmi' all the time. Hence Nidhidhyaasana has to be continued - perhaps until the BMI drops.

  

I suggest the others stop trying to judge others whether one has realized it or not.

  

It is better to assume that the teacher has realized so that one can have faith in the teaching.

  

One of the obstacles to spiritual growth is the lack of faith. Shraddhaavan labhate jnaanam - says Krishna in the 4th Ch, and in the next sloka says 'ajnascha ashraddhadanascha.. affirming that lack of faith can be an obstacle for self-realization.

  

Hari Om!

Sadananda
