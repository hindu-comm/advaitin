To add to your words... it is an unfortunate bane of the moderners who consider reading books as jnAna about everything! That a jnAnI should be only the way one reads in some book is ridiculous. That is like "the blind men and the elephant". The levels of jnAna-niShThA can vary across jnAnIs though the jnAna is the same. Somehow, the word Yoga/ Yogi is also misinterpreted due to perhaps knowing only one particular meaning of the word! Every now and then, someone pops up on these lists to pass a comment on jnAnIs well-established so in the sampradAya, be it Bhagavan Ramana, or whoever else. Only thing that can be analysed about Bhagavan Ramana is whether or not he taught what the tradition teaches considering that he didn't have a study of the tradition traceable in this life. And for that, the great traditionalists such as Vasishtha Ganapati Muni are pramANa enough.

  

Bhagavan Ramana may or may not have been able to create another Ramana, but there is no question of his being a jnAnI.
