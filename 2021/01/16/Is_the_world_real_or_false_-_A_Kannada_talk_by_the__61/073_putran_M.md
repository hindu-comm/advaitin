Namaskaram Bhaskar-ji,

  

Since you identify the jnani as a sthithapragnya(being the nishta), technically he should remain like a lotus leaf on water in all my proposed scenarios. If in ourselves, such would evoke fear or desire, notions of dharma and adharma, passion, anger or disgust, that we will become particular about choosing one way over the other (not for loka-kshema but due to our BMI-identifications), then we can ascertain that we are not yet sthithapragnya.

  

Quote

  

MASTER: "Yes. Once a ship sailed into the ocean. Suddenly its iron joints, nails, and screws fell out. The ship was passing a magnetic hill, and so all its iron was loosened.  
  
"I used to go to Krishnakishore's house. Once, when I was there, he said to me, 'Why do you chew betel-leaf?' I said: 'It is my sweet pleasure. I shall chew betel-leaf, look at my face in the mirror, and dance naked among a thousand girls.' (Because the Master was a Vijnani) Krishnakishore's wife scolded him and said: 'What have you said to Ramakrishna? You don't know how to talk to people.'  
  
"In this state, passions like lust and anger are burnt up, though nothing happens to the physical body. It looks just like any other body; but the inside is all hollow and pure."  

  

Unquote

  

Sri Ramakrishna in this instance says this is the state of a vijnani (as opposed to simply jnani). Whether of vijnani, or yogi, or jnani, or sthithapragnya, or one in nishta, however we put it, the ajnani (or intellectual jnanis like ourselves?) will likely doubt such a state (idealized as being directand necessary consequence of realization and/or nishta) is even possible. Where doubt is eliminated and scenarios as above cannot create confusions.

  

But that is implicitly part of the standard of judgment behind "who is a jnani/vijnani?" or "am i a jnani/nishta?", what will differentiate our "jnani" from the next guy in the street saying the same things. Yes, we do have shraddha guiding us.

  

thollmelukaalkizhu
