I understand from past discussions that there are two camps of advaitins, one who says the jnani is ever in nishta (is sthitapragnya) and the other who says that one can have jnana and yet not be properly established in nishta (hence in my thinking, not yet sthitapragnya) - so can 'forget' or become subject to fear, desire, etc. temporarily under pressure. Since you said "jnani (sthita prajna)", I understood you as belonging to the first camp. I do not have a particular conviction on which version is correct, generally try to keep open for both possibilities.

  

As for jnani abusing his own mother and kids, the word "abuse" is from the standpoint of the ajnani. The question is not whether a jnani would indulge in an act of adharma out of his own volition. I described a variety of acts that suppose the jnani'sbody-mind is engaged in or forced into. "What does the jnani see?" in such situation vs what does the quack and common ajnani see? That is the question and the answer becomes a basis/mark for judgment on who is jnani and whether i am one. We both agreed on the type of reactions that are not to be expected from a sthithapragnya(jnani).

  

As for the quack, he has right to talk as much advaita as you and me. Why not? We talk and say we are ajnanis; he talks and says he is jnanis and a bunch of people follow him for that. That doesn't mean Sri Ramakrishna should have said something else; we can't put rules like that on a jnani.

  

thollmelukaalkizhu
