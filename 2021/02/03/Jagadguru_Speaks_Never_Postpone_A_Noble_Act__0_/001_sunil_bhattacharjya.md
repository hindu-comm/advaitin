Dear friends,

The following is indeed a great message**:**  

*This is what our ancestors have instructed us:**  *** ***  
 न कश्चिदपि जानाति किं कस्य श्वो भविष्यति ।  
 अतः श्वः करणीयानि कुर्यादद्यैव बुद्धिमान् ॥  *** ***  ** It means: No one knows what will happen to whom tomorrow.  
Therefore the wise complete even tomorrow's work right away!*

Can you please mention the original source of th*e above* Sanskrit verse?  

*There is such a popular saying in Hindi, which runs as follows: **  ***

***Kaalkaa kaaam aaj, aajkaa kaam ab***

***Kaun Jaane Maut aayegi kab***

*It means : Tomorrow's job today, today's job now.* who knows when the  

death will come.

**Now coming to the pending jobs, which need our attention, I feel there are quite a few pending jobs,  **

**from the Vedic standpoint. Updating of the Itihaasa (history) is one of these and is very important.**  

In Mahabharata, Hanumanaji told Bhimsena, that it is difficult for one to study the entire Veda, and that is why the  

Veda was divided in the Dwapara yuga into several pats. The last part of the Original one Veda, comprising the  

Puranas and the epics, became the Fifth Veda. As regards the fifth Veda, there are five injunctions, known as the  

***pancha lakshanas***. Out of these five, one is to keep the Itihaasa updated. ***Itihaasa portion has been neglected /***

***distorted in the last quarter of the Kali yuga.*** The Kali yuga started when Lord Krishna completed his Human  

Avatara in February, 3102 BCE ( **-** 3101 CE) .In one of the Mahapuranas, the Lord said that the Kali yuga would last  

for ~ 5,000 years and that means the Kali YUga should have been just over with the beginning of the 21st century  

BCE, and ***it is time to wake up and bring out our authentic past history.***  

  

So, my friends, let us think as to how each of us either individuallly, or collectively with others, can contribute towards  

updating the history of the culture, philosophy and antiquity of our Sanatana Dharma, including the bringing out the  

authentic history of the Advaita Traditon, as upheld by Adi Shankara and his guru and paramguru.  

  

Regards,

Sunil K. Bhattacharjya
