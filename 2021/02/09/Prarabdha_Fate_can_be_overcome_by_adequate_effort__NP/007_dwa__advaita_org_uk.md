Dear Subbu-ji,



The sort of quotation you are probably thinking of is: “*There are two ways to dissolve the mind, O Raghava! The one is yoga and the other is knowledge. Restraining the transformations of the mind is called yoga and right seeing is called knowledge. (laghu yoga vAsiShTha 27.72, yoga vAsiShTha 5.78.8)*” But I don’t know of any corresponding quotation by Shankara.



I confess that I don’t know YV very well, and Ramesam Vemuri also supports what you say. But going off the beliefs of Vidyaranya in jIvanmukti viveka – and he quotes extensively from YV and LYV – what is said differs from Shankara in a number of places. All of which cause confusions in seekers who are reading both. YV also advocates manonAsha and samAdhi as being necessary for enlightenment, both of which contradict Shankara. Accordingly, although it can no doubt be a stimulating read for someone who understands Advaita, I would not recommend it to other than an advanced seeker.
