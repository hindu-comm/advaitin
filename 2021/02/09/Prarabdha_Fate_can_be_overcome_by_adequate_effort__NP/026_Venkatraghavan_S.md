Dear Dennis ji,  

Re "The objection I gave to the original post was that Shankara and shruti use the metaphor of an arrow shot from a bow to explain that prArabdha karma continues inexorably until the body drops. Free will cannot override this."

  

The statement re prArabdha by Shankaracharya that you refer to was made in the context of a discussion on jIvamukti, a jnAni.

  

However, the very notions of actions (whether past or present) or free will (the manifestation of desire to overcome those actions through effort) do not apply to the jnAni. He becomes a jnAni on account of sarva karma sannyAsa (renunciation of all action) and giving up all manner of desire. That being the case, why would he seek to overcome prArabdha, with what free will? As has been said in the brihadAraNyaka, kimicChann kasya kAmAya sharIram anusanjvaret (किमिच्छन् कस्य कामाय शरीरमनुसञ्ज्वरेत् )?  
  
Therefore, all talk of overcoming the effect of past action by present action is only possible for the ajnAni. Even here, one cannot overcome certain prArabdha, for example the one that gave him this birth (as it has already fructified), however the effects of prArabdha that haven't been fructified can certainly be overcome by opposing effort. It was in this sense that the AchArya's statement must be understood.

  

Kind regards,

Venkatraghavan
