Dear Venkatraghavan-ji,



I don’t agree.



The difference between the j~nAnI and aj~nAnI in this context is that the former *knows* that ‘I do nothing at all’ (BG 5.9). The aj~nAnI becomes a j~nAnI as a result of gaining Self-knowledge. Renunciation of action and giving up desire may be entailed but are incidental.



I agree that Shankara’s statements about the ‘ loosed arrow of prArabdha’ are usually in the context of the j~nAnI but if a j~nAnI cannot influence this, how could an aj~nAnI? (See Ch.Up. 6.14.2; Br.Up. 1.4.10; BSB 4.1.15)



Apastamba dharma sutra, prashna 1, paTala 8, khaNDa 23 (Shankara): “Defects therefore arise (even in the case of a man liberated in life) through action performed (in previous births) for the sake of results, and they must be extirpated with efforts proportionate to their gravity. Otherwise they might lead to further interested action.” And later: “…the acts proceeding from the psychological defects of the enlightened one arise merely from merit and demerit that has already begun to fructify (prArabdha) and must complete its course like an arrow that has left its bow.”
