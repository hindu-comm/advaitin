Dear Bhaskar-ji



I would deny that I am ‘voicing my thoughts against the tradition’. On the contrary, what I am aiming to do is clarify the understanding of key topics by refencing back to prasthAna traya and Shankara. Vivekachudamani is an excellent source for Advaita for the most part but does contain a few potentially misleading verses. Those relating to samAdhi are a good example and are strongly suggestive that it was *not* written by Adi Shankara. I understand that many of those scholars who pursue such things claim that to be the case. That Shankara was the commentator on Mandukya and Gaudapada is also questioned by many.



I wrote about manonAsha at [https://www.advaita-vision.org/manonasha-not-the-literal-death-of-the-mind/](https://www.advaita-vision.org/manonasha-not-the-literal-death-of-the-mind/) if you want to read that.



If you want to query the meaning of a kArikA, let me know which one.



Patanjali’s Yogasutra-s are not a source reference for Advaita, although Shankara certainly accepted aShTA~Nga yoga as valuable preparatory practice. JMV is also heavily influenced by Yoga so cannot be relied upon as a traditional source. Might I remind you of Brahmasutra 2.1.3: “Hereby is refuted Yoga”. And Shankara comments: ““Their refutation centers round only this false claim that liberation can be attained through Sankhya knowledge or the path of Yoga independently of the Vedas. For the Upanishads reject the claim that there can be anything apart from the Vedic knowledge of the unity of the Self that can bring about liberation, as is denied in ‘By knowing Him alone, one goes beyond death. There is no other path to proceed by’ (Shvetashvatara Upanishad 3.8). But the followers of Sankhya and Yoga are dualists, and they do not perceive the unity of the Self.”



Your claim that one gets the knowledge and then has to experience something is the sort of idea propagated by Vivekananda and is definitely *not* traditional Advaita.



Presumably you agree that the problem of the aj~nAnI is ‘Self-ignorance’. What is needed to bring about enlightenment is Self-knowledge. For that, the only pramANa is shabda – namely shruti. samAdhi is *not* a pramANa!



Best wishes,

Dennis
