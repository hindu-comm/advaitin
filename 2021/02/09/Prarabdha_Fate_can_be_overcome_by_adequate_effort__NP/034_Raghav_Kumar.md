Namaste Dennis ji

  

Your article on manOnAsha was quite lucid.

  

I was however unable to fathom if you personally felt that even the mANDUkya kArikA or its commentary traditionally attributed to shankara had anything in them which, in your specific opinion, diverged from what one could perhaps call "baseline advaita" as represented by the sutra bhAShya, which is by definition the work of shri shankara.

  

The verses in advaita prakaraNa regarding contemplative practices are easy to fit in to a \*cognitive\* framework, for pratibandhaka nivRtti during the finer stages of GYAna prapti.

  

For example, the well-known kArikA, "laye sambodhayet chittam vixiptam shamayet punah, sakaShAyam vijAnIyAt samaprAptam na cAlayet" (one of Sri Swami Dayananda ji's favorites) is a concise summary of the entire journey of contemplation and is easily seen to be complementary to shravaNam done earlier or ongoing shravaNam. The point is that many "sitting" practices entailing arriving at a very sAttvika and sUxma avastha of mind are also a 'part and parcel' of the process of gaining cognitive insight . And not all sitting practices can be subsumed under karma and upAsanam. In other words, a person cannot be said to be doing some karma/upAsana merely because he is sitting still and facilitating and holding space for vedantic vRttis to arise in a steady flow. While many might employ such sitting practices 'seeking' some experience, those who have had exposure to shruti as a pramANam will still find such sitting still type of practices unreasonably effective in assimilating and embracing the cognitive transformation of Advaita GYAnam which is triggered by shravaNam etc.

  

Next, it is pertinent to note that the verses in VC speaking of nirvikalpaka samadhi etc., were not seen by Sri Chandrashekhara Bharati ji (who commented on VC) as constituting any divergence from the idea that GYAnam, free of pratibandhakas, is sufficient for the mukti.

  

There are certain texts like the yoga sUtras which explicitly avow a dualistic framework.

  

But the situation is different when it comes to texts like VC, the \*practice of nirvikalpa samadhi is easily placed within the cognitive framework\* of shravana-manana-nididhyAsanam.I do not remember reading anything in VC that militates against the idea of Rte-GYAnAnna muktiH.

  

The story of Swami Dayananda ji comes to mind. A student hears a joke from his teacher. But he does not get it. He later tries to think about jt but it still does not click. Much later, when he is \*quietly lounging\* in his garden and not thinking of anything in particular and his mind is uncluttered, things suddenly fall in place and he gets the joke and bursts out laughing.

  

"Getting" the joke is a cognitive event whose primary cause is listening to it. But the later processes of cogitation and \*quietness\* greatly facilitate it.

  

Most acharyas in the Advaita tradition who validated the VC practices of nirvikalpa samadhi (NS) in a nuanced way were quite aware of the primacy of shravaNam. And saw no incompatibility with the "intelligently sitting still" type of vedantic meditative practices for citta-vixepa-nivRtti and also importantly kaShAya-nivRtti.

  

The fact of a significant degree of ongoing or prior shravaNam (ie shruti pramANa based study), distinguishes it (NS of vedanta) from "plain" yogic asampraGYAta samAdhi practice unsupported by and independent of shAstra pramANa exposure from a Guru.

  

Om

Raghav
