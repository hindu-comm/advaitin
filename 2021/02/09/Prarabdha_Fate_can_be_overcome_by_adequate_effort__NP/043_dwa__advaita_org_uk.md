Dear Dilip-ji,



On the face of it your concern is perfectly reasonable. However, over the past 15+ years, via my website and blog, I have received hundreds of questions on many topics. These are from seekers with widely varying backgrounds and exposure to Advaita. For the past couple of years, I have been looking into the various confusions that they encounter and writing what will be a two-volume work analyzing these and clarifying the issues involved by reference back to prasthAna traya and Shankara.



Once it has been accepted that it was Shankara who consolidated and rationalized the teaching, and presented it in a consistent and logical manner, then one is obliged to judge what is said by modern teachers in the light of that understanding. I have no intention of trying to summarize what I have found here. (I have completed Volume 1 at around 120,000 words.) But, just as an example, take Vivekananda – one of the most respected modern teachers who clearly has to be regarded highly for promoting Advaita in the West and for the inauguration of all of the maTha-s around the world today. His understanding of Advaita was significantly influenced by Yoga, Christianity and science as well as Ramakrishna’s mysticism. He did not belong to any sampradAya. In addition to believing that knowledge was NOT sufficient to give enlightenment, he also did not respect the scriptures.



Ramana and Nisargadatta were brilliant teachers no doubt, but most of what we have now comes from translations of their dialogs. So we are at the mercy of their translator’s understanding of the language and subject. This is apart from the fact that dialogs were aimed at the questioner and *their* particular problems and level of understanding, not ours. Satsang style of teaching could never replace the traditional unfolding of scriptures over years of regular lessons.



Enough! As you can see, I feel quite strongly about this and could go on! If this topic is of specific interest, read my book ‘Enlightenment: the Path through the Jungle’.



Best wishes,

Dennis
