Namaskaram Sri Dennis,

  

I found your 5-part article to be an excellent review of various viewpoints, traditional and non-traditional, on the topic of free-will. Thank you very much for sharing! I also briefly read the Q&A and the older article.

  

Did I understand correctly that your view is:

(1) from an advaitic paramarthika (fundamental) perspective, there is no free-will, because the Atman is a non-doer; and

(2) from a dvaita vyavaharika (transactional) perspective, there is also no free-will because a jivatma does what it is supposed to do given its circumstances?

  

If I have understood correctly, then as you point out in your last Q&A, (2) may be in contrast to a traditional view (like the original post in this thread), which claims free-will exists. In this case, the only argument I can provide in defense of the traditional view is that it may be considered equivalent to the adhyaropa-apavada, where one starts with a false assumption (e.g., that the jivaatma has free will) but ultimately leads oneself to the truth that neither free-will nor fate exists.

  

From an advaitic paramarthika perspective, the statement that free-will exists is as invalid as the statement that free-will does not.

  

A few additional thoughts on this subject from the Prasthana Trayi that come to mind at the moment.

  

With regards to sruti-vaakya (statements from the Vedas), I am reminded of Briharanyaka Upanishad, which goes as (1.2.1):

  

*naiveha kiṁcanāgra āsīt, mṛtyunaivedam āvṛtam āsīt, aśanāyayā, aśanāyā hi mṛtyuḥ; tan mano'kuruta ātmanvī syām iti...  *

*  *

*There was nothing whatsoever here in the beginning. It was covered only by Death (Hiranyagarbha), or Hunger, for hunger is death. He created the mind, thinking, 'Let me have a mind.' ...*

  

This seems to indicate that Mrtyu “willed” for a mind and so it had a mind.

  

The “will” of the primal ignorance (mrtyu) is more explicitly stated as the sruti continues with the use of the words “so akamayata...” or “he desired...” (Briharanyaka Upanishad 1.2.4, 1.2.6, 1.2.7).

  

*so׳kāmayata dvitīyo ma ātmā jāyeteti, sa manasā vācam mithunaṁ samabhavad aśanāyā mṛtyuḥ...  *

*  *

*He desired, 'Let me have a second form’...*

  

*  *

*so׳kāmayata, bhūyasā yajñena bhūyo yajeyeti  *

*  *

*He desired, 'Let me sacrifice again with the great sacrifice. '...  *

  

*  *

*so׳kāmayata, medhyam ma idaṁ syāt, ātmanvy anena syām iti...  *

*  *

*He desired, 'Let this body of mine be fit for a sacrifice, and let me be embodied through this,' ...*  

  

  

So it may be argued on basis of the above sruti-vaakya that indeed it is desire (kama) of the primal ignorance (mrtyu) which leads to the duality of samsara and all its vyavahara.

  

Interestingly, you have also mentioned the word “kama” as part of the Sanskrit words for “free will” in your 2012 blog, which I quote below:

  

*A voluntary action, ‘acting of one’s own free will’ iskAmakAra. ThekArapart is the ‘doing’ andkAmameans ‘wish, desire, longing’. Even more specifically,sakAma– ‘acting of one’s own accord or free will’ –literally means ‘with desire’.*  

  

Another reference to the Prasthana Trayi that comes to mind is Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 3, Verses 36 and 37:

  

*atha kEna prayuktO-ayaM pApaM carati pUruShaH*

*anicchan-api vArShNEya balAdiva niyOjitaH*

*  *

*Arjuna asks: By what force does one commit sin? What is that that is dragging a person to act in a certain way?*

*  *

*SrI bhagavAn uvAca:*

*  *

*kAma EShaH krOdha EShaH rajO guNa samudbhavaH*

*mahASanO mahApApmA viddhi-EnaM-iha vairiNaM*

  

*Krishna says: The answer is kaama (desire). The answer is krodha (anger). These are born out of rajo-guna. It is continuously devouring everything, is the ultimate sinner. Understand this desire to be the enemy (shatru).*

  

These again point to one’s desire being the force behind samsara.

  

Both of the references above are, of course, from a vyavaharika point of view, because from a paramarthika perspective, the Atman is a non-doer and the entire samsara is like a mirage.
