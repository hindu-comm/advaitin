Namaste dear SKBji:

Let me begin with couple points on the purpose behind storytelling in India and it varies at different context. Grandparents tell stories to grandkids sometime just to spend time with them and at other times the stories are used to educate them with an implied moral from a story. In the context of Vedanta, I always used to admire the skills of Gurudev Chinmayanandaji who will not miss an opportunity to tell a story to make an important point – which always comes at the end of the story with a punch line. After successful completion of the story, Gurudev with smile on his face will always do a massage of his long beard!

Let me share here with one of his stories with the punch line. In a letter, Gurudev told the story of a father who pointed out to his son a man who was being hustled along the road between four soldiers. The father said: “Son, that one who is walking between the soldiers is a great criminal, and he is being taken to prison.”

After a few yards, the son and father came to the main road and saw a large crowd waiting on the sidewalk. The road had been cleared of traffic. Soon they saw a procession of soldiers on motorcycles, followed by the President of the country riding inside a luxurious limousine, which was protected on all sides by many soldiers.

The boy cried out ,”Daddy, there goes the biggest criminal!” The father hushed the child as he lovingly explained that they were seeing not a criminal but the President. As the punch line Gurudev elaborated further: Both of them, the criminal and the President, have soldiers around them. A man is a prisoner when he is under the command of the soldiers. But when the soldiers are under his command, he is the President or king himself.

Similarly, we all may have our own house, family and possessions. It is up to us whether we want to become their prisoner or we if want control over them! A quick lesson for all of us on how to lead our life.

I am repeating this story and I am hoping that you understood. When you narrated a story after stating that “ In the vyavaharika world, free will does exist.” Honestly, I couldn’t find any connection between that story and the existence of free-will. Dennisji was not questioning you about the story but he politely informed you that the story had no relevance to the existence of fate or free-will.

Story telling is a powerful technique and when it is properly employed, it can certainly bring new insights and if it is stated without punchline, it has high potential to bring more confusion than enlightenment!

Please understand that I am writing this not as a criticism but the purpose is remind us to be more careful in what we want to say.

With warm regards,

Ram Chandran
