Let's be honest. Free will most certainly does not exist. But the motions of deliberation & decision and the theater of pretending to make decisions is itself compelled by God.

  

As the Gita says quite clearly -- after Krishna urgesArjuna to fight, then tells him to make his own decision...

  

18.59-61:

  

*If, filled with egoism, you think "I shall not fight," you resolvewill be in vain; your own material nature will compel you. What you wish not to do, throughdelusion, you shall do that against your will,Arjuna, bound by your own karma, born of your own material nature. The Lord abides in the hearts of all beings,Arjuna, causing all beings to revolve, by the power of illusion, as if fixed on a machine."*

  

That said, at the vyavharika"level" one is doomed to FEEL as if it there is free will. But if the true import of the lack of free will is felt, then total surrender follows automatically. If one objects that free will does exist, then one should inquire who is the possessor of it. That is the only utility of the notion.
