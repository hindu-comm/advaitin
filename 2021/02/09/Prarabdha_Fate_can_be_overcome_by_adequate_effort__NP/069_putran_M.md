Namaskaram,

  

I do not understand what exactly people are objecting to. In "Free will", if the objection is that we do not exercise will-power that is actually free (unlimited), then yes, our capacity to act is constrained by the limitations of body-mind and impelled by the force of our svadharma, vasanas etc.

  

But if the idea is that we have no will-power and whatever happens is entirely robotically pre-destined, this would contradict the quotations of the acharya (here and in the "Life - itscomponents" post). In the dualistic standpoint, the Self is known as endowed with maya-shakti, the power to "will"/determine/manifest this duality. In the context of the jiva, that is realized as its individual power to know, decide/desire and act, thereby to create/dream/project (in part) the next instant of manifestation.

  

BTW can someone tell me if the traditional Vedantin admits the characterization of the jiva's shakti as iccha-, jnana-, kriya-shakti? I understand this is standard terminology in shaiva schools. (?)
