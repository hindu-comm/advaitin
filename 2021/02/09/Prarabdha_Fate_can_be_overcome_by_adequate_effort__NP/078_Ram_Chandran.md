Namaste dear Dennisji:

If you look for direct reference with respect to your question, it may be difficult (though not impossible) to find exact quote. But a careful look back into the Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 2, verse 47 describing Karma Yoga, it does provide the answer to your question.

This is one of the most often quoted verses of Gita and it has inspired many persons. Several Acharyas and authors of Book of Gita have also interpreted this verse to get its full essence. Both Pujya Chinmayanandaji and Pujya Dayananda Saraswati have discussed this verse in greater details. Those who have heard or read either one of their presentation were able to appreciate the essence of Karma Yoga. Let me share my understanding of the essence with you all. If we notice carefully that Karma Yoga provides the correct platform to understand the role Prarabdha and Purushartha whenever we undertake any Karma.

karmaNi eva adhikaaraste maa phaleshu gadaachana  
maa karma phala hetuH bhuH maa sanghaH astu akarmaNi  
  
We only have the "right" to conduct the action and certainly we have no control over the results of the action. We should avoid using the "results" as the motivating force of our action and free our attachment to inaction. The facts will become crisp and clear if we make all efforts to understand this verse carefully. We are part of the Nature and we should understand our rights' and our limitations.' Nature has given us the skill and power to conduct an action of our choice. We can use our skills to set up the goals for the action and should proceed to apply all our energy and skill to accomplish those goals. Our intelligence, energy and knowledge do not give us the authority over the results of the action. This human limitation is also a fact of life and we have no choice other than to accept the outcome. The results can be more, equal, less or even opposite to our expectations (goals). We have to accept the unwritten fact of life that the results of any action are influenced both by Prarabdha and Purushartha.   
  
Any expression of expectation (prejudging the results) before completing an action develops an attitude that binds the action to the results. Consequently, the doer changes the focus from action to the result (as Arjun did before fighting the war). In other words, our Purushartha gets diminished, diverts our full attention. This diversion makes us to be inactive, ineffective and confused. Lord Krishna in Bhagavad Gita emphasizes to Arjun that he has no rights (authority) over the consequences. Implicitly the Lord points out that the Lord only has the authority over the results!

The views of Karma Yoga expressed in this verse are certainly no different from what is emphasized in modern management institutions.
Also, successful political leaders, scientists and sports’ coaches also emphasize the same views. But the message of Gita is much more profound and precise. According to Gita, the humans have the "rights" on their action (Karma) but ‘right' comes along with greater responsibility to maintain Dharma. The doer has the freedom over his/her Karma but he or she is expected to follow the Dharma established by his/her own mind.

It is very difficult (impossible) to describe all the noble things that both these stalwarts of Vedanta (Swami Chinmayananda and Swami Dayananda) emphasized during their discourses and writings. If we pursue to remember this verse at least once a day or once a week, we will likely be protected from anxieties and excitements. Pujya Gurudev used to advice all of us to Plug our EAR - E stands for Excitement for the Present; A for Anxiety about the Future and R for the Regrets of the Past. Learn from the Past and Plan for the Future, but Do not Torture the Mind with Past Regrets or Future Anxieties. Act in the Present with a Serene Mind. Be a Cool Dude!

With my warm regards,

Ram Chandran

Note: Our knowledge of Prarabdha (fate) and Purushartha (self-efforts/freewill) along with the importance of Karma Yoga can help us to make appropriate adjustments in dealing with challenging situations. We know the fact that any Karma (action) that we undertake includes actors other than us and the cooperation and level of their participation depend on our Prarabdha and Purushartha. Any action requires many known actors along with unknown number of invisible actors.
