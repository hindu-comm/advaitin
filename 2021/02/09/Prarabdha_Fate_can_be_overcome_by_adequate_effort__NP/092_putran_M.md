We are talking about karma and will-power as if they are two in the same box. That does not appear to me the case.

  

We know of karma-karmaphala beyond ordinary pratyaksha from other pramana (shastra), then use auxiliary logic and reasoning to support the same.

  

Will power on the other hand is directly affirmed in our individual self-identification as jivas. We do not need outside pramana to posit our knowledge; it is rather universal knowledge. Rather one has to try and use other pramanas and logic to negatetheir inner conviction of will-power.

  

So one can arguably avoid karma-theory in its fuller traditional sense; but the essence of free-will isnot negatable so long as there is jiva-identification. It comes in the package of duality.

  

There is a deeper reasoning for why advaitins affirm maya-shakti in Ishvara (that in the jiva-context is will-power). The Self is manifest. We find both a sense of Order and of the play of jiva's will in this manifestation. Pratyaksha affirms both of these in some competing sense; it is neither absolutely random (free) nor absolutely predictable. So knowledge derived from pratyaksha affirms the traditional vyavaharika position (barring the shastra-based extensions) that the world is an interplay of Order and free-will. If someone seeks to negate one side of the will-Order equation and retain the other as total, then they are likely relying on other presumed pramanas.

  

Consider the position that there is no will and manifestation is entirely deterministic (should we have complete knowledge of the past). What is the pramana that may support such a claim? Is it valid postulation or just a subjectivebelief? Etc. That itself seems ambiguous.

  

The advaitin actually uses shastra pramana to negate the other side of this coin. That Order is mithya and everything is Ishvara (or play of maya). If the cause for manifestation is *really* (inscribed in) the past state of identification, then that identification has to be a real transformation or conditioning imposing on the Self (that it must manifest thus in future). Rather for the advaitin, it is Ajnana, Ignorance, that the Self identifies with (or is identified with) its projection and imagined therein that it has to manifest that way or this way, that what is to come is because of what is now or before. Thus we say that even Ishvara's karma-karmaphala Order or projection of the world is only for the sake of the jivas figuring out this state of ajnana. Ok that would be a tradition oriented explanation but the idea is that everything is manifestation of shakti and the only causal basis for the flow of existence is Ajnana, the limiting adjuncts of identification of jivas! If we learn of some predictable Order, then we think that is Ishvara anugraha (and not Ishvara is a computer).

  

And in the case of the jiva, the Self through the upadhi of mind is self-aware though still subject to the ignorance of identification: there is a competition where the ajnani seeks to manifest his purnatvamthrough the realm of ignorance, seeking perfection in manifestation that will make him complete. But whatever be his decision due to ignorance of identifications and inherent search for knowledge, the Cause for his manifestation is himSelf; all other things like past memories, etc. are indicative of the basis of his ignorance but are not directly causal: the Self is responsible for its manifestation.

  

The Self is the sole Reality, and any explanation of its manifestation has only the Self as the direct cause. Everything else is apparent cause. Both cause-effect Order and exercise of will-power are (identifications of) the determination (will, maya) of the Self (Ishvara) manifesting as and in jagat and jiva.
