Some quotations from Vaitathya-Prakarana of Gaudapada Karika and Shankara Bhashya(Sw Gambhirananda translation). I am quoting selectively, just to give some scriptural support; so one may have to read the fuller text or across scriptures for clarification.

  

16\. First He imagines the individual \[jiva\] and then He imagines the different objects, external and mental...  \[probably "first ... then" is not literally timewise; perhaps as primary-secondary/dependent\]

  

Bhashya: He first imagines the individual ... After that, for his sake, He (the Lord) imagines different objects ... dividing them into action, instruments, and results... what is the reason for that imagination?... The individual that is imagined by (the Lord) Himself and is himself capable of imagination, gets a memory ... In this way He imagines diversely the things, both external and mental to be mutually the causes and effects. ... the imagining of individuality is the root of all imaginations.

  

12\. The self-effulgent Self imagines Itself through Itself by the power of Its own Maya. The Self itself cognizes the objects...

  

13\. The Lord manifests diversely the mundane things existing in the mind. Turning the mind out-ward, He creates the well-defined things (as well as the undefined things). Thus does the Lord imagine.

  

14\. Things that exist internally as long as the thought lasts and things that are externally related to two points of time, are all imaginations. Their distinction is not caused by anything else.

  

19\. (This Self) is imagined to be the infinite objects like Prana etc. This is the Maya of that self-effulgent One, by which He Himself is deluded. (Bhashya: ... by which He Himself seems to have become influenced like a man under delusion..)
