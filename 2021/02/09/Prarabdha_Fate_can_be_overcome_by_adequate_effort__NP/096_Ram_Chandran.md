Namaste:

The quote by [Abhinava Vidyatirtha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhinava_Vidyatirtha)Mahaswami of Sringeri Mutt is profound along with cautiously implied caveats: “Prarabdha or fate cannot be more powerful than one's will because fate itself is a creation of one's will. What one has brought about due to exercise of one's will in past births can certainly be overcome by exercise of will power in the present birth***provided one makes adequate effort.”***

Whenever a message comes from a pious Acharya with the spiritual wisdom greater than us we should respect, listen and comprehend. Through this thread we had interactive discussions by many learned scholars which enabled us to get more insights. Few discussants tried to redirect our focus to the subject on ‘fate and freewill!’ Fortunately we were able to stop such diversions at the right moments.

A good starting point to understand implied message from the quotation is to explore the teachings from the sages and saints of the Upanishads.
In the Upanishads, we have been cautioned not to look for an external judge to sanction rewards and punishments for all our actions. We have been told that human beings become the architect of their own spiritual fortunes and they were no longer subject to chance or the will of God. The Brihadaranyaka Upanishad states that the actions of the human beings only will determine the outcome. The doer of good becomes good; the doer of evil becomes evil. One becomes virtuous by virtuous action and sinful by sinful action. As is one’s desire so will be his/her resolve and the deed. It is hinted that everyone goes through the same natural process of getting rewards determined by the deeds. 

Both Ramayana and Mahabharat were important parts of understanding life lessons and we can certainly gain more insights on Acharya’s quotation.
It may be useful to narrate this interesting Episode from Mahabharat in the present context. This episode describes the attempt by Gandhari to save her son Dhryodhan from Death by using the boon she got from Lord Shiva. At the same time, Bhima had taken a vow to kill Dhryodhan when he ordered his brother Dhuchadanan to disrobe Draupadi in front of everyone present. If either one of them had to succeed then the other one had to fail! The situation became more complicated due to the fact that two individuals have simultaneously wanted to succeed! Additionally, Dhryodhan always wanted that he should be successful. Everyone certainly made the best possible efforts to succeed. But it seemed that the efforts became adequate only for Bhima! It could be quite conceivable to argue that destiny was responsible for the death of Dhryodhan. It is also quite feasible for us to conclude that Gandhari and Dhryodhan did not make adequate efforts and hence they failed.

It may be useful to revisit the episode of Mahabharat war to see what happened on 17th and 18th day. On the 17th day Karna was killed by Arjun. Dhryodhan knowing this was in depression was very upset.
Gandhari the very pious mother of Dhryodhan decided to protect her son who was going to fight Bhima using Gada (Mace). Gandhari being an ardent devotee of Lord Shiva through long penance had received a special boon. With this boon, anyone she would see when she removed her blindfold, the entire body seen by her gets transferred to unbreakable.
Gandhari thought by using this boon, she would be able to protect Dhryodhan from death during the fight with Bhima. So she informed Dhryodhan that he should come and see her immediately after taking a bath without wearing any clothes. She thought when Dhryodhan comes and stand in front of her naked, she will take off the blindfold and protect his body by her eyes with the special power. The power of her eyes will transform Dhryodhan’s entire body from head to toe unbreakably strong.

Gandhari sent a message to Dhryodhan to come before her fully naked immediately after taking his bath in the river. He obeyed his mother’s advice, took his bath and walked back slowly towards her residence. At that time Lord Krishna met him on the way and made a teasing smile which irritated Dhryodhan. Dhryodhan was uncomfortable seeing Lord Krishna and asked him why He smiled. Krishna responded back saying that he couldn’t control laughing because he has never seen any Grownup to walk fully naked in either day or night. Dhryodhan felt embarrassed and defended his behavior by telling that he just obeyed his blind mother’s wishes to come naked. Krishna argued that only a little child would go and see its mother naked and never a grownup son! Dhryodhan felt ashamed and quickly used some leaves to cover below the thigh. When Dhryodhan saw his mother and informed his arrival, Gandhari removed the blindfold and glanced through his body with her powerful vision. She was quite disappointed to see the Dhryodhan’s body covered with the leaves below the thigh. In other words, Gandhari failed to protect Dhryodhan’s body below the thigh. Gandhari with tears scolded Duryodhan and asked why he did not come fully naked. She heard from Dhryodhan that Lord Krishna was responsible for this mishap and she was very upset. After few moments Gandhari also realized that besides her, others were acting to achieve alternative goals and she had no authority to decide the final outcome!

The Moral of the story is profound and we must understand that there multiple number of actors that join with our action knowingly and unknowingly. All that we could do is to conduct our actions with responsibility and at the same time learn to accept the outcome whether we like or dislike. We do not have any control or authority over other actors who play their roles. All that we could do is to act with our best possible efforts.

It is impossible for anyone to find out the exact cause for the death of Dhryodhan or whether it was preventable or not! At the vyavaharika level all we could do is to speculate whatever we perceive! Truth only will be known only at the Paramarthika level!!
