> 1\. Bhava and Abhava are to be understood only with reference to the
> Satta. There cannot be absolute Abhava at all (except in Shunyavada)
> \[ since Brahman is Existence.\] If that is true then Maya is
> Bhavarupa only in Vyaharika satta and Abhava rupa in parmarthika
> satta.{i think you have indicated this in your Blog\] So could one
> then conclude without referring to satta, that it is Bhavarupa only by
> sruthi references? \[My own opinion:*I believe that Sruthi stands as
> the bridge between the two satta and hence all sruthi Vakhya have to
> be understood and interpreted only in reference to the context.\]*

  

Advaitins hold Maya to be bhavarupa based on inferences derived from Shruti passages. For example, the Shvetashvatara says: bhUyashcAnte vishvamAyA-nivrittiH. This shows that Maya does not have absolute sattA like Brahman. Yet it is also not shUnya like sky-flower. It is stated to get 'destroyed/negated'. Upon a person gaining the Brahma jnanam, he realizes that maya and its effects are naught in all three periods of time. Sureshwaracharya'sverse is there for this: tattvamasyaadi vaakyottha....'

> 2, "The Knowledge of the True Nature of the Self, Brahman (avidya
> absence)" and " The experiencing of freedom from the doer-enjoyer
> identification (adhyasa absence)" are not two things - if we postulate
> them as two things then 'which leads which' dosha will arise.
>
>   
>
> I believe that both will happen simultaneously. If Snake vision is
> destroyed rope is self-revealed and if rope is perceived through the
> snake vison then snake is not there \[for all purpose\] . The first
> part is Jagat Mithya and second is Atma sathyam. Snake and Rope are
> not two things - Seeing the Snake different from the Rope is Avidhya

  

Yes, seeing the unreal snake as separate from the real rope is indeed avidya. When the rope-knowledge arises, the effect of the rope-ignorance, the illusory snake, will no longer be there, along with the fear, etc. caused by such snake-perception. This is stated in the shloka I referred to in the earlier paragraph.
