Namaskaram,

  

In the light of recent discussions on will, there are two imageries depicting the two approaches within advaita tradition to "knowing sathya of/underlying vyavaharika". I want to post this for the sake of beginners who are more confused than myself.

  

One is Shiva lying still and Shakti/Kali dancing on Him. Shiva is the unchanging Self, akartha, the substratum, while all creation, duality, change is the show attributed to maya, shakti, prarkriti, Mind. Duality is the play of Maya on the nondual substratum Brahman.

  

The Second is Nataraja or Shivashakti. The Self alone is Reality; any identification (of Self) in dualistic terms corresponds with the identification of Self as Ishvara or Nataraja, the One whose dance (imagination, projection) is the manifest creation. Here Maya is realized as the intrinsic shakti of Ishvara (yes, that's will-power in the jiva-context). Nirguna Brahman is beyond duality-based referencing.

  

(Unless I am mistaken, the first approach resembles Sankhya which posited a vyavaharikaduality between purusha and prakriti and said prakriti acts in the still-presence of purusha (like iron filings move in presence of magnet); and Vedanta added: It is Ishvara.)

  

Both approaches have scriptural support (for example, the quotes from Mandukya Up karika/bhashya I gave in other post (for "the Lord"); and references in BG in Chp 3 (for "doer is Prakriti, not Self")). Discussions usually revolve around whether the philosophical position should be context-sensitive lest we commit some type of category-error, or whether these are just prakriyas (adhyaropa-apavada) to aid in our upasana and the philosophical difference is ultimately immaterial.
