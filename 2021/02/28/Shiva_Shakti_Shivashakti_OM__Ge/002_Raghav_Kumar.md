Namaste Putran ji

  

Thank you for your germane post. I was inclined to think along similar lines.

  

In sanskrit there is no exact word for free will. IcchA shakti is accepted in the empirical realm.

  

Denying the existence of an entity equivalent to free will, even while accepting that other entities like objects, events etc., of the dualistic world exist, is logically untenable.

  

As you (as I understand) pointed out, we can either own up agency (jiva has iccha shakti) or transfer agency to the samaShTi (Ishvara has samagram aishvaryam - I.e., iccha shakti at the cosmic level) .

  

Once we accept that events happen and different entities exist, that presumes that mAyA shakti with its projecting power had already started operating - the very existence of a dualistic empirical realm implicitly assumes Ishvara's exercise of iccha shakti . Such operation necessitates an intelligent agent, Ishvara as given in shruti sentences like "tadaikSahta", " so'kAmayata" etc. Such sAkShyam (intelligent witnessing) by a conscious principle is not different from exercise of ichhashakti etc by that Ishvara (as shakti and shaktimat have tAdAtmya. The power and wielder of the power are inseparable).

  

So, denying free will or some degree of agency to the jiva while still holding that things happen, is tantamount to just kicking the can down the road and assigning it (free will or icchA shakti) to Ishvara.

  

The other option is to resolve all entities to their substratum which is pAramArthika I.e., Brahman.

  

We cannot say, 'multiple entities exist, and events happen in the empirical realm, but we are all like mind-body automata, devoid of any agency.'

  

Om

Raghav
