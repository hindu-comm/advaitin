I was trying to summarize down some of these points as well as add some more thoughts forfuturediscussion, but they were a bit jumbled up. Decided to note down in short-form; need not discuss now.

  

1\. What is pramana for knowledge of free-will or will-power? Direct? What is pramana for its negation?

2\. Vritti-based explanation possible?, or is vritti-analysis wrong direction intrying to explain will-power of Self?

3\. Science experiments; their conclusions, the question of the pramana-validity for their conclusions.

4\. Where, how and if shastra must be used to know of will (or its negation)?

5\. Slipping into Sankhya? The Self affirmed in jiva or world, and then separated into atma and 'automata'.

6\. Ways of "kicking can down the road" to Ishvara that may be acceptable to those anti-will in jiva but ok in Ishvara.

  

a\. Some may say we should not associate will-power to the Self in its identification as a particular jiva, but they stilladmit that the power of will is intrinsic to Ishvara: He is self-determining Reality and not a robot in operation. So He decides and impels each body-mind to action, and that is reflected (post-Decision or simultaneous) in the jiva's body-mind as "i decide thus". In a dream for instance, it is not each character who acts or decides but the one Dreamer who plays out all the roles.

b\. A variant of this: itcan be said that in the dream context, the (Self identified with the) character is still taking the decision (on account of the ajnana of/in such identification), however the locus for the deciding-power cannot be isolated in or traced to the character: it is of the Dreamer manifesting the entire dream.

c\. The point is that these understandings don't negate the power of shakti in Him. Such explanation may also comfort those who want to avoid contradiction ofwill in science-experiments that track body-mind ordering and claim to negate the validity of the jiva's free-will. (But this should only bother us if we establish that such methods of science have pramana-validity.)

  

7\. The special shastra-point of advaita: vivarta vs parinama, ajnana vs bondage. "If the cause for manifestation is*really*(inscribed in) the past state of identification, then that identification has to be a real transformation or conditioning imposing on the Self (that it must manifest thus in future). Rather for the advaitin, it is Ajnana, Ignorance, that the Self identifies with (or is identified with) its projection and imagined therein that it has to manifest that way or this way, that what is to come is because of what is now or before... The Self is the sole Reality, and any explanation of its manifestation has only the Self as the direct cause. Everything else is apparent cause. Both cause-effect Order and exercise of will-power are (identifications of) the determination (will, maya) of the Self (Ishvara) manifesting as and in jagat and jiva. "
