A few thoughts on what you shared...  

The mahApUrvapaxa for advaita today would indeed be the philosophy/worldview driving modern science. All the other older darshanas have been sufficiently addressed or subsumed by earlier advaita acharyas and their works.

  

It cannot be said (as yet) that there is some kind of complementarity between the scientific and vedic paradigms. Or that they deal with different domains - one the objective and the other the subjective domain. Many vedantins fondly believe it to be so but such bonhomie (or one-sided love) is hardly reciprocated by any significant numbers of scientist or scientific bodies!. Not many scientists would countenance any of the claims of the vedas or vedanta, however non-dogmatic and reasonable they may sound to those who understand and accept the latter. No scientist would meekly say that "ok, the vedas deal with non-falsifiable truths about survival, ishvara, karma etc., which don't conflict with the claims and conclusions of science, so we have nothing to say against such claims." On the contrary, massively funded research is only along the lines of scientific materialism with the underlying assumptions of manipulating the 7.5 billion mind-body automata (people?). No reference or relevance to dharma leave alone moXa is encouraged or nurtured.

  

The Abhinava charvakas (scientific materialists) are as complementary to vedanta as the older charvakas are, i.e., there is zero 'complementarity'. For that very reason, Sri Shankara no where in his bhAShyas does any maNDanam (respect/praise) of the charvaka siddhanta the way he praises the sAMkhya and even Buddhist doctrines. So the relationship between vedanta and scientific materialism/reductionism is one of conflict.

  

One of the challenges in addressing scientific dismissal of the vedic paradigm is that the former does not have a clearly defined framework. Broadly, it could be labelled scientific realism/materialism or logical positivism perhaps. But abhinava charvaka matam (the philosophy behind modern science) is quite slimy and evades being pinned down to any conclusive statements or assertions.

  

The claims of quantum mechanics have no doubt dealt a body blow to the cocksureness and premature triumphalism of 19th century Newtonian mechanics. And Gödel with his exposé of the intractable limitations of any axiomatic system, are all chipping away at the edifice of scientific materialism. This has provided some relief to advance some of the truth claims of traditional knowledge systems. But there is still a long way to go before any degree of sanity is back.
