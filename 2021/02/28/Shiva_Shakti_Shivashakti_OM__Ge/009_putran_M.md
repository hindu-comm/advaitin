Namaskaram Raghav-ji,

  

These are nice theories and observations that could support in part what we know via shastra.

  

Logic can be used to support pramana or to oppose; we are not in conflict with logic itself as a mode for enlightening knowledge. But if logic is treated as a pramana within a system of knowledge and our system does not accept it as fundamentalpramana for certain aloukika jnana, then there will be potential for conflict between the followers of these systems due to their different ways of employing logic.

  

In a similar sense, it is not that Vedanta is in conflict with science but that we say that certain conclusions of Vedanta (or shastra) are outside the scope of scientificmethods. So when Scientists speculate "Yes" or "No" on topics outside the range of their methods, we have a conflict of mutual incoherence or incompatibility between our systems of knowledge: we think they lack Pramana foundations ("slimy" as you said) and they think we are religious believers of pseudo-pramanas. And amusingly at the practical levels, they talk with certitude and conviction (even while calling everything "Theory") and we are plagued with doubt and resistance towards shastra (even while claiming it our pramana).

  

I liked reading the second article. At the end of Section 4, he says: "...unless we assume an infinite regression of biofriendly universes being produced by intelligent life, then we must assume that biofriendliness was at some point generated by a nonintelligent process."

  

Whatever be the assumptions that led him to this 'disclaimer', in the Vedanta world-view, the jiva is anaadi; so technically there is an infinite regression of 'biofriendly' universes.

  

With regard to the entropy-theory, I don't know much about it but I could not tell how they were accounting for attractive forces like gravity or magnetism, which has been regarded as the Ordering (non-intelligent) force in the mechanistic universe.
