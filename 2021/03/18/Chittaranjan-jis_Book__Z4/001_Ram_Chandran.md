*5.0 out of 5 stars*FANTASTIC BOOK

Reviewed in India on January 16, 2020

For anyone even slightly interested in philosophy and science, this is a book which will break all barriers. Western philosophy considers itself a progeny of Greek philosophy. Indian philosophy is more ancient than even the Greeks. Unfortunately, the persisting colonial consciousness prevents most educated Indians from even knowing that there are six very advanced schools of Indian philosophy which thought about every single existential question. Very few Western authors bothered to look East and realise that we had something far richer to offer. Unfortunately, Indian traditional scholars never bothered to look at the Western thought and engage with them in dialogues and debates. The discourse has been extremely one-sided thanks to power structures on the one hand and the disinterest of the traditional world on the other.

Chittaranjan Naik, in this milieu, comes up with a stunner of a book based completely on the Indian philosophical systems to reject some well-established paradigms in Western science and philosophy. In a western country, this book would have received a wide acclaim and a wide coverage for the sheer enormity of thought and the radical viewpoints. Radical, at least for the modern world. The standard thought of perception is that light falls on an object, then travels through the eyes, and falls on the retina. Neural impulses travel thence to the brain where the an image representing the object undergoes a reconstruction. In this standard paradigm, the world outside is a representation as constructed by the brain. What exists outside in reality is unknown, what is known is what the brain recreates. This thought deeply ingrains into all sciences and philosophy. Naik questions this. The sentient observer is inside, and the light from this goes outside through the layers of insentience in the form of sleep, mind, and the body to fall on the object and by contact, perceives the outside subject. It is a perception from inside out rather than outside in. In this paradigm, what one perceives as the reality is ‘Natural Reality’ in the words of Naik. It is not a constructed reality. The explanations, consequences, and the experiments to confirm this form the bulk of the wonderful book.  
It is in a simple language and anyone can possibly understand the concepts. And the amazing thing is that all these thoughts formed the basis of Indian philosophical systems for thousands of years never undergoing any modification. The reality has another explanation, which does away with the logical absurdities and inconsistencies of the present paradigm, especially when dealing with the finite velocity of light and images in the mirror. A book worth its weight in gold.
