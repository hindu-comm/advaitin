*I do have a question for Chitta-ji regarding the proposed Simultaneity Experiment. Rather than create a powerful nuclear explosion in space, can we not use the ever available nuclear reactor, the sun? What if we watch the sunrise over the horizon with a video camera to be activated the moment we see the sun peek over the horizon with our eyes? Will the camera show a delay in recording the image since light must travel to the lens before the camera can record the event?  *

  

According to the Vedantic theory of perception, when we watch the sunrise, we would be seeing the sun as it exists at that moment. But light takes (approx.) 8 minutes to travel from the sun to the earth; therefore a camera should detect the light 8 minutes after it is seen by the human observer. If I am not mistaken, this is the reasoning behind your question. Please correct me if i am mistaken.

  

The above reasoning seems plausible but it is missing out one fact -
light does not start emanating from the sun only after we start seeing the rising sun. Light would have been streaming out from the sun even before that moment. When we first see the rising sun, some of the light which has been streaming out from the sun will be near the sun, some of the light will be midway between the sun and earth, some of the light will be just behind the horizon on the earth. When the earth moves out of the way of the light which is just behind the horizon, that light will reach the video camera in a fraction of a second. If the horizon is 25 miles away, that light will reach the camera in .000134 seconds. Thus, the difference in time between the first sighting of the rising sun by the human eye and the first detecting of the light by the video camera will be only 0.000134 seconds. This time difference is too small for conducting the experiment as the time-difference can be masked by measurement errors caused by human latency when they record the first sighting of the rising sun.
