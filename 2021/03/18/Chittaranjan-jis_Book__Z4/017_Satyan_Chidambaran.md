Namaste Chitta-ji,

  

I assume that what you are saying about seeing applies to hearing as well for VP doesn’t make any distinction in the workings for seeing and hearing (where the sense organ reaches out to the sense object, but not so for the case of touching, smelling and tasting where the sense object has to reach the sense organ). This is explicitly stated in the last para of Pratyaksha PrakaraNam of VP. (“For the sense of hearing too being, like the sense of sight, finite, there is the possibility of going out to the locality of the drum, etc.”)

  

Given this, I would like to hear your thoughts on how you would respond to a scientist who raises the following objections to the VP theory perception.

  

1\) if my sense of seeing and hearing are both instantly enveloping the lightning bolt, why do I hear the thunder after some delay?

  

2\) if my sense of seeing instantly envelops a distant star, but the instrument beside me detects its light, would I not see the star if it had exploded long ago but light from it is still reaching the instrument? Science tells us that many stars in the sky we still see are probably no longer there and we are just seeing the light emitted from before they died. (I suppose this has some overlap with Dennis-ji’s question)

  

3\) when a car sounding its horn at a uniform frequency is moving away from me versus moving towards me, and my sense of hearing is instantly enveloping the car, why is that I hear a Doppler shifted sound of a higher frequency in the latter case and lower frequency in the former case and an instrument that responds to sound waves besides me also confirms the same frequencies that I hear.

  

Another related point is that wouldn’t it be more practical in terms of cost and feasibility to propose the simultaneity experiment with sound instead of light given that speed of sound is much much smaller? Perhaps replace the nuclear explosion in outer space with something that produces a loud bang in the earth’s atmosphere and then measure whether a sentient observer hears it quicker than the instrument?

  

Regards,

—Satyan
