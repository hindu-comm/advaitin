*I assume that what you are saying about seeing applies to hearing as well for VP doesn’t make any distinction in the workings for seeing and hearing (where the sense organ reaches out to the sense object, but not so for the case of touching, smelling and tasting where the sense object has to reach the sense organ). This is explicitly stated in the last para of Pratyaksha PrakaraNam of VP. (“For the sense of hearing too being, like the sense of sight, finite, there is the possibility of going out to the locality of the drum, etc.”)*  

  

You are right Sir, in the cases of both seeing and hearing, the sense organs reach out to their respective objects in space (and not so in the case of touching, smelling and tasting). I have considered them accordingly in my book.

  

  

*1) if my sense of seeing and hearing are both instantly enveloping the lightning bolt, why do I hear the thunder after some delay?*

  

I have provided the explanation for the time difference between the sighting of the lightning and the hearing of its sound in the Annexure to the second part of the book titled 'Paradigm Shift and the Incommensurability Problem: the Case of Perception of Lightning'.

  

Briefly, it may be explained as follows. In the case of visual perception of the lightning, what we see is the visual object, the lightning. But in the case of auditory perception, there are two objects to be considered: the sound of the lightning itself and the sound generated by the surrounding air to which the lightning has imparted vibration. In the book, I have explained that the perceptual range for hearing is limited as compared to the perceptual range for visual perception. So, what happens in the case of lightning is that the sound of the original lightning itself is not heard but it is the vibrating air which is travelling through the air that gets heard and it is heard after a time delay because it will be heard only when it comes within the range of hearing. Once the air-vibrations caused by the lightning comes within the range of hearing, it will be heard instantly since the subtle sense organ 'ear' then makes instantaneous contact with that part of the vibrating air.

  

  

*3) when a car sounding its horn at a uniform frequency is moving away from me versus moving towards me, and my sense of hearing is instantly enveloping the car, why is that I hear a Doppler shifted sound of a higher frequency in the latter case and lower frequency in the former case and an instrument that responds to sound waves besides me also confirms the same frequencies that I hear.*

  

As explained, in the case of hearing, we hear the sound that has been imparted to the air in addition to the sound of the original object (if the original object is within the range of hearing). The Doppler effect will take effect since the frequency of the column of vibrating air will appear to be greater or lesser depending on the relative speed between the observer and the column of vibrating air.

  

  

*Another related point is that wouldn’t it be more practical in terms of cost and feasibility to propose the simultaneity experiment with sound instead of light given that speed of sound is much much smaller? Perhaps replace the nuclear explosion in outer space with something that produces a loud bang in the earth’s atmosphere and then measure whether a sentient observer hears it quicker than the instrument?*

  

In the case of sound, there will always be two objects that would be sounding, the object that originally generated the sound and the air to which the sound gets transmitted, and it will not be possible to distinguish between them; so the experiment would not be feasible.
