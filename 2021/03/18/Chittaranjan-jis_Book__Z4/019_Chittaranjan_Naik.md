*2) if my sense of seeing instantly envelops a distant star, but the instrument beside me detects its light, would I not see the star if it had exploded long ago but light from it is still reaching the instrument?Science tells us that many stars in the sky we still see are probably no longer there and we are just seeing the light emitted from before they died. (I suppose this has some overlap with Dennis-ji’s question)*

  

How this is interpreted depends on which theory of perception you take as being true. I would say that since the star is being seen, it has not been destroyed and that the claim of science that it has been destroyed is wrong and that the claim is theory laden by its own explanatory framework. According to contemporary philosophy (see Thomas Kuhn and Feyerabend, for example) theory-ladenness is unavoidable and there is no such thing as absolute truth regarding any matter; the theory that has a better explanatory force is what prevails. Contemporary philosophy is constrained to speak in this manner because it does not have a **culture of pramanas.** I believe that it is for us who have a commitment to the Indian tradition to strive to establish the culture of pramanas once again in the contemporary world. This is how I look at it.
