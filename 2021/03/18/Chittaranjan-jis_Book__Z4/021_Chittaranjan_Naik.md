Namaste Sri Dennis-ji,

  

*why, then, are we not able to hear sounds generated in a vacuum? E.g. the ringing of a bell inside a jar disappears when the air is evacuated. Why does the ear sense not go into the jar to hear the ‘original’ sound, when the jar is standing on the table in front of us?*  
  

For us to perceive an object there should not be any obstruction to the sense organ reaching out to the object. For example, I can't see the chair in the next room because the walls of the room are an obstruction to my sense organ of vision going out to the chair in the next room. Similarly, in the case of the bell in the jar, the walls of the jar are an obstruction to my subtle sense organ, ear, reaching out to the bell which is making the sound. In normal circumstances the vibration of the bell transmits vibration to the air within the jar which then transmits the vibration to the walls of the jar, so in effect i hear the sound produced by the walls of the jar. When the air within the jar is removed and a vacuum is created inside the jar, the sound of the bell is not transmitted to the air in the jar nor to the walls of the jar. And the walls of the jar are an obstruction to the sense organ reaching out to the bell, so in effect there is no sound heard. So, you see, there is a perfect answer available for your question. :-)

  

The Vedantic theory of perception is not just a theory for the museums, it is actually what operations in the world. And it is the only theory that is commensurate with the view that Consciousness is the Ground of the Universe and that the world is merely a pratibimba of Consciousness.
