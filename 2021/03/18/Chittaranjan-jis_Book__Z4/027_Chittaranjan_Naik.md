Dear Sri Putran-ji,

  

  

*Instead you are proposing the possibility that shabda can be tested through this experiment in pratyaksha.*  

  

I am not. Please read Chapter 3 of Part II of the book titled 'Delineation of the Goal of the Experiment' where I have taken the trouble to explain what exactly the experiment is set to prove or not prove.

  

Everybody may not have access to the book, so I shall provide the necessary clarification here. I have argued in that chapter of the book that the case of time-difference in perception is not a case whose truth can be determined by either pratyaksha or anumana, that it is a case of vikalpa, as occurs through expressions like 'hare's horn' or 'married bachelor' and that it is a case of an illegitimate verbal construction which does not even have the possibility of being true. And with regard to what the experiment does, i have explained it as follows:

  

"It is our contention that the time-lag measured in measuring the velocity of light pertains to the time-lag occurring for light to travel from one observed object to another observed object and not for light to travel from the object to the observer. The time-lag attributed to the perceptual process is the time-lag of the former that has been grafted on to the latter and thus erroneously attributed to the perceptual process just as in the case of the perception of a hare having horns the horns of another animal grafted on its head, the horns are erroneously attributed to the hare........... The goal of the experiment is limited to showing this much and nothing more; it is not meant to verify whether Direct Perception holds true or Indirect Perception holds true because such verification is not possible by purely empirical means and must come from a philosophical investigation."
