Yes, commitment to Direct Realism is essential for Vedanta.

  

In the scientific theory of perception, which leads to the position called 'Indirect Realism', the physical brain becomes the substrate of the representations of objects seen as well as of the consciousness in which the representations of objects (called 'phenomenal objects' in contemporary parlance) appear. If this should be the case, then even the notion of being liberated and the bliss of Brahman that is 'experienced' would all be merely kinds of experiences produced by the brain. Instead of the world being dependent on Brahman, it is Brahman that would become dependent on the physical / neural processes of the brain. :-)

  

Of course, one can avoid all this by committing oneself to Idealism. But idealism cannot provide a cogent explanation for distinguishing between hallucination and reality. If pratibhasika sathya and vyaharika sathya are distinguished and vyaharika sathya is admitted (even provisionally), then it becomes Direct Realism.
