> So, what happens in the case of lightning is that the sound of the
> original lightning itself is not heard but it is the vibrating air
> which is travelling through the air that gets heard and it is heard
> after a time delay because it will be heard only when it comes within
> the range of hearing. Once the air-vibrations caused by the lightning
> comes within the range of hearing, it will be heard instantly since
> the subtle sense organ 'ear' then makes instantaneous contact with
> that part of the vibrating air.

But then will not subsequent intervening columns of air between the observer and the last column of air that was the first within range of hearing also vibrate? And then the sense of hearing would reach out to each of those columns, in which case we would then hear multiple similar sounds with some delay based on the number of such imaginary columns there are between the observer and the last column within range of hearing. In which case we would not hear the sound cleanly once from any object that is either within or outside the range of hearing....but that is clearly not the case. When I am in a music concert, I hear one crisp sound of the notes uttered by the musician.

> *3) when a car sounding its horn at a uniform frequency is moving
> away from me versus moving towards me, and my sense of hearing is
> instantly enveloping the car, why is that I hear a Doppler shifted
> sound of a higher frequency in the latter case and lower frequency in
> the former case and an instrument that responds to sound waves
> besides me also confirms the same frequencies that I hear.*
>
>   
>
> As explained, in the case of hearing, we hear the sound that has been
> imparted to the air in addition to the sound of the original object
> (if the original object is within the range of hearing). The Doppler
> effect will take effect since the frequency of the column of vibrating
> air will appear to be greater or lesser depending on the relative
> speed between the observer and the column of vibrating air.

On a windless day, the column of vibrating air would be stationary with regards to the observer. Perhaps you meant to say that the car is moving relative to the column of air and the column of air is vibrating with the Doppler shifted frequency? Ok if you meant to say that. But then wouldn’t we again have the problem of multiple sounds: not just from multiple columns of vibrating air within range with slight delays, but also a mixing of the sound of the original object with the multiple sounds of each column within range which may result in a noisy distortion of the original sound due to overlap and delays, instead of a clean predictable calculable Doppler shift.

  

VP (last para of pratyaksham) explicitly dismisses the possibility of multiple sounds. But then there may need to be some more explaining to be done to avoid that problem in the above mechanism that you have offered.

  

Also, on a more general point, assuming that the the sense of hearing and seeing go out to the sense objects, what would one then say about the function of the vibrating eardrum and the image in the eyeball? Why are those needed if they are not intermediary causes in the chain of causes and effects that results in perception? On the other hand, if we admit those as intermediate causes necessary for perception, what results in those effects?
