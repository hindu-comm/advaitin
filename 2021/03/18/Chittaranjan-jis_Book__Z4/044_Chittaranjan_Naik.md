*But, if the hearing goes out to the ringing bell in order to hear the sound of the ringing bell (as opposed to the sound of the vibrating air), why would it make a difference if there is a vacuum in between?*  

  

The vacuum in between will ensure that the sound of the bell is not transmitted to the wall of the jar. If there is air inside the jar, the bell will transmit vibration to the air which in turn will transmit the vibration to the walls of the jar. The walls of the jar will then make a sound resonating with the sound of the bell and that sound will be heard because the sense organ will reach and make contact with the walls of the jar which is making the sound. The vacuum in between will prevent the sound of the bell from being transmitted to the walls of the jar. And the sound of the bell itself will not be heard because the walls of the jar are an obstruction to the sense organ reaching out to the bell. So, the net effect is that when there is a vacuum inside the jar, no sound will be heard. I hope I am clear now.
