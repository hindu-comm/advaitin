Namaste Chitta ji

Once we acknowledge that we hear the sound of the nearby vibrating air and not the sound as produced at the point a bell/gong is struck, the next question is -

  

How do we say therefore that our mind 'goes out' as far as the point where the bell is originally struck? And not merely upto the point where the air carrying the vibration is present (I.e., inside the ear canal). There is a PhD thesis of one Stephen Kaplan along these lines which seeks to address such questions.

  

(Stephen Kaplan, Hermeneutics, Holography and Indian Idealism. A Study of Projection and Gauḍapāda's Māṇḍūkya Kārikā. Delhi 1987.)

  

My approximate wording of what I recollect reading long back is as follows.-  

  

When we say the mind goes out etc., it implies a certain projecting out. This projection is in two phases/aspects.

  

Firstly the senses of ears and eyes transduce the sthUla physical stimulus "vibrating air" or "light received at the eye" into what is effectively a holographic film. The state of the brain and nerves is the holographic film.

  

The mind, then in the second step, projects a 3D holographic picture experience - the qualia of color and sound are thus created by this projection of the light of consciousness. Please note that a holographic projection does not need a screen as the image is a virtual one. A coherent light source (reflected consciousness) can use the holographic film to project va 3D virtual image.

  

Example [https://youtu.be/4N0Ewb_OVsU](https://youtu.be/4N0Ewb_OVsU)  

  

The hologram in our case is itself the particular state of the physical system (i.e., the brain and nervous system which become or assume the role of the holographic film - the film which is itself created based on transduction, in the first step, into nerve signals of the sensory inputs of sound and light). In the second step of projection of the brain-hologram by the coherent light of reflected consciousness, this light of reflected consciousness \*goes out\* projecting the virtual holographic image with the sounds and sights \*projected to a distance\*.

  

That is why we dont hear the sound of a gong in our ears, rather we hear it as if struck at a distance. We don't experience the sun as if it were inside our eyes, rather it is seen as "out there". (Which is as it should be). The mind thus goes out and creates the spatially projected sound-qualia and color-qualia which we experience 'out there'.

  

When coherent light passes through and projects a holographic image, we don't see the film itself, we instead see the 3D virtual image.

  

In this 'realistic' waking state epistemology, the dream is accounted for by saying that the holograms are spontaneously created based on stored information. There too, the coherent svayamjyoti light of consciousness uses the holographic film of the brain and nerves to project a 3D world for itself.

  

As for the question, whether this model leads to realism, the Kaplan thesis still defends it as non-dualism compatible with mANDUkya kArikA etc. (He has his own take about Gaudapada by emphasizing the 'phenomenological' and playing down the 'ontological' aspects in the acharya - that is a different story.)

  

Kindly treat my above treatment of his thesis as only indicatory. I shared it because of the projection model referred in it.

  

I have not as yet read your book but do certainly hope to do so in future.
