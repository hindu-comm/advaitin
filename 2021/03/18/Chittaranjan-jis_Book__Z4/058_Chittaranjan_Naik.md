It is no more possible for philosophy to pave its own independent path by ignoring science; it must either acquiesce to science and choose a path that is commensurate with its physicalist theories, or it must confront science, as formidable as such a prospect may seem, and dismantle such of those claims of science that trespass illegitimately into the domain of philosophy and thereby clear for itself an unhindered path to pursue its larger goals. The majority of philosophers today seem to have succumbed to the inducements of science without having set their labors to examine its foundations with the acuity that philosophy demands; and the result is a kind of belief that may well be reflected in Will Durant’s words:

“Human history is a fragment of biology. Man is one of countless millions of species and, like all the rest, is subject to the struggle for existence and the competition of the fittest to survive. All psychology, philosophy, statesmanship, and utopias must make their peace with these biological laws.” (Will Durant, *Heroes of History*, Introduction)

The approach that we shall take in this paper is based on the approach of the Indian tradition. Accordingly, we shall not be obliged to accept everything that science says. Our aim is *yathartha-jnana*, or the truth of things, and such *yathartha-jnana* derives from the proposition, the expressed claim to truth, *being aligned with the pramanas*. Therefore, we shall neither accept nor reject the claims of science off-hand merely because they are the claims of science; rather, in accordance with the tenets of the Indian tradition, an assertive claim of science, in so far as such a claim has the potential to cast a doubt on a common tenet of the Siddhandas, shall become the initiator of discourse; and the claim shall be accepted or rejected after a philosophical examination of the claim has been undertaken and its reasonableness has been ascertained or its defect has been shown.

UNQUOTE



  

*Another related observation I have been thinking about is gravitational lensing. As you may be aware, if an observer is looking at the sun during a solar eclipse and the sun has in turn just eclipsed mercury, for a while mercury will continue to be seen beside the eclipsed sun even though it is technically behind the sun. This is due to light rays from mercury being bent by the sun due to the bending of space time due to gravity of the sun. This effect has been observed and written about in considerable detail in science. One can observe galaxies that are behind an obstructing galaxy due to bending of light from the obstructed galaxy by the intervening galaxy. Now if light rays coming from mercury are not responsible for my perception, but it is the observer’s vritti that envelops mercury, how would we explain mercury as being seen even though it is behind the sun because the vrtti cannot cross over an obstruction.  *

  

I will argue that it is not mercury that is seen but an image of the planet mercury that is seen. I would suggest you read the first Annexure to the second part of the book titled "**Paradigm Shift and the Incommensurability Problem: The Cases of Roemer's and Fizeau's Experiments**" wherein I have shown which kinds of cases are to be treated as **perception of the object** and which kinds of cases as **perception of an image of the object followed by an inference**.
