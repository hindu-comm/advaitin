I have read your post twice over and I believe there is a lot of speculation in it. When you say things like "*can be reconciled by asserting that the word viShaya refers to whatever is available for experience at the indriya golaka after possible modification by the mAdhyamam (intervening medium)",* it would call for much discussion.  
  

  

*Also the role of the bhautika indriya golakas is in receiving sthUla inputs at the point of reception and facilitating a sUxma vRtti arising. This interplay between sthUla and sUxma is accepted in the tradition. That would not amount to representationalism. It would be more like the idea that "every pratyaxa qualia of experience has a sthUla bhautika correlation".*  

  

If pratyaksha qualia of experience is not the guna inhering in the sthula object but is a qualia having a correlation to something in the world, **then it is Representationalism according to me.**

  

  

*Also I was unable to follow why peering at an object in a telescope is epistemologically different from seeing that same object with the naked eyes. What would be the situation for those who wear glasses? Its interesting to contemplate that while all those lucky blokes without specs directly see the world, I am only seeing a pathetic
representation of it , never veridically perceiving it? :)*  

  

I have treated this subject in the book including the difference between looking through an instrument contrived to see an object in an attenuated way (like a microscope or telescope) and a vision-correction device like a spectacle worn for the purpose of rectifying a defect in the body/organ. I would urge you to read the book before passing light-hearted comments.
