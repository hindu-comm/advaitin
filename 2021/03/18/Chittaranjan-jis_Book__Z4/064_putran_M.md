Namaskaram Raghav-ji,

  

I did not get to writing a reply and by now you have extended the conversation with Chitta-ji (I am yet to see the details, only had brief look).

  

For those who do not yet grasp the essentiality of Direct Realism to Vedanta (beyond the fact that VP and tradition admits it), they will probably keep a back-up understanding like I presented till they realize whether, why and where one cannot do without DR in understanding Vedanta. In the meantime, we can look at this topic more intently,perhaps read Chitta-ji's book,engage in further tarka on it openly torefine our knowledge.

  

Couple of directions to explore further:

  

1\. That Traditional viewpoint of pratyaksha can coherently explain the observations of science (which may not be same as the scientific interpretations of their observations). This means that DR is not negatable through tarka based on science or pratyaksha. It remains an alternate perspective. (Ideally upon proper tarka, that "alternate" should appear as not some infinitesimalchance of being true but quite sensible in itself. Not sure if this is about training our minds differently.)

2\. Whether, where, why, how is DR integral to Vedanta? Is DR logically necessary in Vedanta,hence to be argued by tarka based on shabda? Or is it to be known directly from Shabda pramana (from prasthanatraya; not later treatises)?

  

Imagine a Vedantin in a physics conference. He may nodto DR among Vedantins and to stimulus-response theory there. Not because they are ignorant but likely he himself is confused and it shows up in such a setting where everyone else is absolutely "not confused". He is quickly intimidated and would rather feel comfortable with the logic of science, its theories and experiments. Many of us (who are still trying to ascertain DR in Vedanta) are going to be in such a lot, since we may haveavoided such topics of conflict with science as having serious implications to Vedanta. It is actually surprising at this point to find someone like Chitta-ji openly stating that we cannot avoid these areas and still be consistent with Vedanta. That at least makes us look at this more carefully and perhaps cozy up to the possibility of traditional viewpoints of world that we generally push under the rug (where they conflict with science). At least privately we begin to think and speak in the traditional terms and to challenge the prevailing notions of the world.
