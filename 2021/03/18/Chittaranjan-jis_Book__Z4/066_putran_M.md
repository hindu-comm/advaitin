I do not have enough knowledge of this subject to answer comprehensively. But if we consider anumana or inference as indirect-knowledge/pramana, it is rooted in pratyaksha which is held to be direct-knowledge/pramana in Indian tradition. Anumana acts as a pramana (for knowing fire exists) only when we have 1. knowledge of vyapti (if smoke then fire) and 2. pratyaksha of smoke. Then we infer "fire" indirectly. There is some debate about what it means to know vyapti; but the ideal picture is that the inference is admitted as attaining (indirectly leading to) knowledge only because we base it on other types of direct-knowledge.

  

In scientific theory of perception, the problem is that we are said to be "seeing" something going on in the brain and that is presumed as being knowledge of a world outside us. However this link between our brain-picture and the world is not based in any sort of direct knowledge. So this would not classify as valid indirect means of knowing the world in the Indian system. If all you ever saw was something going on in your brain and that is telling you that in fact you are seeing a representation of something else outside, there is no way to "know" the truth behind this assumption or even if there is world outside whether what you are seeing matches to that. Etc.
