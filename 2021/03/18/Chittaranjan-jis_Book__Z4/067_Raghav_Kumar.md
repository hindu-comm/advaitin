Namaste Putran ji  
Yes, most advaitins would agree its important to show non conflict between vedAnta paribhAShA epistemology and recent scientific observations.

That is a good point you made about vedantins respecting scientific observations, the raw data, but not necessarily the interpretations and theories purporting to coherently explain them, particularly where such theories overreach and clash with vedanta.

(we may also need to concentrate on the chinks in the material reductionistic armor to create space for alternative non-materialist frameworks.)

Incidentally, some of the questions/issues raised in recent posts for which I could not yet find convincing answers to , I am recapping here. The intention is to defend/strengthen VP epistemology and show its capacity to accommodate scientific observations.

If a star is being seen now, and the vRtti goes out to the star now - what if the star has since exploded. How can the present moment vRtti extend to a currently non-existent star? Like what might have been in the case of the star seen in the 11th century which later became the crab nebula.

Another similar example - When we see with the naked eyes, the role of the optical apparatus is causative. The image formed on the retina is directly observable. And pratyaxa vRtti is chronologically later to that
-in any case its atleast not prior to the retinal image. Now it can be observably demonstrated (not just theoretically) that a perceptual event (such as a solar flare) is recorded and only after a time delay the retina forms the image and the event is experienced. This time delay needs to be addressed. The event is over and gone. Much later it is experienced/perceived. What does the mind go out and wrap on to?

Another issue - If we use a \*single\* convex lens in the form of
reading glasses, we are still experiencing the object as pratyaxa, but if we use \*two\* convex lenses as in looking through a telescope, we are seeing only a representational image of the object? How so? There is no transduction in using a telescope. (The object seen in telescope perception is representational and not direct pratyaxa is what I understood, to be an assertion of DR.)

Even the thunder and lightning example needs elaboration. We hear a single crisp clap after a delay w*.*r.t. lightning. No hint of sound is heard at all at the actual instant of lightning which is when the thunder shock wave is produced.

If any kind of transduction amounts to representationalism. we need to give a comprehensive theory about the role of the sthUla indriya apparatus - the physical eye with identical retinal inverted image formation etc.

Thanks again for your posts.
