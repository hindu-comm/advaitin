I have not looked at the article, but your above statement seems agreeable to me: the jnani "sees" the truth that the ajnani learns from shastra pramana but is yet to realize as truth. I returned from a grocery shopping trip a short while ago. Upon returning to the car, I realized (like Narada after coming back to Krishna) how completely bewildered I had been in the store, of people and things, what I do and what others will think, and so on; lost in a world of duality of jivas and jagat. That is vyavaharika-drishti. From shastra we learn "sarvam kalu idam brahma", so we have to work to cleansethe mind and center it in Ishvara: to see Him initially as the karmaphaladata, the creator, sustainer and destroyer, the support, refuge and solace for our existence and for the Order in our world;and later to see the All as His svarupa. It is a constant back-and-forth struggle, with more back and less forth. The jnani has crossed this river of ajnana and no longer subject to doubt and confusion: what we see as world and beings is to the jnani nothing but nama-rupa svarupa (appearance) of Brahman.
