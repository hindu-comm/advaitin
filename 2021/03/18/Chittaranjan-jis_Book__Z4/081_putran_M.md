Namaskaram,

  

small expansion on the thoughts:

  

1\. I returned from a grocery shopping trip. Upon getting back to the car from the store, I realized (like Narada after coming back to Krishna) how completely bewildered I had been in the store, of people and things, what I do and what others will think, and so on; lost in a world of duality of jivas and jagat. That is vyavaharika-drishti. From shastra we learn "sarvam kalu idam brahma" \[all this is Brahman alone\], and to remove the ajnana that obstructs this knowledge, we work to cleansethe mind and center it in Ishvara: to see Him initially as the karmaphaladata, the creator, sustainer and destroyer, the support, refuge and solace for our existence and for the Order in our world;and later to see the All as His svarupa. It is a constant back-and-forth struggle, with more back and less forth. The jnani has crossed this river of ajnana and is no longer subject to doubt and confusion: what we see as world and beings is to the jnani nothing but nama-rupa svarupa (appearance) of Brahman. We go to different temples and see Vishnu in different forms; yet no matter the form it is the same Lord Vishnu. Likewise the knower 'sees' in all manifest nama-rupa the same Self.  

  

2.The jnani "sees" the truth that the ajnani learns from shastra pramana but is yet to realize as truth. Or we may think that an ajnaniwho is a sadhakawith some shraddha "sees" the truth via shastra pramana but just as easily tends to forget when he engages back in his "regular life"; here the shastra acts like our eye-sense; when we open the eyes, we remove the obstruction to "seeing" (say) the chair in front. Suppose we close the eyes or look elsewhere thereafter. The jnani knows still the chair is there; but the ajnaniforgets and soon behaves like he never knew it existed.
