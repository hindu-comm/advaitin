Namaste Subbu ji

Thank you for sharing a meaningful anecdote from literature on Sri Ramana Maharshi.

  

"As Pure Spirit or Self, He exists in the utter silence and aloneness of the spiritual dimension of existence which totally transcends the illusory physical and mental levels of existence."

  

Incidentally, I am reminded of Swami Dayananda who in his unforgettable style would make fun of the indiscriminate use of the word "spiritual" and say,

  

  

He would say, "What is this popular word 'spiritual'? I know of the word spirit which is alcohol. So to become 'spiritual' is to become drunk with intoxication or what?!"

  

The need to use clear and unambiguous words in vedanta teaching was a lesson none of his students would forget.

  

The sentence in question in the anecdote is more poetic than didactic. There is also the challenge of translation of words like mithyA (illusory) and atItaH (transcending).

  

However the context in the meaningful anecdote of Sri Ramana Maharshi fortunately makes it quite clear that the word "spiritual dimension" is used for Reality transcending the transactional reality of body and mind which is relegated as mithyA (illusory). The disciples are urged to not identify their Guru with just the physical frame bearing his name. So there is luckily no confusion.  

  

The word 'transcending/trascendental' was another favorite of Swami Dayanandaji. He would quip, "I know only dental. But what is this word 'transcendental'? " And then go on to explain that the word should be used in the sense of our understanding/appreciation of the clay which "transcends" the nAma-rUpas of pot, spoon etc.

  

Om

  

Raghav
