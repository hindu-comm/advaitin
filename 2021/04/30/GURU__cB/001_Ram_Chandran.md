Here is what the explanation for the above mentioned verse from Katopanishad by you.

na nareṇāvareṇa prokta eṣa suvijñeyo bahudhā cintyamānaḥ \|  

ananyaprokte gatiratra nāstyaṇīyān hyatarkyamaṇupramāṇāt \|\| 8 \|\|

 ‘This Self (Ātmā) can never be well comprehended if taught by an inferior person, even though variously pondered upon. Unless taught by another (who has realized his oneness with It), there remains no doubt about It. Subtler than the subtlest is It, and beyond logical reasoning.

In this mantra, Yamarāj is glorifying the teacher. This Ātmāgñānam requires an extraordinary teacher who is an expert in communicating the incommunicable. Muṇḍaka Upaniṣhad calls such a teacher śhrotriyah-Brahmaniṣhṭaḥ. He is called ananyaḥ here. When such a guru teaches, the śhiṣya will certainly understand. This is the glory of the āchārya. The problem is that it is not easy to know whether our āchārya is a śhrotriyah-Brahmaniṣṭhaḥ. The only thing we can do is to pray that our āchārya is one.

When taught by a man of worldly understanding, the Ātman is not easily knowable. Ātmā is variously speculated or imagined by various philosophers. Speculations about Ātmā seem to be infinitely many. Some speculate that there is no Ātmā; some accept the existence of Ātmā, but they speculate variously about its nature – limited in time and space or all-pervading; number of Ātmās etc. Is Paramātmā different from individual Ātmā? Is Ātmā inert or sentient? Some claim that Ātmā is Consciousness and some others say it is a mixture. Thus, Ātmā cannot be convincingly and doubtlessly grasped if revealed by an incompetent teacher.

The Ātman is subtler than even the subtlest. In the śhāstras, subtlety is inversely proportional to the number of properties an object has. Of the five elements, the earth has five properties – sound, touch, form, taste and smell (śhabda-sparśh-rūp-ras-gandh). It is accessible to all the sense organs and therefore, it is the grossest. Water is the next grossest because it has all properties except smell. Fire, air and space are next in the order of subtlety. Space has only one property, which is sound and therefore, it is the subtlest amongst the five elements. Ātmā is even subtler than space because it has no property. It cannot be studied by any instrument because the instruments can only study properties. In fact, we never study the substance, we only study the properties.

Ātmā is not even available for logical reasoning. Logic is developed through the analysis of data collected through perception. Tark (logic) works only in fields where data is available. Since Ātmā is not at all available for perception, data collection is not possible and therefore, one cannot give logical conclusions about It. Whatever is presented without data is imagination or speculation.

How then can the Ātman be well-known is explained in this verse. If the Ātman is taught by a preceptor who is free from the notion of duality and who has become one with Brahman, none of the various doubts exists anymore. We can know this Ātmā only if we are taught by someone who is not different from Ātmā (that is, who is Self-realized or is a Brahmaniṣṭhaḥ). The guru must know that he is Ātmā; only then he can confidently tell every śhiṣhya that he/she is Ātmā (*tat tvam asi*). Secondly, the guru must be śhrotriyaḥ – the one who knows the method of revealing Brahman (how not to objectify Brahman). The method of teaching the śhāstras is as important as the śhāstras themselves.

Ātmāgñānam is not mere words, but spirit spoken. The Guru is like the honeybee who draws the honey from all books and gives you the essence. The force behind the words spoken by the Guru is important. It is his power that is conveyed through his words. Not merely this; the proximity to the teacher is itself a force. The words he speaks, his thoughts and the good notion he has about the disciple are important. Unless it is taught by a superior teacher, it cannot be understood.

The words *ananyaproktē gatiratra nāsti* can be taken to mean that when the Ātman is taught by a preceptor who has become one with Brahman, there will be no non-comprehension in the bosom of the student. The meaning is that as in the case of the preceptor, the hearer’s knowledge of the Brahman will take the form, ‘I am none other than That.’ *Gatiratra nāsti*, may also mean there is no travelling into *Samsār* when the Ātman, not distinct from the Self, has been taught.
