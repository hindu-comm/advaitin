Namaste Putran ji,

Just to provide some context to the discussion - Someone had asked a question about how advaita explains the time delay between seeing the flash of lightning and hearing the sound of thunder. If both occur at the same space and time, and the mind goes out to both places at the same time, why the delay? Does sound exist at the site of lightning when the mind reaches it, or has it ceased to exist?

  

To explain if it is said that bothlight and sound exist at the site of lightning, but it takes the mind longer to get back - what about the situation where one sees the light from stars? We may be seeing a star today based on light emanating from it millions of years ago. In the most extreme case, the star may no longer exist when we are seeing its light in the present. So if it is held that the mind goes to the object, does the mind go to the galaxy in the present, or does it go to the galaxy in the past? If the former, there is no galaxy in the present, so what is seen? If the latter, the mind cannot be expected to travel back in time to see the galaxy when it existed.

  

To explain this, I had suggested that instead of the mind travelling to objects, it may be accepted that light / sound waves etc arrive to the subject, and that what is seen / heard is light / sound. That is, the viShaya of the indriya is not the object itself, but the light reflected off the object (prabhA). Therefore, where in the current theory, perception occurs when the pramAtR chaitanya, vRttyavacChinna chaitanya and viShayAvacChinna chaitanya become one, in this model, perception occurs when the vRttyavacChinna chaitanya and prabhAvacacChinna chaitanya become one. As the vRtti does not travel out of the body, the pramAtR chaitanya and vRttyavacChinna chaitanya are effectively one always. As the prabhA is the viShaya, viShayAvacChinna chaitanya and prabhAvacChinna chaitanya are identical always. The idea was to preserve as much of the existing vedAntic theory of perception as possible while seeking an explanation forthe above phenomenon.

  

The question then arose whether such cognitions can be considered valid - in Sri Raghav's words,

  

"we can never get veridical perception (pramA) of the distant object, we only recreate an unreliable image/representation of it in our

mind. The knowledge of the object-in-itself cannot arise in this prabhava-mediated pratyaxa prama."

  

To this, the response is whatI had suggested in the link you had provided below. In essence, the idea was - because thelight being reflected off the object does so in a specific way at aspecificwavelength because ofa specific property in the object itself, one is not creating an unreliable image of the object. Reflected light does show theobject as-is, as the light would not be reflected in the manner it has, if the object was different. In effect, what one is seeing is the object itself.

  

I had not suggested that the validity of such cognitions was derived from shabda - nor that this is a theory that is validated by the vedas themselves. It was literally something made up on the fly over a day or two. Therefore, I'm sure there are errors in such a conception and must be viewed for what it is - a thought experiment, no more, no less. No pride of ownership here. Feel free to critique it - ultimately we have to find a way of reconciling the vedAntic theory of perception to account for the specific problem - seeing objects which no longer exist in the present.

  

Regards,

Venkatraghavan
