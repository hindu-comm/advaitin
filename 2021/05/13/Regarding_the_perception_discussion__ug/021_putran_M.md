Namaskaram Venkataraghavan-ji,

  

We should note from this preliminary discussion that while some advaitins are amenable to changes in the traditional contact-theory to corroborate with science and thinkit will not cause advaita-hani, others are positively against this. Likewise some say theentire topic is in the realm of avidya and irrelevant to the Upanishadic teachings; others oppose this viewpoint and see something holistic where the answers we see to these questions matter. This shows some serious difference in the way the siddhanta is being understood by advaitins even while we often talk the same things. We may be differing at the basic levels and not know it. This topic therefore points to the need for a more fundamental debate about what Advaita says; and we can approach with a view of deciphering this difference: perhaps will reveal subschools (traditional vs scientific) that can independently claim consistency with shastra and/or science, or through tarka, establish rightvs wrong understanding of advaita among them.

  

I recently wrote the dream analogy as helping understand the relation of advaita with the contact theory:

  

"One way to explain this is to use thedreamanalogy. When you see the sun in thedream, do you see the sun as it was projected earlier? The character in thedreamdue to its identifications with thedreambody-mind-world may imagine the seeing in terms of thedream-events; but the true seer is the one Dreamer. Whatever is seen by the Seer is the here and now as projected by the Seer. So it is the Seeing of the Self that manifests as events of body-mind-world and not viseversa; so if we look at how the body reacts to external stimuli, brain acts after that, etc. and finally land up in "seeing happens", we are doing things in reverse order: rather the Self sees/manifests itself as the Sun and manifests as the light and body-mind reactions."

  

But then, I may be using the analogy specifically to align the philosophy with contact theory. Another person who seeks to modify the perception theory can argue that thepurpose of the dream analogy is not this type of comparison, but to point out advaita in a different way that need not have contact theory of perception. Thus we first sort out our differences regarding advaita and then seek to build with or without the traditional standpoints on these types of topics.
