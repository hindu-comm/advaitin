Namaste Sri Venkatraghavan-ji,

  

*No advaita hAni is possible, because it is a fact.*

  

Advaita hani is not possible in reality because Advaita is the truth of reality. But in these conversations, we do not use the term ‘*Advaita hani’* to refer to possibilities in reality as it exists in itself but to a view or understanding of Advaita that would not be commensurate with Advaita and which would thereby commit the fault of Advaita hani. I shall take the example of the theory of perception that we were discussing about to illustrate the point.

  

When we incorporate a theory of perception into the Advaita-philosophy that would result in Representationalism, it would essentially imply that the world we perceive in the field of our consciousness is not the real world but a mere representation of it. In technical terms, it would be a ‘*phenomenal world’* and not the real world. The real world would then be something that lies beyond the field of consciousness. (In Representationalism, the real world is held to be something that is known indirectly through the representations; that is the reason why Representationalism is also called *Indirect Realism*.) Such a world, which lies beyond the field of consciousness, would be independent of consciousness. The *‘jagat*’ that we refer to in the expression ‘*jagan-mithya’* would then refer merely to the phenomenal world appearing in the field of our consciousness and not to the real world which would be existing beyond the boundaries of our consciousness. If this should be the case, the sublation of the world that would take place on the rise of supreme-jnana would cause the sublation of only the phenomenal world and would leave untouched the independent world lying beyond the boundaries of consciousness thus leading to kind of Eternal Duality. This is clearly a case of Advaita hani; and it is a logical consequence of holding a theory of perception that leads to Representationalism.
