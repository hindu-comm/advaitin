The idea that the world vanishes after brahmajnAna is an erroneous notion. Even within the advaita theory of perception, the world does not vanish upon brahmajnAna. The appearance of the world continues after brahmajnAna, otherwise there would be no jIvanmukti, and no guru.

  

Nor does the world cease existing, because it has never existed for it to cease existing. As Gaudapadacharya says प्रपञ्चो यदि विद्येत निवर्तेत न संशयः । मायामात्रमिदं द्वैतमद्वैतं परमार्थतः ॥

  

Holding that a theory of perception should prove that the world is founded on ignorance, or that theworld will disappear after its sublation is an unreasonable expectation. The shruti has proven the former, and the latter is not accepted within advaita. In fact, the theory proposed was consistent with ignorance as the cause of bhrama / adhyAsa. See here for example:[https://lists.advaita-vedanta.org/archives/advaita-l/2021-May/056665.html](https://lists.advaita-vedanta.org/archives/advaita-l/2021-May/056665.html)

If all that brahmajnAna was doing was the negation of perception, then what has been said above would have been true. However, that is not the case. The knowledge of Brahman is not merely the cause for the negation of perception and the phenomenal world. It is a cause for the destruction of ignorance, whose consequence is the negation of all of its products simultaneously. Ignorance is the common cause for both the phenomenal world and the putative world. When that is destroyed, the products of ignorance, being the phenomenal and putative world, are realised to not exist.

advaita has been established not because of the theory of perception endorsed in the vedAnta paribhASha. advaita has been established by the shruti, validated by experience. That being the case, expecting anyone to prove advaita using a theory of perception is a case of the tail wagging the dog.
