Namaste Sri Venkatraghavan-ji,

  

I see that you have skirted the main point of my post. So, here it is again.

  

I am not asking you to defend the conclusions of the Charvakas. I am pointing out that a Charvaka cannot make the assertion that sublation will occur based on the Charvaka philosophy because such an assertion would be incoherent with the Charvaka philosophy. I am saying furthermore that the assertion that sublation will occur can be made only from the standpoint of Advaita philosophy in which the perceived world is something whose existence is entirely dependent on the Self. Since the world in Advaita is entirely dependent on the Self, it has the potential to be sublated on the rise of brahma-jnana.

  

Now, if you should incorporate the new theory of perception into Advaita, the Siddhanta that results from such an incorporation will not be Advaita as it has come down to us from the tradition but will be a new Siddhanta in which, as a logical consequence of the new theory of perception, there would be two worlds: the phenomenal world that is dependent on consciousness and an imperceptible putative world which would be influencing how the phenomenal world is perceived in consciousness but which would itself not be contained within the field of consciousness. This putative external world is a logical consequence of positing the new theory; it will not go way just because you deny it or don't accept it. And the most important point is this: the Siddhanta which results from incorporating the new theory of perception into Advaita, would not be Advaita but would be a Pseudo-Advaita philosophy in which putative external world, being outside of consciousness, would not be sublatable. Now, you cannot hold on to this Pseudo-Advaita philosophy and claim that sublation will occur just as a Charvaka cannot hold on to the Charvaka philosophy and claim that sublation will occur because in both cases there will be incoherence between the philosophy and the assertion that sublation will occur. This is the point you need to address in a proper manner.
