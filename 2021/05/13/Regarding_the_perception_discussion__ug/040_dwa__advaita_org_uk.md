According to Swami Bhajanananda in an article in Prabuddha Bharata 2010, there are four basic principles of Advaita Vedanta. One of these is viShaya j~nAna – objective knowledge. He says:  
  
“According to the Sankhya-Yoga theory of perception—brieﬂy described by Vyasa in his commentary on *Yoga Sutra*, 1.7—the *antaḥkaraṇa* goes out through the eyes to the object and takes the form of the object; this modiﬁcation of the *antaḥkaraṇa* is known as *vṛtti*. The light of the Purusha or the Atman then gets reﬂected in this *vṛtti*, and this reﬂected light reveals the object. Thus, *viṣaya*-*jñāna* or objective knowledge is invariably *vṛtti*-*jñāna.”*



In fact, Yogasutra 1.7 says that “Perception, verbal cognition and inference are real cognitions” and Vyasa (not our Vyasa, I understand) comments that:



“Perception is the mental modification which cognizes chiefly the specific appearance of an object, being of the nature of both the generic and the specific qualities, and which has for its object, by means of the impression caused therein by the external object through the passage of the senses.”



I cannot make either the sutra or Vyasa’s comment equate to the Vedanta ParibhAShA theory. Nor does Vachaspati Mishra’s gloss on Vyasa say anything about this.



This appears to be yet another example of an authoritative source claiming something that is simply not true in order to support their prior understanding.



(Note that I am referring here to Rama Prasada’s translation. It is of course possible that *he* is translating the original Sanskrit in accordance with *his* prior understanding…)



My own view is that the *detailed* explanation of Consciousness ‘going out through the sense organs to the object’ etc. is a post-Shankara theory. But I am perfectly willing to be proven otherwise by an authentic quotation from scripture or Shankara.
