Dear Dennisji, Venkatraghavanji, Raghavji, Chittaji,

  

The question you are looking into about whether there exists Shruti references (or references from Shankara) for what VP says is a very pertinent one.

  

A reference that came to my mind from Sankara's writings for VP support is in Dakshninamurty Stotram Verse 4 which seems to imply that the intellect goes out to the sense

objects from the sense organs:

  

नानाच्छिद्रघटोदरस्थितमहादीपप्रभाभास्वरं  
ज्ञानंयस्यतुचक्षुरादिकरणद्वारावहिःस्पन्दते।  
जानामीतितमेवभान्तमनुभात्येतत्समस्तंजगत्  
तस्मैश्रीगुरुमूर्तयेनमइदंश्रीदक्षिणामूर्तये॥४॥

  

  

I had also discussed this reference with Dennisji a few months ago but was unable to follow up further on this. Today, I finally did some follow up and

was curious as to what Sureshwaracharya had to say about this in mAnasollAsa. I looked up my copy and found the relevant pages on [archive.org](http://archive.org).

Note the links to the two pages below:

Swami Maheshanandagiri in his Hindi commentary stresses on the wordviṣayacchāyām and takes it to mean a shadow/semblance of the object and not the original object itself.

Below is an image (if this inlined image doesn't work, again you can refer to the attachment).

  

![Inline image](https://groups.google.com/group/advaitin/attach/4b0f3c32e8033/Screen%20Shot%202021-05-17%20at%201.26.36%20PM.png?part=0.2&view=1 "Inline image")  

  

  

For the benefit of those who don't understand Hindi, I am providing a translation of the above:

  

***"It should be remembered that in SAnkhya prakriyA, the mind goes to the location of the object and assumes the form of the object****.***

***In our case (i.e Vedanta), the object comes into the mind. Modern science is also in agreement***

***with this Vedanta prakriya. It is surprising that in the past, the SAnkhya prakriyA was more popularized. In the coming verses,***

***the description of NAdis and sense organs is also confirmed by modern science. Accepting that the mind travels to the location of***

***the object is completely against the conclusions of modern science of light. There are stars from whch light has left crores of years ago, there are sounds created***

***lakhs of years ago, but whose source has been destroyed, but they are still seen/heard. In such cases, to which location can the mind travel***

***(as the source is no longer there). If the mind has to go to the source, we would also need to accept that it has to go back crores of years in time.***

***Therefore, the Vedanta prakriyA****that only the shadow/semblance of objects falls in the mind (not the object itself) is valid.***

***  ***

***Moreover, when the same pot is seen by ten men, then their minds will come on top of each other! Only the one whose mind is***

***bottommost will see the******object, and all others will be separated (from the object). This is also against experience. In general, sages, purANas***

***and smRti texts**have accepted SAnkhya's prakriyA and it is respectable from a simplicity perspective, but the supremacy of***

***the prakriyA of Vedanta is undisputable."***

  

  

I must admit that I found the ten men looking at a pot argument in the second para to be a bit hard to accept as a refutation of VP :-),

but I found it quite surprising that Swami Maheshananda Giri had already thought of the distant stars objection that we have been raising here

on this list and he did this probably as early as 1963 when the first edition of this book was published. And, of course, I was a bit shocked that whereas I had started

out with the feeble hope to find some support for what VP says through the route of the Dakshninamurty Stotram verse hoping to find some support from Sureshwara in mAnasollAsa, what I found here was quite the opposite view expressed quite strongly and apparently attributed to Sureshwara's commentary on it (according to the interpretation of Swami Maheshananda Giri of course).
