> According to Swami Bhajanananda in an article in Prabuddha Bharata
> 2010, there are four basic principles of Advaita Vedanta. One of these
> is viShaya j~nAna – objective knowledge. He says:  
>   
> “According to the Sankhya-Yoga theory of perception—brieﬂy described
> by Vyasa in his commentary on *Yoga Sutra*, 1.7—the *antaḥkaraṇa* goes
> out through the eyes to the object and takes the form of the object;
> this modiﬁcation of the *antaḥkaraṇa* is known as *vṛtti*. The light
> of the Purusha or the Atman then gets reﬂected in this *vṛtti*, and
> this reﬂected light reveals the object. Thus, *viṣaya*-*jñāna* or
> objective knowledge is invariably *vṛtti*-*jñāna.”*
>
> 
>
> In fact, Yogasutra 1.7 says that “Perception, verbal cognition and
> inference are real cognitions” and Vyasa (not our Vyasa, I understand)

> specific appearance of an object, being of the nature of both the
> generic and the specific qualities, and which has for its object, by
> means of the impression caused therein by the external object through
> the passage of the senses.”
>
>
