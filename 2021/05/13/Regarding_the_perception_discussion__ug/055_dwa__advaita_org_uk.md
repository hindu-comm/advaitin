Dear Putran-ji,



As I said, I have recommended ‘Methods of Knowledge’ to many people and provide a very positive review of it at my website. I reiterate now: it is an excellent source for the epistemology of Advaita. However, he was a monk in the Ramakrishna Order. Ramakrishna was a mystic and his most important disciple, Vivekananda, was significantly influenced by sources other than Shankara’s Advaita. The net result of this was that many of the ideas that are now disseminated as traditional Advaita are not. This is why teachers in that tradition are called neo-Vedantins. (Note that this is NOT the same as neo-Advaitins!)



The passage that I mentioned, which contains many statements that are not in agreement with Shankara did not come from ‘Methods’ but from an article in a journal. Nevertheless, you can find erroneous statements in ‘Methods’ without much difficulty, particularly (if I recall correctly) in the area of what enlightenment is and how to get it.



I don’t understand what you are asking about Maheshanandagiri or basis for vedAnta prakriyA – I think you are mixing up with Satyan-ji’s post.
