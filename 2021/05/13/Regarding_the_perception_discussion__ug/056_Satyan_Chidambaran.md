Namaste Putran-ji,

  

Thanks for your thoughtful follow up points.

  

At the outset, let me make it clear that I am neither for nor against VP. I have found it a useful prakriyA

personally as it helps "reclaim the shining from the cognized objects, and assign the shine back to the AtmA" in a manner

of saying, thereby helping one come to the understanding that the AtmA/Bramhan is what ultimately lights up the world.

  

The real question though is whether it has transactional/vyAvahArikA validity. I am open minded about it.

Note that every prakriyA need not have vyAvahArika validity: A prakriyA could just be a way of thinking or looking at things, a trick, a benevolent lie,

or a life hack (to use a more modern term!) to bring one back to the Self as the ultimate reality. As long as it brings one to the tAtparyam of advaita

"bramha satyam jagan mithyA jIvo bramhaiva nAparaH", it is a successful prakriyA, whether or not the method has vyAvahArikA validity, because

ultimately vyavahAra is mithya.

  

An example of this is AbhAsavAda versus avaccheda vAda versus pratibimba vAda. Use the one that works for you. There is no need to

get into an endless discussion about which is valid....because all are descriptions regarding mithya, and there may be no finality to

the discussion either. Ultimately, the only thing that one can know with finality in this creation is the Self. Rest is mAyA. The more one delves

into mAyA, the more questions that it will raise. This is what is happening with science....as someone said that

every discovery in science expands the frontiers of ignorance because it raises more questions than it answers.

  

Moreover, that which is mithyA has to be dropped. Hence, if one has come to satyam somehow, one

may not need to beat the mithyA horse to death as to which strategy is right....and that debate may not be conclusive

either......just as once you have woken up from a dream, one need not worry about the exact mechanism of understanding

how the dream was projected.

  

यया यया भवेत्पुंसांव्युत्पत्ति:प्रत्यगात्मनि। सा सैव प्रक्रियेह स्यात्साध्वीत्याचार्यभाषितम् ।  

  

Ācārya Sureśvara has said that: whatever method helps one understand clearly the indwelling Ātman is valid/acceptable. - Panchadashi, 8.73

  

Venkatraghavanji was perhaps trying to convey a similar point.

  

So we need to be aware that there is a disctinct possibility that trying to establish vyAvahArika validity for

a prakriyA could ultimately be a misguided effort in the bigger picture. We don't know if that is so in this specific case,

but we cannot entirely discount that possibility either.

  

Having given this background, I will respond to a few points that you have raised in various emails:

  

1\) You wrote:

*"He was taking care about how he presented Indian knowledge about pramanas; again, the point is that if vedanta prakriya (of perception) was so definitely known with regard to its variance from Sankhya, this swami would have elaborated that fact in his book. It would have been the "neo" thing to do, given the conformance with science."*

  

What you raise is a valid question as to why Swami Satprakashananda who wrote a well researched book did not make such a distinction. I wasn't

aware of the difference between Vedanta and Sankhya prakriyAs until I read Swami MaheshAnanda Giri's gloss.

  

However, I would also raise a counter question to this. If this VP theory of perception was so central to establishing advaita, wouldn't you imagine that

Sureshwara should have at least mentioned it in mAnasollAsa in the context of this very verse that talks about the workings of perception? The fact that he did not

seems to imply that the VP prakriyA was not considered central/indispensable perhaps? Moreover, the fact that Sureshwara uses the terms

गृह्णाति विषयच्छायाम् (gṛhṇāti viṣayacchāyām) whichwhich literally means "grasps the image of the object (not the original)", seems imply that the

VP theory was not as prevalent then, or Sureshwaradid not subscribe to its validityfroma vyAvahArika standpoint and perhaps Swami MaheshanandaGiri

has a point there?

  

2) You wrote:

*"With the Swami Maheshanandagiri, he is not claiming to derive vedanta prakriya from Sureshwaracharya's commentary; rather he is writing as if this was already standard among vedantic scholars, only not popularized in the general purana-based opinions which went with Sankhya. That appears very neo-twisting to me; where is the basis for this vedanta prakriya so obvious?"*

  

As I said, this distinction was not obvious to me either. But it appears to have been obvious to him. From this, we can minimally conclude that

within the Vedanta tradition, there was perhaps room for a difference in the tradition with regards to the vyAvahArika validity of the prakriyA, even

if it was considered generally useful as a prakriyA by many.

  

3\) You wrote:

*"Anycase, all this (about Dakshinamurthy stotra) is not within Shankara prastana-traya and sruti that you are focused on. Subbu-ji, a few posts back, did mention of references in Gita and elsewhere."*

Dakshinamurty Sotram is nevertheless a righly respected prakaraNa grantha in the tradition. Even Sureshwaracharya considered it important enough to write

a vartika on it. So there is no doubt about the authenticity of authorship of either the stotram or mAnasollAsa.

  

4\) You wrote:

*"If Swami Bhajanananda was fitting Shastra into his prior understanding, Swami Maheshanandagiri seems to be doing the same explicitly."*

Swami M did not discount the usefulness of the prakriyA though...he says it is simple and respectable as a prakriyA. He is only questioning its validity in vyavahAra.

  

5\) You wrote:

*"The one thing I do not see addressed in these discussions is the assertion of instantaneous reaching of object and forming vritti, etc. That has to be incorporated into our analysis."*

The utility of the prakriyA doesn't seem to require instantaneous reaching. Nor have I seen the necessity of instanteneous reaching being described in the works

where I have seen the descriptions of the prakriyA. So I am not sure from what the authoritative source of instantaneous reaching requirement is.

  

regards,

--Satyan
