> Note the usage of the words गृह्णाति विषयच्छायाम् (gṛhṇāti viṣayacchāyām).

I think that it just means विषयाकारवृत्तिः। The antaHkaraNa taking the form of the viShaya is being called as viShayacChAyA here. Sw. Maheshvaranandaji seems to be making a samanvaya with science by interpreting this too literally. (Somewhere in the Brihadaranyaka introduction, he even talks about western philosophies, Christianity and Islam, which tells me that Swamiji addresses a wide variety of audiences, \_repackaging\_ the traditional viewpoint).

> "Like unto a clear mirror, Buddhi, because of the predominance of
> Sattva in it and in virtue of the reflection of Atman in it receives
> images of external objects".

What Alladi Mahadev Shastrijiis doing is just translating ChAyA as images, which could be vRtti too.

  

I urge you to kindly read the 4.11 Manasollasa right after that goes so:

ताभिस्तु गोलकान्ताभिः प्रसर्पन्ति स्फुलिङ्गवत्।

करणानि समस्तानि यथा स्वं विषयं प्रति॥

  

If the viShayasreach the antaHkaraNa, why would the sense-organs go through their golakas towards their viShayas? With all due respect, Swamiji has \_nicely\_ translated this तृतीयान्त word गोलकान्ताभिः as a पञ्चम्यन्त as those (nADIs) reaching upto the golakas (गोलक पर्यन्त जाने वाली), whereas it clearly reads as through those nADIs ending in the golakas, all the karaNas (indrIyas and antaHkaraNa) go out towards their objects! He justifies this translation saying that the nADIs reach only till the golakAs because the viShayas also reach the golakAs! I wouldn't say that those are Bhagavan Sureshvaracharya's words explaining Bhagavatpadacharya's words in any case. This is a sub-commentary Madhuri with its own flavouring from science. Of course, Swamiji has also mentioned a pAThabheda as गोलकान्तानि as an adjectiveto करणानि, but he hasn't used it in his publication or anvaya; I wonder why. Even if it be considered so, ताभिः would be out of place. So would the स्फुलिङ्गवत् प्रसर्पन्ति as there is no need for the karaNas to spread like sparks when they are right there in the golakas!
