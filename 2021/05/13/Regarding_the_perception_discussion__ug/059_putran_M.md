The question is whether cognition is a fundamental property or shakti of the Self, or whether only a phenomenon of the projected world (like fire generates heat). If the latter, then this topic belongs to vyavaharika and what one assumes about it need not impede into one's realization of advaita.

  

If however it is the Self/Chaitanyam that is the cognizer/perceiver/knower of its projection/manifestation/appearance, then there is the possibility that one's theory of perception is incoherent with advaita when it reduces cognition to be a product of the world. An "advaita" that is compatible with such a theory may not be what the Vedic tradition or scripture teaches.

  

This is where I understand this debate to be centered at. I am not the expert to defend VP but I think people who consider the topic as merely of vyavaharika/mithya interest may be missing this central issue that others think has direct bearing on the way the siddhanta is understood.

  

One place where this subtlety may be indicated is the following: Advaita does not merely say that there is a maayicshow of world and an unchanging reality that is entirely distinct; not just jagan mithya, Brahma satyam. It says further jivo brahmaiva naaparaha, that "aham brahmaasmi".

  

One way to understand this: The Self is the only knower, seer, projecting and cognizing this world and also assuming this individual identity in the context of these upadhis. When the obstruction to jnana is partially cleared (manifest in a modification of the upadhis), the Self's cognition of the object of knowledge, that is its "seeing" of its own manifestation (not outside Self), is immediate and direct. What I in jiva see is what I in Ishvara manifest here and now. And based on this seeing, the Self manifests vritti and other body-mind changes.
