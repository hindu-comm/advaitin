Namaste Praveen-ji,

  

First of all, thanks for your kind and insightful response and a pointer to the later verse which is also very pertinent to this discussion.

  

Before getting into the specifics, at a high level, there are two positions possible. Please let me know if this makes sense to you:

  

1\) Position 1: VP model is a useful prakriyA that doesn't necessarily need to have vyAvahArika satyatvam. It is just

a way of thinking that brings one to the chit svarUpa AtmA and reinforces jadatvam of the world.

The tAtparyam of vedAnta is only in communicating "bramha satyam jagan mithya jIvo bramhaiva nAparaH". As

long as that the tAtparyam is accomplished, it doesn't matter whether the prakriyAs used to get there have vyAvahArika

satyatvam or not. Vedanta doesn't have tAtparyam in the prakriyA. This is akin to saying that a roaring lion in nightmarish dream

wakes me up. In the fact that the dream lion woke me up, it accomplished its purpose. It doesn't need to have

vyAvahArika satyatvam to wake me up, and nor do I need to assert that it has vyAvaharic satyatvam after waking up.

  

2\) Position 2: This is a stronger position that asserts that the VP prakriyA has vyAvahArika satyatvam. But if this position is adopted,

VP theory has to be consistent with other vyAvahArika observations....such as a dead star emitting light, or why I see

a bent pencil placed in a beaker that is half full with water, or why I see mercury during a solar eclipse even though

it is behind the sun. I am not necessarily saying samanvaya with all scientific theories...but at least

samanvaya with all yukti and anubhava, and some of those yukti and anubhava are supplied by verifiable scientific

observations (not theories).

  

I have currently adopted Position 1. I cannot in all intellectual honesty adopt Position 2 knowing that I see some holes with

VP that are not yet reconciled with some yukti/anubhava. And I know that I am not alone here, there are many in this list in the same position.

There are also some highly respected scholars and teachers whom I shall leave unnamed, who have also felt that there needs to be some response

to such objections to maintain Position 2...or else Position 1 is a safer place to be. I would be glad to move to Position 2

if possible, if someone (you perhaps?) can help reconcile these objections. But so far, I have not yet seen any explanations that have satisfactorily

convinced me to move to Position 2 keeping VP theory intact. But in the eventuality that Position 2 cannot be rescued from the objections also, I am quite comfortable

with Position 1 also as it doesn't in any way affect understanding of the tAtparyam of Vedanta.

  

Having said this, I think Swami M probably saw a similar dilemma with Position 2. Therefore, somewhat courageously, he has taken an interpretation that

reconciles VP prakriyA with yukti and anubhava in order to be able to assert vyavahAric satyatvam for the prakriyA. I am not sure if it was his own

interpretation, or if he got it from his Guru (and if there was a tradition behind this thinking)....for he differentiates between Vedanta and Sankhya prakriyAs.

In any case, the intent behind it is probably noble: to not make Bhagavan Sureshwaracharya's words apramANam in his own mind and the only option he

probably saw was to interpret in this fashion. As, you very well know, Shankara Himself has taught us: If a thousand Shruti statements say that fire is cold,

one should not take it literally. If we encounter such statements, to make sure that Shruti doesn't lose pramANatvam, the tradition reinterprets such

statements with reverence in keeping with yukti and anubhava.

  

Having said that, I will move down to the specific comments:

  

Firstly, thanks a lot for pointing out the next verse because it clarifies Swamiji's position even further, and it is also pertinent for discussion

in this context.

  

1\) You wrote:

*I think that it just means विषयाकारवृत्तिः। The antaHkaraNa taking the form of the viShaya is being called as viShayacChAyA here. Sw. Maheshvaranandaji seems to be making a samanvaya with science by interpreting this too literally.*  

  

I agree with your observation. He is doing samanvaya. But I wouldn't necessarily say samanvaya with science, but samanvaya with yukti and anubhava, which

all our achAryAs have taught us to do. If there is some other way to do this samanvaya with yukti and anubhava with which would be more agreeable to you,

I would be very excited to know what you think! Having said that, Sw. M is probably not overstretching/manipulating the meaning ofविषयच्छायाम् either for it

seems to be amenable tohis interpretation also.

  

2) You wrote:

"What Alladi Mahadev Shastrijiis doing is just translating ChAyA as images, which could be vRtti too."

  

If we follow what Allady Mahadev Shastri has to say later in the text:

  

Page 135

There are nâdîs woven in the antah-karana, like unto threads woven into a net. **By them, verily, reaching up** **to the physical regions of sensation, all sense-organs move,** **like sparks of fire, towards their respective objects.....**

***When passing from the*** ***sushupti to the jagrat or waking state of consciousness, these*** ***sense-organs pass up to the very physical regions of sensation,*** ***such as the eye, the ear, etc**.*

Page 139

***By the sense-organs which, starting from*** ***the heart—the seat of Antah-karana—reach the extremities of the*** ***physical organs of sensation,** **Atman becomes conscious of sound***

***and other sense-objects within and without the body.***

Alladi Mahadev Shastriji also doesn't seem to write about sense organs extending beyond their physical extremities. Note the usage of "up to the physical regions of sensation", "reach the extremeties of the physical organs of sensation" etc Perhaps he is also doing a similar samanvaya like Swamiji?

2) You wrote:

"I urge you to kindly read the 4.11 Manasollasa right after that goes so:

ताभिस्तु गोलकान्ताभिः प्रसर्पन्ति स्फुलिङ्गवत्।

करणानि समस्तानि यथा स्वं विषयं प्रति॥"

  

Thanks for providing your understanding of this verse. The way I understand this, गोलकान्ताभिः is a विशेषणम् for ताभि: which refers to नाडीभि:. Is that your understanding as well? In which case both गोलकान्ताभिः and ताभि: are तृतीयान्त.....and the meaning is "through the nADIs ending in the golakas", which I also arrive at, is the same as yours. But isn't the Hindi meaning "गोलक पर्यन्त जाने वाली" (नाडी) also the same? He is saying the nADI goes only upto the golaka, which is correct. Overall, Swamiji's literal word by word translation for the shloka below:

![Inline image](https://groups.google.com/group/advaitin/attach/4d7760f898f71/Screen%20Shot%202021-05-20%20at%203.46.12%20PM.png?part=0.1&view=1 "Inline image")  

tallies with your "through those nADIs ending in the golakas, all the karaNas (indrIyas and antaHkaraNa) go out towards their objects". I think that the difference is only in the meaning of how he has taken विषय/object versus how you have taken विषय/object. He has taken विषय to be the object that has already arrived at the golaka. You are taking विषय to be external object.

  

3) You wrote:"Of course, Swamiji has also mentioned a pAThabheda as गोलकान्तानि as an adjectiveto करणानि, but he hasn't used it in his publication or anvaya; I wonder why. Even if it be considered so, ताभिः would be out of place. So would the स्फुलिङ्गवत् प्रसर्पन्ति as there is no need for the karaNas to spread like sparks when they are right there in the golakas!"

I am not sure I see why ताभिः would be out of place. As I understand, it would still refer to नाडीभि:. The anvaya would then be समस्तानि गोलकान्तानि करणानि ताभि: (नाडीभि:) स्वम् विषयम् प्रति प्रसर्पन्ति स्फुलिड़्गवत् यथा. In that case, the English would be "all the sense organs ending in the golakAs go towards their sense objects through the nADIs like the sparks". If this pAThabheda was indeed there, it would help make the case for Swamiji's translation more explicit as it says that the sense organs that end in the golakAs (.....implying that they don't proceed further). Overall, with the heavy emphasis in rest of the text about the description of nADIs beginning in the heart/antaHkaraNam and ending in different golakAs, it seems like the emphasis is on travel of sense organs via the nadIs from the heart/antaHkaraNam to the golakAs like sparks....so the sparks part could be explained in that manner? It doesn't seem to say much at all (at least in clear/explicit) terms about the travel of the sense organs/mind to the external object like what VP does, no mention of vRtti vyApti etc, leaving it possibly more amenable to Swamiji's interpretation as well.

  

4\) Lastly, Praveen-ji, on a related note: Congratulations on the wonderful work of completing the VS classes! I completely agree with you that it is a really great work worth its name and there is almost no area of Vedanta that it leaves untouched. I consider myself fortunate to have gotten the opportunity to study it with Swami Paramarthanandaji. I had one question in that context. Clearly, NischaladAsa lays a lot of emphasis on Drishti Srishti vAda (DSV), in which the whole VP model of vrtti vyApti and phala vyApti would not be applicable for how can thought envelop an object that yet needs to be projected? In DSV, the triad of pramAta, pramANam and prameya is projected every moment as in a dream. That again seems to be point to the dispensability of the VP model as it is incompatible with DSV prakriyA, which seems to reinforce the idea that VP prakriyA is not indispensable/central in Vedanta in general. Your thoughts on that?
