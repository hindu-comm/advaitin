Namaskaram Venkataraghavan-ji,

  

The 'uttarapaksha' argument is that what all you described belongs to the world (seen) and that you are superimposing them onto the Self (seer). It is the intrinsic shakti of the Self to see/know; only ajnana needs to be removed: the light of the Self reveals at once.

  

In science it is assumed that the observer's cognition is non-different from the process by which a measuring instrument obtains information. Chitta-ji argues that when we open our eyes and 'see' the object, it is by direct instantaneous contact of subtle sense-organ etc. with the object, although the phenomenon of light traveling to our body etc. happens as well. The latter reaches our eye or measuring instrument with time delay. So, in you mail, where you write "observer", you are referring to the measuring instrument: this is error.

  

Now is it possible to establish beyond doubt that our cognition and consciousness are the endpoints of sequences ofgross events in the world? Perhaps people can formulate experiments like Chitta-ji's simultaneity expt; but we should be careful thatwe usethepramanas in the right manner. For example, here we have shown that many of the conclusions of science regarding the observer is based on an unwarranted comparison of the self with the body-mind-world.
