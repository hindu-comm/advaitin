So, this gets back to the point that when we speak of jiva, its sense-organs, its act of perceiving etc., it is logically necessary that reference to Brahman includes within it an intrinsic mayashakti. It is this mayashakti (the Lord's power to imagine, as per Mandukya) that in the context of jiva self-identification (in ajnana, or vyavaharika) is manifest as its power to know, desire, act (jnana, iccha, kriya shakti). We cannot create two separate realities, talk about the jiva and world as if they exist but as if entirely distinct and separate from Brahman. The jiva is no doubt mithya; but its aham is non-different from Brahman. The jiva's seeing (as an act of perception in its world) is also mithya; but it is sublated as the eternal light of the Self modulated by the mayashakti of the Self.

  

I have discussed this before. If anyone talks anything of jiva, world, jnani, etc. and then of Brahman alongside, they are not talking paramarthika standpoint where the Dreamer-dream duality is entirely negated in thenon-dual Brahman. They are very much talking of that Reality from within their vyavaharika standpoint, and hence will be positingin some sense the Dreamer-dream duality. And when they posit this duality, my understanding is that the dream is of the Dreamer. The seer in every dream character is non-different from the Dreamer of the entire dream.

Continuing from above. But that does not mean that self identified as this dream-character is not also identified as the actor in that dream. There is the individual gross-body that is part of Viraat, the universal body. There is the individual subtle-body (mind) that is part of Hiranyagarbha, the universal Mind. The shakti of the Self manifests in the context of this individual identification; while removing the onion peels of identification, we keep affirming the Self+Mayashakti as the only reality; and when the peels all fallout, what remains?
