Praveen-ji,

  

Thanks for taking the time for your valuable responses.

  

My comments below:

  

1\) You wrote:

The very name Vedanta Paribhasha tells us that its goal has to be Vedanta, whether it serves the purpose in vyavahAra completely to everyone's interest doesn't seem relevant to me, particularly. It is similar to alaukika upAya of Vedas; there is another way that no one else needs to agree with as long as it serves the purpose of the yajamAna. Or yogipratyakSha is pratyakSha to the Yogi, whether others agree or not is irrelevant.  

  

In this sense that you don't care about establishing its validity in vyavahAra as long as it is useful and as long as it servesits purpose to you personally, you also seem to be leaning towardsPosition 1, which is also where I stand presently.

  

2) You wrote:

He is quoting science, not yukti and anubhava. The light from the stars millions of years old being seen now are definitely neither yukti nor anubhava. They are clearly science. Tomorrow, if (even though a big if) science changes their view, VP has to be rewritten again!  

  

The light from distant stars that are no longer there, being seen now, are an established vyAvahArika fact, a part of the physical/natural order of Iswara. A vedantin perhaps need not refute such things that don't conflict with the tAtparyam of vedanta. They are clearly established yukti anubhava and can be verified and repeated on a smaller scale with a flare that lights up for a few seconds and dies and observers at a distance will continue to see the flare even after it dies.

  

What about a bent pencil in a glass of water? If the vrtti was enveloping the object, I should see a straight pencil.

  

Swami M's interpretation has the possibility of explaining the pratyaksha of such phenomenon as it is the light that reaches the golaka. I am not sure what VP's explanation for that is. I haven't seen satisfactory answers as yet.But as you appear to be quite happy in Position 1, and likely not interested in Position 2 at all if I understand right, such observations are probably of no concern to you, which is fine. It is really a concern for someone who is in Position 2, or wants to be in Position 2.

  

3) I had written:

Alladi Mahadev Shastriji also doesn't seem to write about sense organs extending beyond their physical extremities. Note the usage of "up to the physical regions of sensation", "reach the extremeties of the physical organs of sensation" etc Perhaps he is also doing a similar samanvaya like Swamiji?  

  

to which You wrote:

I particularly don't think so because of "Without the body".

  

Shastri-ji's statement was:

***By the sense-organs which, starting from**the heart—the seat of Antah-karana—reach the extremities of the*** ***physical organs of sensation,** **Atman becomes conscious of sound***

***and other sense-objects within and without the body.** *

Shastri-ji's position looks (at least on the surface) similar to Swami M's because he claims that sense organs **"*****starting from**the heart—the seat of Antah-karana—*reach the extremeties of physical organs of sensation"**, which seems to imply not beyond the golakas, but still he says, **"Atman become conscious of sounds and other senses without the body"**, meaning sounds and sense objects that are outside the body, that could only be if in his view, the sense objects reached the golakas somehow. I don't see how both of his statements in bold could be true otherwise (arthApatti). Unless of course, he left unstated that the sense organs go beyond the golakAs, but such an important point should have been explicitly stated perhaps!

  

  

4) You wrote:

Perhaps so, and that is a goodsamanvaya, but I still can't place sphulingavat because the example is like the sparks fly off, not stay within!

  

Sparks fly off from the heart/antaHkarana to the golakAs via the nADIs is what he is interpreting.

  

5\) You wrote:

dRShTisamakAlInashRShTiH or dRShTiH eva sRShTiH.In the former, VV and PV are surely possible.  

  

Can you elaborate a bit on the difference between both the types? Did NishaladAsa discuss this distinction, and if so could you please provide a reference to VS?And are you saying that in the latter VV and PV are not possible, or not needed?

  

6) Lastly, if indeed there is gamanam of sUkshma sharIra vrtti to vishayAs outside, and vrtti vyApti and phala vyApti etc, they all sound like apaUrusheya vishaya, beyond the range of laUkika pramANas available to humans. Normally we know of such sUkshma matters from the shruti. We have discussed one Shankara/Sureshwara reference. Do you know of any shruti references? How else would we explain the origin of this knowledge especially the fact that it seems to have been really formalized only in the 17th century in VP which is clearly a paUrusheya work?

  

regards,
