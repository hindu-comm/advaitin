I did not know about this last assertion on sparks being seen after a few seconds. I also think even in science this would not be considered valid interpretation of this phenomena, due to the incredible speed of light.

  

For the first part, is there any example of a star that astronomershave proved does not exist any longer but which humans still see with the naked eye? Can they point at a star in the night sky and tell us that one is no longer existing? If yes, and we can confirm that their method of proof of non-existence also does not have an implicit circularity bias to their theory of perception, then that may serve to contradict the notion that the human observer sees the star by direct contact. If No, then we have no way of making this assertion that stars no longer there are still being seen now (by the human observer).

> What about a bent pencil in a glass of water? If the vrtti was
> enveloping the object, I should see a straight pencil.
>
>   
>
> Swami M's interpretation has the possibility of explaining the
> pratyaksha of such phenomenon as it is the light that reaches the
> golaka. I am not sure what VP's explanation for that is. 

  

To know the pencil as it is, there must not be obstruction ("avarana") in the path of the sense-organ. When the obstruction of eyelids are opened, the eye's path is clear to the glass but not further. In this case, the refracting glass and the water are obstructions, so what is seen when the eye is opened is the image formed by the light at the outer surface of the glass.
