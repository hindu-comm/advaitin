You were likely referring to this sentence:

  

"The Self is the only knower, seer, projecting and cognizing this world and also assuming this individual identity in the context of these upadhis. When the obstruction to jnana is partially cleared (manifest in a **modification of the upadhis**), the Self's cognition of the object of knowledge, that is its "seeing" of its own manifestation (not outside Self), is immediate and direct. What I in jiva see is what I in Ishvara manifest here and now. And **based on this seeing, the Self manifests vritti and other body-mind changes.**"

  

I did not say 'the Self' is now 'seen'. I was referring to cognition of objects,and let me keep thefocus on that topic here.

  

If my eyes are closed, there is an obstruction to jnana of the chair. So by the kriya shakti of the self, the obstruction is cleared through "modification of upadhis", i.e. by opening the eyelids. The VP theory implies that once the obstruction is cleared (by effecting the needed changes to body-mind-world), the knowledge of the corresponding object is immediate and direct.

  

Why immediate and direct? Because the self is non-dual and all knowledge is already in the self. However the jiva's ajnana, manifest in its upadhi identification, hides from cognition the total knowledge of the self's manifestation as Ishvara. But it has the power to partially clear the obstruction through modification of its upadhis and surrounding world; this opens its senses+mind to a larger expanse of the manifest (say that includes the chair). This is the direct contact and (logically) subsequent manifestation of vritti in the mind. The light of the self shines on this vritti and further body-mind changes follow. Etc. That is the idea.

  

Putting aside the scientific theory, let's imagine being in a dark room. The obstruction of darkness is removed when a lamp is lit. Immediately we see the chair. We should think that our consciousness is already everywhere in the room, only restricted by the darkness; so as soon as the darkness is removed, the light of awareness 'expands' to include the chair. Figuratively perhaps, we say the sense organ goes out and reaches the object as if it is a time-drawn process, then this happens and that happens. But the fact is they all happen instantaneously at the subtle level, so it is more like what was already in the knowledge of the self merely comes into awareness when the ajnana is removed. Once this instantaneous cognition happens, at the gross level, subsequent changes follow and this part is time-drawn.
