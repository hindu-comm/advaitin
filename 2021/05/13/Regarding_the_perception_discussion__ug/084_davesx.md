Namaste Advitins:  

  

I've been following this discussion with amusement and now that it's apparently suspended may I interject with the following comments:

  

The theory that the subtle sense-perception-mind (Sukshma Sharira or Antarkarana) goes out of body, takes shapes of the objects, and returns with the form, has been an argument postulated by the old school of Nyaya ever since but to this day proven incorrect, falling outside the ambit of Vedanta and by extension Advaita. I have not read Sri Chittaji book except for a few extracts here and there enough to get the flavor. If his theory of perception is peer reviewed and approved by scholars and the academia, he will certainly go down in history as the first proponent of Nyaya to do so!  

  

The western schools of realism say the object produces an impression on the retina and is then communicated to the brain which interprets the image as a representation of the object. The idealists like Berkley say the object is an idea inside the mind and not outside it. It can be argued that additionally both the realists and idealists viewpoints offer incomplete explanations.

  

There is an appearance seemingly outside, that cannot be denied as idealists do, but Vedantins say it is only ideas projected. We may perceive such sensations as if external, but investigation is necessary to verify what the senses report. After inquiry it is realized an imagined snake was actually a rope as the old cliche goes. This simply shows that one cannot rely on the senses. Then if one's senses distort, have limited bandwidth and therefore unreliable how does one know that the object perceived really exists at a location in space and time?  

  

Those who believe the object is there are accepting the imputation of their own imagination. Sensations are but imaginations. The first step in Vedanta is to know the nature of the world as transitional ideations, not quite different from dream, and then proceed to investigate who (or what) knows or is aware of this.  

  

Until one know that the world including oneself are conceptual constructs, explanations must be sought but they will always be unsatisfactory.  

  

In BG (Chap.13) it says one can’t speak of either Sat or Asat in Truth. Mandukya however explains all things as Aja (unborn) and there is no real creation of apparent objects; here the notion of creation is untenable!
