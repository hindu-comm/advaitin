> > The clearing of obstructions to formation of a pratyaxa vRtti may
> > well include not only the jiva exercising kriya shakti to open the
> > eye lids, but also Ishvara's niyati / manifestation (physical Order)
> > allowing the light to fall on the eyes as a precursor to the vRtti.
> > Such an admission of the role of light as a pratibandhaka-remover
> > does not amount to acceptance of the scientific materialistic theory
> > of perception. (In other words, the possible need for other factors
> > other than one's own kriya shakti may well hold. For example if
> > someone has covered an object, he needs to uncover it before we see
> > the object.) Such a role for Ishvara's shakti in the role of light
> > as a \*precursor\* to the arising of the vRtti does not necessarily
> > imply it (such a theory) is contrary to Advaita. Such is my
> > understanding.
> >
> >   

Yes, The jiva's kriya based on itsdecision to follow iccha or dharma will have only a partial bearing in Ishvara'sfurthermanifestation even where it pertains to this jiva's body-mind. He is maintaining the larger Order in which this jiva's kriya may not attain the desired results. If the jiva does open the eye-lids, it still will not see the chair in a darkroom, no vritti will form unless the chair also is illumined by light thus 'removing' the darkness covering it.

  

Now if we say that the vritti may form only when the reflected light from the chair reaches the eye, that I think willlead to the representationalism discussed before since the knowledge presented by the light and recorded in the vritti is presumably of the object as it was. Not sure if this is how you meant.

  

A couple of related questions:

  

1\. Why can it not be that the jiva's upadhis are such that a jiva cannotattain direct knowledge of object as it is? The argument is that all objects are knowable. Yes, we agree to Ishvarasince everything is His manifestation and theircognition/knowledge is in Him; but why should objects be knowable to the jiva as well? Why cannot it be the case that their upadhis allow only for reperesentational knowledge? (Of course, we would have to utilizeshabda pramana and not merely logic.)

  

2\. If we are ok with limited range for auditory and touch sense, why is it a big deal if the jiva's eye-sense also has limited range only? (Here the jiva can attain direct knowledge including the vision of object but only within a limited range for all senses.)

  

They may not be trivial to answer (from VP standpoint), and may require a comprehensive traditional shastra-based treatment of the subject. When Chitta-ji comes back to discussions, I hope to ask him on these.
