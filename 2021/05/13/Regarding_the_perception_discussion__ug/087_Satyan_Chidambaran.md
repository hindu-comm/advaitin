Namaste Putran-ji,

  

You wrote:

The question is whether cognition is a fundamental property or shakti of the Self, or whether only a phenomenon of the projected world (like fire generates heat). If the latter, then this topic belongs to vyavaharika and what one assumes about it need not impede into one's realization of advaita.

  

If however it is the Self/Chaitanyam that is the cognizer/perceiver/knower of its projection/manifestation/appearance, then there is the possibility that one's theory of perception is incoherent with advaita when it reduces cognition to be a product of the world. An "advaita" that is compatible with such a theory may not be what the Vedic tradition or scripture teaches.  

  

The Self/Chaitanyam is just sat, chit, anAnanda. It is not a knower (pramAtA).

If we take AbhAsavAda prakriyA for understanding what constitutes the pramAtA, the pramAtA is a mixture of

AtmA (original consciousness) + antaHkaraNam (mind) + chidAbhAsa (reflected consciousness)

  

AtmA alone cannot be a knower as it is nirvikAra (changeless)

Mind alone cannot be a knower as it is jadam (inert)

chidAbhAsa alone cannot be a knower as it cannot exist without the AtmA (original consciousness) or the Mind (which is the reflecting medium)

  

Therefore, in order to be a knower, the mixture of the three is needed.

  

Yes, Bramhan (with mAyA) projects both the jaDa world and the sUkShma and sthUla sharIras of the jIvAtmAs. And then it as though enters the
sUkShma sharIra as the reflected consciousness (chidAbhAsa). Objects in the world are jaDa. By themselves,

they can neither know anything, nor make themselves known. For anobject to be known, the vRRitti in the sUkShma sharIra needsto assume the form of the object to be known, and then thevRRitti shines with the consciousness reflected/borrowed from AtmA,

thus making the object known. All of this happens in the mere presence of Atma/Bramhanwithout which there can be no existence for anything, nor any shine (reflected/borrowed consciousness) for the vRRittis/minds.

  

Once by mahAvakya shravaNam, one has understood aham brahmAsmi, I am the very sat (existence) which is the substratum of

all and everything else is superimposed upon Me, everything else is a lower order of reality, both the individual knower (pramAta) and the known world are reduced tomithyA. What to talk of the knowing process that happens within the knower? That is alsoclearly in the domain of mithyA only.

  

A theory of perception is primarily defining how the vRRitti assumes the form of the object.VP says the vRRitti goes out andpervades the external objectto assume the form. Then the consciousness reflected/borrowedfrom Atma makes it shine.

  

Fine, that is an explanation. But if for some reason that explanation is compromised, and we need to replace it with another

explanation, it isn't going to affect the tAtparyam that "aham brahmAsmi, and the pramAta and world are mithyA". It would

just provide perhaps a different explanation for how the vRRiti assumes the form of the object (which doesn't in any way affect

the tAtparyam of Vedanta).

  

You wrote:

Anycase, my point here is abouttheunwarranted looseusage of prakriya in thistopic.  

  

I don't see anything unwarranted or loose about it. It may be better to see a prakriyA as a prakriyA, rather than mistake it for

tAtparyam or go on a wild goose chase to establish its vyAvahArika satyatvam, which may not necessarily be there. It is

no doubt a simple and extremely useful prakriyA for understanding the tAtparyam though.

  

  

regards,

--Satyan

The question is whether cognition is a fundamental property or shakti of the Self, or whether only a phenomenon of the projected world (like fire generates heat). If the latter, then this topic belongs to vyavaharika and what one assumes about it need not impede into one's realization of advaita.

An "advaita" that is compatible with such a theory may not be what the Vedic tradition or scripture teaches.

  

This is where I understand this debate to be centered at. I am not the expert to defend VP but I think people who consider the topic as merely of vyavaharika/mithya interest may be missing this central issue that others think has direct bearing on the way the siddhanta is understood.

  

One place where this subtlety may be indicated is the following: Advaita does not merely say that there is a maayicshow of world and an unchanging reality that is entirely distinct; not just jagan mithya, Brahma satyam. It says further jivo brahmaiva naaparaha, that "aham brahmaasmi".

  

One way to understand this: The Self is the only knower, seer, projecting and cognizing this world and also assuming this individual identity in the context of these upadhis. When the obstruction to jnana is partially cleared (manifest in a modification of the upadhis), the Self's cognition of the object of knowledge, that is its "seeing" of its own manifestation (not outside Self), is immediate and direct. What I in jiva see is what I in Ishvara manifest here and now. And based on this seeing, the Self manifests vritti and other body-mind changes.

  

Anycase, my point here is abouttheunwarranted looseusage of prakriya in thistopic. I cannot say I am fully clear of the necessity of VP either, at least with regard to why one cannot restrict the jiva's vision and depend on Shastra to confirm the external world. (Provided one can support properly with shastra.) I will leave that debate to Chitta-ji and other scholars of the shastra and traditional viewpoint.
