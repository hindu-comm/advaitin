Putran-ji,

  

Lets ignore stars for the time being for being too far away it is not feasible for astronomers to tell which are there and which are not.

But when you look at the night sky, let there be no doubt that many of the stars that appear to be there are not there any more.

  

If you ask how do we know, one can create an experiment on a smaller scale and then extend it to a larger scale.

Make your own small star (say a strong flare) that stays lit up for a short while (say N seconds). Place a first observer next to the flare. Place a second observer N-light seconds away (1-light second being the distance that light travels in 1 second).. You can choose N. Now provide each observer a high resolution clock and their clocks are synchronized.

  

Step 1. Start the flare and wait for N seconds. At the end of the Nth second, both observers should see the flare shining.

Step 2. Now Extinguish the flare and each observer has been instructed to press the clock the moment they see the flare disappear.

Step 3. Collect the timings from each observer.

  

If the understanding of laws of nature as reported by science is right,
the second observer should see the flare go away about N seconds after the first observer does, which means N seconds after the flare actually goes away.

  

Now it is possible to see how this works on a larger scale. Replace N-light-sec distance by an observer who is N-million-light years away and the flare shines for N milliion of years and it is the same phenomenon on a larger scale.

  

The flare experiment would be pratyaksha/anubhava.

The extension to stars would be an anumAna/yukti based on the pratyaksha experiment.

  

To know the pencil as it is, there must not be obstruction ("avarana") in the path of the sense-organ. When the obstruction of eyelids are opened, the eye's path is clear to the glass but not further. In this case, the refracting glass and the water are obstructions, so what is seen when the eye is opened is the image formed by the light at the outer surface of the glass.
