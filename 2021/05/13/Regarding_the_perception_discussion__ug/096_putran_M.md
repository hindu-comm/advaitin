> 1\) How do you know that there is an image formed? What you see is a
> bent pencil, not an image of a bent pencil.

> 

  

What I know is that the glass and water obstruct directvision of thepencil. So I cannot tell that I am seeing inside of these obstructions and also directly the pencil, or whether what I see is a composite of the glass, the water, the pencil; or whether the presence of pencil+water's reflected light at the surface of the glass (which I called image) dueto the glass obstruction not allowing the sense organ to go further. I took it to be the latter but in any case, It is clear that these obstructions prevent a vritti that separately and wholly forms the image of the pencil in itself, and whatever it does form of the objects met by the organ and mind includes the bent appearance of the pencil.

  

This is not a case of bhramawhere we see the rope and think it is a snake. What we see forms a vritti of a bent pencil (along with the intervening water and glass). That is what is available to the direct contact of the sense-organ and mind. But it is not directly the pencil alone unto itself, so this question of "why is the pencil appearing bent when we make direct contact?" is misplaced. There could be bhramaof the type "I am seeing the pencil directly", "the pencil is bent", "I am seeing the light after it has entered my eye", etc.

> 2\) If you quote science to say that there is an image formed
> somewhere, I would ask what are the rules for engaging science?

> science when it is convenient to support VP, but then dismiss science

VP does stand on its own. That an 'image' forms at the outer surface of the glass is my logical conclusion (arthapatti) of the fact we see the appearance of pencil inspiteof the glass obstruction.

  

Or there may be certain type of non-distorting glass that allows the sense-organ to pass through and the water may still be an obstruction that does not. 

  

Either way, you are always seeing the object or objects directly and as they are manifest at their location; in thiscase, that which you see includes the bent appearance of the pencil. Perhaps you only see the pencil directly above the water whereas you see the image presented by the light at the surface of the water.

  

As to rules of engaging the purvapakshin, yes I think we can invoke their beliefs in a discussion, where they align with our siddhanta or where their objections to our siddhanta contradicts their own beliefs. So the vedantin (or VP proponent) can see for himself (i.e. as per his accepted pramanas) the empirical observations of science in the gross world. But with regard to the arthapatti and anumana that scientists do off their experiments, we can admit them provisionally to the extent they are not contradicting us. We have to be careful here since science may be using pramanas in ways that we do not accept.
