Putran-ji,

  

Thanks for your pertinent and thoughtful responses. Comments below:

  

1\) You wrote:

Or rather, in the sense that the final reduction from this reference standpoint is Brahman+Maya, in that Maya is the now intrinsic shakti of Brahman to imagine/project and know/cognize as Ishvara-jiva-jagat. So Brahman or Self is the knower of its projection: there is no other Knower but He.  

  

You are right in saying that Bramhan + mAyA projects the world, but I wouldn't say Bramhan is a Knower (which generally translates as pramAtA in Vedanta/Sanskrit). One can say Atma/Bramhan is a changeless witness (which translates as sAkShi), perhaps that is what

you meant, but that is only with reference to the world, but in reality, it cannot be said to be a witness/sAkShi also....it is just existence, consciousness (sat, chit). Perhaps, it is just a matter of using the right words. The words in sanskrit convey the meanings more accurately as there is a differentiation of pramAtA/sAkShi concepts.

  

2\. The issue is whether the jiva cognizes/knows the object, or something else that is representative of the object.

  

Science says the latter; VP aligns with those who hold the former.

  

Agree. Personally, I don't have a problem with either. Both are different ways of explaining how the vRiitti is formed.

Such a theory needn't be perfect, for VedAnta's goal is not to make us understand a theory of cognition, but the

goal is Atma GYanam. Atma GYanam comes through ShravaNam of Vedanta (and in particular the mahAvAkya)

from a shrotriya bramhaniShTha Guru and not by understanding a theory of cognition from books or proponents

of such theories.

  

Note that knowledge of a theory of cognition only removes one thing: "ignorance of the theory of cognition".

It can never remove Self Ignorance, which is only removed by Atma GYanam, and the means for it are different

as stated above.

  

  

3\. I am myself not clear why one cannot the claim that the**jiva**may not have knowledge of object. The reason being it is the jiva.

  

I am not sure why you say jIva may not have knowledge of object. I would say \*only\* jIva (=pramAtA) can have knowledge

of any object! As described earlier, pure Atma/Bramhan being nirvikAra cannot be a pramAtA! All GYanam (including Atma GYanam)

takes place in the intellect only, which is clearly a component of the jIva.

  

4.Subbu-ji mentioned ofscriptural references of direct contact of sense organs with the sense objects. Those deserve a deeper study.

  

This again is something that in my mind has to be a point of debate, whether the understanding on perception/cognition is tied to the tatparyam of Vedanta.  

  

Sure. Looks like one may find "literal"references of of contact of sense organs with sense objects....but it would be good to see if there is a clearstand on the issue of whether it means sense object outside, versus sense object at the golaka. My understanding is that the Shruti/Shankara/Sureshwara etc did not state/elaborate anything explicitly. Silence on an issue can also be quite loud....perhapsit was not of much consequence to them? And clearly they were GYanIs, and there were other GYAnIsbefore the 17th century when VP was formalized! If it was so central to GYAnam, how could there have been GYAnIs before VP ideas came into being?

  

As I understand, most ofthe VP work was likely the result of later/post Shankara AchAryas in order to create a helpful prakriyA for students, or to respondto other schools, or both. It is quite possible that Shankara and Sureshwara etc did not even know what the literal terms: vRRitti vyApti andphala vyApti etc meant for those terms were probably not even existent then, and nor do similar ideas (if not the terms) find elaboration in the Shruti or their works. If this was so central to understanding Advaita, why would the Shruti and our primary AchAryas havenot bothered to even casually elaborateon those ideas?

  

All these can be indicators that the theory of cognition is not tied to the tAtparyam of Vedanta which is only in understanding "brahma satyam jagan mithyA jIvo brahmaiva nAparaH".

  

5.Well, this is a point of debate, right? We can't just assume it. There may be alternate explanations and conclusions for observations of scientificexperiments as well.

  

Yes, which is why I said that a theory of perception needn't be perfect, because there can be no finality in vyAvahArika plane.

The more one probes into mAyA, the more questions it raises rather than answers. Science isn't perfect, and we needn't

assert that VP explanation is perfect either. It just needs to be good enough to get the job done. A pole that the pole vaulter

uses to cross over the bar. When he goes over to the other side, job of the pole is done. That is what a prakriyA is.

Use it, but don't get stuck with it, or one will not be able to leave it.....because proving it may not be possible because

of the nature of vyavahAra/mAyA, and proving or establishing it in vyavahAra is not required for GYanam either. Even

scientists do not claim that they have proved their theories. A proper scientist says "this is my current understanding based

on my current observations, but if I discover something new that doesn't fit in my framework, I need to revise it.".

This is the nature of vyavahAra. There is no finality. If that is true for science, it is probably true for VP as well because VP

has also ventured into explaining vyavahAra only. Our Acharyas who developed it intended it as a prakriyA (the pole!) to

arrive at GYanam/tAtparyam, and not as the last word for the phenomenon of perception, which is also in vyavahAra.

  

Regards,

--Satyan
