Namaste,

  

One issue that I am wrestling with is how in the alternative theory of light from objects reaching the eyes, it is consciousness that manifests objects.

  

The sarvaprakAshakatva, all illuminating nature of consciousness would be lost - ie shruti statements such as तस्य सर्वमिदं विभाति (tasya bhAsA sarvamidam vibhAti, by its illumination is all this illuminated) would be called into question, because, arguably, what is illuminated by consciousness is the light reflecting off objects and not the objects themselves.

  

The vedAntic epistemology has been postulated to explain how a connection can be possible between the relationless consciousness and objects - ie nothing should be cognised because (1) there is nothing apart from consciousness and (2) consciousness can have no connection with anything. (asaNgo hyayam puruShah).

  

Hence yatra vA asya sarvamAtmaivAbhUt.. tatkena kam pashyet.. tatkena kam vijAnIyAt.

  

To address this conundrum, it is said that the connection between consciousness and objects is by means of a vRtti.

  

In the case of perception, the vRtti goes to the object leading to the consciousnesses delimiting the object to be manifested / ignorance in the object delimited consciousness to be removed. When that happens, that consciousness illuminates the object. As the vRtti from the knower has reached there, the knower consciousness becomes one with the object delimited consciousness, leading for the knower to claim "I see the object".

  

In the case of paroksha, indirect cognitions, the vRtti does not go out to the object, so all that is removed is the ignorance of the existence of the object (asattvApAdaka ajnAna), but the abhAnApAdaka ajnAna (the ignorance obscuring the appearance of the object) located in the consciousnesses delimited by the object, is not removed, as the vRtti has not gone out to the object. So the knower says "I know it exists, but I don't see it". However here too, it is consciousness that reveals the object's existence, even when the object itself does not appear.

  

This allows consciousness to be sarvabhAsakam, the one that is illuminating objects, while preserving the dRgdRshya sambandha anupapatti - the untenability of consciousness and objects having a real relationship.

  

It is the latter (dRgdRshya sambandha anupapatti) that is used as the anukUla tarka, the supportive reasoning, for the dRshyatva hetu being sufficient to prove mithyAtva, ie the world is mithyA, because it is dRshya (cognised). Why should cognisability prove the mithyAtva of the world? Because there is no real relationship between possible between the knower and the known, despite which, objects are known.

  

If we agree with the scientific theory of perception, or a modification of it, while dRgdRshya sambandha anupapatti is preserved, sarvabhAsakatva is not.
