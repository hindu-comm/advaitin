Namaste Venkat ji

A small follow up question/point.

  

In denying existence to objects unless illumined by chit, the "vishaya reaching the golakas" based approach encounters a problem. Either we assert non existence of such viShayas Or we do not have sarvaprakAshatvaM of AtmA. This is the challenge.

  

Any representational model has this problem, as I understand which direct contact seeks to bridge. (I am personally speaking, convinced however that what we see and what an instrument detects are going to be the same. And the experiment suggested by Chitta ji and Satyan ji would yield a non-synchronized perception by two observers stationed at different distances as suggested.)

  

But can we bring in Ishvara sRShTi and say that the "original" pAnchabhautika object (whose prabhavas form a viShayacchAya which is illumined by jiva's chit, is itself ishvara sRShTi and its (object's) existence (asti) and knowability (bhAti) is always 'maintained' so to say by Ishvara whose svarUpa is sat-chit? An objection could be that in such a scheme, the actual objects themselves remain nitya paroxa for the jIva - not a desirable assertion. And how do we assert that the perception we have is veridical w.r.t. the actual object? Also, given svataHprAmANyam of pratyaxa, we should not have to bring in any kind of inference or Ishvara (shabda) to assert the veridical nature of what we perceive by pratyaxa.

  

Overall, the VP and Dakshina moorthy Stotram have the authenticity of sticking to a single aparoxa consciousness which lends its shine to objects as Satyan-ji put it. That makes it more direct as a prakriya to take the shine off the objects and transfer it in our understanding to where it belongs viz., aparoxAtmA.

  

Om

Raghav
