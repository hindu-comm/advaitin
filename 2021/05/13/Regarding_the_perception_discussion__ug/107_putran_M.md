When we talk about jiva and world, then reference to Brahman includes maya as its shakti. That is what I meant by Brahman+maya; it is only this Brahman that can be referenced in the standpoint that includes reference to the projection.

  

When we say Brahman (+Maya) projects the world, it is inherently the Knower of the world it projects. Put another way, it is Brahman's cognition that is this projection/imagination/appearance of ishvara-jiva-jagat. What He sees is what is manifest, and what is manifest He sees. In the case of the jiva, this cognition is superimposed by its ajnana, projecting a corresponding mithya world and hiding that its Reality is Brahman alone.

  

This is similar to the dream world that is nothing but the projection of a Dreamer; yet all cognitions in the dream, of jiva-jagat-ishvara, are the cognition of the Dreamer alone. But the content of the dream, the jiva, the veda, dharma, the jiva's identification as pramata in that world, etc. are all based in the ignorance of the Knowledge that all this is Brahman alone, appearing as many etc. The negation is not of the fact of cognition but of the bhramaregarding the reality; not of the rope but of the identification as snake.

  

Now then, what about the reference of Brahman as if devoid of association with maya? As nirvikara, sakshi, etc.? Here I do agree with you that there is a prakriya in operation. The Brahman being referred to in this manner is strictly non-dual; there is no tag-along jagat that needs pointing as mithya or maya. The duality of Dreamer and dream is not there in Turiya or the paramarthika standpoint: that duality is itself mithya and not simply the duality that presents within a dream. But this Brahman that is satya is truly beyond the access of words and thoughts.

  

Yet, we must refer to it as the Reality of all this, and so we do. Only problem is that people think in terms of a new coeval duality between the non-dual Brahman and their dualistic world! This may be fine as a prakriya but it starts to mislead in the conversations of cognition/perception thatwe are having.

The scientific theory of perception/cognition leads to contradiction of Vedanta in some fundamental sense (say by implying an irresolvable duality of self and not-self). You may not agree with this but let us just for the moment assume this was the case.

  

Now suppose we have ajiva who is convinced about the scientific theory, does not see the conflict thinking it is after all mithya, and says that the Ignorance is removed only through the methods stated above.

  

Is his shraddha in a theory that leads to contradiction of Vedanta liable to be an obstruction to his attainment of Knowledge of Self? It seems to me that at some point his knowledge will have to dispel the ignorant notion he is presently convinced about, and till then it will be a subconscious impediment to his progress along the traditional path.

Again, nirvikara Brahman is not in this discussion. The jiva's reference of Brahman is only Brahman+Maya. Brahman is the seer who sees the seen. Resolve the projection in the cognition in the Self; that is nirvikaarawhere words and mind cannot reach. But if you talk of projection of Self, the cognition is also of that Self. He projects as this jiva in body-mind upadhis, constrained by a manifest ajnana that obstructs total knowledge of His projection as Ishvara. But upon partial removal of ajnana/avarana for this jiva, there is immediate and direct cognition of the object that is unobstructed thereafter.

  

That is the principle on which I see VP is based on: the direct and immediate cognition of the object upon the removal of obstruction to that knowledge. So YES the VP person definitely asserts that the jiva can have direct knowledge of object(through cognition or direct contact). Only I wanted to know why, because for me, the object is ever in Isvhara's cognition: i.e. we do not suggest the projection is not in cognition of Self. The jiva however has already upadhis that obstruct in part, so why can it not be that these upadhis fundamentally prevent all direct knowledge of object? That is the purvapakshin's doubt in me. Note that the scientific theory by proposing that the jiva can only see (simplifying even here) the light that has entered its eyes concedes that direct visual nowledge/cognition of object is impossible for the jiva.
