Namaste

I had occasion to briefly talk to Acharya Haribrahmendrananda ji who is proficient in brihat-prasthAnam and teaches Vedanta and nyAya in Uttarkashi.

  

One positive development is that in uttarakhand most eligible citizens particularly the acharyas and swamis have been vaccinated by government initiative. This is btw.

  

He gave a few inputs about VP without too much opportunity for elaboration unfortunately. So I am not intending to write these as ideas to be accepted per se, but that a traditional acharya well versed in vedAnta, nyAya and brihatprasthAnam holds the following to be true.

  

1\. VP has several elements opposed to shankara bhAShya.

2\. VP draws heavily from mAnameyodaya a mImAmsaka grantha on pramANas. Many of the ideas being not necessarily in consonance with Shankara vedanta.

3\. VP also is influenced by sAmKhya to a point where deviations from bhAShya are seen in its treatment of mind and indriyas. In sAMkhya both the mind and indriyas are vyApaka . By themselves they are not limited to the body. Only our golaka/body as upAdhi gives us a partial manifestation of their illumining capacity. VP accepts such sAMkhya assertions without needing to do so from vedantic necessity.

4\. The caxu, tejas tattva in the object, and rashmi (light) all have tejas tattva origin and the idea of light mediated vRtti formation in pratyaxa is quite defensible in vedanta. In other words, viShayacchAya idea is defensible. PUrNa GYAnam of any object is never anyway obtained. Neither do we get GYAnam of the inner material which constitutes the object. Only the tejas tatva of the veneer/reflecting surface of the object with light (also the dame tejas manifestation) as the avAntara kAraNa gives pratyaxa knowledge.

5\. The primary pUrvapaxIin VP is the naiyayika and so even certain acceptable ideas of nyAya such as the role of indriyas and golakas in pratyaxa is contradicted and replaced by an alternative theory of going out to the objects etc.

6\. VP denies any bhAgatyAgalaxaNa in understanding mahAvAkya

  

So at least a few contemporary scholarly acharyas do not view VP as sacrosanct at all.

  

Om

Raghav
