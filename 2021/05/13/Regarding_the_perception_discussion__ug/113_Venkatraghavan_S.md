I am not sure whether the new prakriyA "brings us to" (I am not sure in what sense you mean it, but I mean it in the sense of "conveying") the tAtparyam, explaining as it does how perception occurs in vyavahAra - but for that matter, the traditional prakriyA in the vedAnta paribhASha is also only explaining perception in vyavahAra.

  

The difference between the two is not with regard to whether the prakriyA conveys the tAtparya of vedAnta or not . Rather, the difference is whether either prakriyA suffers from contradiction with other pramANa-s.

  

The traditional prakriyA is contradicted bythe findings of science in the respect that we have seen. There are many, even in this list, that do not agree with this view. But since you agree that itdoes, I am mentioning it.

  

The new prakriyA however, is contradicted (in my view) by shruti statements such as "tasya bhAsA sarvamidamvibhAti".

I do care for the prAmANya of shruti. We insist that a mithyA jagat is illumined by consciousness because the shruti says so. The tarka that we employ must be shrutisammata tarka - श्रुतिमतस्तर्कोऽनुसंधीयताम् as AchArya says.

  

Someone may say - the shruti is also mithyA so why should one care? To which we should reply, when we say shruti is mithyA what we mean is that shruti undergoes bAdha by one one only one thing brahmajnAna. Until that happens,its prAmANya is supreme -प्रमाणं त्वाऽऽत्मनिश्चयात्.

  

So we should not stand by a prakriyA which falsifies shruti in vyavahAra, because if shruti's prAmANya during vyavahAra undergoes bAdha, there is no other way to transcend vyavahAra.

I had something similar in mind. I was thinking that every chakshu/shrotrendriyam has a particular span outside the body that it can reach. Objects within that span are grasped exactly as in the VP prakriya - the vRtti goes out to the object, takes on the AkAra of the object, AvaraNa abhibhava happens, it is perceived.

  

Light and sound hitting the outside edge of the boundary is objectified in the same way as objects within the boundary - however, here the object is not objectified. Only light and sound are objectified.

  

However, there is no experience "only light and sound are objectified" because weare so used to objects within the sensory field being directly objectified, thatwethink the same phenomenon works for objects outside the sensory field too - that is the cause of the tAdAtmya between the two experiences. That is why even when only perceiving the light from the stars we think it is the stars that are being seen.

  

That way,

\(1\) consciousness has the capacity to objectify everything - only the limitation of the senses limits which are directly objectified. तस्य भासा सर्वमिदं विभाति holds good

\(2\) At the same time, there is accommodation with empirical evidence from science.

\(3\) The experience is always of "I experience the object", not "I experience aspects of the object"
