Namaste Satyan ji and Venkatji

  

You wrote - (and corroborated by Venkat ji)

"One could say that the agni tattvam from the surface of object pervades the space around the object. Therefore the rUpa guNa of the object is also stretching out in space. That agni comes in contact with the chaKshurindriya, the image is formed. In this sense, you are still contacting the object directly but the boundary of where the object ends becomes blurred because one of its guNas stretches out into space around the object. Hence, in VP mind goes out to the object, but in VP2, one could say that the agni tattvam of the object could stretch out to the chakShu golaka. This would help establish more direct contact with the object if that pleases you more. And may also help address thesarvaprakAshakatvaconcernif you think it is tAtparyam (but I would like to understand the reasons for why one should insist that it be tAtparyam)."

  

Thats an excellent explanation by you. (VP2)

Actually Acharya Swamiji was saying something similar.

  

The following is only my restatement of what you and Venkat ji wrote.

  

If as per VP, the mind which is a pAnchabhautika entity can '\*stretch\*' and take on '\*exactly the same\*' form of the viShaya without being labelled as a representational prakriya (after all, the antaHkaraNa too can be accused of merely going and forming a representation/image of the object), then why can't the rUpa guNa that inheres in the surface of the object also, facilitated by the light/rashmi (also of the tejas tatva), \*stretch\* out to the golaka and impress upon the manas+indriya the same AkAra as the object - this should also by the same logic constitute a direct contact non-representational prakriyA but also have the advantage of accommodating time delays.

  

This latter prakriya VP2 would be more universal in accounting for both nearby and far away objects without falling foul of empirical observations.

  

In this latter VP2 prakriya too, there is only a minor modification (i should think); there is direct contact and abheda between viShayAvacchinnachaitanyam and pramAtRaavacvhinna chaitanyam - \*only the location\* where the abheda occurs is at the location of the pramAtA.

  

Am I missing something in VP2 which renders it too as problematic as VP1 in some other way?

  

If VP1 preserves sarvaprakAshakatva, so too does VP2. The role of light remains the same in both. Because even in VP1, without light the vRtti does not form, even though the mind carrying the reflection of chaitanyam could still stretch.

  

Again, the agency is still retained by the pramAtA because unless his mind is joined to that particular indriya, the vishaya may well stretch upto the pramAtA, but the vRtti will not form. As in the case of a daydreamer not noticing what is in front of him, or a person engrossed in a book does not hear a sound nearby. So the tasya bhAsA sarvamidam vibhAti is maintained. Perception is not passive.

  

If VP1 is shrutisammata so also would VP2?

  

Om

Raghav
