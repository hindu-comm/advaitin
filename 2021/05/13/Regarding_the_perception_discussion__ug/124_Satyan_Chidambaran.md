You wrote:

Even in the AbhAsavAda prakriyA, it is the viShaya adhiShThAnachaitanya that is admitted to be the illuminator of the object,

  

There are certainly traditional texts that assert that in abhAsavAda, the chidAbhAsa illumines the object, I am providing references from vichAra sAgara and also Panchdasi:

  

1\) First, Vichara Sagara Topic 200. Please see the **attachment** (**Vichara Sagara.pdf**).

  

Note that he says that even as the vRRitti is going out from the mind, it already has chidAbhAsa.

  

Then NischaladAsa goes on to say (Topic 202...this part not in attachment) which is titled**बाह्यार्थप्रकाशे वृत्तिचिदाभासयोरुपयोग:**

**(The benefit of thought mode and reflected consciousness in illumination of external objects)**

where He says "चिदाभाससहितघटाद्याकारौ योवृत्तिभाग: स धटादीनामावरणं निवर्तयति।**चिदाभासभागस्तु**

**घटादि विषयं प्रकाशयति**" i.e. "in the thought mode endowed with the reflection of consciousness,

the thought mode part removes the concealment of the pot.**The reflected consciousness**

**part illumines the pot**".

  

2\) Next, Please see Verses 10, 11, 12, 13 from Chp 8 **attached** (**panchadasi.pdf)** that clearly say that the cognition is due to chidAbAsa.

  

You wrote:

This phalam or samvit is the one that reveals the object.  

  

**Sw VidyAraNya says that what varttikakAra means by samvit (the point that Venkatji brought up) is**

**chidAbhAsa only**. Also, the tIkA on Verse 12 says that there is a tussle between AbhAsavAdins

and avacChedavAdins to read a certain meaning to what varttikakAra said ("samvit") to support their own

prakriyAs in the UpadeshasAhasri verses. (please see the English commentary on the PD verses below

especially verse 12 and that discusses this).

  

Swami Paramarthanandaji also had repeatedly clarified during these classes that in AbhAsavAda,

chidAbhAsa is responsible for knownness of an object. Chit is responsible for knownness of

the knownness of the object. Exactly what Verse 13 says in the attachment.

  

I don't know the context of Venkat-ji's last reference. Did siddhikAra made the comment assuming avacChedavAda?

Or did siddhikAra not accept AbhAsavAda at all? If that were the case, that would be consistent with what an

avacChedavAdin would say for an avacChedavAdin would not admit chidAbhAsa at all.

Or did siddhikAra refer to knownness of the knownness?

  

If none of the above, and siddhikAra's statement was indeed in the context of AbhAsavAda prakriyA, it would be contrary  

with what Nischaladasa and Panchadasi say, then that might imply that there are some differences (sibling rivalry) in

the tradition with regards to AbhAsavAda prakriya. In that case, I really don't want to get into a debate as to

who is right etc, for there an be different views in the tradition with regards to prakriyAs, and Shruti's

tAtparyam is not in prakriyAs. So the differences are also fine. With prakriyAs, there is no one right way necessarily.

  

In any case, to step back to our main topic, in both AbhAsavAda and avacChedavAda though, a common element

is the mind going out to the object. We see some yukti and anubhava issues with the mind going to the object,

and hence are trying to modify the prakriyA so that the object comes to the mind (vishayacChAya). In

doing so, we cannot expect conformity with existing tIkAs and prakaraNas etc clearly as we are in

uncharted territory to some extent. The main thing that we should perhaps look for conformity with is the Shruti

and perhaps ensure that we don't contradict our early AchAryas who were probably not as "invested" in the

idea of the mind going out etc and other related prakriyAs.

  

  

You wrote:

  

The तस्य भासा सर्वमिदं विभाति mantra is not just saying that everything seen is seen by consciousness - it is saying that*everything*is cognisedby consciousness (येनेदंसर्वंविजानाति).  

  

The Shruti contradiction question is the more important one. I looked at the shA~Nkara

bhAShyam on tasya bhAsa sarvam idam vibhAti. I was interested to see how He commented on idam.

He says "idam jagat". I read the rest of the bhAshyam also and found nothing there to

contradict the view that "idam"***\*can also\****be taken to mean "idam dRRiShyam jagat" i.e to what I see.

After all, I say "idam" only when I see something. So an understanding that "all that I see (as idam) is illumined by chit" 

that I presented was not being contradicted either in the mUla shruti or bhAshyam.

  

The former would imply that things there are things uncognisedby consciousness,and hence remain unseen, which is not accepted within siddhAnta.  

  

Isn't mAyA an example of a "thing" that is not dRRiShyam? So are various things within mAya such as kArana sharIra,

and effects of mAya such as sUkshma sharIra etc which are beyond the range of perception of jIva. Why should everything in the SRRiShti becognizable (that too especially be a jIva???). And even in VP prakriyA, what is cognized by the sense organs

are the guNas only, not the underlying object! There is no denying that sat-chit pervades everything though. It is only

that certain "things" may not be cognized, which is already the case.

  

As the sAkshi never makes contact with the object in this theory, the object is never illuminated - only its image arriving at the eyes through the medium of light is seen.

  

Even in VP, only the guNas are known. But even though only the guNas are known, the end experience is that the object is illumined. Here also, in VP2, the rUpa guNa is known, but the end experience is that I know the object....same as VP.

  

Yes, there is no contact with the object directly....but isn't that the point of the new prakriyA as we are trying to address

some yukti and anubhava issues with existing VP and see if there is an alternative vishayacChAya explanation without direct contact

that will address the yukti and anubhava issues and of course not contradict the shruti. Note that in this, one has to be ready to even interpret the shruti a bit differently perhaps as long as it doesn't compromise on the tAtparyam.....e.g in AbhAsavAda prakriyA mahAvAkya uses bAdha sAmanAdhikaraNyam interpretation whereas avacChedavAda prakriyA, it uses Eikya sAmAnAdhikaraNyam.

  

Also, we could also perhaps have 2 versions of VP2 (one for AbhAsavAdins and another for avacChedavAdins)

  

VP2.1 (for AbhAsavAdins)

Agni tatvam with rUpa guNa reaches the chakShurindriya. The vRRitti with chakShurindriya envelopes the rUpa that reaches

the indriya (actually 2 images) and assumes the form of the object in space and shines with chidAbAsa. The end result due to a mysterious process in the mind is that "I see this object of such and such a form is located there in space". The fact that I get 2 slightly different images is somehow translated into the depth perception by the same process in the mind. Thus in this model, it is not needed for the vRRitti to go to the object.

  

VP 2.2 (for AvacChedavAdins)

Perhaps similar version of VP2.1 but there is no chidAbhAsa. It may need same mysterious mechanism as VP 2.1 to project the image out in space....

  

At least both may address some of the yukti and anubhava concerns that VP has and both may probably make a whole bunch of  

things such as dead star, dead flare, doppler effect, red shift etc etc easier to explain.
