Namaste Raghav-ji,

  

Thanks for taking the time to describe this elaborately. It is a very interesting/novel way of looking at it!

  

I can see that it will help explain the dead star case very well indeed.

  

Wouldn't the same then hold for delays in sounds also? e.g lets say a few miles away, there is an explosion and the source is now gone and the sound travels.....I hear it some delay. Again VP may have some difficulty here enveloping an object that is not there? So would you say that even for sounds, Iswara presents the proportionate past? The lightning case would then involve two proportional pasts (seeing the lightning almost immediately) and then hearing the sound is from the past...  

  

You wrote:

All other explanations for doppler, echos, seeing partially immersed objects, remain unchanged from the existing VP framework. (Use of prAtibhAsika categories, bhrama GYAnam, sopAdhika bhrama, karana dOSha etc., are used to explain altered visual perceptions.)

  

It would be good to see these explanations written down in one place. I am still not sure I understand how VP would explain all of these. Do the explanations in all these cases look convincing to you? I would add Lightning, mirror image and vision correcting glasses on the list (and why the latter doesn't cause representationalism).  

  

How about the gravitational lensing case (mercury behind the sun but seen next to it?)

  

The explanation of things such as lightning, doppler effect, partially immersed objects etc by CT proponents looked quite complicated and somewhat unintuitive (at least to me). A simpler explanation is generally preferable (Occam's razor). In any case truth (i.e in the relative world, Iswara's order) will always prevail and sooner or later, any theory that is inconsistent with this (relative) reality will face greater difficulties in offering sensible/simple explanations or may need to handwave and classify more things into prAtibhAsika categories, bhrama GYAnam, sopAdhika bhrama, karana dOSha etc, whenthere may be a simpler/easier/more precise way to explain it consistent with yukti and anubhava.

  

(Or we could accept Representationalism and say that there are objects out there which send out physical level signals which reach our golakas and we form or recreate the experienceable image or viShayacchAya. That creates some other issues. If those issues can be resolved then even Representationalism is ok. Chittaji incidentally was emphatic that such representationalism is incompatible with advaita.)  

  

This direction seems like leading to a simpler/more pragmatic explanation...but then any issues need to be sorted as issues/non-issues, and the real issues would need some responses.

  

--Satyan

Namaste Satyan ji

You wrote-

  
b) It might be possible that I am seeing a dead star and also conversing with my friend...would the intellect then be  
in two times simultaneously (both present time and 1 million years ago)? Or perhaps I am seeing the moon and next to the moon is the dead star much further away. Would the intellect again be in two times simultaneously? Or I am looking at the night sky and I see hundreds of stars...does that mean my intellect is in hundreds of past times simultaneously? Could that be problematic?  

My brief answer is that, Ishvara unfolds a vista of various objects like a panaroma unfolding before our eyes, moment to moment. In this Order as revealed to jIva, not all objects are presented with the same timestamp so to say. Ishvara chooses to present before us far away objects with a proportionately older timestamp - this is the scheme/postulate .

(The idea of finite light velocity is only one explanation for such differential timestamping of objects in our \**empirical\** experience. Current quantum explanations seem to have dispensed with even the objective entity called light etc., and postulate only a mathematical wave function to explain everything. Any explanation that works is fine since it is overlaid by my direct experienced vRtti of several objects like stars at different distances and different times etc. That vRtti is the primary irreducible datum. We have to figure out sone inference based theory at the golaka level which gels with the primary data viz., objects experienced with different timestamps. )  

Like I said this involves largely retaining VP framework with some modification to take care of the time delayed experience of distant objects. All other explanations for doppler, echos, seeing partially immersed objects, remain unchanged from the existing VP framework. (Use of prAtibhAsika categories, bhrama GYAnam, sopAdhika bhrama, karana dOSha etc., are used to explain altered visual perceptions.)

To elaborate-  
As you can see, i am only doing anuvAda of what is perceived when we merely look up at the canopy of stars etc. Each such slice or frame of space and time (or a part of it if we focus on one object) presented by Ishvara is then, during perception, covered by my manovRtti which envelops whatever Ishvara has chosen to present before us at that moment with those differing timestamps. I would like to think of this manomaya process as complementary to what is happening in the nADis and golakas which are in the prANamaya and bhautika sharira.

\*Any\* explanation at the manomaya level is inherently an inside to outside scheme. (From sAxI to pramAtA to indriyas to viShayas.) tasya bhAsA sarvamidam vibhAti,.  

When we see two objects in the same visual frame which are at different distances from us, such as the trees seen against the moon in the background (or seeing hundreds of stars of varying distances in the same *frame)*, we see the trees nearby and the moon in the background in the same *visual/*perceptual frame. According to VP, the antaHkaraNa seems to have no issues with reaching \*two vastly different *distances\** in the same instant viz, the trees nearby and also simultaneously the moon millions of kilometres away. . In fact in seeing hundreds of stars at different distances from us, the VP has to postulate the mind being all over the place. The same logic can be extended and said to hold for experiencing two objects as they obtain at different times but seen at the same time in the same instant of perception. ( a scheme which would accomodate what current observations of celestial objects reveal viz., when we see in to the distance we are seeing in to the past - as in seeing the Moon and Venus in the same frame - they are experienced as they were at different times.). This can be taken as antaHkaraNa dharma viz., it faithfully envelops whatever Ishvara presents before it.

Swami M.'s explanation is based on classical physics. (any other similar explanation is also ok such as using recent quantum physics which denies the objective reality of any physical entity called light anymore, except as a mathematical concept, because all such perceptual theories are anyway \*overlaid\* finally by the manomaya vRtti formation.) Using the gross physical and subtle nADI level description (elaborated throughout in mAnasollAsa) is \*complementary\* to the higher-level manomaya level description of perception as VP seeks to do. When we talk at the manomaya (I.e., antaHkaraNa) level the explanation for perception will necessarily be inside to outside or an experiential description (GYAnam ...caxurAdikaraNa dvara bahisspandate).
Correspondingly there will be changes occurring at the nADi and golaka level, the latter being even measurable. Agency is retained by the pramAtA and perception is not passive response to stimuli. ( strangely even *current quantum* paradigms of perception/observation have returned agency to the conscious observer.)

To give an example from a different context, if I close my eyes and vividly visualize memories or images, the physical counterparts of the 'nADis' corresponding to the visual cortex located in the occipital region will show a certain extra excitation. This organic process if described at the manomaya and higher levels will read "I am recollecting and reliving my visit to the Himalayas" while the nADi level (which is a prANamaya description) will read "so-and-so nADIs are more active." The latter is a lower level readout while the former is a readout at the higher level of the same integral process/event. Only the former is actually directly experienced. The latter is an inferred model whether from science or from traditional texts. The latter can never by definition contradict the former.

*(*Btw the super large flare experiment you suggested was what I understand Chittaji suggested as a way of testing the vyAvahArika validity of VP.  
My current expectation is different from Chitta-ji's and it is in fact the same as what you suggested*, viz.,* that the perceptual experience of the observer who is near the flare will not be synchronised with the far away observer's experience of the flare - there will be a delay observed \*both\* visually and by instruments at the far way observer - that is my expectation. )  

We could maintain that it will still not amount to contradicting the VP provided we hold that in Ishvara's Manomaya Order, the \*antaHkaraNa does not experience all objects as they are at one instant\* - rather, when it peers in to the distance it necessarily (proportionately) peers in to the past. (which is what I hold to be antaHkaraNa dharma). Apologies for repetition.

(Or we could accept Representationalism and say that there are objects out there which send out physical level signals which reach our golakas and we form or recreate the experienceable image or viShayacchAya. That creates some other issues. If those issues can be resolved then even Representationalism is ok. Chittaji incidentally was emphatic that such representationalism is incompatible with advaita.)

The reason why I am not particular about light striking the retina at the physical level is because the scientists keep undergoing these 'paradigm shifts' every now and then leaving us in the lurch if we are too deeply invested in any one particular physical explanation correlating to our unchanging sUxma (mental-level) experience of manas and *indriyas presenting direct experiences in the form of vRttis*. So even if they say (as per the current holy *grail of quantum* physics) that when we make an observation of some distant object, the mathematical device/model called the quantum function of that distant object which is again observer-specific, collapses or suffers a discontinuity, that too is fine.. Thus quantum physics (Aspect c.1980) has (in effect) empirically observed that making an observation here can \*instantaneously influence\* something millions of miles away. (No less astonishing than VP's outgoing antaHkaraNa). Thus agency has been restored to the observer. Such an explanation which is in vogue today without postulating any objective physical entity called "light", would also be just as fine by me.

I entirely agree with you that a pragmatic view of prakriyas is in order.
