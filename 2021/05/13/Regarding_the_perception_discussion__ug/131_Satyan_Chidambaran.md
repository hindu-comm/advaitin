Putran-ji,

  

Thanks for your clear and detailed email. I am with your analysis on most of what you said.

  

You wrote:

When we say Brahman (+Maya) projects the world, it is inherently the Knower of the world it projects. Put another way, it is Brahman's cognition that is this projection/imagination/appearance of ishvara-jiva-jagat. What He sees is what is manifest, and what is manifest He sees.  

  

In general Bramhan refers to nirguNa.

Ishwara. which is Bramhan with mAyA upAdhi becomes sarvaGYa.

So I would be more comfortable with the wording that "Ishvara is sarvaGYa" than "Bramhan is sarvaGYa".

But I understand now that in some places where you used the word Bramhan, you meant Bramhan + mAyA. So that is fine.

  

Is his shraddha in a theory that leads to contradiction of Vedanta liable to be an obstruction to his attainment of Knowledge of Self? It seems to me that at some point his knowledge will have to dispel the ignorant notion he is presently convinced about, and till then it will be a subconscious impediment to his progress along the traditional path.  

  

As you stated earlier, I don't see it that way because the theory of cognition is only a prakriyA. It cannot lead to a contradiction in Vedanta's ultimate message.

  

Let us put it another way....assume that a person becomes a GYAnI. It depends on ones adhikAritvam etc. An uttama adhikAri for example needs very less logic and teaching. What is the GYAnam? It is bramha satyam jagan mithya jIvo brahmEiva nAparaH. If he has understood this message clearly, that is enough. The theory of perception is within the mithyA jagat. Even if his understanding of it was flawed, is that going to stop him from getting mokSha? No, having known that he is already bramhan, that he is the substratum of the jagat and that he has no connection at all with the jagat, he is already a mukta, in fact he was never bound and doesn't need to be liberated, that his svarUpa is mokshA, he doesn't need to get mokshA. His is totally done....accomplished what needs to be accomplished. A GYAnI can continue to be ignorant about worldly matters such as physics or politics. The theory of cognition could very well be one of those matters. If passing an exam in the theory of cognition was one of the criteria for becoming a GYAnI, perhaps we wouldn't have had the Guru Parampara at all! We cannot say the same though about the shruti and Guru, Both the shruti and Guru are needed to communicate the main message of Vedanta. You could say that some working theory of cognition is needed to satisfy the intellect provisionally to communicate the main message....and I accept that, that is why it is a prakriyA.

  

regards,

--Satyan
