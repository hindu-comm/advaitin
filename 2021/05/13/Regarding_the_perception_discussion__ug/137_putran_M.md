> This is: When obstruction to the jiva's cognition/jnana of object is
> removed, there is cognition of the object and naught else.
>
>   
>
> 1\. Removal of obstruction involves a change in manifestation of the
> jiva'supadhis relative to the object. For example, opening of eyes.
>
>   
>
> 2\. When the obstruction is removedat the body-mind-world levels, the
> consciousness or awareness is automatically expanded to include the
> corresponding unobstructed range in Ishvara'smanifestation.
>
>   
>
> 3\. The principle here is that you are like all-pervading Space. The
> pot in question is a certain obstruction due to upadhis, wherein there
> is conditioned awareness or ajnana. Modify this and your cognition of
> the object in the "outside space" is instantaneous and direct, because
> you have only become aware of the Knowledge of manifestation that is
> in the Self that you are and not something outside of the Self.
>
>   

> Note that the vritti formation is not necessarily causative of
> cognition. Jiva's cognition is of the Self's cognition conditioned in
> the context of the jiva's upadhis; it is automatic to the extent
> ajnana is removed. However this cognizing corresponds with the
> projection of vrittis in the jiva's body-mind. This is done by
> Ishvara. The fact that all this is regarded as being instantaneous
> means that one can describe the process in whichever logical sequence
> one is comfortable with. Like sense-organs going out to object, vritti
> forms, light of self shines on vritti, finally cognition takes place.
> OK fine; but the philosophical basis is simply: remove ajnana, jnana
> is already there, and here and now shines in awareness. You the Self
> as jiva are cognizing what You the Self are projecting/cognizing as
> Ishvara - only your awareness in the context of the jiva upadhis will
> be partial.

Quote (pg 97)

  

Broadly speaking, there are in Advaita Vedanta three different views as to how the mental mode (antahkarana-vritti), united with the external object, leads to its perception. These three ways are known as:

  

1\. CiduparAga, tinging the subject-consciousness (with the object).

2\. Avarana-bhanga or AvaranAbhibahava, the removal of the veil (of ignorance that hides the object from the subject).

3\. abhedAbhivyakti, manifestation of the oneness (of the consciousness underlying the object with the consciousness underlying the subject).

  

Instead of going into the details of each method, which is related to a particular advaita view of the jiva, we shall delineate the main function of the perceptual process on which most advaitins agree. The followers of PadmapAda and SureshvarAchAraya.. generally recognize a twofold function of the mental mode in external perception, i.e. I.removing the veil that hides the object from the subject and II. associating the object with the subject-consciousness. In fact, this dual course comprises the first two of the three aforesaid ways.

  

As pointed out by Madhusudana Sarasvathi, the twofold functions of the mental mode, namely, I. Avaranabhanga and II. cidupAraga, is acknowledged by the advaitins who conceive the individual soul as a finite being apparently limited by the mind (antahkarana), and also by those who conceive the jiva as consciousness reflected in avidyA and all-pervasive and detached. It may be contended that in case the individual soul is held to be all-pervasive, then it must have contact with all objects at all times, and there cannot be any need of the mental mode for cognizing them. The point is this. The jiva, all pervasive as Consciousness reflected in avidyA though it be, functions as the percipient (pramAtA) through the limiting adjunct through the limiting adjunct of the mind located in the body. Consequently, such a case does not preclude the twofold purpose of the mental mode.

  

The advaitins who regard the jiva as finite and limited generally uphold the method of abhedAbhivyakti. It has been especially dwelt on by the author of VedAnta-paribhAshA.
