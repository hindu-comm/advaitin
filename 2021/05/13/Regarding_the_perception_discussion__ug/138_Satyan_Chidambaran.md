You wrote:

VV and PV are non-falsifiable (in the scientific sense), being experienced all the time (non*-*falsifiable as much as my experience of a vivid color like red can never be contradicted by any physical model or*theory of*colors).  

  

Yes, VP appears to be largely non falsifiable by science unless some simultaneity conditions are tagged along as necessary aspects of VP, which may (or may not) be proven otherwise by experiment? Eventually, VP is also a model though....a "provisional explanation" pending rise of knowledge. The fact that there are a few such explanations corroborate the fact that it is a "model" (prakriyA). e.g How abhAsavAda and avacChedavAda explain perception are different as we have seen.

  

As I understand it, the main question we are exploring is not as much whether VP is right/wrong in the context of Atma vidyA, but whether object coming to mind (instead of mind going to object) is a variant model that is defensible and whether it may offer simpler explanations for those who tend to raise more questions or do more samanvaya with yukti and anubhava.

  

The main objection that we are discussing in this context is representationalism. You have summarized the objections as:

  

we have to rely on shabda or anumAna to draw any conclusion about whether our pratyaxa prama is veridical or not (true to the nature of the original object or not). But we should not have to bring in anumana, upamAna etc., to say that the image we experience is \*exactly the same as\* the actual object.Pratyaxa prAmANyam has to stand on its own feet as giving veridical knowledge. Also the original objects whose chAya alone we are perceiving, are all nitya paroxa in VP2  

  

if we relegate the \*entire pratyaxa world of the original viShayas\* to a nitya paroxa realm whose shadow alone is perceptible, that seems to compromise the prAmAnyam of all pratyaxa pramA  

  

  

You had also mentioned:

Like I said there may some workarounds which i am not clear*about,*to defend a representational looking framework of VP2- Can we assign*the*original viShayas, whose indriya-pratyaxa chAya is directly seen, as sAxI pratyaxa"

  

I was wondering along perhaps similar lines for the last few days. I had stated this argument before

"The underlying object is not seen, only its guNas are seen"....whether it be VP or VP2."

  

This was convincing enough to me, but perhaps not to others. Would this line of thinking make it more convincing?

  

Note shankarAcArya bhAShya said for "sarvam idam vibhAti", "idam jagat".

  

What is the jagat?The following shloka provides some understanding:

  

asti bhAti priyam rUpam nAma chetyansha panchakam

adya trayam brahma rUpam jagat rUpam tato dvayam

  

The jagat is just rUpa and nAma.

What is that whatever else (underlying object) that is not rUpa/nAma? It is "asti bhAti priyam". In the final analysis, it is nothing other than bramhan (adya trayam brahma rUpam) which is sat, chit, which is the self evident Self.

  

Thus, whatever "guNa-less" "underlying object" which is claimed to be ***nitya parokSha*** by those who charge VP2 with representationalism, isn't that oddly ***nitya aparokSha***, and actually nothing but Bramhan that is the Self evident Self?

  

Note that this is different from svarga, pApa, puNya etc, which are nitya paroksha because their guNas can never be pratyakSha.

  

Whereas here, whether it is VP or VP2, the guNas are pratyakSha. So if VP2 can assert that what one sees are the guNas of the original object (not the guNas of the image), then VP2 cannot be charged with representationalism because the "underlying object" which is never objectified by senses (either in VP or VP2) is bramha rUpam, which is the self evident subject "I". The streching of the agni tattvam from the surface of original object to senses with rUpa guNa is rUpa guNa of the original object....we could say that the chakshu indriya objectifies the guNa of the original object, not the guNa of the image. One could also take the position that the chakshu indriya objectifies the the rUpa Guna/agni that strikes the image, not the rUpa guNa/agni that inheres in/reflects off the image....the rupa guNa/agni that strikes the image is that of the original object only. Wouldn't either of these take care of the issue?
