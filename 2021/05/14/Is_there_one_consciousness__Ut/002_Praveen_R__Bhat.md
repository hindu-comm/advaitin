> How can we know in certainty that there is one common consciousness
> and not individual ones. This is probably dealt with in topics of
> Vashishta dvaita vs Advaita, but can someone explain this in simple
> terms here?
>
>   

The example used is of space. Is the space divided even if you build walls around it? Even where there are walls dividing the space, the space pervades. A single space appears to be different across rooms, bldgs, pots, etc. So to one consciousness appears to be divided across various bodies.

  

A more technical answer as to why consciousness needs to be one is because it is also the cause of space as revealed in the Vedas.

  

An even more technical answer as to "why can't consciousness be there in each individual and missing between them causing many consciousnesses?" is that you wouldn't even know that there is anything existent in between two individuals unless there is underlying consciousness. For this, you have to understand how any cognition takes place as per Vedanta.
