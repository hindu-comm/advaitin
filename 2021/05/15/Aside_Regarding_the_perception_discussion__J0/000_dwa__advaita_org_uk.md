Hi Venkatraghavan-ji and Chittaranjan-ji,



Thank you for a fascinating discussion. It was a model for how discussants holding conflicting views should conduct an exchange. And please, if you are going to continue, do so on the group and not privately.



Just an aside point, Venkatraghavan-ji, if I may:



You said (14/5 12:02):



“The idea that the world vanishes after brahmajnAna is an erroneous notion. Even within the advaita

theory of perception, the world does not vanish upon brahmajnAna. The appearance of the world continues after brahmajnAna, otherwise there would be no jIvanmukti, and no guru.”



but then (14.5 13:19) you said:



“That is the point I am trying to make which you seem to have missed - the external world is unreal not because of or in spite of a theory of perception. It is unreal because the Shruti says so.”



You are probably not aware but the topic of ‘disappearance of the world (or not) on enlightenment’ has been addressed on and off on Advaita Vision for months. I firmly hold that it does NOT disappear. What happens is that it is realized to be mithyA, but its appearance continues – like the mirage and not like the rope-snake.



Your statements above suggest firstly that you agree with my own view but then latterly that you do not. I assume that you are using ‘unreal’ loosely (figuratively, gauNa) in your second statement?



You may feel that I am being nit-picking here but my latest book is specifically on the topic of ‘Confusions’ amongst seekers resulting from ‘casual’ rather than pedantic statements from teachers and books. And there are MANY of these, even amongst respected teachers! This is illustrated by the fact that, in the AV discussions, everyone except myself has been arguing that the world really does disappear!
