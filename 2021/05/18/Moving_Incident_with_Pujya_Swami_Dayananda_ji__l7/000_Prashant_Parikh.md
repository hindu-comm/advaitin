A Moving Incident with Pujya Swami Dayananda ji

  

Sw. Tadatmananda ji says: I'd like to share a highly instructive story about my Guru. Several years after I first met Pujya Swami Dayananda in the 1980s, he returned to Santa Barbara to give another lecture series I helped organize in the very same hall where he taught before. On the first evening of the program he walked in front of the hall and sat before the audience for 2 or 3 minutes without saying anything. Several times he opened his mouth as if he were about to begin, but then he remained silent. When he finally got started, he gave a great lecture captivating us all with his wit and wisdom.

  

After lecture was over I asked him why it took so long for him to get started His answer surprised me.

  

"When I look at everyone gathered, I don't see any problem. Everyone is fine just the way they are, and I don't see any reason to teach".

  

Swami Dayananda understood the true nature of everyone there- limitless, full and complete. In his vision, each person is a flawless creation of Ishvara, a perfect expression or manifestation of what he calls Ishvara's order, or the intelligent nature of the universe.

  

Seeing this perfect order he saw no need to teach Vedanta. Of course he also knew that everyone's true nature was covered by a veil of ignorance which could be removed after Vedantic teaching.

  

Only after shifting to this perspective could he begin to teach.

Swami Dayananda ji often said you already are what you seek. From the standpoint of your true Self you don't need anything at all, including the teaching of Vedanta.

  

Therefore, a Guru has nothing to give you, but in fact the Guru's role is not to give you something, it is to remove something. To remove the veil of ignorance so you can forever appreciate your innate fullness and completeness. In this way, the Guru is indeed the remover of darkness, one who removes ignorance removing the veil that covers your true nature, to reveal the divinity within. Boundary-less, limitless, complete.

  

Shri Gurubhyo Namah
