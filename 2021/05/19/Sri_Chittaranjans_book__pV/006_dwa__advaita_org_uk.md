Dear Bhaskar-ji,



The word ‘apocryphal’ actually comes from the Christian term ‘apocrypha’ so it is perhaps not the best word to use here! I mean it in the sense that it is a story or myth used to present an idea. The thesaurus gives words such as unauthenticated, unverified, unsubstantiated, unsupported, questionable, spurious, equivocal, doubtful, dubious, fabricated, concocted, fictitious, made-up, imaginary, legendary, mythical. Shankara would have used the word gauNa I suspect, and the dictionary translations of this that are appropriate are ‘metaphorical’ or ‘figurative’.



I am aware that even Shankara uses terms such as chakra in his commentaries but these come from Yoga philosophy. Yoga was prevalent and historic in Shankara’s time so it would be natural for him to use them. But they are not Advaita.



Also, although I certainly mean no disrespect by this, you can ‘push aside’ much of the teaching if it is not proving helpful in your progress towards understanding. In the end it ALL has to be dropped!
