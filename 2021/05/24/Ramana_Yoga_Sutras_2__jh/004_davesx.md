Namaste - I was holding back on further commenting on Sri Ramana Maharishi but maintaining silence will lead this distinguished Advaita forum over the precipice and into complete insanity....

  

Vedanta inquires: How do you know that the scriptures reveal the truth? How do you  

know that the Maharishi, who may be a perfectly truthful man, speaks the Ultimate Truth? How do you know that he was not mistaken? The spirit of Vedanta is to doubt and inquire and then only can one be able to discriminate Truth from untruth.

  

Maharishi picked up a few Sanskrit words and Slokas here and there, but unfortunately he was never properly educated in Sanskrit. Hence he makes errors like stating a realized  

man has to destroy his mind or the world will go into oblivion. The correct meaning is that the futile activities of the mind chasing after evanescent objects has to come to a stop; but the mind or world itself cannot be destroyed.

  

Look there are two culminating stages in Vedanta after the preliminaries and examining the nature of the world: Firstly - recognition of the Drik (the Seer) as unaffected. Secondly – that everything is Brahman. Traditional Yogis and Sri Ramana it seems, stop at the first stage divorcing from the world, which is incomplete, individualistic and leads to selfish indifference. The Jnani must pass through both and established in the 2nd stage be of service to humanity and the universal well being of the whole.

  

If the Maharishi had a proper Guru and not the incompetent crackpot Seshadri, he would have been told like Lord Rama, "Realize now the world is an idea arising and setting in your own Self; now go about and live cheerfully in the world, keeping this insight constantly. It is not necessary to remain apart, isolated in solitude any longer."  

  

Peace!

dave
