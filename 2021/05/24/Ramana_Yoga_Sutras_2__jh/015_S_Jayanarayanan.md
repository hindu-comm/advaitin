I will only take up one of your objections:

  
  
"To state that Maharishi lived without mind or thoughts is impossible.  
How could he carry out a conversation or respond sensibly to questions without mind?"  
  

What people saw was the \*body\* of the Maharshi speaking. The Maharshi has repeatedly said that  
the Jivanmukta does not identify himself with the body.  
  
The idea of a Jivanmukta being detached from the body has been outlined in the Bhagavad Gita 5.8:  
  
naiva ki~nchitkaromIti yukto manyeta tattvavit.h .  
pashyaJNshR^iNvanspR^ishaJNjighrannashnangachchhansvapa~nshvasan.h .. 5\\8..  
  
"Even as (the body continues) seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, going, sleeping, breathing,  
The One who knows the Truth will think 'I do nothing!'."  
  
This very same idea has been reiterated in the Srimad Bhagavatam as well:  
  
"Whether the impermanent body is at rest or moves about,  
or whether by reason of prarabdha it clings to him or falls  
away from him, the Self-realised siddha is not aware of it,  
even as the drunken man blinded by intoxication is unaware  
whether his clothes are on his body or not."
