Namaskaram,

  

On the topic of the mind and the realization of Self, Gaudapadacharya (Shankaracharya's guru's guru) has expressed his thoughts in hisKarika on Mandukyopanishad (III.31-47) and Shankaracharya has provided his commentary on it. Please see attached English translation by Nikhilandanda.

  

In short, it is said in Gaudapadacharya's and Shankaracharya's words that:

  

III-31: duality is never experienced when the mind ceases to act

  

III-32: when the mind does not imagine on account of the knowledge of the Truth which is Atman, then it ceases to be mind and becomes free from all idea of cognition, for want of objects to be cognized

  

III-34: the behaviour of the mind that is free from all imaginations and endowed with discrimination is not like the condition of the mind in deep sleep

  

III-37: This Atman is beyond all expression by words beyond all acts of mind (for, the Shruti says, "It is verily without Prana and without mind")

  

III-40-41: Yogis who look upon the mind as separate from but related to Atman and who are ignorant of the knowledge regarding reality of Atman can experience fearlessness as a result of the discipline of the mind. To them, the destruction of misery (and eternal) is also dependent on mental control; (however), the mind can be brought under control only by an unrelenting effort like that which is required to empty an ocean, drop by drop, with the help of a blade of Kusha grass

  

III-42: Is untiring effort the only way for bringing the mind under discipline? We say, in reply, no.

  

III-43: What is the way of disciplining the mind? Remember that all duality is caused by Avidya or illusion and therefore afflicted with misery. Thereby dissuade the mind from seeking enjoyments produced by desires. In other words, withdraw the mind from all dual objects by impressing upon it the idea of complete non-attachment. Realise from the teachings of the Scriptures and the Acharyas that all this is verily the changeless Brahman. Then you will not see anything to the contrary, viz., duality; for it does not exist

  

III-44: When the mind is immersed in oblivion, i.e., in Sushupti (deep sleep), then rouse it by means of knowledge and by detachment. That is to say, turn the mind to the exercise of discrimination which leads to the knowledge of the Self. Bring the mind back to the state of tranquility if it is distracted by the various objects of desires. When the mind is thus, by constant practice, awakened from the state of inactivity and also turned back from all objects, but not yet established in equilibrium, that is to say, when the mind still dwells in an intermediary state, -- then know the mind to be possessed of attachment. Then the mind contains within it the seeds of desires for enjoyment and inactivity. From that state also, bring the mind, with care, to the realization of equilibrium, that is, when it is on the way to realize that state, then do not disturb it again. In other words, do not turn it to (by attachment) external objects

  

III-45: The mind should not be allowed to enjoy the bliss that arises out of the condition of Samadhi. It should be freed from attachment to such happiness through the exercise of discrimination. If the mind, once attaining to the state of steadiness seeks externality, then it should be unified with the Atman, again, with effort.

  

III-46 When the mind does not merge in the activity of oblivion (Laya / deep sleep) or become distracted by desires, that is, when the mind becomes quiescent and does not give rise to appearances, it verily becomes Brahman

  

III-47: This highest bliss is based upon the realization of Self, it is peace, identical with liberation, indescribable and unborn. It is indescribable as nobody is able to describe it; for, it is totally different from all objects... The Knowers of Brahman describe this bliss verily as the omniscient Brahman, as it is identical with that Reality which is omnicient

  

What I have understood from the above is the following:

\- According to Yogis who look upon the mind as separate from but related to the Atman and areignorant of the knowledge regarding reality of Atman, destruction of misery and attainment of eternal peacecan be achieved through mind control; this is because, duality is not experienced when mind ceases to act

\- however, such mind control is extremely difficult (like emptying the ocean drop by drop with a blade of grass)

\- according to Gaudapadacharya and Shankaracharya, the mind should be made to exercise discrimination which leads to the knowledge of the Self

\- the mind should view all happiness, including that which comes from Yogic Samadhi / Sushupti etc., as that arising out of ignorance of Reality

\- the mind should be freed from attachment to such happiness through the exercise of discrimination

\- when the mind thus becomes quiescent, it verily becomes Brahman

\- the highest bliss based upon realization of the Self indescribable for it is totally different from all objects

  

In conclusion, it seems to me that cessation of the mind and its associated lack of experience of duality is Yogic Samadhi. This is very different from Vedantic Samadhi, which is based on exercise of discrimination and complete non-attachment. In this process, the mind is freed from attachment to all happiness, including that from Yogic Samadhi and related states of Sushupti, which are also viewed upon as arising from Avidya. Another point of difference is that, while through intense efforts the mind ceases (and duality disappaears) in Yogic Samadhi, it appears that upon realization of the Self through exercise of discrimination and non-attachment, the mind verily becomes Brahman that results in the highest bliss which is indescribable.
