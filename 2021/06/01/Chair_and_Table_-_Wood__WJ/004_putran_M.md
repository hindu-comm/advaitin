Namaskaram Sada-ji,

  

Perhaps theexample is not a good analogy. Here Wood represents Brahman. But your point that the Wood also is not an object of perceptual knowledge probably would apply with Brahman as well since what we perceive are the nama-rupa manifestations. We may require an external source to inform us of the substratum being Wood; likewise we need shabda to learn of Brahman.

  

With regard to perception of chair itself, yes the knowledge we gain from perception is limited due to the body-mind-world obstructions to the jiva's total knowledge of the chair. But the rest of your message seems to be about whether pratyaksha is pramana, topic just recently discussed in the other threads. Here I simply keep with the traditional position that indeed it is pramana, hence capable of giving true knowledge of the manifest object - though of course the jiva could think incorrectly of what it sees. If the jiva sees the chair and thinks it is a table, that is bhramaon its part; but it nevertheless cognized the chair as it is to the extent its vision was not obstructed from the chair.

  

And further, the chair is a nama-rupa in the knowledge of Ishvara (which is manifest as this world and revealed in Veda). That is why a jiva sees it as chair and not merely as a sum of parts. An axe is not merely the sum of blade, handle and screws. The "axe" exists as an entity in the knowledge of Ishvara; the nama and rupa go together in a generic sense. That is how I understand.
