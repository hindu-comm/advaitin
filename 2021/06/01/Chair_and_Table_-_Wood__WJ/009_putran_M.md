Namaskaram,

  

Bird in the Cage

  

There is a Bird in a Cage.

  

It feels bound and miserable, seeks freedom and joy.

  

What should it do?

  

Some of the other pets in the house tell the bird that Freedomis getting out of the Cage and Joy is being in the constant immediate Presence of the Master.

  

That its Existence is entirely dependent on His Grace, and He alone can grant it freedom and eternal Joy if only the Bird proves its worth and devotion.

  

So the Bird must chirpcorrectly and behave in ways that are according to the Master's wishes; and truly yearnto sit on the Master's shoulder forever.

  

The Master then cannot but feel the total devotion of His loving bird, and one day will release the bird from its cage. The Bird will rush to Him, become One with Him and thereafter roam free and full of joy in His House which is nothing but the extension of Him only.

  

  

Much of this is aligned with or not opposed to what iscalled vyavaharika satya in advaita. Yes the Bird finds itself in a Cage, built out of its own Karma, and it must follow the path of Dharma and Bhakti tothe Master that He may loosen the chains on this Cage. Except there is a plot twist; the final flying out ofthe cage is not quite what the bird envisions presently.

  

In Advaita this Ishvara-jiva-jagat existence is given a status quite different from other schools of Vedanta. In other schools it is Satya/Real; here it is referred to as Mithya, Maya, Ajnana, Adhyaasa, Avidya; as Imagination, Superimposition, Appearance, Dream. 

  

We cannot trivialize this distinction.

  

The Master-Bird-Cage is in reality only the Self appearing as if. The individual Bird identification is based in Ignorance of the Self. So what to say about this Bird needing freedom or attaining it only when the Master opens the Cage etc.? All Imagination.Liberation for the Bird is Liberation from the Bird, in the Realization 'aham Brahmaasmi', in the Knowledge of the true Self.And this Self I am is already Satyam-Jnanam-Anantam, Sat-Chit-Anandam.
