Namaskaram,

  

Along the same lines:

  

Suppose we have a jivan-mukta/sthithaprajnain a room. A table in the room has two glasses of water. When the body develops thirst, he chooses one of the two glasses and drinks from it.

  

Now we may assume his glass-choice was made in a detached manner, without particular desire or revulsion based on adhyasa of self with body. The choice and action will not accrue adrishta or effects of karma requiring to be resolved through future karmaphala.

  

So the choice of this jivanmukta is entirely aligned with Ishvara'sOrder like water flows naturally according to gravity.

  

But the fact that this jivanmukta chose to drink from the first glass, does it mean that somehow it was THE natural/dharma/Ishvara's Order-aligned choice for him given the initial body-mind-world manifest state? Or is it the case that given the initial state, both choices could have been natural or aligned with Ishvara'sOrder and even in the absence of ajnana-of-self, the jivanmukta is making a true choice among several available dharmic-directions - i.e. it is not entirely predetermined in the initial state?

  

How does Vedanta understand this?

  

(Note: Here Ishvara'sOrder is understood as constantly guiding the manifestation towards resolution of jivas' karma/adrishta even as jivas constantly add to their adrishta through their karma etc.)
