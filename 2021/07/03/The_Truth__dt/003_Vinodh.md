Namaskaram,

  

When talking about "facts", what the right means of knowledge (pramana) for establishing facts becomes relevant.

  

The most familiar means of right knowledge for establishing facts is what is called the scientific method. Scientific method relies only on two means of knowledge: observation (pratyaksha) and anumana (inference). Verification of statements as facts can done through experiments, observing the outcomes of the experiments, and inferring the conclusion.

  

However, any statements about the observer who is involved in these two actions (observation and anumana) lies outside the scope of such a scientific method. Then how does one know about facts about the observer and verify them? To address this, in Vedanta, another means of knowledge called aaptakvaakya, i.e., the words of one who is a trusted/reliable/unbiased source who has direct experience of the truth, is also accepted.

  

An example of aaptavakya is in the original post above. For the verification of such aaptavakya, one has to develop shraddha (trust/belief in the those words) to invest one's efforts in the direction prescribed by the seer of the truth so that one may directly experience the truth and thereby verify the truth of the statement. One who has no shraddha unfortunately will always remain in doubt. Therefore, it is vital that one finds a person (a guru) that one thinks he can trust and have shraddha in their words and invest one's effort in to realizing the truth himself. This is the only way, because the advaitic truth is beyond words and imagination. It is only indicated in words by what it is not (like what is said by Sri Ramana in the post above).
