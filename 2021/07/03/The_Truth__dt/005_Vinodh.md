[[Vinodh	2021-07-04, 08:55:57 [Source](https://groups.google.com/g/advaitin/c/dtlknRPTnHM)]]

Shraddha is not a defense, for it is a mere starting point for understating statements about things that are not within the scope of science (observation and inference).

  

Nor is there a mere difficulty in providing an explanation for the truth. Scholars of the Vedas may be familiar the Shruthi vaakya “yatho vaacho nivarthanthe apraapya manaasa saha” which claims the Brahmam (Truth) to be beyond the grasp of words of the mind (for It is the underlying support for both of these and because of which these seem to appear) as well as the Shruthi vaakya “na iti na iti” (the Truth is “not this, not this”) which shows the Truth by pointing to everything it is not. Followers of the Shankara sampradaya will be also well aware of the adhyAropa-apAvada (superimposition of the untrue on to the Truth followed by negation of the superimposition).

  

It is also not valid in anyway to claim that those who have realized the Truth have “abandoned” the jagat, just as it is illogical to claim that the snake has really disappeared to become a rope. This is because the snake never really existed in the first place for it to disappear. The Truth was always and will always remain a rope only. The snake was a mere illusion, something seems to exist / happen, but never is (like any other magic trick that we are all familiar with, e.g., a lady who appears to be cut with a saw and divided in half only to reappear in full some time later, but who in reality was never cut in the first place).

  

Whatever illusion is there for whoever is perceiving the illusion will remain an illusion until the reality dawns. For one whom the reality has dawned, there is no reality to the illusion. Therefore, from a perspective of an ajnani who is seeing the illusion of a person realizing the self, the illusion will continue in whatever form it is perceived — either as someone who becomes a parivrajaka (wanderer) or as a maharaja (like in the case of Janaka) — until the body falls away. Krishna explains this as lokasangraha (preserving the consistency of this apparent world). Therefore,it is also invalid to claim that every realized advaitin appears to behave as though the world does not exist.

  

Nor can such a person be really be kicked and ruled by others (although he may be appear so within the illusion, just like a lady being sawed in half). This is because a knower of Brahmam is no different from Brahman, and It is of a subtle nature (like that of space) which can neither be cut by weapons, nor dried by wind, nor wet by water (as Krishna explains to Arjuna).

  

Om tat sat
