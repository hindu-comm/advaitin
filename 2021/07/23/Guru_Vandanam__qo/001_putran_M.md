Namaskaram,

  

Who is a guru?

  

Is there an actual definition from a scriptural standpoint that if we are strict about it cannot be translated to other settings as is commonly done?

  

Is a guru only the person who guides one in the spiritual shastra-based path, with a view to dispelling our spiritual ajnana? Or can we use the word for our teachers in worldly disciplines like music, school, etc.?

  

I ask because I see people adopt and adapt the word Guru in all contexts where they study under someone, and likewise they offer respects to those "gurus" on Guru purnima day.

  

Is this a deviation from the original purpose of the word and the day? Or should we accept that the generalization is within the norms of dharma and it is ok that people regard Guru for their various teachers and offerrespectsto them onGurupurnima day?

  

I would think that since Drona is regarded the guru for the pandavas (in archery), it is likely meant generally. However I am wondering if this is regarded improper usage in some technical or traditional sense wherethe traditionalist will say only the one who guides on the "spiritual" path can be Guru. Or that it should be the guide in the path of svadharma - as Drona taught kshatriyas archery as is their dharma? But I cannot for example go about calling my music teacher my guru if learning music is not my svadharma as per shastra?

  

thollmelukaalkizhu
