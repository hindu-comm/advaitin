> The above actually supports V.Advaita

No, although there is nothing wrong per se if advaita and vishiShTAdvaita have aspects that are similar. However, here I do think there is difference between the two systems.

  

In advaita, the qualifier is also mithyA - hence it is classified as an upalakshaNa. Like the crow which is not even present when it is used to identify the house, the identifiers "shivam" and "advaitam" after serving to identify the shuddham, are themselves dropped as mithyA. Brahman is not shivatva / dvaitAbhAvatva vishiShTa. It is shivatva / dvaitAbhAvatva upalakshita.

  

> as name/form/body always denotes the atma/soul without forming a part
> of the svaroopa.

The name / form and body are mithyA here.

  

> Even the identification of oneself as "chidAnanda rUpah Shivoham"
> makes the self, qualified with aprithak siddha (inseparable)
>
> vishesha of shivatva/auspiciousness.

Shankaracharya did not use those terms as visheShaNa-s (e.g. nIlotpalam) of shuddha, they are its upalakshaNa-s.
