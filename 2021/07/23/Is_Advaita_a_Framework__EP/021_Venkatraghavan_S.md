I had pretty much said so two days ago. Please see the paragraphs below for reference from that email:

*"The absolute is by definition beyond any description, whether relative or otherwise. So how can one teach it? Shruti uses words to distinguish it from the relative, not to denote that it is different from the relative (for that would make the absolute also relative), rather it is to identify it uniquely, for that knowledge of the relationless, transcendent absolute is the means to moksha.*

*  *

*Do relative descriptors make the absolute also relative?*

**

*Not necessarily - ie when they belong to a class of identifiers in vedAnta termed as upalakshaNa-s, they identify the svarUpa of the denoted, without forming part of the svarUpa."*

  

The crucial point is that the absolute cannot be defined. Words can at best indicate it. Those definitions are not aspects of the defined entity. The issue with your approach is that you are looking at the relative world and relative words used to define the relative world, and are seeking to extend that to definitions of the absolute. A definition of the absolute is a contradiction in terms. If the absolute can be defined, it will not be the absolute, नेदं यदिदं उपासते. Therefore, all relative descriptors will fail to truly describe It. That is neither a fault of the descriptions nor of the Absolute. That is just the nature of the problem.
