Dear Suresh ji,

  

Namaste. The very idea of 'dependent reality' is nothing but the Advaitic 'vyavaharika satyam' or in other words, 'mithya'. There is only one example for the idea of 'dependent reality', the rope-snake.
The superimposed snake is 'real' for the one who is under the delusion. There, ideally he should have said 'the rope is'. But due to his ignorance about the rope, he says / thinks 'the snake is.' Now, the isness, reality, of the snake is not its own, svatantra, but it is paratantra, dependent, on the (reality of) rope. So, the idea of 'dependent reality' automatically translates to the Advaitic mithya. In the above verse the meaning is: the world has no reality of its own but is only a superimposition, it, being jaDa, is not sva-prakasha but has to shine only on the borrowed shine from the Atman/Brahman. There is no true ananda in the world but what is experienced is a reflection, a vivarta, of the Ananda that is Brahman. There is a popular verse that the Panchadashicites:

  

अस्तिभातिप्रियंरूपं नाम चेत्यंशपञ्चकम् ।  
  

आद्यं त्रयं ब्रह्मरूपं जगद्रूपं ततो द्वयम् ॥

  

Every object in the world has these five aspects: asti, bhAti, priyam and nAma and rUpam. The first three are Brahman's aspects: sat, chit and ananda and the last two are of Maya: name and form. While the first three are constant, same, in every object, the other two are variables and thus mithya. When the object is analyzed thus by the aspirant, he realizes that Brahman alone is satyam. This is the purport of the Gurugita verse.
