Namaskaram Sri Sunilji,

  

Thank you for sharing your thoughts. Indeed, "agama" or the word of a reliable person is a pramana, i.e., one of the right means of knowledge. While the vedas are typically considered part of agama, there is no reason to exclude the words of any other jnani that have been captured in the various texts that we have now.

  

What is interesting about Gaudapadacharya's argument is that he establishes the unreality of duality through \*pure reasoning alone\*, that is, \*\*without relying on any scriptural authority\*\*. This remark has been made by Shankara Bhagavadpada in his Bhashya of the Gaudapada-Karika in the Vaitathya Prakarana.

  

The main reasoning used to establish the illusory nature of dream and waking states is "adau ante ca yannaasti vartamaane ca tat tathaa" or "what does not exist in the beginning or in the end does not exist in the middle".

  

Objects in a dream (including the dream mind-body-senses etc.) did not exist just before falling asleep or just after waking up from sleep, and therefore are non-existent during the dream as well. Similarly all objects in a waking state (including the waking mind-body-senses etc.) do not exist just before waking up from sleep or just after falling asleep (from the point of view of the person sleeping). Therefore, by the same logic the objects in the waking state are also non-existent just like the objects in a dream.

  

This is illustrated by a story of Janaka and Ashtavakra. Janaka once goes to sleep and imagines himself being attacked and exiled from his own kingdom. After a lot of suffering without food and water, he finally comes across some food to eat. However, just as he is about to eat it a dog snatches it away from him, leaving him in a lot of misery. Then he suddenly wakes up and finds himself in his palace with all the comforts he is used to and the misery of the dream was no longer there. He then went about asking everyone "Is this real or is that real?" His ministers unable to answer this puzzling question, Ashtavakra finally answers it. He asks Janaka if all the comforts he is experiencing now was there, that is, whether all this was there in his dream. He says no. He then askes Janaka if all the suffering in the dream is here now. He says now. Then he asks him if he was there. He says yes. He asks him if he is here now. He says yes. Then Asktavakra says that that is the answer to his question. Neither that or this, meaning the dream or the waking state, is real. He, that is, the witness to both of these states, is alone is real.

  

Om tat sat🙏
