In my opinion the Advaitic absolute stand that the world is mithya does not come in the way of an aspirant practicing bhakti and karma yoga.
Even a person who does not really believe in the existence of heaven or hell can conform to the rules of vidhi and nishedha - do's and don'ts. This is because the aspirant can reason: such adherence clearly gives me chitta shuddhi which is quite verifiable. On the same logic a person who knows that in the absolute view there is no formed god/s, a bhakti towards such an 'accepted' god form/s is sure to give verifiable chitta shuddhi, chitta samadhana, positive reassurance, relief, etc. The other positive offshoots of such a practice are increasing and firming up, crystallising of shraddha towards the shaastra and Guru. The fact that even those who have realized the Nirguna Brahman do conform to rules, practice saguna bhakti and derive great joy therefrom are enough reasons for an aspirant who is well informed of the Advaitic absolute position to engage in bhakti, vihita karma, etc. practices.
