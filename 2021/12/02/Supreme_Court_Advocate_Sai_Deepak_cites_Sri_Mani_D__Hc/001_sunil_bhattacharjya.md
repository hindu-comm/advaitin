Dear Subbuji and friends,

  

Shri Sai Dipak spoke nicely, and what he mainly touched upon was, what are popularly called as the ***Lokachara (societal norms/discipline)*** and the ***Vedachara (scriptural norms/discipline)***. Both are important.  

  

In the *"*tribhaaga-shesha*" (i.e., the last one-third)* of his talk, he looked at the area, over which the Judiciary has jurisdiction**;** and for the rest the judiciary has to recognize the importance of the societal needs. He appropriately cited the example of the case of restrictions for visiting the Sabarimala temple of Lord Ayyappa (Shri Hari-Hara-Putra), where the ladies of a certain age-group are not welcome.  

  

As regards, the somewhat similar practice in the North-eastern India, particularly among the brahmin families, that I know of, permit me to add that till recently, the ladies were given complete rest (including optional bed-rest), during the first three days of the so-called unclean days (i.e., of their monthly menstrual period), and during that period, they are relieved from doing any household work and any walking also gets restricted during this period. However, during that restricted period of rest, a lady could read, write, knit and all such work, where there is no physical exertion. A lady could not leave her house during that period, and obviously, the lady is not expected to visit any temple (including the family-temple) during this period. No-one should consider this area of ***Lokachara*** to be within the ambit of the judiciary.  

  

Regards,

Sunil K. Bhattacharjya  

  

***PS***. BTW, the compound word "tribhaagashesha", was used by Vedavyasa in the Mahabharata, and it has confounded almost all the Mahabharata-researchers, and this appears to be one of the several stumbling blocks in the dating of the Mahabharata war.
