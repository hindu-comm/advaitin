Namaste Prof ji,

  

Lyabanta suffix, as pointed out by our friend, is the ending in the word 'upanyasya' in the bhashyam. When there are two acts performed by the same person in a sequence, the usage of lyabanta or its variant ktvaa is used. Here Shankara says, the Chandogya mantra, *having said* that the Atman is to be known for transcending misery, the Mundaka *says the direct realization is to be had*. In this you can see a sequence.
Shankara sees the Chandogya and the Mundaka and further the Isha Upanishads to be the same person. 'स्नात्वा कार्यालयं गच्छति',(After bathing he leaves for the office 'कलशं संस्थाप्य पूजयति' 'After having placed the Kalasha he worships'. In these instances there are two actions by the same person. The difference between the ktvaa suffix, in the first sentence, and the 'lyap' suffix in the second one is: the latter is used when the word has a prefix: sam+ sthaapya. The first sentence has the word snAtvA where there is no prefix.
