Namaskaram,

  

This is an old question but I need a little review.

  

Suppose I see a brown chair and another person sees it as black.

  

1\. Does this mean there is no such thing as colourapart from the mind involved in the observation? How to tell whose version is correct?

  

2\. Is all such knowledge of the world relative (*hence* maya?) *because* the knowledge is product of an interaction between a body-mind and matter? For me chair is brown; for another person it is black; for an animal it may show up differently. So (the argument goes) none of this is absolutely true; shrishti is maya - only truth is Brahman the substratum.

  

Is this aligned more to drishti-srishti vada?

  

I think srishti-drishti vada regards objects as real in vyavaharika and independent of our mind. Moreover that pratyaksha is a valid pramana that has to potentialto obtain knowledge correctly if the organ is without defect and not obstructed. However it is **difficult to logically argue** for this position that pratyaksha is not filtered relative to the individual body-mind. And if we say pratyaksha is relative, then the knowledge that the "pramana" gives is relative to the individual body-mind,including notion of worldexisting etc. Some people try to say thatthis relativism of knowledge is itself the import of Maya.

  

If this is not correct perspective, how to go about countering and established right siddhanta?
