Namaskaram,

  

I wrote the below on different viewpoints on Ishvara.

  

--------------------------------------------  

  

1\. Ishvara is Consciousness (Brahman) associated with Maya/Shakti. The ishvara-jiva-jagat prapancha is nothing but a vivarta (apparent) projection of this Ishvara. The seen is the seeing of the Seer. All internal causality and individuality between thenamarupa "entities" is mithya and reduces to the play of Maya of this Ishvara.

2\. Ishvara is Brahman associated with universal Form (vishwarupa) that He manifests, guides, withdraws back within, by recourse to His intrinsic maya-shakti.

3\. Ishvara (God) is Brahman associated with the functionality of being Lord of jiva-jagat and karmaphaladaataof jivas. We can deduce that this identification corresponds with a svarupa within the vishwarupa corresponding to this function and role, that distinguishes Him from the jiva-jagat.

4\. Ishvara is Brahman associated with a namarupa of a Devata.

5\. You are that Ishvara.

  

1\. Ishvara at the Essence/primal level.

  

Says the BSB I.i.17:

  

"But from the highest point of view, any witness or hearerother than God \[Brahman+Mayashakti imu\] is denied in such texts as, "There is no other witness but Him" (Br. III. vii. 23). God is different to be sure from the one imagined through ignorance to be embodied, the agent, the experiencer, and called the Self conditioned by the intellect, the difference being made in the same sense that the magician standing on the ground is fancied to be different from the magician holding sword and shield in hands and climbing up a rope to the sky, though in reality the first is the very essence of the latter, or it is so in the sense that the space, unlimited by any conditioning factor, is different from the space delimited by such conditioning factors as a pot etc."

  

This "highest point of view" is the highest reading of Brahman possible from within vyavaharika, since the jiva-jnani knows "sarvam kalu idam brahma" and yet experiences duality in the context of body-mind identification. The snake that is seen is therefore known as nothing but the mayic projection of Rope/Brahman (God in above quote).

  

Note that we are shifting attention to the fullest extent possible to the underlying shakti and away from any independent positing of namarupa. So it is not Rope+Maya+Snake; snake is entirely sublated in Rope+Maya. In particular, we do not associate namarupa to this substratum conception of Ishvara.

  

2\. Ishvara at the Universal Total level.

  

In this case, first recognize that Maya/Prakriti and namarupa manifoldness are two sides of the same coin; they both stand posited together in some sense or other. As a consequence, we can identify Ishvara in terms of His manifesting namarupa (that is anaadi). He is therefore theTotal Being (Saguna Brahman) (qualified by) with a universal form (vishwarupa) that He manifests, guides, withdraws.

  

But by shifting attention to the namarupa (instead of maya), we somewhat diminish the emphasis that it is all vivarta only. There is a difference between saying the rope has a snake svarupa vs the rope appears as snake due to maya or ignorance. For the advaitin therefore, Saguna Brahman is ‘within the context of creation’, akin to the visishtadvaitin’s understanding of Brahman.

  

The 9th sloka in Dakshinamoorthi stotra describes the Universal form of Ishvara as follows:

  

"He whose eight-fold form consisting of Earth, Water, Fire, Air, Ether, Sun, Moon, and Man the Sacrificer, manifests itself as this universe of movable and immovable objects, and besides which supreme, omnipresent Lord, there exists nothing to those who reflect, to Him, incarnate as the blessed Teacher, to Sri Dakshinamoorthi is this Salutation." (RKM translation)

  

It is a matter of language. When we speak of the "form of the Lord", we talk in terms of a duality between the Lord and His outer form; that description will be of saguna brahman. Yet when it is said, "besides which ... Lord, there exists nothing", we are hinting at a non-dual (nirguna) Brahman. This is the paramarthika standpoint where the seer-seen duality of Him and His form/manifestation is transcended.

  

3\. Ishvara at the Personal Universal level, as Lord and Karmaphaladata.

  

Next we take the perspective of the jiva, corresponding to whom Ishvara is the Lord of the jiva-jagat part of 'creation'. When the jiva identifies Him specifically as Lord or askarmaphaladata, He is associated with an essential svarupa corresponding with this function that allows Him to be distinguished from jiva-jagat. This ishvara is therefore Consciousness identified with *a part of* the Total Body-Mind of Saguna Brahman, in which association, He 'knows' jiva-jagat and acts as the overall coordinator of its manifestation. This allows the jiva to identify Him as an Individual Entity (God) within the Total Existence.

  

The point is that just like the jiva is associated with a body-mind namarupa, so also we can associate the corresponding ishvara with an essential "svarupa" in which association He does what He does (in the context of apparent creation). And this ishvara, as God, is different from the jiva. By svarupa I don't mean something that can be seen, heard or touched separately ofjiva-jagat, for He pervades and is the antaryami of all. Consider this way: If we say Hiranyagarbha is "total subtle body" or total Mind of the Universal Ishvara, then it includes the part that corresponds specifically to His role as thekarmaphaladata*that is distinct from the individual minds of the jivas*. So, Consciousness that identifies with the individual minds also identifies separately with limiting adjuncts that correspond to Lordship over jiva-jagat, omniscience, role askarmaphaladata, etc. These become the svarupa of God in creation.

  

Pictorially, consider a Tree. The leaves and flowers constitutejiva-jagat while the Roots-Trunk-Branches part is the underlying Being that is their Ishvara, manifesting and 'withdrawing' them according to an Order. So while the Tree is the whole Entity of Universal form, the leaves-flowers look upon the Tree specifically by that Trunk-Root part that is distinct from them and relates to them as their Lord.  

  

Says BSB I.i.21: "God, the internal Ruler, does exist as an entity different from the individual souls identifying themselves with the bodies of the sun etc.; for their dissimilarity is stated in another Upanishad, in these words: "He who inhabits the sun, but is within it, whom the sun does not know, whose body is the sun, and who controls the sun from within, is the internal Ruler, your own immortal Self" (Br. III. vii. 9). By saying, "within the sun" and "whom the sun does not know", it is clearly shown that the internal Ruler is different from the sun which is the knower and which is an individual soul identifying itself with the intellect."

  

For me, it appears BSB I.I.21 and BSB I.I.17 quoted before are emphasizing different notions of difference between Ishvara and jiva, corresponding with the viewpoints on Ishvara I have described in 1. and 3. I.I.17 talks of “no other”, “essence”, “different from one *imagined through ignorance*”, space unconditioned vs space delimited by conditioned factor. All of this indicates that the jiva and ‘God’ here are different categories of existence, with God being reality/cause of
jiva and jiva being superimposition on God. Whereas in I.1.21, the comparisons like sun vs its internal Ruler point to both belonging to the same plane of existence, one as relatively real as the other, but God being the Lord of the jiva.

  

4\. Ishvara at the Personal devotee level.

  

Finally the bhakta sees Ishvara in the Ishta devata.  

  

Says BSB I.i.20:

  

"Even for God there may be forms created at His will out of Maya for the sake of favouring the aspirants, as is declared in the Smrithi, "O Narada, it is a Maya, created by Me, that you see Me in this form possessed of all the substances and qualities. You must not understand Me thus".

  

So the Lord assumes a form that appears within and as a part of the Total, that the jiva can mentally grasp and relate to, but His consciousness and knowledge even in that form is really united with Himself as the Lord of jiva-jagat. The same Krishna, Arjuna’s friend, suddenly gave Arjuna the vision of His vishwarupa. So the Lord described in 3. above can by His mayashakti appear as devatas and avataras and the latter can still be legitimately regarded by the devotee as omniscient, omnipotent, even omnipresent, etc. Within the context of creation, this devata is non-different from that Ishvara (the all-pervading Lord, karmaphaladata) for it is the One who by Hiswill *appears* as the other for the sake of the devotees, but He is different from the jiva.

5\. Ishvara as non-different from jiva.

  

Advaita of course does go the step beyond and says jiva and Ishvara (no matter how we identify Him) are both mithya (apparently but not absolutely real), and the only Reality/Self that underlies them both is nondual Brahman/Consciousness. The Upanishad conveys this message in the mahavakya: "tat tvam asi" - That You are. The point being, You who are identified as jiva is non-different from That which is identified as Ishvara; for both are only Self.

  

Like it is Space that is being identified variously in various pot-spaces. Like it is Water that is identified as waves and Ocean. Or rope identified as snakedue to ignorance - it alwaysis non-dual rope no matter how weimagine snake. (I find this one useful: Or it is Water that is identified as Ice, Steam, Liquid.)

  

Consider the 8th sloka of the Dakshinamoorthi stotra:

  

"He who as Man whirled in Maya, sees the world of cause and effect, as parent and child, or owner and property, or teacher and taught, in a state of waking or dream, to Him, incarnate as the blessed Teacher, to Sri Dakshinamoorthi is this Salutation."

  

That He, the universal Lord of sloka 9 quoted before, the same He is here the man whirled by maya and both are the one incarnate as the blessed Teacher and Ishtadevata. How so? Because the limiting adjuncts that define and differentiate them are all superimpositions on the only Existence, the nondual Consciousness, the Self of All, that is referred to as Brahman.

  

Says Shankara therefore:

  

Brahma sathyam.

Jagan mithya

Jivo brahmaiva naaparah

  

~ Brahman is absolute Reality, the world is apparent (not absolutely real nor absolutely unreal), the individual soul is naught else than Brahman.
