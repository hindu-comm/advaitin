> 1\. Ishvara at the Essence/primal level.

  

At the primal level, Ishvara is simply Brahman + Maya, the Reality that has a special creative power. Ishvara by the power of Maya appears as the triple ishvara-jiva-jagat. The latter can be thought of as resembling a movie projected by a Screen. (We assume in this analogy that the Screen projects the movie, for the sake of convenience.) A movie has two aspects: one is the appearance of duality and the other is the assumed content of the appearance. The content is the characters, their supposed actions, the storyline. Knowledge of “it is only a movie; Screen is reality” negates independent reality apart from Screen to any aspect of the content; all content reduces to being superimposition on the Light display that the Screen projects. Likewise all individuality and internal causality in the ishvara-jiva-jagat movie is superimposition on the projective play of Brahman+Maya.

  
The important point is that the Screen-movie Knowledge does not negate the experience and awareness of duality; it only reduces all identification related to the movie as being nothing but of the Screen's projection. Likewise even the "jnani", in the context of identification with a projected body-mind, is aware of the ishvara-jiva-jagat movie, and as a consequence can only affirm the Reality in dualistic terms as Brahman+Maya=Ishvara. This Ishvara (Brahman+Maya) is the highest understanding of Brahman possible from within the context of vyavaharika standpoint, where one has to understand the non-duality of Consciousness (the Reality) in the context of the duality of namarupa experience (the appearance).  
  

Says Gaudapada in the Mandukya Karika 2.12:  
  

"The self-effulgent Self imagines Itself through Itself by the power of Its own Maya. The Self Itself cognizes the objects. Such is the definite conclusion of Vedanta."  
  

He alone is the witness, He alone is the witnessed. He alone is the hearer, He alone is the heard. He alone appears by mayashakti as the duality of hearer and heard, seer and seen, Dreamer and dream, Ishvara and ishvara-jiva-jagat. But implicit to such description is acknowledgment of a basic dualistic structure for describing reality, which necessitates Maya association with Brahman, hence we have Ishvara still. The Nirguna Brahman (non-dual Reality) of the paramarthika standpoint, beyond the reach of ordinary word and thought, transcends this type of duality based description of Brahman. Nirguna Brahman is not to be described as the seer who sees the seen; It is also not the Seer who sees the seer-seeing-seen. All such dualistic wrangling are silenced in the final advaitic Knowledge of Reality pointed to in the shastras.
