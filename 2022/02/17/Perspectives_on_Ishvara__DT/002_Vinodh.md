Namaskaram Putran ji,

  

Very good points on Ishwara especially from the fundamental (paaramaarthik) point of view of Advaita. In essence, as you have mentioned in 5., Ishwara and Jiva are the same Self albeit appearing as different entities due to different upaadhis (apparently limiting / conditioning features).

  

From a transactional (vyaavahaarik) point of view, these upaadhis have very different effects. For example,

a\) Ishwara has jnana of his true Self and due to this, even though in Maya, Ishwara is in control of Maya and not the other way round; on the hand other, Jiva is in ajnana and due to this, he is under the control of Maya

b\) It is Ishwara who is also the bestower of jnana for a Jiva; the power of the Ishwara helps provide chitta-shuddhi (purification of mind) to the Jiva and helps Jiva see the truth about his Self

c\) Although, from a paaramaarthik view, both Jiva and Ishwara are the same Self, from a vyaavahaarika view, a Jiva never becomes Ishwara. When the Jiva identifies with the Self, it identifies with the Self of all, including that of Ishwara. However, from the vyaavahaarika perspectivie, the Jiva does not get another upaadhi to become Ishwara (by getting aishwarya etc.).
