> Although I recall hearing things like devatas like Indra and Brahma
> are *jivas* who have acquired such a position (temporary) due to their
> Punya and karma. I don’t know if Advaita Sampradaya subscribes to this
> view. The devatas if understood as conditioned to a particular
> function are not equivalent to Ishvara, but they would still possess
> extraordinary powers etc.

  

True, various devatas like Indra and Brahma are indeed said to be jivas that have acquired their current status due to their past karmas and will retain that status for a long albeit finite period of time. After this time, they may take other forms depending on their karma. Therefore it is useful to think of these as “posts” like the various government posts (ministers etc.) that are taken up by different jivas for a certain period of time.

  

My understanding within the Advaita sampradaya is that based on the Vaidika shaastras Adi Sankara has identified six deities - ganapathy, subrahmanya, shiva, vishnu, shakti, and surya - who can each be individually worhipped as Ishwara. This may imply that these deities have not attained to their status through past karma, but permanently retain their status, that is, these are not like temporary posts that jivas take up due to past punya, but rather various forms of Ishwara itself.

  

I am interested in hearing more about this from other scholars in the group. 🙏
