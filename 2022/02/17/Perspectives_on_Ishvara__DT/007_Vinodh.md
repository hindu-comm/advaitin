Regarding this and the note about going beyond words, I am reminded of the passage from Taittiriya Shaka’s very last chapter (also called Mahanarayana Upanishad), 79th anuvaka, which translates to English as:

  

LXXIX-14: The year is the yonder sun.**That Person who is in the sun****is**Hiranyagarbha; He is Parameshthin (the protector of the universe) and**Brahmatman – Supreme Reality that is the innermost Self of all creatures**.  
LXXIX-15:**Those rays by which the****sun gives heat**, thesame**rays transform water into rain-cloud**which showers the rain.**By the rain-cloud herbs**and treescome into existence.From herbs and trees**food**is produced.By the use of food the**breaths**and**sense are nourished**. When the life-breath is**nourished one gets bodily strength**. Bodily strength gives the**capacity to practise Tapas**(in the shape of self-control, religious fast and so forth). As the**result of such Tapas, faith in scriptural truths**springs into existence.**By faith mental power**comes.**By mental power sense-control**is made possible.**By sense-control reflection is engendered. From reflection calmness of mind**results.**Conclusive experience of Truth follows calmness. By conclusive experience of Truthremembranceof It is engendered.Remembranceproduces continuousremembrance. From continuousremembranceresults unbroken direct realization of Truth. By such realization a person knows the Atman.**For this reason, he who gives food gives all these. For, it is found that the vital breaths and the senses of creatures are from food, that reflection functions with the vital breath and the senses, that unbroken direct realization comes from reflection and that bliss comes from unbroken direct realization of Truth. Thus having attained bliss one becomes the Supreme which is the source of the universe.  

  

This sruti is part of thereply of Prajapati to his son Aaruni for his question about the supreme means of liberation (among sama, dama, etc.).

  

It starts with identifying the sun with the Purusha, which is the same as the Brahman, which is within the self of all creatures, and then describes how it is connected to the various means of liberation (sama, dama, etc.) and how they lead one to the realization of Truth.

  

Here, interestingly, tapas (austerity/penance), which is made possible by the strength provided by food, leads one to “shraddha” (faith in scriptural truths). Recall also the meditation of the Vijnanamaya Kosha (sheath of intellect) as a Purusha in the Brahmanandavalli, where shraddha is to be imagined as the head of the Vijnanamaya Kosha of the Purusha (“asya shraddha eva shirah”), the Rtam (what is right/correct) is the right-hand side (“rtam dakshina pakshah”), etc.

  

This shraddha gives the mental power

to the manas (mind) that makes dama (sense-control) possible. Sense-control enables mananam (reflection on the scriptural truths one has heard). From reflection comes sama (calmness of mind). From sama experience of Truth. From experience,remembrance. From continuousremembrance, unbroken direct realization. By such realization one knows (and becomes) the Atman.

  

In this way, the sruti not just beautifully connects the macrocosm (universe) with the microcosm (individual) through the sun, rain, food, etc., but it also shows how all these are connected in a cause-effect chain which enables the individual to realize the atman as the same as the one in the sun and the innermost of all creatures.
