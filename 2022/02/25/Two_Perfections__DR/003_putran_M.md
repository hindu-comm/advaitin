I read of a doctor in a well-known US University hospital today, listing among his clinical interests, "Mindfulness". Based on the little I have read (mainly from Rajiv Malhotra), it is a "digestion" and adaptation of vippasanameditation from Buddhism. It made me think that this present writeup is basically on *advaitic/hindu**mindfulness* that an astikawould want to have. If one is centered in (or strives, with commitment and shraddha, for) such a 'mindfulness' in dailylife, no doubt he will be shielded from the deeper mental struggles that follow being wantonly lost in adhyasa and will have confidence in being able to restart afresh at any moment. That was my thinking in sending it to more people in the family and hoping it will reach or get passed on to younger generation. But one cannot know how it will resonate with others since we often assume an astika mindset to begin with and that itself may be lacking nowadays. Without it, people may fancy the mindfulness preached in a medical institution even while disregarding the powerhouse of Knowledge readily available in their own religion. And the higher purpose for such techniques and mindfulness is deliberately done away with. Our intention however cannot become to repackage - divorced from the principles of our religion - simply so that it will appeal to nastikas and the charvaka mindset. Its always better and safer if our intended audience is astikas or those at least formally part of the traditional background. Let others partake as they can.
