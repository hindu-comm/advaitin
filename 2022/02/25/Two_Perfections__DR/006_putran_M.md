The Self is Purna ("Perfect"); the Lord is Purna (Perfect). The Lord is vivarta-manifestation of/adhyasa on Self. Having taken away through Knowledge the duality-based identifications of "Lord" (ishvara-jiva-jagat), the Self the Purna remains (realized) as non-dual Reality.

The Self is Purna ("Perfect"); the Lord is Purna (Perfect). The Lord is vivarta-manifestation of/adhyasa on Self (here, Ishvara=Brahman+maya). Having taken away through Knowledge the duality-based identifications of "Lord" (ishvara-jiva-jagat), the Self the Purna remains (realized) as non-dual Reality.  

  

When there is reference to duality (such as Lord/ishvara of jiva-jagat), we have to defer to a dualistic construct when speaking of the underlying Self as well; that is, the Self is associated with maya-shakti,*due to which*it appears as if ishvara-jiva-jagat. This "appearance as if" is what is said to be Vivarta; it is an apparent (dualistic) manifestation that is not real. There is no real room for any sort of separateidentity apart from or multiplicity within Brahman/Self. So any such identifications is as-if. At the same time, theappearance is also not absolutely non-existent or unreal as it is experienced "as if real". That is why we say it is vivarta, a sort of projection/appearance without real change in the substratum,*due to maya*. Other ways of referring to this include "it is mithya/anirvachaniya, neither real nor unreal"; "it is adhyasa, due to superimposition on the Reality - one thing mistaken for another"; "it is a product of ignorance or ajnana". A famous analogy often given is "A rope mistakenly taken to be a snake". A superimposition due to ignorance that upon removal (of the defect/obstruction) results in right Knowledge; but until there is realization, the apparent snake cannot be negated in absolute terms. After knowledge, the question does not arise in oneself; but where it is raised, one calls it maya or ajnana and points to the real. But each has to realize himself and dissolve the "self-world-God" triple in the 'vision' of the Self. (And till that happens, the fun is the puzzle of making it happen.)
