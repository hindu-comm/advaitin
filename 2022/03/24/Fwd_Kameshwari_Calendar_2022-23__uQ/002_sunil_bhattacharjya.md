Dear Ramanji,

  

Mother Kameshwari is Mother Lalita or Mother Kamakshi. She is also known as Mother Shodashi, with her temple in the Southern India in Kanchipuram. Another temple in the Northern India is in Kamakhya in Guwahati in Assam. Lord Krishna said that for Mukti, one has to pray to mother Goddess Durga, who has manifested in the dasha (10) Mahavidya roopas, of which Lalita or Kamakshi or Kamakhya is one. In the Panchayatana puja there is Mother Bhavani or Durga, and there is also the addition of the sixth deity, Lord Kartikeya or Lord Murugan.  

  

Adi Shankara's last composition was the commentary on the Lalita Trishati and he wrote it, as advised by Sri Gaudapadacharya, his parama guru. To my knowledge, Adi Shankara wrote on the Lalita Trishati, while he was in Kudali-Shringeri, at the confluence of the rivers Tunga and Bhadra, and this math is 14 KM from Shimoga town in Karnataka.

In the Kudali-Shringeri Math, the original statue of Mother Sharada Devi is in the standing position, and this Math is at the confluence of the rivers Tunga and Bhadra, as mentioned in the second verse of the "Sharada Bhujanga Prayātāshtakam". This relevant verse is as follows:

कटाक्षे दयार्द्रां करे ज्ञानमुद्रां कलाभिर्विनिद्रां कलापैः सुभद्राम् ।

पुरस्त्रीं विनिद्रां पुरस्तुङ्गभद्रां भजे शारदाम्बामजस्रं मदम्बाम् ॥ २ ॥

  

After the Muslim attack on that math about 900 years ago, Swami Vidyashankara Teertha left Kudali for Kanchipuram. Later on his disciple Sri Bharati teertha, shifted to another new math constructed at Sringeri, about 100 km upstream on the bank of the river Tunga, and installed there a Murti of Sri Sharada devi, in the sitting position. The Vijayanagara kings helped him to make the new math there. However the original Shringeri math is still there at Kudali-Shringeri, with the statue of mother Sharada devi in the standing position. .  

  

As regards Adi Shankara, he shifted from Kudali Shringeri to Kanchipurama and established a math there and soon thereafter he had his liberation in Kanchipuram.  

  

Best wishes,

Sunil K. Bhattacharjya
