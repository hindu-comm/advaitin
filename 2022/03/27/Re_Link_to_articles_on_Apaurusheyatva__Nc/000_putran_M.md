Namaskaram Chitta-ji,

  

The pdf version I uploaded of that weblink splits the work into only 12 pages, and I completed only till page 5 and in the start of 6. What a great contribution!

  

I did see that you are identifying the nastikas who will oppose apaurusheya in different places: like when you talk of the followers of science, or of the method of avirodha vs samanvaya; and you are telling us of who the traditional proof is meant for.

  

I wondered whether the modern historians and linguists, who look at the nature of words and the natural events and locations depicted in the Vedas to postulate ('prove') the chronology within the past 3000 years - people like Elst - should they not be treated separately from the scientist or the category of science?

  

Then when I read about avirodha, the question came up: how should or how does an astika become an astika? He is not an apta. Is it a matter of training the mind or is the person simply convinced by the traditional proof as opposed to the nastika logic? Because in avirodha, the astika is focused not in proving the Vedic position but in dismantling or finding flaw in the opponents' arguments. And in samanvaya, both are already in agreement that Veda is pramana. Elsewhere you said that the proof is not for the atheist. Then should the astika come with a shraddha in tradition and be humble and therefore go along with the traditional proof and reject the nastika logic, as being a means to the higher end which is the knowledge? And his way of tackling the nastika, who will not accept the traditional proof, is grounded in the fact that there is no perfect tarka that can disprove the traditional position; they will rely on speculative theories and uncertain data to try and appear concrete but ultimately will be incompleteor incoherent. Hence the astika has nothing to fear. Is that the idea?

  

(Noting the thoughts, you may have answered later.)

  

thollmelukaalkizhu
