*The pdf version I uploaded of that weblink splits the work into only 12 pages, and I completed only till page 5 and in the start of 6. What a great contribution!*

  

Thank you for your kind words, Putran-ji.

  

  

*I did see that you are identifying the nastikas who will oppose apaurusheya in different places: like when you talk of the followers of science, or of the method of avirodha vs samanvaya; and you are telling us of who the traditional proof is meant for.*  

  

Yes, because there is the question of adhikara involved.

  

  

*I wondered whether the modern historians and linguists, who look at the nature of words and the natural events and locations depicted in the Vedas to postulate ('prove') the chronology within the past 3000 years - people like Elst - should they not be treated separately from the scientist or the category of science?*

  

Yes, the historians, anthropologists and sociologists need to be treated differently from the scientists since they use different kinds of arguments to discredit the Vedic tradition.

  

  

*Then when I read about avirodha, the question came up: how should or how does an astika become an astika? He is not an apta. Is it a matter of training the mind or is the person simply convinced by the traditional proof as opposed to the nastika logic?*  

  

It is matter of two things: (i) training the mind through the application of Nyaya / reasoning, and (ii) performance of obligatory karma and avoidance of prohibited karma. The efforts at intellectually understanding the shastras may not bear fruit due to the presence of internal mental obstructions and this can only be cleared by performing nitya/nimittika karmas which have the power to remove these obstructions and confer the required mental purity for grasping the truths mentioned in the shastras.

  

  

*Because in avirodha, the astika is focused not in proving the Vedic position but in dismantling or finding flaw in the opponents' arguments.*  

  

Yes, because it would be futile to attempt to make someone who does not have the adhikara to see the higher truths of our shastras. So the best that can be done is to dismantle all his/her objections to it and create a clearing in which the tradition can continue to hold sway.  

  

  

*And in samanvaya, both are already in agreement that Veda is pramana.*  

  

Yes, true.

  

  

*Elsewhere you said that the proof is not for the atheist. Then should the astika come with a shraddha in tradition and be humble and therefore go along with the traditional proof and reject the nastika logic, as being a means to the higher end which is the knowledge?*  

  

Yes. But the astika needs to do ne more thing. He/she needs to continue to perform the obligatory karmas so that he/she would attain the mental purity to actually cognize the truth of the Vedas being apaurusheya. It is cognized when the nature of Sabda is cognized.

  

  

*And his way of tackling the nastika, who will not accept the traditional proof, is grounded in the fact that there is no perfect tarka that can disprove the traditional position; they will rely on speculative theories and uncertain data to try and appear concrete but ultimately will be incompleteor incoherent. Hence the astika has nothing to fear. Is that the idea?*  



Yes, that is the way I understand it.
