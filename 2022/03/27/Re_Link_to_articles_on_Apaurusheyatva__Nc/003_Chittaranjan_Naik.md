Dear Sri Putran-ji,

  

  

*Here is Elst's conclusion in that blog article. I haven't read the main part, will do that after finishing your exposition; but it should be an elaboration of the contention he is making in this conclusion. The conclusion also indicates that his attack is not on traditionalism in general but on apaurusheya particularly.*

  

I have had the pleasure of interacting briefly with Dr. Koenraad Elst on the Vadavali yahoogroup many years ago. He was trying to make a case that the Vedas were composed by intoxicated drinkers of Soma juice. I asked him to provide proof to substantiate what he was saying since otherwise his statement would amount to a mere dogmatic assertion. He got back with some vague replies, so I challenged him to put his reply in the framework of epistemology by telling me which of the epistemological instruments like pratyaksha or anumana he was using to claim that the Vedas were composed by intoxicated Soma drinkers. He responded by saying that he did not want to get into a theological discussion!

  

So, according to Dr. Koenraad Elst, using pramanas like pratyaksha and anumana in the discussion would transform it into a theological discussion!! Unfortunately, Yahoogroups has shutdown; otherwise I would have pointed you to that amusing conversation. :-)

  



***Conclusion***

*Those who disagree with us and stick to the belief in an eternal and*

*uncreated Veda, shouldn’t convinceus, they should go and convince the*

*Ṛṣis.It is they, not the cabal of Western India-watchers or Orientalists, who*

*have**described a pre-Vedic stretch of history**, and who gave us a collection*

*of hymns expressing the human viewpoint and addressing the gods. They*

*included**all manner of historical details, from contingent and convoluted***

***family sagas through the names of rivers to descriptions of battles**, all in*

*between their main purpose of singing praise to the gods.*

  

This is an argument put forth by someone who has no inkling of what the Veda is. Vedic words do not point to concrete objects in the world; they point to their own meanings which are all universals (samanyas). This is the position of both Purva Mimamsa and Uttara Mimamsa. Has Dr. Koenraad Elst engaged these arguments of Mimamsa and disproved them? If not, on what ground does he assume that the meaning he confers to the semantics of Vedic vakhyas are valid and that the Vedic semantics put forth by Mimamsa are wrong? Dr. Koenraad Elst is not a Pope that he can make proclamations from a pontifical chair and have us follow his edicts. He needs to engage with Vedic philosophies before seeking to so blithely brush them aside and foist his own dogmatic assertions on to our tradition.

  

  

As I agreed with you in my previous mail, people like Koenraad Elst and others who come from a historical, anthropological, sociological, etc, framework need to be engaged separately from the scientists.

  

  

Warm regards,
