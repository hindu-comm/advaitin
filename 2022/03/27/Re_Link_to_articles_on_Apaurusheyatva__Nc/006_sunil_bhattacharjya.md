Dear Putranji, Chaittaranjanji and friends,

  

Nice to see the good discussions on Apaurusheyatva of the Vedas, and I see the name of our friend Koenraad Elst is also appearing and there is indication of the need for determination of historicity.  

  

Being involved in the determination of the historical dates of the events of Ramayana and Mahabharata and also the dates of several other past events and personalities, I have observed that the ancient texts have given us a lot of valuable data for determination of the
historicity of the past events as well as of the great personalities of the past. However, most of our present-day scholars are not very careful about the meaning of the words and phrases in the vedic texts and even in the fifth-veda texts of the Mahabharata and Ramayana and the Puranas, because of which they failed in their attempts to ascertain the dates of the ancient events as well as of the personalities.  

  

Permit me to cite one instance from the Mahabharata war, which took place when ninety-nine (99) percent of the Dwapara yuga was already over, and only the last one (1) percent was remaining . The Bhagavata Purana and other Mahapuranas tell us that the Kali yuga appeared on the very day Lord Krishna left fLord Hanumana himself, that the Kali yuga is expected to start very soon, thereby meaning that the Mahabharata war took place at the fag-end of the Dwapara yuga. Yet, one great Sanskrit scholar late Prof. Raghavan claimed that the Mahabharata war took place in the Kali yuga. The details are as follows:

  

The gada-yuddha or the mace-duel between Bhimasena and Duryodhana took place in the presence of Lord Krishna and his elder-brother Lord Balarama, and the rest of the Pandavas. It is relevant to mention here
that on an earlier occasion, when the Pandavas were defeated in the game of Dice, the victor Duryidhana insulted Draupadi by calling her to sit on his thigh and Bhimasena became furious and he promised to break the thigh of Duryodhana. ***At last the gada-yuddha appeared to give him the last chance to fulfil his promise.*** In the gada-yudhha Bhaimasena tried his best but he did not get any advantage over Duryodhana, and the ne decided to kill Duryodhana by adopting foul tactics by hitting Duryodhana below his waist-line. When Duryodhana fell flat with his thigh broken, Lord Balaram became furious and he rose to punish Bhimasena. Then Lord Krishna controlled his brother saying *"***Praptam Viddhi** **Kaliyugam"** **and also that Bhimasena has to keep his vow to break the thigh of Duryodhana****. While the intelligent Lord Balarama understood what Lord Krishna said, but the present-day scholars like Prof. Raghavan could not understand what Lord Krishna said. In the Adi Parva of the Mahabharata it is given that Duryodhana is Kali-reborn. Vedavyasa also told in the Mahabharata "***Kalim Viddhi Diryudhanam***". In the light of this statement the statement Thus "***Praptam Viddhi** **Kaliyugam"*** actually meant ***"****Praptam ***Viddhi Diryudhanam***" ***and also that Bhimasena has to keep his vow to break the thigh of Duryodhana****.******

******  ******

The word "viddhi" has been used in Upanishad too as follows: ******  ******

\(i\) ***atmamam vidhi rathim*** means consider  **the atmana is the rider**, and  

\(ii\) ***Sareeram viddhi ratham*** means consider **the body is the vehicle**.  

  

There are many such instances, where **Paroksha ukti** in the pre-paninian texts have baffled the Paninian Sanskrit scholars. There is another example, where too most of the Sanskrit scholars have not been able to understand the meaning of the word "Tribhagashesha", appearing in the Mahabharata.  

  

My 2 cents

Sunil K. BHattacharjya
