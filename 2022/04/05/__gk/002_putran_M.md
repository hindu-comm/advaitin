This should be taken as a lazy statement on my part, where I wanted to avoid having to write too much - since it can mislead. What I meant (and understand) is that if two parties accepted the validity of a pramana (not necessarily pratyaksha-based, can also be shabda) and it revealed a certain knowledge which they understood differently, then tarka can aid illuminate that knowledge correctly by removing the ignorance, wrong deductions and superimpositions that led to wrong understanding for one of them. This is possible because they both are seeking to know correctly what that pramana is revealing, provided obstructions and defects are removed - and tarka can help clear them.

  

But tarka based in one pramana may not suffice to "prove" (remove all doubts superimposed on) the knowledge obtained from another. So if the pramana for certain knowledge is shabda and my tarka resorts to examples from pratyaksha, then the tarka has a secondary supporting role in that itmay support the shabda-based knowledge in my mind simply because I am able to relate better to the pratyaksha-based example. It however cannot overcome the error of me looking at one pramana's evidence as actually "proof" for another's knowledge; that is not the purpose here.

  

Similarly in a situation where two persons are fighting over some knowledge and they don't agree on what is pramana for it (or even whether there is such a thing as pramana), then the tarka the first person uses to defend himself or negate the other may not necessarily reach a mutually acceptable "Proved!" for both of them. For example, in deference to the opponent's own beliefs, he may even be using tarka based in pratyaksha to defend knowledge that is founded in shabda. Or he may use tarka that accommodates the opponent's speculative methods rather than insist on staying aligned with the pramanas accepted by tradition. In these cases we have to know the limits to what can be achieved by tarka and what cannot, and suitably adjust our expectations and goals in the debate. It may simply be to reiterate the logical grounds clearly in his own mind and if possible to do avirodha of the other.
