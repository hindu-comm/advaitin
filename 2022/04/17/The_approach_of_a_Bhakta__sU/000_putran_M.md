Namaskaram,

  

I had some occasion to start some work with someone (a ‘senior’ to me) and decided on my own to ask an old friend (whom I had not contacted for near 10 years) for possible help, to join the project should my co-worker agree. The friend who is a Bhakta of Matha Amritanandamayi (Ammachi) wrote the following (after preliminary mails where I expressed concern of whether the co-worker will agree to his joining the team)

  

“Thank you… Wecan start the work. I will call you tomorrow after messaging. Even if \[he\] says no, we can work for knowledge and can help you to give some pointers …. “

  

This could involve much time and effort, and he had done similarly on my request 10 years back as well, in work that did not obtain worldly recognition. A lesser person would think “Where’s my credit, my recognition?” I am amazed sometimes when I see such people, manifesting philosophy ‘naturally’, and know I have to try and learn from them.

  

I wrote the below in the reply.

  

Quote

  

When I read your inspiring attitude of doing the work just for knowledge, it reminds me of some recent thoughts.

  

The ‘meaning’ of “world” is Brahman alone; to think otherwise is ignorance. Knowledge of anything is knowledge of Brahman if we remove the “thing” and realize the reality it denotes, the nondual Consciousness that manifests/appears thus. That object of Consciousness is nothing but Brahmasvarupam, and it is ignorance to see it as apart (or even different) from Consciousness and as if a thing unto itself. If I see you, I realize You; it will be silly if instead I am thinking I see a body, speak a name \[…\] and so on.. Likewise it is Brahman that appears as world, and to seek to know “world”, we are seeking abidance in awareness of Him, for that Consciousness is alone the reality of all namarupa manifestations. Sarvam kalu idam Brahma.

  

The Bhakta sees (or seeks to see) God everywhere and in everything, and to know a new form of Mother is simply a new way to know Mother and focus on Her. So he doubles down on the opportunity for that sake rather than through the lens of ignorance that most others wear.  

  

Of course we are all still sadhakas facing the complications and confusions of our own mind, but we should strive to keep reinforcing the right perspective as we go about life.

  

Unquote
