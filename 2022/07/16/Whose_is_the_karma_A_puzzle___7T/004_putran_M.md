Namaskaram Subbu-ji,

  

Thanks for the explanation. As I understand fromthis, it is one's own karma (past actions) whose phala is one's present experiences including what comes via others' actions and the "reactions" in the mind that follow. B's actions have set up its subtle body in such a way that it must undergo certain joy, sorrow, etc. "A" merely is the catalyst for that to happen. A's own past karma have also set it up in a karmic connection with B, where the potential for it to act in a certain way and the potential for B to experience what it must are both in place.

  

However, where A and B have the choice or free-will, there they have the possibility of choosing to act as per dharma or otherwise. According to their karma, they may or may not have the right guidance regarding dharma at that point. However be that, the action that they will into manifestation is still their own responsibility/karma and cannot be blamed on the other. "A" is not obligated into action by B's karma, and vice-versa.

  

In your example, the terrorist is fully responsible for his act of terrorism; and the people who suffer from that act are actually fully responsible for their suffering - which their karma has set them up for, whether it comes via a bomb or an earthquake. As per Vedanta, the "karma" of the terrorist is in his willing action of terrorism and not in terms of the experience of his victims.

  

The only caveat I was thinking of is how dharma comes into the picture here. When one aligns with dharma by volition and in a detached frame of mind, it is perhaps non-different from the natural flow of Ishvara'sOrder. The "doer" is being responsible without acquiring responsibility for his action. If A's action triggers in B a reaction at the end of which B is presented with the option to follow Dharma or not, and B in fact opts Dharma in a state of surrender to Ishvara'swill, then B should not acquire new karma nor should B's action be regarded his personal responsibility for it is aligned with the impersonal Order of the Lord. Of course, as you said, the shastra acts as our pramana regarding dharma; we can't simply imagine something is dharma and pretend surrender.

  

I will think further if there is more to this I want to understand better.

  

thollmelukaalkizhu
