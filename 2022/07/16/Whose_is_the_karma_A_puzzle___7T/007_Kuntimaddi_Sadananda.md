--------

Let's look at the sequence. I am not bringing in the dharma dimension below, only the karma and causality.



1\. A does an Action.



If we take this as the starting point for this closed problem, then A certainly is the cause of this action. B has no responsibility yet.

If I perform the action irrespective of whether someone is watching or not – Lord will be watching. He ensures that the results of the action both tangable and untangle belongto me. The action may cause a series of other actions by other doers – who are part of the universe.

2\. B obtains knowledge of A's action.

That is B’s problem!

This also we may assume happens without B's own volition. Rather let us add the twist that A knows that B will obtain knowledge of its action (possibly because A does it in B's presence).

If A does the action – that is one part, If A does the action and make sure that B knows that action is being formed – that is part of A’s responsibility and must bear the consequences of doing that action with the knowledge of B. B may be his boss, and A wants to make sure the boss knows what he is doing!

---------------

But either way, B naturally obtains the knowledge; it is not responsible in an active sense here. (Although in a karmic sense, we can argue otherwise; but we can leave that aside here.)

If B obtains that knowledge, then that is part of his action. The Boss wants to know how everybody is performing in his group! That is part of his duty. His boss may be watching B!

If B hears about the action or inaction, that is part of A’s praarabda.

3\. The knowledge of A's action triggers a reaction within B - due to the makeup of its subtle body - ending in its iccha.

B’s reaction can be just at a a mental level – if he gets disturbed that is part of the results he has to bear. We are hearing many Hindus are being victimized – that is our knowledge. The question is what we are going to do with that information depends on our BMI capabilities and commitments.

This is where the problem gets interesting.

Since this happens all the time, I do not see any problem.

In the karmic sense, B is responsible for the makeup of its subtle-body that is a consequence of its own past karma.

You mean how he reacts – it depends on the past karma and his discipline of the mind – in terms of controlling the feelings, his intelligence, etc. all involved. You can put it as part of his subtle body – but that is too gross level!

b\. But in the practical sense, B is already in that state of identification, ignorance, etc., so the jnana of A's action triggers a certain reaction based on mental pathways that B follows as if hypnotized and which leads it to the iccha for a particular kriya.

Not necessarily. We have heard the action of the Hindu lady – We are all B’s now. How each B reacts depends on our mental state and the course of action varies with each B. some let it go, some criticize and bare the consequence of that action too as we have witnessed.

-------------

Should we consider B's mental-condition as an intrinsic fact of its being and hence regard A as directly responsible for the end result of B's iccha?

A is responsible only to the extent that he is insuring that B knows his action. If A is an employee, A performs to his best making sure that B is aware of it. What B does with that info and acts, that action may affect A – if he gets a promotion or demotion!

-----------

what if A also knew of B's identifications beforehand but regarded it should not be held responsible for B's delusions and ignorance?

If the action of A involves B indirectly as in the employee – Boss situation, there is nothing delusional about it. A is happy that B is not delusional but responds properly to A’s action. I do not see any problem. If you are implying some emotional things as A and B are husband and wife! Then both are responsible for each other’s action or inaction as they affect both.

-------------

ii\. what if A did not know? Would it still be a case similar to manslaughter since A's action invariably led to B's iccha?

Ignorance cannot be the basis for things when A is required to know or B is required to know about A’s action as in employee-boss or husband and wife. If it is general action and B should have, could have, or would have (or have not) that is not A’s responsibility. The question of how B responds depends on B and his action could affect A, and if it affects then B is accountable for that. 

----------------

Or should we not hold A to be responsible for B's subtle body and its active reinforcing of self-ignorance?

No. No one is responsible for other’s subtle body. If B is closely related to A in some form, then actions and results mutually affect each other depending on the accountability of each for the other’s actions. Employee- boss or husband-wife situations.

------------

That is to say, even though it appears B's prior conditioning automatically triggered its iccha, should we refute that notion and say that B is responsible for actively reinforcing this mind-state and following the jnana of A's action to the iccha?

Not sure what you mean – take the case of employee and Boss situation – the actions and motivations are accountable for each other depending on the nature of the action. If it is husband and wife – the accountability affects each -subtle bodies too. As per Hinduism half of punyam of huband’s, wife gets while he gets half of her paapams. He keeps all his paapam and she keeps all her punyams – provided she is sahadharma chaarini!

-----------------

Where does self-will (over robotic prior conditioning) come into the picture here?

At every stage of action, self-will is involved since actions are done by will. Hence one is accountable for their actions as karmanyeva adhikaraste. If I put my finger into the fire and got burned, it will affect my wife. She will be worried, take me to hospital and all the rigmarole. If A as the employee does bad, B will be affected and His boss will make sure of it!

---------------------

4. B follows through on that iccha and does its own kriya as response.

Then B is accountable for his action- like the action of the boss. It may affect A, and also others who may not have done any work when they are supposed to do!

--------------

Yes, B actively does the kriya but it would not have done so if not for the iccha that would not have happened if not for A's initial action. So is A responsible still for B's action? How/how not?

Yes, for employee-boss situation you can see how B acts depending on A’s action and also how much B is aware of that action when he should be aware. What A does at home which is nothing to do with employment, B whether he knows or not will not have any effect since it is unrelated to the employment. 

Most of it is just logical, as I see it.
