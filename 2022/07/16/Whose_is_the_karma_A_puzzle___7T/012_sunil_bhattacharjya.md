Dear Putranji,

  

I appreciate the great discussions. Another way is to take the layman's approach. We are Lord Krishna's children or Mother Kali's children and we are a family (in the Vyavaharika world), and we have to:

  

**(i) SPEAK THE TRUTH**, and ensure what we know and think to be the truth is really the truth, even though it is the Vyavaharika truth. Untruth is permitted only for the innocent Jokes or for saving someone's life (including yours) and without causing any harm to anybody. For example, if one knows or thinks Adi Shankara to be born on a particular year, ensure in all possible ways and with an open mind that it is really so. One is responsible for one's own beliefs and actions. One can't say that one believe(d) such and such person's statement. If your human guru or any other respected person tells you that Adi Shankaracharya wrote the Bhagavad Gita Bhasya and that was around such and such year, do listen to it respectfully, but don't forget for a moment that "To Err is Human". Keep it in mind that it does not hurt, if you try to check what you heard, in all possible ways to find out the truth. You can respectfully share the findings even with your respected Guru or the respected person and if he is spiritually enlightened, he would bless you.  

  

**(ii) LOKASAMGRAHA** should be the aim of our Vyavaharika life. Don't forget that we are a family and we have to think and do all that is good for all of us, and not for any short-term individual personal gain, at the cost of others.  

**&**

**(iii)** **GIVE UP DEHABHIMANA (देहाभिमान)**, realizing that our deha (including both the sthula and the sukshma sareeras) is changing every moment as all of us took birth as a baby and most of you would die as older person, and lose our present identity on death. We have to return and be one with our Divinity (or we can say that we have to be back to our divine Mother and be one with Her).

  

If you think there is value or otherwise in this simple presentation, you may kindly share your thoughts.

  

Regards,

Sunil K. Bhattacharjya
