There are two related problems that we see in social/personal conflicts. I will pose them generally; members can ponder for themselves if not indulge the topic here further.

  

The question in these cases would be whether the principles of Vedanta and Indian logic can pinpoint Dharma, Adharma, in a decisive manner, with clarity regarding boundaries in action and responsibility.

  

1\. What if A curses B's mother andB punches A in response? (Here the power/influence structure is not key). In an extreme case, we can replace "punches" with "shoots".

  

There is instigation here from Ato B, that maybe deemed intrinsically adharma.

  

Is B's reaction aligned to dharma in general? Will the answer depend on varna, religion, gender etc?

  

Does it cross the bounds of dharma since the reaction can be deemed as being incommensurate to the original action (punching/shooting vs cursing)?

  

Again, remember B has karma-built up its subtle body that reacts 'uniquely'after acquiring jnana of A's action. It can (appear to robotically) trigger the iccha to smile back, to curse back, to punch back, or to shoot back, depending on the "past-programming". Still B is conscious of right and wrong and the play of its self-willis not entirely absent as it moves from jnana to iccha along the trodden or drawn-out paths in its mind or actively chooses new directions, and certainly from iccha to kriya.

  

If B's response is deemed adharmic unto itself, then that means B has deviated from the bounds of dharma as defined by and in spiteof all that came before (A's instigation, B's subtle body, etc.). That deviation is its responsibility and not A's.

  

So as I said before, we are often confronted by the question "what is svadharma for B given A's action?" and no Krishna to tell us in clear terms.

  

The subtlety in this example is that A's action does not inherently instigate B's response - A is not trying to invoke B's punching or shooting or suggesting thus to B. Rather it is B's subtle-body makeup and its own decisions that call for its shoot/punch response. And we are left asking whether this incommensurate response is within the bounds of dharma. When/when not?

  

2\. What if A (instead of cursing) had tried to punch/shoot B? (Or actually punched/shot at B).

  

The difference here is that B's reaction of punching/shooting in response appears commensurate with A's action. If B feels physically threatened, there is an argument that its svadharma should be to defend itself and a physical response is warranted. Does Indian tradition/ dharma shastras agree? Does it still depend on varna, religion, gender etc? What if A punched B and B punches back in revenge and not for self-defense? Is it within the bounds of dharma? Here, the revenge action (by B) is separable from the original cause (by A),but should A be regarded as instigating B's parallel response?

  

These are tough questions that come up in our lives and society in a variety of complexrupas. Religion should be a clear guiding force in such circumstances; ideally however it should not merely appear emotive, idealistic or philosophical but also conform with rigorous logic except where we can only ascertain via shabda.
