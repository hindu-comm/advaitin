Namaskaram Sada-ji,

  

Thanks for the reply. I noticed however that you did change the context of the problem/example. I had said, "Let's say the officer's order is in violation of military law and the soldier knows this." But you took the question of action/responsibility in the context of the civilian violating the law and the officer's order being considered lawful. So that changes things and your answers likewise for some of the questions. I should have formulated my example more carefully.

  

Its fine however - it does provide your answers for the modified problem and how a Vedantin (or normal law-abiding person) should approach that scenario.

  

In your example, suppose you *did follow* the boss's orders (due to fear of getting fired etc.) Then (from what you said) you would agree that you have deviated from your svadharma. That means you accept that you have responsibility for your action in the sequence: Boss orders -\> you follow it. Yes, you will be judged differently (here or hereafter) taking into account that you were in a situation of duress due to Boss order. But though the boss will be judged in a bad way, you have your share of karma that cannot be faulted on the boss since ultimatelyit is your choice to act here as per dharma or otherwise.

  

And if as you said you did not follow the boss's orders (and not doing so is dharma in this case), then you have abided by your svadharma in that complex situation and will be "doubly awarded".

  

(I complicate things in trying to be precise. When these problems occur in the world, we are confronted by people who play on uncertainties and illogicalarguments.)
