> Thanks for the explanation. As I understand fromthis, it is one's own
> karma (past actions) whose phala is one's present experiences
> including what comes via others' actions and the "reactions" in the
> mind that follow. B's actions have set up its subtle body in such a
> way that it must undergo certain joy, sorrow, etc. "A" merely is the
> catalyst for that to happen. A's own past karma have also set it up in
> a karmic connection with B, where the potential for it to act in a
> certain way and the potential for B to experience what it must are
> both in place.
>
>   
>
> However, where A and B have the choice or free-will, there they have
> the possibility of choosing to act as per dharma or otherwise.
> According to their karma, they may or may not have the right guidance
> regarding dharma at that point. However be that, the action that they
> will into manifestation is still their own responsibility/karma and
> cannot be blamed on the other. "A" is not obligated into action by B's
> karma, and vice-versa.
>
>   
>
> In your example, the terrorist is fully responsible for his act of
> terrorism; and the people who suffer from that act are actually fully
> responsible for their suffering - which their karma has set them up
> for, whether it comes via a bomb or an earthquake. As per Vedanta, the
> "karma" of the terrorist is in his willing action of terrorism and not
> in terms of the experience of his victims.
