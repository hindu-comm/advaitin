Namaste Putran ji,

Re "*If Ramu watches a cricket match on TV, is that for Chitta shuddhi or for following Acharya instructions? Is he going to say “Let Him decide if this is dharma or not”? Rather he already decided in his heart of hearts this is svadharma in his body-mind-life context and is following it!*

*  *

*But actually … No. The kids are to blame, it is the wife, it is Indra and Chandra, ahem, no I am a Vedantin - it is vasana, it is prarabdha; Ishvara only knows if this is dharma, let me watch and let Him decide.*"

  

It is not clear to me from the above if you have already taken this into account or not, but:

  

What is dharma and what is adharma is not determinable by us. It is determined by *shAstra* - if the shAstra enjoins an action as something that is to be done, that is dharma. If the shAstra prohibits an action as something to be forbidden, that is adharma.

  

The performance of actions enjoined in the *scriptures* without a desire for the result, results in chitta shuddhi - not any actions done in general that may be outside the scope of scriptures (e.g. watching a cricket match).

  

I think the passage above may be the thoughts of someone trying to do a post hoc justification of their actions, but in all the various examples that you have mentioned, the test for what is dharma or adharma needs to be done from the standpoint of what the scriptures enjoin for each individual, and not personal preferences, or situations that are outside the purview of the shAstra-s.

  

Therefore, if an action is performed that is illegal according to man made laws, it is not necessary that it is adharma; equally, if an action is performed that is perfectly legal according to man made laws, it is not necessary that it is dharma either. In each case, the determination of dharma and adharma is whether such an action is:

  

a\) prescribed to me / prohibited for me by the shAstra-s.

  

b\) at that place.

  

c\) at that time.

  

d\) in that manner.

  

Kind regards,

Venkatraghavan
