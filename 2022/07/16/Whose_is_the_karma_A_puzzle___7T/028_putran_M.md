From my first mail:

  

Quote

  

A does an Action. (Kriya)

  

B obtains knowledge of A's action. (Jnana)

  

B desires a new action in response. (Iccha)

  

B does that new action. (Kriya)

  

When/How/To what extent is B's kriya the responsibility of A (i.e. A is cause for B's action)? When/How is it not?

  

I want to understand this in terms of karma and dharma, and whatever else is relevant.

  

Unquote

  

**If the wife shouts at the husband, and the husband kicks the cat in anger, thenis the wife's karma/'adharma', only her shouting at the husband or is she also (or only) responsible for her husband kicking the cat?**

  

The wife's shouting may be the catalyst for the husband to experience anger and frustration - as part of his past karma's phala. The wife in the process of shouting at husband does a potentially negative/adharmic karma.

  

It seems to me that if the husband is not a robot or a jnani, there is no basis to actually put the husband's kicking of cat on the wife. His iccha and kriya is his responsibility/karma. Either we can say that the husband accrued negative karma for choosing to kick the cat and experienced the phala for the past karma (in the form of a weak or perverted mind) that resulted in the iccha to strike the cat. Or we can say that the Husband had to explode as per his past karma and the cat had to experience the kicking, so he became the catalyst: his kicking is a natural outburst from the anger etc. that he had to experience but otherwise it was not driven by iccha or active self-choice. Even still, could the Vedantin "blame" the kicking of the cat on the wife who had no such iccha in her mind and whose shouting can only cause the husband to experience his karmaphala of a disturbed mind? How to best interpret the word "blame"?

  

The idea is that the question can be placed to anyone. The general world will have its answers and an extreme answer is that the wife aloneis to be faulted for thehusband's kicking and the cat getting kicked. The Vedantin should have a clear answer as well,and which if based on pratyaksha based logic, we ought to be able to do tarka against otherviewpoints and establish our own in a definitemanner.

Dharma is prarabdha karma phala. Whether youhave guidance or knowledge of it is prarabdha karma phala. Karma is based on what you choose to do now; karma may or may not align with your dharma.

  

Ishvara does not decide (iccha) your cap independent of your past karma; he is karmaphaladaataonly. He gives the cap that is suitable and appropriate phala for our karma, and we have the opportunity in that body-mind, circumstance, etc. to abide by dharma (follow our pravritti-marga, for chittashuddhi as you said above) or to deviate from dharma (following the preyas path of desires delusions etc.).

  

The Cap you have defines your dharma at this moment. When karma perfectly aligns with dharma (perfectly meaning true karma-yoga), you, the individual jiva, is not coming into picture. You have merged with Ishvara'sOrder/iccha and "your" action is one with His direction.

  

Such "karma" is not your responsibility but belongs to the flow from the past: the momentum of the past karma manifesting naturally per Ishvara'sOrder and through your body-mind - others think you are doing this or that but it is Isvhara iccha playing out through the body-mind and you are a witness only.

  

But that is the ideal situation, perhaps only true of jnanis and avataras. What must happen through them happens; they are not "influencing" in any self-motivated way.

  

For the rest of the jivas, karma *involving their self-will* is invariably prompted by desire that is based in ignorance of self; and such iccha-driven kriya deviates from svadharma to some extent. To the extent we can identify dharma via shastra and perform as yoga, we obtain the phala of chitta-shuddhi etc. But its a process since our hold on ajnana will not allow perfect surrender. So even though ourkarma may follow a guided path of "dharma" (rather than adharma or paradharma) and the pravritti marga route, there will be a certain level of deviation. (Strictly speaking, the deviation is a measure of 'adharma' for the jiva.)

  

NOW, this deviation is not going to be fully known or identifiable by the jiva, but WHATEVER it is is the Responsibility of this jiva and not of past prarabdha, the Cap, the space or anything else. All that was already accounted for, in your present Dharma; but you are now presented the opportunity to choose and act. We are specifically talking about your new karma following that choice and itsdeviation from svadharma.

  

Who did that? YOU. Not the wife or the kids, not the vasana or prarabdha, not even Ishvara. It is you the jiva caught in the web of ajnana who decides (iccha) and manifests (kriya) at the mind and body level in a way that reinforces your Ignorance and deviates from the dharmic flow of Ishvara's Order. You had the choice and if you chose to deviate, then that is your karma not anyone else's.
