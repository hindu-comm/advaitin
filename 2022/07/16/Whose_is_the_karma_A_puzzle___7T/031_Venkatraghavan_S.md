Namaste Putran ji,

  

The results (phala) of the actions (karma) of one person shall accrue to the doer and no one else. In the manubhAShya by Medhatithi, which is a commentary to the manusmRti, there is a line - न ह्यन्यकृतैनसोऽन्यत्र गमनमस्ति - the pApa that follows from the actions of one person shall not go to anyone else.

  

The reason is because each person has control / free will to act / react. What they don't have control over is other people's actions / reactions. Therefore, each of us bears responsibility for our action and no one else's (there are certain exceptions to this, such as the responsibility of the parent for the actions of a minor child).

  

In the example below, there are two actions - shouting ( by the wife), and kicking the cat (by the husband). The wife will get the karma phala of shouting and the husband will get the karma phala of kicking the cat.

  

The husband does not have to kick the cat, he does that out of his free will. He may have a greater tendency to do that because of his past karma (his mind may be prone to violence due to past samskAra-s), but in the present he has a choice - to kick or not to kick the cat. If he chooses to kick the cat, he will face incur sin for violating a basic principle (mA himsyAt sarvA bhUtAni) and face the consequences of his action.

  

If the cat is kicked, it is a karma phala of some past actions of that jIva.

  

The wife will face the consequences of shouting, not the kicking by the husband. The degree of consequence the wife faces for shouting will be determined by Ishvara and will be dependent on her prior knowledge and intentions.

  

Did she know how he would react and acted anyway? Or were his subsequent actions a complete surprise to her? If she had knowledge over how her husband will react (i.e. she is well aware of his tendency to be violent), but shouted at her husband to goad him into violence anyway, the degree of punishment she will receive for her shouting, will be higher than otherwise.

  

The knowledge, the intention and the conviction of the actor is a factor in the degree of results - be it good or bad - that the action will bring (यदेव विद्यया करोति श्रद्धयोपनिषदा तदेव वीर्यवत्तरं भवति as the ChAndogya upaniShad says).

  

You made a point in your email that the knowledge of dharma is prArabdha phala - not so. It is the duty of everyone to know what is dharma and what is adharma (some more so than others). The favourability of circumstances to learn about what is dharma and adharma may vary from person to person (depending on each person's prArabdha), but whether we study it / seek other's guidance or not is still our choice. Ignorance of the law - be it human or dharma - is no defence against breaking it.

  

Kind regards,

Venkatraghavan
