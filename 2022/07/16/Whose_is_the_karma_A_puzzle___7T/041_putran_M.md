Namaskaram Bhaskar-ji,

  

  

That is fine, you think my premise itself is faulty. You may be right; I take it in the context of our dharma being called sanatana. Let me put it more generally.

  

Of course suffering itself is a universal phenomenon. One can also identify groups like native americans, jews, blacks who have suffered immensely. Recently there has been the case of Tibetan Buddhists and Uighur Muslims in China.

  

I want to know if instead of thinking of suffering as an individual phenomenon, we can look at it as a group phenomenon and explain whether it makes sense in the karma theory. When a group struggles for survival at its identity level, does it indicate the group’s dharma is fundamentally flawed, or past members of that group failed in their svadharma, or jivas who were Adharmic elsewhere were sent to this group to suffer its collapse? Best explanation would be the middle one, but hard to digest that in many cases.

  

The native Americans, their culture and civilization has been wiped out due to invasions by those carrying the “Christian flag”. I don’t see something particularly Adharmic in the NA side that this should happen to them. Should we attribute that communal experience to the karma of the jivas who in past lives may have elsewhere done crimes as the Christians had done?

  

But logically why make the NA community/dharma suffer extinction when that civilization had not particularly strayed from its dharma?

  

Or should we think that such the Tibetan Buddhists of today may have been the Christian and Muslim invaders of yesterday? But then why should the Tibetan dharma suffer thus? It suggests that the NA and Tibetan dharma were fundamentally not Sanatana and that the Christian and Buddhist ones are. (Albeit my time frame is trivial in Ishvara’s scale.)

  

In our case we claim it is sanatana. Doesn’t appear to me that varnashrama dharma is sanatana as it is going, apparently either the past Brahmins/Hindus were really that bad that they undergo whatever communal difficulties (pertaining to following and keeping together their Hindu dharma) they sometimes claim to undergo; or Ishvara took people who did bad elsewhere and sent them to the brahmin community now to experience such difficulties. The latter explanation is difficult for why should *sanatana* dharma drown in order to manifest karmaphala for jivas who forsake dharma elsewhere?

  

As to the former explanation, that Brahmins/Hindus themselves have swerved from svadharma in past and hence the present Hindu must bear with oppression on that “sanatana” dharma, that is actually not insensible given the running arguments against orthodoxy and Hinduism itself. Not sure we accept this, but again would that explanation work for other groups mentioned above?

  

thollmelukaalkizhu
