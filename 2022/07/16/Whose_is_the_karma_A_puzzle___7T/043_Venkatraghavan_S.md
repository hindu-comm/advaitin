Namaste Putran ji,

  

A lot of what you describe is simply the nature of kaliyuga. That each of us is born in such a Yuga is our prArabdha. What the world is going through is a result of the samaShTi karma-s of jIva-s born in this age.

  

Within this age, each of us has varying degrees of pleasure and pain, which is a reflection of our respective vyaShTi karma.

  

Now, some of what you describe is a subjective assessment of the world - but how I see things may not be how the world really is. A person may feel that the place they are in is particularly troubled and everywhere else is happy and contented, or vice versa. Mostly that is simply because we don't know what others are really going through.

  

Moreover, the world is neither good nor bad - it is what it is. What we make of it is a function of our mental make-up, and that is a function of past karma. There is a story in the Mahabharata that illustrates this. Once Dronacharya gave each of Yudhishthira and Duryodhana a task. Yudhishthira had to find one evil person and bring them to the Ashram by the end of the day. Duryodhana had to find one good person and bring them to the Ashram. Both of them returned empty-handed. Try as much as he could, Yudhishthira could not find anyone without some redeeming feature. And similarly, Duryodhana could not find anyone who did not have any bad qualities. What each saw of the world is a reflection of who they were.

  

There is also an objective reality - which is that in Kaliyuga there will be the suppression of dharma. However, the one thing we can be certain of is that despite the seeming difficulties to dharma, it will ultimately be protected by bhagavAn. There have been innumerable kali-yugas in the past and there will be innumerable ones in the future - however sanAtana dharma will be ever present.

  

That doesn't absolve us of responsibilitythough - each of us must strive to protect dharma, and the best way to do it is by the regulation of our thoughts, speech and actions in accordance with dharma - dharmAcharaNameva dharmarakshaNam.

  

Regards,

Venkatraghavan
