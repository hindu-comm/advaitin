Dear Putranji,  

  

Upanishad did use the word ***Mithya*** and that suited the Vedic Sanskrit, where the language was supposed to be cryptic or rather convey indirect meaning. Thanks to the Great Panini Mahodaya, the paninian language has become direct. Our very own Adi Shankara seems to have preferred the use of the word ***Vyavafarika Satya***, w.r.t. existence for a limited/short period. The compound word Vyavaharika Satya is in Buddhism also. Lord Buddha declared that he became the Buddha or Jnani when he could see his past births. Lord Krishna also indirectly indicated to Arjuna similarly at the beginning of the Bhagavad Gita, that Arjuna was yet to realize that his human birth was one of his many births.  

  

As soon as we are thoroughly convinced that we have this short human life and as long as we don't forget this fact, this jnana will push us to do good karma (or karma for the good of all of us) and make us desist from doing akarma (or those karmas, which are not good for all of us). This Jnana would also inspire and invigorate us to reproach ourselves for any occasional lapses of doing akarma, and make us do the Nishkaama karma (good karmas done in a selfless way), and that would also wipe out (neutralize) the occasional lapses, if any.  

  

I think any detailed analysis of the types Karma and Akarmas and who suffers from whose karma, should be more a part of the Niti-shasras than of the Advaita. Please do not hesitate to correct me, if I am wrong.

  

My 2 cents
