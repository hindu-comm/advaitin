There is another way to think of the vyashti logic in casethe above is difficult to digest insome cases.

  

Imagine a child goes to the school playground and starts building a mound of sand. From all sides it pushes the sand to the middle, or digs and pours onto the side or the top.

  

Now the school janitor or caretaker has the job to keep the playground level and clean. So upon seeing the mound, she comes over to level it.

  

How does she do it? Pushes some this way and some that way; shovels at the top and to the side and drops them where it is imbalanced and spreads the sand.

  

In general it is not important to her what sand came from which side; her action is meant to take care of the net action of the child and not counter each particular action the child did to add to the mound. (In special cases like with large rocks, she may want to return them in the same area where they came from.)

  

Some of her strikes on the mound look very big that one cannot imagine as directly corresponding with any of the child's individual actions. But when we realize it is just sand accumulated that is getting leveled again, we don't bother about such correspondence.

  

  

Likewise the karma mound that the jiva accumulates need not be thought of as a discrete collection of karmas retaining their past character,that Ishvara has to offset back in a mirror fashion. It is rather a continuum of accumulation and the phala that Ishvara gives the jiva has the same end effect of leveling it in the best manner possible as per Ishvara'sdiscretion.

  

From earlier writings on this logic:

  

"every 'action' begets appropriate result":

  

We don't have to think of this as always in a one-to-one sense (like this phala has to be for that particular action), but again in a holistic way. We shape our "subtle body" through our actions and our experiences will correspond with this "shape": if we experience a certain "virus" phala, then it means our subtle-body is karma-built to act as its receptor and getting such a phala is natural and 'necessary' to resolve that karma-accretion.

  

The natural Dharmic flow in Ishvara's Order leads to a resolution or dissipation of the karmic accumulations ofIgnorancedue to and in jivas, and the jiva has the ability to align itself to this flow or to create instead new karma that adds to the karmic tension in Hiranyagarbha (total subtle body), which in turn becomes the succeeding basis for the flow of Dharma.  

  

The jiva is a state/locus of karmic in-equilibrium. The Lord thus sends to the jiva’s cognition the entities/experiences manifest in mind-world/sense-world that will dissipate or mirror the karmic imbalance/stress built up in the jiva.
