> There is another way to think of the vyashti logic in casethe above
> is difficult to digest insome cases.
>
>   
>
> Imagine a child goes to the school playground and starts building a
> mound of sand. From all sides it pushes the sand to the middle, or
> digs and pours onto the side or the top.
>
>   
>
> Now the school janitor or caretaker has the job to keep the playground
> level and clean. So upon seeing the mound, she comes over to level
> it.
>
>   
>
> How does she do it? Pushes some this way and some that way; shovels at
> the top and to the side and drops them where it is imbalanced and
> spreads the sand.
>
>   
>
> In general it is not important to her what sand came from which side;
> her action is meant to take care of the net action of the child and
> not counter each particular action the child did to add to the mound.
> (In special cases like with large rocks, she may want to return them
> in the same area where they came from.)
>
>   
>
> Some of her strikes on the mound look very big that one cannot imagine
> as directly corresponding with any of the child's individual actions.
> But when we realize it is just sand accumulated that is getting
> leveled again, we don't bother about such correspondence.
>
>   
>
>   
>
> Likewise the karma mound that the jiva accumulates need not be thought
> of as a discrete collection of karmas retaining their past
> character,that Ishvara has to offset back in a mirror fashion. It is
> rather a continuum of accumulation and the phala that Ishvara gives
> the jiva has the same end effect of leveling it in the best manner
> possible as per Ishvara'sdiscretion.
>
>   

  

Of course subject to correction, if this alternate viewpoint is specifically contradicted in shastra or tradition.
