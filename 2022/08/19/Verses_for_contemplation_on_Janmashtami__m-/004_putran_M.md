Namaskaram Sundar-ji,

  

Yes, "fix" has doublemeaning. Your example is more like repair, correct, set right; the translation of Krishna's usage is more like 'focus', "fasten (the mind) securely in a particular place or position." So, to follow what Krishna advises would require that the mind is already "fixed" in the prior sense. For many people I know, this type of suggestion to strive as would a yogi would be outlandish, impractical, plain wrong; they will resent it, get even upset or angry at those who quote such things, and say it is not for us. They are not wrong either since in no way are most people yogis or mumukshus of that order to be taking the Lord's words literally to heart in their lives. However, for those who wish to be sadhakas and mumukshus, this awareness of goal and path should be part of their regular sadhana and mananam. We have to unteach our minds of dvaiticidentifications of self and direct it towards advaitic processing of duality (as mithya, vikshepa of Self, as naught else but nama-rupa of Brahman - Aham, etc.). When we (the mind) are established in Self, the movie of mithya prapancha cannot confuse us of the immutable Self and we are sakshis at best, or better yet, we are not even "thinking of anything \[apart/separate\] whatsoever". It is also by this advaitic mananam that ideally an advanced sadhaka would want to go past the level of "all desires arising from thoughts" etc.

  

The Lord says:

  

6.18: A man who has become free from hankering for all desirable objects is then said to be Self-absorbed when the controlled mind rests in the Self alone. (ref. 6.29)

  

6.23: One should know that severance of contact with sorrow to be what is called Yoga. That Yoga has to be practised with perseverance and with an undepressed heart.

  

6.23 is immediatelyrelevant for all people because the sorrow stemming from falseidentifications hits us all hard. However we typically try to avoid a blunt reckoning by escaping/forgetting into the world of desires and temporary fulfillments. But eventually life catches up with us in some janma and we start yearning for a real solution. It is at thatpoint that the'life-giving'truths of the scriptures start making sense and we begin following the guidance to fix the mind and fix the mind. Even then however, the sadhaka is faced with vacillation and reluctance, back and forth unwillingness to commit, for by no means is he fit for the yoga of 6.24-25 - its still way too much. But the process begins and he starts working to remind himself of God, advaitic truths, etc. and fix the world of dualities in the non-dual knowledge of Self. He is now on the path of Yoga.
