Namaskaram, as rewritten for others outside our group:

  

---------

  

With regard to Yoga, the Lord says  

  

6.24-25: By totally eschewing all desires which arise from thoughts, and restraining with the mind itself all the organs from every side; One should gradually withdraw with the intellect endowed with steadiness. Making the mind fixed in the Self, one should not think of anything whatsoever.

  

6.26: (The yogi) should bring (this mind) under the subjugation of the Self Itself, by restraining it from all those causes whatever due to which the restless, unsteady mind wanders away.

  

6.27: Supreme Bliss comes to this yogi alone whose mind has become perfectly tranquil, whose rajas has been eliminated, who has become identified with Brahman, and is taintless.

  

6.28: By concentrating his mind constantly thus, the taintless yogieasily attains the absolute Bliss of*contact with*Brahman. \["identifed with, homogeneous with, in essential oneness with"\]

  

6.29: One who has his mind Self-absorbed through Yoga, and who has the vision of sameness everywhere, sees his Selfexisting in everything, and everything in his Self.

  

"fix" (translated in 6.25) has a doublemeaning in English. One meaning is to repair, correct, set right; the translation of Krishna's usage however is more like 'focus', "fasten (the mind) securely in a particular place or position." To follow what Krishna advises would require that the mind is already largely "fixed" in the prior sense. For many people, this type of suggestion to strive as would a yogi would be outlandish, impractical, plain wrong; they will resent it, get even upset or angry at those who quote such things, and say it is not for us. They are not wrong either from the perspective that in no way are most people yogis or mumukshus of that order to be taking the Lord's words literally to heart in their lives. However, for those who wish to be sadhakas and mumukshus, this awareness of goal and path should be part of their regular sadhana and mananam. We have to unteach our minds of dvaiticidentifications of self and direct it towards advaitic processing of duality (as mithya, vikshepa of Self, as naught else but nama-rupa of Brahman - Aham, etc.). When we (the mind) are established in Self, the movie of mithya prapancha cannot confuse us of the immutable Self and we are at most sakshis of Ishvara's Order/maayic Play, or better yet, we are not even "thinking of anything \[apart/separate\] whatsoever". It is also by this advaitic mananam that ideally an advanced sadhaka would want to go past the level of "all desires arising from thoughts" etc.

  

The Lord says:

  

6.18: A man who has become free from hankering for all desirable objects is then said to be Self-absorbed*when the controlled mind rests in the Self alone*. (ref. 6.29)

  

6.23: One should know that severance of contact with sorrow to be what is called Yoga. That Yoga has to be practised with perseverance and with an undepressed heart.

  

6.23 is immediatelyrelevant for all people because the sorrow stemming from falseidentifications hits us all hard. However we typically try to avoid a blunt reckoning by escaping/forgetting into the world of desires and temporary fulfillments. (Where some are unable to offsettheir sorrow like this even, they may get into greater mental problems and turn to abuse of themselves and others at physicallevel as well.) But eventually life catches up with us in some janma and we start yearning for a real solution. It is at thatpoint that the'life-giving'truths of the scriptures start making sense and we begin following the guidance to fix the mind (of the false) to fix the mind (in the true). Even then however, the sadhaka is faced with vacillation and reluctance, back and forth unwillingness to commit, for by no means is he fit for the yoga of 6.24-25 - its still way too much. But the process begins and he starts working to remind himself of God, advaitic truths, etc. and root the world of apparent duality in the non-dual knowledge of Self. He is now on the path of Yoga.
