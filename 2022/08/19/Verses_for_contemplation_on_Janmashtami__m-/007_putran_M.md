Namaskaram Bhaskar-ji,

  

I don't much think of dvaita yoga shastra when I say yoga, just like if I talk of bhakti, Ishvara or "Vedanta", it does not mean dvaita Vedanta, or that Shankara was a Vaishnavite because he refers to Narayana here or there. Yes, there are dvaitinsof various kinds who use the same words; but when we use the terms it has to be in a vyavaharika or sadhaka context that is consistent with the final philosophy of advaita. The same has to be true when we refer to Nirvikalpa samadhi. As I understand, itis total Self-absorption where there is no room for the "other", whether as thought or object. But it is not deep sleep; it is merging in nirvikalpa awareness of Self whereupon the yogi/jnani "sees his Selfin everything and everything in his Self." Where it is a call for action, it is to be understood as having the goal of eliminating ajnana: the jnana that shines forth is not a forced mental construct or state, it is simple unobstructed awareness of reality/self ("aham brahmasmi") as it is.

  

Sri Krishna says in 6.29: One who has his mind Self-absorbed through Yoga, and who has the vision of sameness everywhere, sees his Selfexisting in everything, and everything in his Self.

  

Different schools are able to find coherence for this verse in their philosophy. For dvaita systems, "seeing Self in everything and everything in Self" is not negation of reality for "everything" distinct from Self. For advaitins, jnana leaves no room for reality apart from Self; so if Yoga were to give only this mental state of seeing Self everywhere and in everything but leaves room for imagination of distinct (apart from Self) reality for the 'everything', then this is only an intermediary attainment at best. It has to be backed by advaitic SMN/shraddha so that the experience gained from yoga is then rooted back in right knowledge and not in wrong dvaiticconfusions.

  

When we refer in this forum to such verses of Sri Krishna, we automatically assume that they shouldnot be taken as stand-alone and final, and should be supported by a fuller study of scriptures and sampradaya teachings for proper unifying comprehension.

  

All this has to be separated from the question "Is NS necessary or particularly important in order to become a jnani or have jnana-nishta?" That's an in-house discussion among advaitins. For the proponents, NS is the culmination of nidhidhyasana. See this interesting article by a swami of Chinmaya mission:[https://ropeandsnake.files.wordpress.com/2021/05/the-need-for-nirvikalpa-samadhi-essay-41.pdf](https://ropeandsnake.files.wordpress.com/2021/05/the-need-for-nirvikalpa-samadhi-essay-41.pdf)
