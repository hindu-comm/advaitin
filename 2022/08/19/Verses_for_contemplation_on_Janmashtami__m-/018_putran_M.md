Namaskaram Bhaskar-ji,



> Now is samadhi *necessary* *after* SMN? No. Samadhi coincides (for
> many) with atmasakshatkara at the end of dhyana based on SMN. For
> others, atmasakshatkara/jnanacan happen through SMN without samadhi.
>
>   

  

I came across this small audio reference from a lecture by Swami Paramarthananda on Uddhava Gita 3.28.38. Between -36:53 to -35:55, he quotes this phrase "[taḿ](http://vedabase.net/t/tam)[sa](http://vedabase.net/s/sa)-[prapañcam](http://vedabase.net/p/prapancam)[adhirūḍha](http://vedabase.net/a/adhirudha)-[samādhi](http://vedabase.net/s/samadhi)-[yogaḥ](http://vedabase.net/y/yogah)" and says:

  

"And as far as jnani is concerned, he has attained the jnana nishta in the atma. "adhiruda samadhi yogah" means the one who has gone through sravanam, mananam and nidhidhyasanam ***up to samadhi*** - samadhi means *internalization of that wisdom*. And adhirudha means the one who has completed the process of jnana yoga. So that jnani, "tam na bhajate", he no longer holds on to the body with aham, mama.."

  

Here, samadhi is admitted as the final (necessary) step or culmination of SMN but it is understood as the assimilation of jnana rather than as simply a yogic experience. It sort of answers my earlier question in the affirmative:

  

"Now we have to ask, when the jnani following SMN attains aparokshanubhuti or atmasakshatkara, why is that moment of realization not equivalent to this highest state referred to by Krishna and Sruti? Perhaps, that is equivalent to NS except it is enlivened by SMN, resulting in jnana. The yogi lacking SMN gets misguided and does not attain jnana from NS."

  

The yogic samadhi, if it happens as well in the sense of Ka U., would be secondary or accessory to the main thing which is self-realization: the occurrence of atmasakshatkara manifests at the body-mind level as NS. It reminds me of how moksha for the advaitin is fundamentally liberation from Ignorance whereas it is understood generally in terms of the adjunct happening: the liberation from the cycle of birth and death.
