One cannot show any bhAShya reference for yogic dhyAna not being denied, because that is looking for proof of the absence of a thing, which is quite difficult, but a few pointers:

  

a\) He does say सम्यग्दर्शनाभ्युपायो हि योगो वेदे विहितः etc - the yoga here is vedic meditation.

  

b\) Later when he saysयोगशास्त्रेऽपि — ‘ अथ तत्त्वदर्शनोपायो योगः’ इति सम्यग्दर्शनाभ्युपायत्वेनैव योगोऽङ्गीक्रियते, he is acknowledging that in yoga shAstra itself, yoga is said to be means to moksha. However, here he qualifies the upAya, by calling it abhyupAya, by adding the pre-fix 'abhi' (referring to proximity) in the phrase samyag-darshana-abhyupAyatvena yogah angIkriyate.

  

c\) When he makes the comment that the term yoga in the veda refers to vedic dhyAna, it is in the context of yoga being said to be the cause of moksha (तत्कारणं साङ्ख्ययोगाभिपन्नम्) - यत्तु दर्शनमुक्तम् ‘तत्कारणं साङ्ख्ययोगाभिपन्नम्’ इति, वैदिकमेव तत्र ज्ञानं ध्यानं च साङ्ख्ययोगशब्दाभ्यामभिलप्येते प्रत्यासत्तेरित्यवगन्तव्यम् ।


  

In my view, he is denying that yoga is the kAraNa (direct means) of tattvajnAna, but he does not have a problem saying yoga is an abhyupAya (or a proximate cause / supporting cause) for the rise of tattvajnAna, with shruti being the direct means / main cause.

  

The bhAShya support for the second part of the statement "there is an affirmation of such a dhyAna being vedic" is more straight forward, ie वैदिकमेव तत्र ज्ञानं ध्यानं च साङ्ख्ययोगशब्दाभ्यामभिलप्येते

> In which aspects there is an overlap between vedic dhyAna and yOgic
> dhyAna to conclude that there is no denial!!??

  

Because, respected advaita AchArya-s and maThAdhipati-s are practitioners of aShTAnga-yoga, and Apta-vachana / Apta-dharmAcharaNa is prAmANika for us (yadyadAcharati shreShThah as the gItA says), it follows that yogic dhyAna / samAdhi must have overlaps. If it is completely different, advaita AchArya-s would not be following it. The overlap between the two is the object of dhyAna. If it is a dhyAna of tattvamasi akhaNDa vAkyArtha, being the AtmasvarUpa in the absence of all anAtma, such a dhyAna is nididhyAsana / jnAna niShThA.
