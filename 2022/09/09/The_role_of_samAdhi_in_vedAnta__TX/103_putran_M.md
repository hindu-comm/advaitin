Members can carefully read the bhashya of BSB 2-1-3 "Refutation of Yoga".

  

The gist of Shankara's message regarding the place of Yoga in advaita sadhana is:

  

Quote (Swami Gambhirananda translation)

  

"**Yoga having been.enjoined in the Vedas as a means to the realization of full illumination**, as in,"TheSelf is to berealized-tobe heard of, reflected on, and profoundly meditated upon" (Br. II. iv. 5). Moreover in the SvetaSvataraUpani~dwe come across an elaborate injunction about Yoga, comprising the arrangement of seat and the rest, which commences with the verse, **"Holding the body in balance, with the three limbs****(chest, neck, and head) erect"** (II. 8). And thousands of Vedic signs indicative of Yoga are met with, as in, **"The holding of the senses and organs unperturbed and under control is called Yoga by the adepts" (Ka. II. iii. 11)**, "Getting fully **this knowledge (of Brahman)** **and** **the process****of****Yoga**" (Ka. II.iii.18), and so on. In the philosophy of Yoga also,itis said,"Nowthen, starts Yoga which is a means to the realization of Reality", where it is admitted as a means to complete illumination. So *a**portion of the Yoga Smrti being acceptable* to either side, it cannot be discarded justasmuchasthe Smrti about theAstakaceremony cannot be.2...Though thereis anagreement in respect of a portion of the subject-matter, still since disagreementisin evidence in respect of others,asshown above, an effortisbeing made against the Sankhya and Yoga Smrtis alone, though many Smrtis dealing with spiritual matters are extant.For the Sankhya and **Yoga** are **well recognized** in the world**as****means for the achievement of the highest human goal (liberation)**, and they are **accepted** **by the good people** and **are supported by Vedic indicatory marks**,asin, "One becomes freed fromallthe bondages after realizing the Deity thatisthe source of these desires and is attained through Sankhya and Yoga". Thereis noother path to proceed by" (Sv. III. 8). But the *followers of samkhya and Yoga are dualists*, and they do not perceive the unity of the Self."

  

Unquote



This is far from being an outright refutation of yoga; it is a clarification of what elements in it are acceptable and what are not. Basically, Yoga as a process is not inherently averse to Vedas but it shouldnot taken up independently: it has to be rooted in the Vedas sothat it aids in the attainment of advaitic Knowledge of Brahman that is the truecause for liberation. But yoga as a "process" can play a secondary role in obtaining this knowledge. Yes, Shankara specifically points to Ka.U. 2.3.11 and 2.3.18, which refer to the yogic-methods and essential components of samadhi (like holding senses and organs unperturbed and under control; 2.3.10 : organs of perception become still, together with the mind, intellect does not function). Sundar-ji had also mentioned earlier Shankara's referencing samadhi in Ka.U. 6.11:तांयोगमितिमन्यन्तेस्थिरामिन्द्रियधारणाम्।tAM yogam iti manyante sthirAm indriya-dhAraNAm.

Note that the expected argument would be that the first quote Br.II.iv.5 refers not to PY but to "adhyatma yoga" since the process mentioned in that quote is SMN. But Shankara does not stop there. All his other references are indicative of PY only or yoga as understood in general. He (and sruthi) sees yoga in a holistic manner where PY also has its valid place as "process"/"method". And that exactly is why he had to clarify that while certain parts (the "process") of this well-known system are compatible with theVedas and the goal of liberation, there are other aspects of it as understood by ignorant yogis (the "dualistic philosophy") that stray from the Vedas. This superimposition of a wrong philosophy onto the yogic-process must be removed and replaced with the right Vedic foundation, after which the process fine-tuned by Vedantic perspective is valid and capable of aiding in the realization of Self.

  

When it is said that the refutation centers ONLY round the false claim that Yoga *independent of Veda* can obtain liberation, then it essentially means the outer components of the yogic process (including yogic-samadhi) are not inherently at fault with respect to the goal - they can be among the (subordinate/secondary) means to liberation. Only they have to be rooted in the Vedas through proper sravanam, etc. so that the yogic sadhana can aid in the proper realization of Self.

  

This position has been argued for consistently by myself and others. At least for me, there is little doubt after reading this bhashya about Shankara accepting Yoga as a valid part of advaita sadhana.
