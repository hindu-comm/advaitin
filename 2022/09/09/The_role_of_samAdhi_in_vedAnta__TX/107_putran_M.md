As for "meditation", yes a case can be made on the basis of this dhyana that it need not involve samadhi. There is a logical route for that. However it does not decisively negate the Ka.U. quotes also cited which go to the extent of saying basically that in the highest state (of this Vedic yoga), the one in meditation ~ "holds senses and organs unperturbed and under control; \[his\] organs of perception become still, together with the mind, intellect does not function". If that endpoint is attained via this Vedic "meditation", then that is time-bound Vedantic/yogic samadhi state. At least that is the conclusion for those who associates the jnani with the body-mind he has from prarabdha and also think 'his' organs of perception together with mind are not still forever.
