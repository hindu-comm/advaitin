I need to backtrack a bit, with regard to whether there are references to jnani's mind not taking forms. It was a foolish 'challenge' in fact. There are parallel statements earlier like "when mind ceases to be mind, duality disappears"(bhashya for 3.33), "free from ideation" (3.34) etc. So it is *not necessary* that3.46 is saying some mindstate that is not considered permanentfor jnani, given his jnana standpoint.

  

From my reading, 3.31-3.39 deal with jnani and jnani's mind, who is said to be in 'contactless' Yoga (3.39). For the jnani, the mind is steadfast in Self and hence it may be said to be 'free from ideation' etc. Point is, the shastra can use such language to describe the jnani'svision to the point one likely cannot exclude "not appearing in form of objects".

  

3.40-3.47 is directed to the (3.39 bhashya) "Yogis who are other than \[the jnanis\], who are still treading the path, who have inferior or medium outlook, and think of the mind as something different from the Self, though associated with It", for such inferior yogis/ajnanis, (3.40) "fearlessness, the removal of misery, knowledge (of the Self), and everlasting peace, are dependent on the control of the mind."

  

3.46 "When the mind does not become lost nor is scattered, when it is motionless and does not appear in the form of objects, then it becomes \[identified with\] Brahman"

  

3.46 is therefore at the end of a sequence of verses describing sadhana, directed towards the lesser yogis, and it tells what state the mind has to attainso "it become \[identified with\] Brahman".

  

The word used here to describe the mind not appearing in object-forms is anAbhAsam which I could not find before (on cursory glance only) in the descriptions of the jnani'smind. However, I don't want to fuss on that since 1. I have no clue regarding what special connotation belongs to these different words, 2. one can probably consider that it as equivalent to some of the descriptions used for the jnani's mind, given that "duality disappears" from the standpoint of discrimination.

  

One can certainly imo take Bhaskar-ji's position even for 3.46 that it should be read only as attaining the jnani's state and not as a reference to a yogic-samadhi state. That can be the purport of this section since after all the focus here is highlighting the jnani'svision as the ultimate goal. (One can also argue for the alternate samadhi-reading based on context and word analysis; but that is not my point now.)

  

But I disagree that one can simply take the content here and slap it onto other places where separate discussions are happening. The context, purpose, and focus can differ.
