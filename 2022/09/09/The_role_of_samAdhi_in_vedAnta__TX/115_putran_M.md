Namaskaram Bhaskar-ji,

  

Yes we can agree to agree & disagree. Your last para sort of took off the fire from mymind and I am also in wind-down mode.

  

I don't want to restart new discussion, but want to jotdowncertain points.

  

Agree point:

  

There is not much that I personally have against your viewpoints on adhyatma yoga. As in the car analogy, the engine is changed from PY to AY (SMN or SM(N)VS) model and that affects the car's capacities and performance.

  

Both systems may have dhyana for example, but

  

each system cannot be separated from its goal(s).

  

When the context of discussion is the ultimate-goal of the system, the process of each system cannot be separatedfrom the methods that are essential to achieving its goals.

  

Process may be dhyana,but methodin AY is SM-based but not (entirely?) so in PY. The defining object (angam) for AY is SM-based-Dhyana and not Dhyana+ a separable tag of SM. Our dhyana must be seen as inseparable from the method involving SM, and therefore distinct from dhyana in other systems.

  

So while we can say "We are both practicing yoga when we do dhyana", we cannot say "Advaitins are practicing PY when we do dhyana", and vise-versa. It will also be inappropriate to say that advaitins are practicing dhyana from PYS that is rooted in (or modified with) SM(N)-vichara - because dhyana is common to all and it is the method of doing it that distinguishes one from the other. So we would be missing the main point if we think the two systems overlap on account of having dhyana in common: the dhyana should not be separated from the method that is integral to the whole.  

  

Potential Disagree points:

  

1\. This does not mean that there cannot be secondary goals like mind control where one cannot speak of common elements. Perhaps AY tradition directs in one way but PY methods can also be safely adopted or adapted by sadhakas. That is a different discussion.

  

2\. Where we have to agree to disagree for now is on the issue of time-bound samadhi and its playing a role at the point of realization. In my opinion, it is not at all obviousthatBG 2.53, MK 3.46, Ka.U. 2.3.10,11 cannot refer to samadhi analogous to yogic-samadhi. They are places where "Yoga" is front and center. They are places where the wording can verily be taken to imply the samadhi of PYS; the parallels are stark. The question in some cases is explicitly focusing on the transition from ajnana to jnana, as opposed to the state of a jnani. Shankara had opportunity to hammer right there against possible misinterpretation; he did not. In MK 3.37 bhashya, he says the Self is realizable "through the insight arising out of deepest concentration (samadhi)." So one can argue that he accepts samadhi as process and method for goal.

  

My point is that there is room for people to interpret "Vedantic samadhi" in a very similar sense to the NS of PY, albeit the method of reaching it will involve SM-based sadhana. In the case of PY as well, it is incorrect to suggest that it is some sort of jada dead or sleep state: Yoga sutra 1.3 clearly tells there is special insight on self from NS for that yogin. Following the AY SM-based methodology to attain samadhi is essential for the proper knowledge of Brahman/Self to arise in VS.

  

3\. This apart, there is also another way of thinking about it. PYS gives certain "knowledge" of self. Ask whether there are certain things about the atma that it says that are right. Ok. Now, NS of PY gives insight into that jnana+ajnana combo with regard to self and world. Fine. But the advaitin has also done SM(N), so he does SMN-vichara to remove wrongsuperimpositions. The NS experience gives insight/conviction in some right way and the further vichara removes some wrong additions. It is the second correction via SMN that makes PY into AY, in this case. A question here is not whether such a route to jnana is possible (since as per SDS, NS is just another experience) nor whether a purerAY path is preferable (yes, it is), but whether it is not entirely a useless route to take or even whether it is particularly harmful and would create greater obstacles harder to remove through SMN; or whether it is a valid route that takes the good and removes the bad later.
