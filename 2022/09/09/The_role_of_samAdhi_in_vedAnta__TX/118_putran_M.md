126. When meditation on the attributeless Brahman is mature it leads to Samadhi. This state of intense concentration at case leads on to the Nirodha state in which the distinction between subject and object is eliminated.  
  
127. When such complete cessation of mental activity is achieved, only the associationless entity (Atman) remains in his heart. By ceaseless meditation on It based on the great Sayings, arises the knowledge 'I am Brahman'.  
  
128. There is then a perfect realisation of Brahman as the immutable, associationless, eternal, self-revealed, secondless whole, as indicated in the scriptures.  

  

  

Since this describes samadhi and its consequences, I just wanted to pose some natural questions that come up in its context. Anyone who has studied the text can answer if you think it will help in general. (I am not intending to go back and forth on it however.)

  

**Q**: What is meant by Meditation on the attributeless Brahman? How is this to be done?

  

This Dhyana leads to Samadhi, a state of intense concentration that leads to nirodha state where distinction between subject and object is eliminated.

  

Nirodha state = "Complete cessation of mental activity" = "distinction between subject and object is eliminated".

  

But the nirodha state is not like sleep where also sub-obj distinction is absent,

  

because in samadhi, 1. "only the Atman remains in his heart", (**Q**: meaning what?), and 2. By ceaseless meditation on It based on the mahavakyas, arises the knowledge "aham brahmasmi".

  

**Q**: If there is complete cessation of mental activity, what does it mean to say "ceaseless meditation on It based on mahavakyas"?

  

**Q**: Is this meditation a reference to some automatic discernment of the mahavakya tattva that happens while abiding in pure self-absorption?
