Namaste Putranji,

  

I had the good fortune oflistening to the first ten chapters of Panchadashi sitting at the lotus feet of my Guru, Swami Paramarthanandaji, over a period of 8 years (2014-2022). My understanding is purely based on what I learnt from those classes.

  

First of all, VidyAraNya in Panchadashi never compromises on the core principle that MokSha is“attained” only with AtmaGYanam and that AtmaGYAnam arises only from the shruti vAkyas. Thus, there are no two paths to MokSha. Atma GYAnam is the only path to MokSha. And shruti is theonly means for Atma GYAnam.

  

Please see the following verses from Panchadashi that corroborate this.

  

PD 7.70

ब्रह्मापरोक्ष्यसिध्यर्थम् महावाक्यमितीरितम् ।

वाक्यवृत्तावतो ब्रह्मापरोक्ष्ये विमतिर्नहि ॥ ७.७०

brahmāparokṣyasidhyartham mahāvākyamitīritam ।  
vākyavṛttāvato brahmāparokṣye vimatirnahi ॥ 7.70

In Vākyavṛtti it is said that the Mahāvākyas are intended to give direct knowledge of Brahman. There is no doubt about this fact.

  

PD 7.79

एवम् सति महावाक्यात्परोक्षज्ञानमीर्यते ।

यैस्तेषां शास्त्रसिद्धान्तविज्ञानम् शोभतेतराम् ॥ ७.७९

evam sati mahāvākyātparokṣajñānamīryate ।  
yaisteṣāṃ śāstrasiddhāntavijñānam śobhatetarām ॥ 7.79

Such being the case, those who suppose that theMahāvākyas can give only an indirect knowledge of Brahman, furnish brilliantly shallow understanding of the scriptural conclusions.

  

PD 7.81

स्वतोऽपरोक्षजीवस्य ब्रह्मत्वमभिवाञ्छत: ।

नश्येत्सिद्धापरोक्षत्वम् इति युक्तिर्महत्यहो ॥

svato'parokṣajīvasya brahmatvamabhivāñchata: ।  
naśyetsiddhāparokṣatvam iti yuktirmahatyaho ॥

Everyman’s knowledge of himself is a direct experience. It isindeed a remarkable argument to suggest that in our attempt at identification of ourselves with Brahman this direct knowledge, already present, will be destroyed.

  

  

PD 7.180

अनपह्नुत्य लोकास्तदिन्द्रजालमिदं त्विति ।

जानन्त्येवानपह्नुत्य भोगं मायात्वधीस्तथा ॥

anapahnutya lokāstadindrajālamidaṃ tviti ।  
jānantyevānapahnutya bhogaṃ māyātvadhīstathā ॥

People know a magical show to be unreal, but this knowledge does not involve the destruction of the show. So it is possible to know the unreality of external objects without causing their disappearance or the cessation of enjoyment from them.

  

  

  

PD 7.185

निर्विकल्पसमाधौ तु द्वैतादर्शनहेतुत: ।

सैवापरोक्षविद्येति चेत्सुषुप्तिस्तथा न किम् ॥

nirvikalpasamādhau tu dvaitādarśanahetuta: ।  
*saivāparokṣavidyeti cetsuṣuptistathā naKim॥*

Objection: Direct knowledge is achieved in Nirvikalpa Samādhi in which there is no duality.

Reply: Then why not apply the same argument to deep sleep?

  

PD 7.186

आत्मतत्त्वम् न जानाति सुप्तो यदि तदा त्वया ।

आत्मधीरेव विद्येति वाच्यं न द्वैतविस्मृति ॥

ātmatattvam na jānāti supto yadi tadā tvayā ।  
ātmadhīreva vidyeti vācyaṃ na dvaitavismṛti ॥

Objection: In the state of deep sleep there is no knowledge of the Self

Reply: Then you admit that it is not mere absence of duality but the knowledge of the Self that really matters!

  

PD 7.187

उभयं मिलितं विद्या यदि तर्हि घटादय: ।

अर्धविद्याभाजिन: स्यु: सकलद्वैतविस्मृते: ॥

ubhayaṃ militaṃ vidyā yadi tarhi ghaṭādaya: ।  
ardhavidyābhājina: syu: sakaladvaitavismṛte: ॥

Objection: True knowledge combines in itself both the knowledge of Self and the absence of knowledge of duality.

Reply: Then inanimate objects like pots in which the knowledge of duality is absent are alreadyhalf-enlightened.

  

PD 7.188

मशकध्वनिमुख्यानां विक्षेपाणां बहुत्वतः ।

तत्त्वविद्या तथा न स्याद्घटादीनां यथा दृढा ॥

maśakadhvanimukhyānāṃ vikṣepāṇāṃ bahutvataḥ ।  
tattvavidyā tathā na syādghaṭādīnāṃ yathā dṛḍhā ॥

Then the pots are superior to you, for even the buzzing of mosquitoes often distracts your attention, and they have no such awareness of duality.

  

PD 7.189

आत्मधीरेव विद्येति यदि तर्हि सुखी भव ।

दुष्टचित्तं निरुन्ध्याच्चेन्निरुन्धि त्वं यथासुखम् ॥

ātmadhīreva vidyeti yadi tarhi sukhī bhava ।  
duṣṭacittaṃ nirundhyāccennirundhi tvaṃ yathāsukham ॥

If, however, you admit, the knowledge of the Self alone constitutes realization, you have accepted our position. Again, if you say, to have realization the troubling mind is to be controlled, we bless you. Be happy, do control the mind.

  

  

  

This then brings up the question of what VidyAraNya is talking about in Chapter 9. The discussion is more nuanced than it seems if you pick a few verses out of context. One has to look at the entire Chapter to get the broader context. I am providing some“highlights below”:

  

The Chapter 9 of Panchadashi starts with the description of a“benign error” called samvAdi BhramaH. Verse 2-6 talk about this. Imagine this scenario: there are two people outside two rooms. Within one room, there is a lamp. In another room, there is a jewel. The two people outside their respective rooms see the light from the lamp and the light from the jewel. Both assume it is a precious jewel and rush into the room. One person is disappointed to see a lamp, another is happy to find a jewel. VidyAraNya says that the first person made a mistake in taking the“light from the lamp” to be a jewel, and he was disappointed. The second person, too, made a mistake in taking the“light from the jewel” to be the jewel, but he was rewarded. The former mistake that leads to an undesired result is a visamvAdibhramaH. The latter mistake that leads to a desirable result is a samvAdi bhramaH.

  

Verses 7-12. VidyAraNya points out several examples of such“benign errors,” which are an error, but lead to a desirable consequence. A person goes to Godavari and thinks it is Ganga and sprinkles the water on himself. He made an error, but it is a benign error because that act too leads to his purification. All upAsanas come under this category. For example, one invokes a deity on a stone and worships it. It is an upAsanA. The diety is not the stone. Hence, it is an “error”. But then such worship leads to puNyam. Thus, it is a“benign error”.

  

Verse 13. VidyAraNya says that similarly Brahman upAsanA (or meditation on Brahman) also is an example of such a samvAdi BhramaH, a benign error, because it can lead one to liberation.

  

Verses 14-19 describe the candidate for thisnirgun brahma upAsana.There are two types of students who have done S, M under a Guru. Both have heard theessence of Vedanta“brahma satyam, jagat mithyA, jIvobrahmaiva nAparaH”.

  

The first student is able to see this entirely and is able to claim“aham brahmAsmi”. For him MokSha is a present fact and not something to be accomplished in future. He is a GYAnI. He doesn’t have anything more to do but to pursue NididhyAsanam forviparita bhAvanAnivRRtti. He is convinced that he is Brahman and he just needs to work on refining the mind to remove habitual identification with body and mind.

  

The second student is only able to appreciate“brahma satyam jagat mithyA”. He is not able to claim“jIvo brahmaiva nAparaH” which is nothing but “aham brahmAsmi”. He says“Indeed a GYAnI knows very well that he is Brahman, but I am not a GYAnI. I cannot say I am Brahman because my mind has a lack of qualifications, I am still astruggling jIva with so many problems to deal with. How can such an I be Brahman in my stage of spiritual growth? Hence, I have more work to do, I will only get MokSha sometime in another future birth etc. etc.” He gives a long list of reasons as to why he cannot claim his Brahmanhood.

  

It is for this second student who inspite of S, M cannot claim his own Brahman nature that VidyAraNya prescribes thisnirguNa Brahma upAsanA for. Just as one invokes VishNu in a finite picture or mUrti, so also such a student who sees himself as being finite invokes the limitless Brahman upon one’s limited self.

  

Verses 30-52 Here VidyAraNya highlights the importance of vichAra which is nothing but S, M. ViChAra is to be done along with listening to the teacher. vidyAraNya points out that everyupAsaka should note that“aham brahmAsmi knowledge, conviction” will come only through vichAra. If vichAra doesn’t give rise to the knowledge, it only means vichAra is insufficient. Don’t try to practice any other method. No other method can give rise to knowledge.

  

ब्रह्मसाक्षात्कृतिस्त्वेवं विचारेण विना नृणाम् ।

आप्तोपदेशमात्रेण न सम्भवति कुत्रचित् ॥ ३०॥

*brahmasākṣātkṛtistvevaṃ vicāreṇa vinā nṛṇām ।*

*āptopadeśamātreṇa na sambhavati kutracit ॥ 30॥*

The Direct Knowledge of Brahman is however never possible with only instructions from a competent teacher without vichAra

  

परोक्षज्ञानमश्रद्धा प्रतिबध्नाति नेतरत् ।

अविचारोऽपरोक्षस्य ज्ञानस्य प्रतिबन्धकः ॥ ३१॥

*parokṣajñānamaśraddhā pratibadhnāti netarat ।*

*avicāro'parokṣasya jñānasya pratibandhakaḥ ॥ 31॥*

Lack of shraddhA alone obstructs the rise of indirect knowledge. Lack of enquiry is the obstacle to the rise of Direct Knowledge.

  

विचाराप्यपरोक्षेण ब्रह्मात्मानं न वेत्ति चेत् ।

आपरोक्ष्यावसानत्वाद्भूयोभूयो विचारयेत् ॥ ३२॥

*vicārāpyaparokṣeṇa brahmātmānaṃ na vetti cet ।*

*āparokṣyāvasānatvādbhūyobhūyo vicārayet ॥ 32॥*

Even with vichAra, if one does not know directly that AtmA is Brahman, one should repeatedly do vichAra until the rise of direct knowledge.

  

Verses 53-73 The method of practicing nirguNa brahma upAsanA is given.

केषांचित्स विचारोऽपि कर्मणा प्रतिबद्ध्यते ।

श्रवणायापि बहुभिर्यो न लभ्य इति श्रुतेः ॥ ५३॥

*keṣāṃcitsa vicāro'pi karmaṇā pratibaddhyate ।*

*śravaṇāyāpi bahubhiryo na labhya iti śruteḥ ॥ 53॥*

  

For some, the enquiry itself is obstructed due to unseen obstacles such as past pApa karma.

  

अत्यन्तबुद्धिमान्द्याद्वा सामग्र्या वाप्यसम्भवात् ।

यो विचारं न लभते ब्रह्मोपासीत सोऽनिशम् ॥ ५४॥

*atyantabuddhimāndyādvā sāmagryā vāpyasambhavāt ।*

*yo vicāraṃ na labhate brahmopāsīta so'niśam ॥ 54॥*

*  *

If one is not able to do vichAra due to dullness of the intellect or due to other reasons (some unseen obstacle), let him practice this upAsana.
