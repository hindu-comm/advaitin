Dear Santoshji,

  

Congratulations for publishing a new book on "Agneyashtram". However I am unable to agree to your statement that there are academic distortions in the Mahabharata. Many a times people have been finding difficulty in understanding the pre-Paninian Sanskrit text. That is why many scholars failed to find the date of the Mahabharata war, though Vedavyasa made all data very very clear.  

  

I will point out another case. In the Mahabharata, it is written that in the Bhagavad Gita, there are 745 verses, out of which 620 verses are spoken by Lord Krishna. The original Bhagavad Gita of 745 verses was available in Benares, Kashmir and a couple of other places. I have published that in the year 2014 CE. Unfortunately, in the 8th century AD (CE) one Bhaskaracharya dropped 45 verses of Lord Krishna and Abhinava Shankaracharya, contested the incomplete version of the Bhagavad Gita by writing his bhashya on it. It is most unfortunate for the Hindus that many reputed people, still wrongly believe that the vulgate version of the Bhagavad gita of 700 verses to be the correct one and ***they also wrongly believe that Adi Shankaracharya wrote the bhashya on it.*** ***Further they don't trust the three Shankara mathas which have their authentic Guru-Parampara from the 5th century BCE.***  

  

However, if you feel there are academic distortions in the Mahabharata, you are welcome to cite some examples and convey how you came to find the distortions in the Mahabharata.  

  

Good wishes,

Sunil K. Bhattacharjya
