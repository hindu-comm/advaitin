Namaste dear Sri Putran ji,

  

To the best of my understanding nothing that I said is not in line with the discourses that I heard for years. Not everything can be ignored terming them as arthavAda though in Vedantic circles it is used as like as distributing chocolates on birthdays. I have shown two topics where arthavAda has been employed and have enough evidences why arthavAda holds true there. But everywhere it cannot be employed, is my humble opinion.

  

These scriptures specifically mention "surakAryaM..." as the cause behind all the actions of the story underlined in the scripture. The stories of human lives as you might see in biographies could be as per the choices made by them. After my 12th class I had options to choose medicine or engineering as the career path. I chose engineering and hence my career path is set to thisdegree. I cannot now go back and become a doctor because I already made a choice at the turning point where I was supposed to make it. There is always a WHAT-IF scenario with respect to our lives. We can certainly think what if I had chosen medicine as my choice? Then my career path would have been different.

  

That choice we have and we steer our lives by the choices we make. But to apply the same rule on the stories of Puranas and Itihasas is not correct.

  

Every Indra in his life slays Vritra. Every Narasimha would slay Hiranyakashyapu. Every Rama would slay Ravana. Every Pandavas would slay Kauravas. This is the template which would never change. The choices never change. There is no Purana which has given an alternate story where Rama abducted Mandodari and Ravana avenged and slayed Rama. It is always the other way round. Why?

  

Because oor human lives are ours they don't happen to teach something to the world so we don't get talked in scriptures. Scriptures however have a bounded duty to teach dharmas via various stories. They do so by role playing and enactment of the dharmas. Hence all stories are Deva Asura sangrama in a high level. There will always be a moral lesson of how virtue prevails over the vice.

  

All the scriptures manifest at the beginning of kalpa itself. The script always remains ready for the actors to later assume their roles and perform the deeds. There is no evidence that these scriptures were written after seeing the actors and their deeds. They aren't documentation of chronicles. The actors rather act as per their roles destined by the scripture.

  

This is why we see the scriptures giving statements like such and such thing was a devakAryam and happened as per the destiny. As an example, when Draupadi was born, the announcement was heard, "surakAryaM iyaM kAle kariShyati sumadhyamA. asyA hetoH kShatriyANAM mahadutpatsyate bhayaM". Meaning, "In time this slender waisted lady will achieve the divine purpose. For Her as the root cause, there will be a great fear among the Kshatriyas". If choices were real such proclamations wouldn't make sense in scriptures. Just how Lalita manifested from fire altar for "devakArya samudyatA" here same way the Shakti of Mahabharata is Draupadi who has arisen for the purpose of the gods "surakAryaM..".

  

There are many evidences that Itihasas follow a common template - shown some in the Section2 of the book.

  

The stories aren't random. These Itihasas and puranas manifest at the beginning of kalpa and when the time comes they are seen by the respective Rishi who brings these gifta to the humans. But before Vyasa made the Mahabharata public to all it is not true that people didn't know the story already. Drona was said to be learned in the Panchamakhyana (Mahabharata). Which means people with high learnings has knowledge of all five vedas. The Mahabharata was composed by Vyasa before even Dhritarashtra et all were born and published the same in the world of the gods via his four disciples and his son Shuka. The first public disclosure to humans happened in the Janamejaya sarpasatra meaning by that time all Kauravas and Pandavas were all dead. Such late in the game it was made public but still Drona knew, Bhishma knew etc. How? Because, the way Gita says "this ancient knowledge has been getting faded hence it is revived by me now" same way in every cycle these rishis republish the ancient knowledge only. They do NOT create anything anew. None of the works of Vyasa were in real his authorship. He was just the seer and whatever he saw he taught the mankind hence they are rishis and drasta-s not karta-s.

  

Another example I would give at the expense of the length of my email for which readers may kindly forgive me.

  

Janamejaya kills several thousands of snakes in the sarpasatra. Astika comes and asks him to stop the sacrifice. Janamejaya calls for the Brahmanas learned in puranas and asks them what should be the outcome of my deed. How does it end always? They tell him saying, "In all previous cycles also this sacrifice has always ended in stopping when Astika comes and stops, so should you too desist from further killing the snakes". Then only Janamejaya stops his sacrifice after ascertaining that in every kalpa that sacrifice ends that way.

  

He was taking revenge with snakes for killing his father. He could have chosen to not go with the words of Brahmanas but he didn't because that's how the script for every Janamejaya was and he followed the same.

  

There are many examples we can discuss but I would stop here.
