***Ram Chandran’s review of the book: “*Ramayana Trio: Valmiki, Kamban, Tulsi” – July 30, 2022 **by[Prof. V. Krishnamurthy](https://www.amazon.com/s/ref=dp_byline_sr_book_1?ie=UTF8&field-author=Prof.+V.+Krishnamurthy&text=Prof.+V.+Krishnamurthy&sort=relevancerank&search-alias=books)(Author)

**I was very fortunate to have many years of personal acquaintance with Prof V. Krishnamurthy (I want to call him as Profvk), the author of this book.** ***I have read many of Profvk’s previous books on Hindu scriptures from early Eighties. What I have been noticing is that Profvk at the age of ninety five continues to be a faster thinker and writer. As the founder and moderator of the Advaiin mailing list (currently in Google Group) I had the opportunity to know Profvk who had been an active contributing moderator. His scholarship and humility is always appreciated by the Advaiin list participants. It is an honor and privilege for me to review this book which I believe will benefit those who want to buy and read the hidden treasures of Ramayana discovered by Profvk. ***

**A first glance of the title of this book will likely create the incorrect assumption that this book focuses on comparing the writings of Valmiki, Kamban and Tulsi. Profvk fortunately did not follow that path because there are many published books focus on such comparative analyses. I am happy to report that Profvk took better alternative approach. Consequently, this book offers 150 of Profvk’s favorite choices of narrations from the epics of Ramayana presented by Valmiki, Kamban and Tulsi. As a matter of justification to the title of the book, Profvk did provide a brief and quick comparative analysis integrated it in one of the snapshots. Profvk’s informative interpretations of the exposition by the three poets are commendable and his narration is comprehensive and useful. **

**Some readers may be curious to know the reason for Profvk to write this book at age 95 when most of us want to rest. From what I know about Profvk, I am of the opinion that he believed that writing this book is an obligatory duty to fulfill the wishes of his beloved father and Guru, His father, Sri R. Vishvanatha Sastri (1882-1956 had left a handwritten manuscript of ‘Amayana Advayam,’ a 243 verse piece presenting the undercurrent of advaita in Ramayana. This current book by Profvk is a reflection of the ideas of his father from the handwritten manuscript. This may also explain why he chose the 150 snapshot narrations of Ramayana from the works of Valmiki, Kamban and Tulsi. Profvk has great admiration of Rama the hero of Ramayana who was loyal to Dasaratha. Profvk contemplated, pitR*-vAkya-paripAlanaM*(accept and follow the command of one’s father) as the central Dharma of Ramayana.**

**Among the 150 snapshots of wisdom buried within the Ramayana, eighty of them were taken from the verses of Valmiki Ramayana. Thirty eight of them came from the verses of Kamban and thirty one came from the verses of Tulsi. The original Valmiki Ramayana written in Sanskrit consisted of 24000 verses in 7 chapters. The Ramayana of Kamban written in Tamil language contained 11000 verses in 6 chapters. The Ramayana of Tulsi written in Awadhi language of Hindi Literature in 5500 Doha’s (verses).
Profvk has gone through those monumental works and chose to summarize them. Eight among the 150 snapshots in the book were not directly taken from Ramayana but they are special contributions from Profvk mostly his own reflections and also from Ramayana, Mahabharata, Bhagavad Gita and the Upanishads. He also mentioned that he was very inspired and motivated by the writings of his father (from the manuscripts left by his father). I was very impressed by the first ten snapshots and I found them quite profound with a subtle message. They will certain to enable the readers to acquire and practice human virtues such as Truthfulness, thoughtful friendship and resoluteness.**

**As always, Profvk has included more illustrations at various locations of the book that can provide as a guide to seekers who want follow a spiritual path of life. Extensive appendices are bonus supporting materials that can make life more enjoyable to readers who have digested the narrations of the snap shots reflections of Valmiki, Kamban and Tulsi.
