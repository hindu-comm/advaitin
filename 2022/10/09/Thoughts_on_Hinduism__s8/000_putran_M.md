------------ (June 2020)

  

Hinduism is the modern reference for what we call the Sanatana Dharma.

  

The Sanatana Dharma is the Foundation of all valid Religion.

  

A Religion has an Outer form as practiced by the people, and may have both

  

a\) A Valid Inner form of the Sanatana Dharma, an inner form that corresponds to the Sva-Dharma of some jiva that will allow that jiva to progress towards moksha. This inner form corresponding to the sanatana dharma may however have limitations in its scope to guiding the jiva all the way to moksha; and a jiva abiding by this Dharma may eventually have to transition from it.

  

b\) Invalid \[outer\] elements thatare man-made and not corresponding to the svadharma for any jiva. Such accretions and/or deletions result in practices that contradict the sanatana dharma. The classic example is "My dharma is to convert all humans to follow my Religion." This is fundamentally invalid because "My Religion" will have a valid inner form that corresponds to some jivas but not all.

  

…

  

We can say perhaps, in some cases the invalid Outer form dominates the valid inner elements to such extent that an inner form cannot be separately accessed. It is \[then\] no longer Hinduism. When the outer invalid elements are subsumed by a pervading valid inner form that can be separately accessed, it is still Hinduism.

  

---------------

  

A Religion's validity for a jiva is contingent on the jiva's own adrishta. In other words, the sanatana dharma as reflected in the jiva is its own sva-dharma; and as reflected in the jiva's consciousness of the world is the Path of Action and Seeking that will allow this jiva to fulfill its svadharma, clear its adrishta and move optimally towards moksha.

  

…

  

The sanatana dharma therefore underlies all valid forms of religion, each form being suitable to the jivas according to their adrishta. Thus the outer form of Christianity (an aggregate of both valid and invalid) can have an inner form that is valid and suitable for some jivas, a deeper form that is suitable for some others, and a core that is beyond name and form. The outer form may include notions like "Convert one and all to think Jesus is only saviour" - clearly a wrong idea incompatible with the sanatana dharma which validates multiplicity of forms according to the jivas' inner forms. This is more political than religion. The inner form may include the notion "Jesus is the only saviour", which can be valid for certain jivas who require that type of emphasis initially. Beyond that may be the teachings that transcend the Person; and finally a possible realization of Upanishadic truths.

  

Thus there will be facets of the Christian religion that will be valid form of the sanatana dharma. And there willbe facets of Judaism that find validation as form of sanatana dharma. And there may be a greater form that complements the non-contradictory but non-intersecting facets of both these religions. Such a religion may also have validity and perhaps allow the jivas a greater possibility of clearing adrishta and seeking moksha. That all-comprehensive Dharma of whom each of these Religions are particular forms is the sanatana dharma.

Quote (same thread; June 2019)

  

There are two analogies that I was thinking of, in trying to describe the Hindu's perspective on Hinduism or the Hinduism perspective on Religion.  

  

1\. Tree and its Branches

  

We have a tree with many branches. There are birds and bees and flowers and leaves on every branch. The Hindu understanding is that the Tree is One and the branches are many. Some branches may rise higher and some flowers may bloom larger. If you are a bird, you may seek the taller branch since that shows the highest heights; if you are a bee, you may seek the larger flower since that gives the sweetest honey. The one Tree is providing for many according their needs and wants and best allowing them to grow from within. If they evolve to seek otherwise, higher or better, they may naturally move from one branch to another.

  

This Tree is the Sanatana Dharma, aka Hinduism. (In another sense, it is Ishvara who has manifested in the many valid branches to fulfill the varying spiritual/material needs of the jivas.)

  

Each branch offers a certain possibility, vision, whether it is height, or length or its flowers and many leaves. Yes, the animals that gather onto them do so thinking such is the best for them. In many cases, the animals think that the same vision is ultimately shared by others in other branches though the apparent form of fulfillment appears different. In other cases, they*may*think that the vision offered by their branch is the best/goal for others as well, eventually and ultimately. But even such a Hindu does not confuse the fact that the Tree is still providing for one and all according to their present needs and wants, and that it is a process of evolution that must naturally direct the animals to that branch. This is a subtle but crucial point. The bird is not thinking every bee should seek its height; it knows that the bee is where it is best for it, and the same Tree is nourishing its growth and evolution. The bird may think that when the bee evolves (over multiple lives if necessary - no problem) under the nourishment offered by the Tree, it will become a bird and naturally seek its branch. And the Bee may be thinking in likewise manner in favour of its branch. And they may debate each other without the slightest intent that somehow they should convert the other - if it happens, it happens; but such is never the goal\* because there is complete contentment in knowing that the Tree is the Provider and Guider of all, in correspondence with their state of existence.    \[\*I am talking of attaching such an agenda to the vision of religion. There can be special cases like Shankara vs Mandana Misra, where the primary intent is to establish the Siddhanta/one's own viewpoint in a setting of tarka following established rules.\]

  

The point is that the Hindu vision of Hinduism can afford a certain degree of "best vision", but there are implicit bounds that will never be crossed because of the larger vision of the Tree/Sanatana Dharma.

  

The trouble comes from the animals who think that their branch is the real True Entity/Tree unto itself, others are false, and that all should seek only their branch - they lack the Hindu Vision of the Tree, and to them, Hinduism appears a fake identity conferred upon a group of branches. Such animals are destructive to the Tree itself, since their goals become an unnatural manipulation of the ecosystem so that one branch dominates and all others wither at its expense, so that all animals are unnaturally engineered to seek only that branch. These are not Hindus, and their conception of Religion/Tree is not Hinduism. And to top it, there are also animals claiming to not have anything personally to do with any of the branches that seek to define the tree according to the limited perspectives of these "my branch only" animals, and join hands with them to discredit Hinduism.

  

The problem for the Hindu is that these non-Hindu animals will also come forth with a similar evolution theory as above, to manipulate, deceive and appear genuine, that we cannot discern the right processes from the wrong and do not know how to stop their machinations. But we have to figure out how to deal with them if the Tree is to survive in a relevant manner.

  

2\. The trunk and the Elephant

  

This is a well known analogy for people's understanding of God. We can also apply it to understanding Hinduism.

  

We have say 3 blind men asked to touch and describe something.

  

Man A describes a hard smooth curved pointed thing. He calls it "Tusk" and says this is the real thing.

  

Man B describes a long bending thing with a wet end. He call its "Trunk" and says this is the real thing.

  

Man C describes a large rotund thing. He calls it "Belly" and says this is the real thing.

  

Man D who can see says "You are all touching the Elephant, in different places."

  

D has the Hindu vision and his Elephant is Hinduism. He has no conflict with A, B, or C's description. However, A, B, C have an Abrahamic vision, and to them, the Elephant is a false construct superimposed on disparate identities, of which their perception alone is true and the others' false.
