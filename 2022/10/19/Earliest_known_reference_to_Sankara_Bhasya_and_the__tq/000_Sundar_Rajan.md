The Movie from Kalki's Epic Novel Ponniyin Selvan is resurging interest in Chola history and on the Chola Kings: Raja Raja (referred to asPonniyin Selvan both in the book and in the movie), his son Rajendra and other Chola's support for all religions at that time and specifically their chosen religion- Shaivism.

  

While the teaching of Sankara's Bhasyas might have already taken placeduring Raja Raja's time, we have the first explicit reference to the Bhasya in a dilapidated temple on the outskirts of Trichy built during the reign of Virarajendra Chola (1063-1069 A.D). Interestingly this temple is only a short distance from the the sprawling BHEL township and the NIIT campus in Thiruverumbur.

  

In a bizarrelytitled article "Supplementary to Adi Sankara's work", The Hindu paper carried this story a few years ago, you can read the details in the link.

On the premises of the temple,Tamil Nadu Archeological Department has a board that references the Sankara Bhasyam inscriptions

  

Some of the key points are:

  

1\. Sankara was addressed in the honorific name of Bhagavatpada that we use even today

2\. Bhasyam in question was the Brahma Sutra Bhasya :BhagavadpadIyam Saareeraka-Bhashyatukku

3. Chola Govt granted land for maintenance of a teacher (Vaarthikan) to teach the Vartika called Pradeepaka (now extinct). The term Vaarthikan apparently occurs in other Tamil literature as well referring to someone who expounds or provides commentaryon Vedantic subjects.

4. Another interesting term used in the inscription and commoneven today is the wordvakkaanipaar- one who expounds

5. Also Intriguing is how the inscription explains the land endowmentsfor this effort, made not by the king but a town committee - decentralizeddecision making and democracy thousand years ago!
