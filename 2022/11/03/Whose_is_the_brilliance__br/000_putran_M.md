Namaskaram,

  

A relative mentioned a youngster in the family: ~ "He is a brilliant kid."

  

I interjected rather un-brilliantly, "Why (make a big deal of) brilliant?!"

  

The comment was not well received, and I was looked upon as being negative etc.

  

It may be true but there is an alternate way of looking at the situation.

  

Whose is this brilliance? Are we praising the youngster for qualities he earned in this body-mind or for qualities he seems naturally bestowed upon?

  

The fact is when we praise (with joy and pride) a child or youth for unusual gifts, it tends to be the latter situation where he or she is put on a pedestal for an apparently special starting point in its body-mind conditioning rather than as an appreciation of how the jiva utilizes that starting point in this life.

  

The starting point for the jiva is the phala for the karma it had done in its past lives. So the brilliance we see in our Ramu may be the fruit of the intensive labour done in that jiva's past life as some Mr. Ryan. Of course, there is nothing particularly wrong in appreciating the fact that Ramu has such and such unique qualities, for the purpose of encouragement that he may fulfill his potential in this life; but they are not necessarily achievements of "our Ramu" but of the unknown "Mr. Ryan" whom we care not for. Our joy over such bestowed characteristics in "our child" has to be thus tempered by the knowledge of the larger karma-karmaphala journey of that jiva in its quest for moksha.

  

Let me give a similar example. Say I am rich, wearing cool dress and driving fancy cars; and people start lauding and respecting me for all that. But suppose I just inherited the wealth from my parents who did the hard-work from theground-up to achieve whatever I am benefiting from. In such a situation, would it make sense to credit me for the wealth and lifestyle that I have? Rather it is just my starting point (the credit for which goes to the hard-working parents) and the real question is whether and how I build upon it meaningfully in this life.

  

In a different context, suppose a Brahmana is born with a higher degree of intellectual acumen. Even then, this birth and its varna-associated qualities that the jiva has are only the phala for the jiva's karma in the past lives (wherein varna, gender, etc. can differ). It puts the jiva in the best position possible, given its karma, in order that it may work its way forward (spiritually). So, the real credit to the individual in this life is earned in this life by using such bestowed abilities (and life circumstances) corresponding to one's svadharma for the "higher purpose" of human life. That is not about the starting point but what we do with it.
