Namaskaram,

  

My responses below. The same cousin had asked the same question 2 years back after our uncle had passed and I had chronicled the discussion on Jan 6, 2021 post titled "varom... porom..."

  

------------------

  

> But edhukku varom?

  

Due to discontentwith bondage and in search of liberation, "perfection", "fulfillment", "completion", "contentment", "happiness-bliss".



>   
>
> If its all about finding the Self, why did the Self create in the
> first place???

  

There is no first place in samsara: the Ishvara-jiva-jagat triangle is eternal. The Self as Ishvara creates in order to distribute karmaphala forthe jivas and present them the dharmic path that will optimally resolve their obstructions to liberation; and the Self as jiva in ignorance creates (does karma) in the course of their search forliberation, "perfection", "fulfillment" etc.

  

It is about finding the Self because "finding the Self" is equivalent to realizing what the jiva is seeking.

  

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  

There is no Rule written in the sky that you have a purpose for existing and that is to find the Self.

  

The Self is not hidden somewhere that you have to find it. It is You the ever-present Consciousness, except due to ignorance, you identify with a limitedbody.

  

And because the body comes and goes in Consciousness, we (as Consciousness reflected in mind and identifying with body) ask why do we "varom" and "porom"?

  

We don't. The Self that you are is the Self of the Universe: You neither come nor go. You do not change in any real way simply because the name-form identifications change (or you change your appearance identifications).

  
A couple of examples given are snake superimposed on rope and water superimposed in a mirage. So long as snake or water is "seen"/"imagined" as real, one is impelled by fear and desire and responds as if to a snake or real water. And when one knows it is only rope or mirage, the response will be natural to that knowledge and detached from fear and desire for an imagined existence.

  

Likewise, when there is knowledge of Self - that I am already complete and do not varen or poren - one is not impelled by fear and desire to make it complete or to save it from destruction. The world is no longer an entity apart from self that one must confront and subdue/resolve in our best interest. It is only an appearance or denotation of the Self, and no matter the appearance the nondual Self remains unchanged and unchangeable. Whether Durga or Kali, it is the same Mother.

  

Does this mean the knower of Self becomes inert? No. In the realm of manifestation or appearance, the knower of Self will also appear to act like anyone else. But with the caveat that the actions are aligned with Dharma or Ishvara's natural Order and not prompted by ignorance of Self. If the deity is Durga, he may offer her flowers; and if it is Kali, he may offer a goat's head - whatever be the Dharma. If the body signals hunger, he may offer food like anybody else.

  

When the momentum of prarabdha karma is expended, the gross body of the knower reaches its "death". Unlike for others however, his 'subtle body' will also disintegrate and with that will cease the jiva-consciousness that is reflected in it. This is because the "glue" of ajnana is needed for the Self to retain its identification with a limited subtle-body (mind etc) and hence to believe it is a jiva and want thereby a new body so it can somehow find "perfection", "happiness", "fulfillment", "completion", "contentment". Knowledge has annulled this glue in that jiva, so its transmigratory journey will end with this body. We say the jnani-jiva attains videha mukti after the fall of the body.
