    anyathAgrahaNa 4.  avidyA which is the dharma of neither jeeva nor Atman (geeta
    bhAshya) 5.  avidyA in the form of agrahaNa, anyathagrahaNa (mithyAjnAna)
    samshaya etc. has to be said as antaHkaraNa dOsha ( vide BG bh. &
    tait. Up. Bha.) 6.  avidyA and Atman need not be proved by any pramANa former is
    kalpita/adhyArOpita and later is svataH siddha. 7.  avidyA = jnAnAbhAva & adhyAsa = vipareeta grahaNa or mithyAjnAna 8.  avidyA as both jnAnAbhAva & adhyAsa (and saMshaya as well) quite
    natural to human mind and lOka prasiddha and it is anAdi
    (beginningless) and ananta (endless) (ref. vide AB) 9.  avidyA and its related talks like its ashraya (locus), vishaya
    (subject), kAraNa (cause) etc. are within the sphere of adhyAsa only
    means prior admission that he is taking himself as
    jnAtru/pramAtru/katru/bhOktru.



Now we have to look at why this avidyA is kevala jnAnAbhAva and not bhAvarUpa ( a positive entity) but before that let us have some bhAshya vAkya-s wherein bhAshyakAra shows us for adhyAsa how the absence of knowledge is kAraNa. In the bhAshya avidyA of the nature of agrahaNa is also called ajnAna, apratibOdha, anishchaya, anavagama, anavabOdha, tamas etc. In any case as said above (g) because of the reason that agrahaNa and anyathAgrahana have been called avidyA alone in PTB. If anywhere avidyA used in a particular context then we will have to thoroughly study that context to discern as to what exactly its meaning according to that particular context. adhyAsa bhAshya says : evamayamanAdiranantO naisargikOdhyAsO mithyApratyayarUpaH, and in that same bhAshya bhAshyakAra says mithyAjnAnanimittaH satyAnrute mithuneekrutya ahamidaM mamedamiti naisargikOyaM lOkavyavahAraH, and in geeta bhAshya 13.26 kshetrakshetrajna svarUpa viveka abhAvanibandhanaH.
Let us not go into the details of why this simple mithyAjnAna nimittaH has been tortured to mean mithyA ajnAna nimittaH by vyAkhyAnakAra-s, as that is not the task here. But all these quotes simply showing that our inability to distinguish between Atman and anatman is the cause for adhyAsa. Because of the reason that this inability to distinguish and discern is verily avidyA of the nature of agrahaNa ( jnAnAbhAva) and it further amounts to saying that the avidyA of the nature of jnAnAbhAva is responsible for wrong knowledge . And in this lOka prasiddha vyavahAra there is no room to accommodate the positive existence of some type of avidyA which has the locus in brahman.



OK, without beating around the bush, in simple terms : the reason for the wrong thought or anyathAgrahaNa or vipareeta pratyaya or mithyAjnAna is just the absence of the right thought (absence of correct knowledge/jnAnAbhAva) in the mind, namely “I am Brahman.” In other words, avidyA as adhyAsa is only because of the absence of vidyA. Sri SSS says this jnAnAbhAva itself called as mUlA by sureshwaracharya in vArtika and is the kAraNa for the adhyAsa. Following can be considered for the further substantiation to claim why avidyA is jnAnAbhAva not bhAvarUpa.



1.  It is jnAnAbhAva precisely because it is not an existent thing that
    ajnAna cannot be directly the cause of grief. (kindly see
    yesterday’s quote with regard to this). 2.  From jnana the avidyA’s three types would go simultaneously : yadi
    jnAnAbhAvaH, yadi saMshaya jnAnaM, yadi vipareeya jnAnaM vA uchyate
    ajnAnamiti sarvaM hi tat jnAnenaiva nivartate ( br.bh.3.3.1). If
    this really an existing entity it will not go by jnana as we have to
    remember bhAshya jnana is not kAraka it is only jnApakaM.
3.  At some places it has been explicitly said absence of jnana itself
    is ajnAna when explaining the ‘tattvamasi’ bhAshyakAra for details
    one can see su.bh.4-1-2. 4.  As explained earlier avidyA is not natural feature of Atman it is
    mere antaHkaraNa dOsha due to absence of correct knowledge : sa chA
    avidyA nAtmanaH svAbhAvikO dharmaH ([br.bh](http://br.bh). 4.3.20)
    by this it is quite evident brahmAshrita mUlAvidyA vAda quite
    conspicuous by its absence in mUla bhAshya.
5.  And in IshAvAsya bhAshya : Atma shuddhaM nirmalaM
    avidyAmalarahitamiti kAraNa shareerO pratishedhaH, Atman / brahman
    is ever pure there is no question of giving ashraya to dirt like
    avidyA in HIM. 6.  In the katha bhAshya shankara says how this is adhyArOpita not
    really an existing thing : People experience grief caused by actions
    done with desire which is itself caused by their avidyA
    ***superimposed*** by them on themselves. But that avidyA does not
    really exist in their Atman. Na tu sA paramArthataH svAtmani
    (2.2.11). 7.  Geeta bhAshya 2.69 : Just as the darkness in the night vanishes the
    moment sun rises, avidyA is totally lost the moment vidyA dawns and
    if it were an existent stuff like mUlAvidyA it cannot be destroyed
    completely : na hi bhAvAnAm niranvayO nirupAkhyO vinAshaH saMbhavati
    clarifies bhAshyakAra in sUtra bhAshya 2.2.22. 8.  And that famous shloka in geeta : nabhAvO vidyate sataH, jnana even
    at its full force cannot destroy an existing thing nor create
    anything afresh.
9.  And this avidyA (jnAnAbhAva) which has been termed as existing in
    sushupti since in that state our upAdhi-s are upashAnta in nAdi-s
    jagat beeja ( not avidyA beeja which MVV says) too will be merged in
    brahman but this knowledge of ekatvaM is not there since jeeva
    enters into sushupti with this bedha jnana he comes back with that
    ajnAna only hence transactionally it has been accepted that jeeva is
    merged with brahman only in sushupti but there is no time / avastha
    that jeeva is aloof from brahman.
10. And finally, for those who assert that avidyA is mere jnAnAbhAva and
    therefore it has no objective existence, should know that the
    oneness in Suṣupti is not missed by the acceptance of avidyA since
    avidyA as jnAnAbhAva noway come in the way of this oneness and
    ONENESS and absence of this knowledge can coexist since avidyA as
    jnAnAbhAva not anartha hetu. Now, coming to those for whom avidyA is
    bhAvarUpa or an objective existence, to establish the oneness in
    sushupti the shAstra tells that at that time the indriyA-s, the mana
    and all the objects of the world of effects are merged in
    parabrahman. Those who say that avidyA is bhAvarUpa existent, have
    to accept going by this declaration of shruti that bhAvarUpa avidyA
    or mUlAvidyA or kAraNAvidyA also merges in Brahman. The indriya-s
    the manas and the jagat whether in their vyAkruta or avyAkruta from
    are only effects of brahman and so not opposed to it. Therefore,
    their merger in their cause parabrahman is possible ( take the
    example of spider in mundaka). But if the objectively existent
    avidyA should also merge in brahman, it would imply that it is not
    opposed to the svarUpa of parabrahman. What on earth can the jnana
    mArgi jignAsu-s gain from that brahman who has given Ashraya to
    anishTa avidyA and what is the use of realizing that tattva which is
    not opposed to avidyA? Absolutely nothing. And it is as good as
    saying avidyA is on par with ultimate reality parabrahman!!

Post shankara sub-commentators have been considerably exercised over determining the locus, object and the effect of avidyA, mostly because of their postulate that it is positive potency clinging to some one and projecting or evolving into the unreal appearance of not-self and the universe. Three different views (a) that the jeeva alone (2) brahman itself (d) pure consciousness devoid of the distinction of brahman and jeeva-s, constitutes the locus of this avidyA, have been alternatively the dogma of the different schools of sub-commentators and hot disputes have been raging among the followers of these sub-commentators for defending each one of these alternatives and refuting the other two views. That all this display of speculation is so much labour lost from the genuine shankara stand point, is obvious from shankara’s express statement in his introduction that avidyA is only a mental mixing up of the real and the unreal.
