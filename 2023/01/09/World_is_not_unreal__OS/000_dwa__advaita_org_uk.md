Just a brief note – I do not wish to enter into arguments here.



I was a bit worried by Subbu-ji’s scholarly post on quotes from Bhagavatam and elsewhere on the ‘reality’ of the world. There is a danger that seekers may take some of these literally, without the benefit of a teacher to explain them in context.



I have spent the last year writing the second volume of the series on ‘Confusions in Advaita’ and the topics of this one are ‘Ignorance’ and ‘What happens on Enlightenment’. A major aspect of the second topic is the notion that the world disappears. (Some readers may be aware that I spent around 18 months arguing about this on my website!)



The simple truth is that the world is NOT ‘unreal’. It is mithyA – name and form of Brahman. So it cannot ‘disappear’, since Brahman is eternal and changeless.



But it is certainly true that some (many?) seekers believe that it does disappear, and/or they believe that it is unreal. And whether or not they do is, unfortunately, likely to depend upon which texts they happen to own and read. As just one example of this, consider Brahmasutra bhAShya 2.1.14.



Here is what George Thibaut says for this passage:



In this manner the Vedanta-texts declare that for him who has reached the state of truth and reality the whole apparent world does not exist. (Ref. 108)



One might have expected that the Penguin Classics book “The Roots of Vedanta, Selections from Shankara’s Writings’” (Ref. 328) could be relied upon for authenticity. But, apart from the fact that many of the introductory, author-written statements show significant misunderstanding of Shankara, the Brahmasutra version that is used is the one by Thibaut.



Swami Gambhirananda translates it as follows:



In this text it is shown that in the state of the highest Reality, all transactions as between an ordainer and things ordained cease to exist. (Ref. 5)



The actual Sanskrit for this sentence is:



इत्यादिना च एवं परमार्थावस्थायां सर्वव्यवहाराभावं वदन्ति वेदान्ताः सर्वे

ityAdinA cha evam paramArthAvasthAyAM sarvavyavahArAbhAvaM vadanti VedAntA: sarve



Meaning literally:



ityAdinA – by these (quotes)

cha – and

evam – in this manner

paramArthAvasthAyAm – in paramArtha avasthA

sarvavyavahArAbhAvaM – (compound) absence of all transactions

vadanti – say

vedAntA: – Vedanta texts

sarve – All



Shankara gives a few quotations from the Upanishads and states that “*By these (quotes), in this manner, there is total absence of all transactions in paramArtha avasthA say all the texts of Vedanta.*”



(Translation by courtesy of Satyan Chidambaran.)



There is no mention of the world disappearing! (Which is understandable as the present existence of the statement would be evidence of its invalidity!)



So far, I have written nearly 14,000 words on the status of the world viz a viz enlightenment and think I have now dealt with most of any contentious quotations. (I do not know of any that cannot be explained by either mistranslation or misunderstanding of the translator.) The book should hopefully appear around the end of the year or early next year.



I will certainly read any responses to this but, as I said, I am not going to enter into arguments – I did all of that around 2 years ago and do not wish to repeat it! (It is VERY time-consuming!)
