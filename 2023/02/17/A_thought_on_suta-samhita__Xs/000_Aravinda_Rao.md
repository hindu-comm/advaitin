Namaste,

There is a famous sloka which says that Shankaracharya looked into Suta Samhita eighteen times and then wrote his commentary, the Sariraka bhashyam, on the Brahma sutra-s. The sloka runs as follows:

तामष्टादशधा विलोक्य शङ्करः सूत-संहिताम्।  
चक्रे शारीरकं भाष्यं सर्व-वेदान्त-संमतम्॥  

Suta-samhita is a strong Shivaadvaita text in which the principles of advaita are very precisely explained, but Vishnu is shown as a devotee of Shiva, often falling on the feet of Shiva. I felt that Shankaracharya took the shuddha-advaita principles from it and wrote his bhashyam on the brahma-sutra-s. He did not see any bheda between Shiva and Keshava.

Aravinda Rao
