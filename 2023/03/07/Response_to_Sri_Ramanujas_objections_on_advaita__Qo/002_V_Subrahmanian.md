In the Bhagavadgita itself we have the Lord himself saying:

  

नासतोविद्यतेभावोनाभावोविद्यतेसतः।  
उभयोरपिदृष्टोऽन्तस्त्वनयोस्तत्त्वदर्शिभिः॥ १६ ॥ 2.16

  

There is existence for the unreal and the Real never goes out of existence. He who knows these two 'Truths', is a Jnani.

  

The Lord should be making this statement only with reference to the world and Brahman. And only because the world is so very visible, experienced, that he implies the absolute non-existence of the world.
Brahman is that which is said to be ever-existent.

  

So, it is clear that for Bhagavan the world only seems to exist but does not really. Yet he chooses to instruct Arjuna who is not yet enlightened about the above two Truths.

  

There are many more instances in the Gita itself:

  

नान्यं गुणेभ्यः कर्तारं यदा द्रष्टानुपश्यति ।

गुणेभ्यश्च परं वेत्तिमद्भावंसोऽधिगच्छति ॥ १९ ॥  14.19

  

He who knows that it is only the guna-s of prakriti that is the doer and not the sentient Atman comes to realize the identity with Bhagavan, Brahman.

  

Knowing very well the jiva is non-different from Brahman, the Lord chooses to instruct Arjuna the truth when the latter is in ignorance.

  

Bhagavan says in the 3rd chapter: naishkarmyam, no-action absolutely, is the nature of the jiva. Yet the Lord instructs karma yoga and jnana yoga to attain freedom from ignorance and realize oneness with Brahman, naishkarmyam.

  

The 13th chapter ends with the statement: He who knows himself as distinct from the body-mind complex but the witness to the BM and also knows that the prakriti, kshetram that includes the BM, is non-existent - bhuta prakriti moksham cha, is the Jnani.

  

So, knowing very well that the world, kshetram, does not really exist, the Lord teaches Arjuna in that apparently real world. 

  

Thus, from the many instances of the Gita itself we can conclude that there is absolutely nothing odd in the Lord who is free from avidya teaching Arjuna. 

  

The Veda:

  

यत्र हि द्वैतमिव भवति तदितर इतरं जिघ्रति तदितर इतरं पश्यति तदितर इतरं शृणोति तदितर इतरमभिवदति तदितर इतरं मनुते मदितर इतरं विजानाति यत्र वा अस्य सर्वमात्मैवाभूत्तत्केन कं जिघ्रेत्तत्केन कं पश्येत्तत्केन कं शृणुयात्तत्केन कमभिवदेत्तत्केन कं मन्वीत तत्केन कं विजानीयात्। येनेदं सर्वं विजानाति तं केन विजानीयाद्विज्ञातारमरे केन विजानीयादिति ॥ १४ ॥ Brihadaranyaka 2.4.14

  

Where there is as it were duality, difference, one sees, smells, etc. another. However when one realizes that everything that is is Brahman alone, then there is no real perception of the world.

  

  

The Veda does know the ignorant and the enlightened positions. Yet it chooses to enjoin the ignorant one the karma, upasana, etc. and eventually lead him to the absolute realization of the Truth.

  

Thus we have the Veda itself, despite knowing the 'right' position successfully teaching the one in the 'wrong' position. If anyone faults with the Advaita, one is faulting Bhagavan and the Veda.
