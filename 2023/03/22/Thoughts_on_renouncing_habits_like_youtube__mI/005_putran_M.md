Namaskaram Subbu-ji,

  

I was not advocating total withdrawal from youtube. It was an analysis of some of the issues we face*should we want* to renounce a "bad habit". The word habit indicates the negative connotation that it has become a bondage we are not able to control on our own terms. If it is a matter of us using an object as and when we need and leaving aside as and when we want, then there should be no problem and no reason to consider renouncing it.

  

In the case of youtube, yes it offers many good things but it is also dangerous for those who seek to singularly channelize their energies to a "goal". Such are thevasanaasand proclivities etched in a person's memories from their past for samsaric dualism and such is the capacity of this type of medium to indulge these temptations immediately that for many people, as soon as they enter the arena, they forget the path for sreyas, and instead run headlong for to get an addict's dose of preyas, or they lose their spiritual stamina by jumping in all directions. One moment it is advaita, next it is sports, politics or cinema. This causes the frustration and loss of confidence expressed by Bhaskar-ji. The apparent inability to filter the sugar from the sand and take only sugar frustrates many to the point that they think it is easier to throw away the bath-water completely, baby and all. For beginners especially, this may actually be a good thing: abstinence through redirection and replacement may be far more practical than to try and reform the damaged uncontrolled unfit mind even while prematurely dragging it into the battle arena where it will be overwhelmed. Such abstinence is by no means easy however, for the "redirection and replacement" (of A with B) has to be uncompromising and this can be revolutionary to the system that is presently subordinated to the fulfillment of A, even more so when our involvement in these things is tied to our interactions with other people. Like, "I have to watch this movie because my wife or son wants to." But I was ruminating on this approach and some of the overlying principles governing it.

  

Here is another example that we may be able to relate to. Suppose we are invited to a "party" or "social gathering". The buffet has a few select veg and a great number of non-veg items along with alcoholicdrinks. Now we have neither vasanas for non-veg nor confusion regarding our dharma. We go to the party, take a few bites from the veg menu, drink some juice or coffee, and leave. There is no temptation for the rest of it, no fighting against thecurrents, and no regrets afterwards. I just was on a flight where the vegetarian meal had some sort of cake-bread slice. I asked the attendant whether it had eggs and her reply was "Probably". Even if it was an "I don't know", I would have not eaten it. The point is that I am so clear and resolute about my dharma in this regard that even the temptation for the nice looking food is kept at bay if I have doubt regarding the ingredients, and once I have knowledge later on that it has eggs/non-veg,the object simply falls off my consciousness.

  

Interaction in youtube is perfectly fine for those who have this clarity about their svadharma and limits. They have judiciousness as you said to use the tool optimally. Unfortunately formost others, youtube etc. offers a buffet for minds burdened by exploitable vasanas and lacking a sharp dharmic compass to wade through unwanted clutter. So, establishing self-control over such avenues of desire becomes essential sadhana for those seeking 'freedom' from them - and that may initially include following the principle of abstinence from A, finding and rooting in B, so that we can return to the presence of A and still be anchored in our (higher) svadharma.

  

thollmelukaalkizhu
