*A Classification of Objects.*  

  

Let us classify four types of objects that appear to our waking consciousness.

  

1\. Objects that are projected in the mind and which cannot be corresponded with objects in the world through recourse to valid pramana.

  

2\. Objects that are projected in the mind but which can be corresponded to objects in the world by recourse to Memory.

  

3\. Objects that are projected in the mind but which can be corresponded to (knowledge of) objects in the world (known through valid pramanas that are adjuncts to pratyaksha, or through shabda). Like the thought of fire when smoke is seen.

  

4\. Objects that are known directly through pratyaksha pramana.

  

The first three categories constitute the mind-world for the jiva. The fourth is the sense-world.

  

**Objects in the mind-world are the jiva-self's own projections in consciousness. Therefore a fundamental general sadhana is the following.Instead of allowing an image or thought to attain a separate real self-status, as a real object I am seeing, Take charge by affirming the knowledge that I am the Self and this image is merely my projection/appearance/form in the mind that in no way whatsoever changes Me or necessitates that I imagine further.The center of attention thus has to be reverted from the mind-object back to Me, the unchanging Self only appearing to assume such forms.**

  

Such determined awareness loosens the hold of the mind-world upon us and deters a free-fall into worlds of our imagination. However,because we are typically cognizant of a body-world extension "outside" to which the mind-world stands as an intermediary, we have to ascertain valid correspondence between our mind-world and the sense-world based on which we can interact, desire and act in a necessary dharmic manner. For this purpose, we need to delineate the mind-world in a more nuanced manner and treat the appearance of objects in our consciousness in an apt manner.

  

**This is the purpose of the four-fold categorization of objects given above.**

  

I want to mainly discuss Categories 1 and 2 since most of the confusionregarding how we understand and deal with the mind-world pertains to them. I will use a couple of examples.

  

I. Suppose the image of Virat Kohli shines in my mind. (By past knowledge stored in memory, this Kohli image corresponds to a real person Kohli of the world. So we can deem the mental object to be of Category 2.)

  

Now I recall an image of me on the big screen while attending the match last week. (It seems to be category 2; but in reality that occurrence of the past is no longer an object of the world. It exists in my mind only and perhaps independently in the minds of others (or in video). So we can demote it to Category 1.)

  

This remembrance of myself triggers the thought of people I know thinking ill of me, which depresses me. (Wait. What is the pramana for people thinking ill of me presently because of how I behaved in the stands that day? If there is none, relegate it to Category 1. If someone told me then of how disappointed they were, then at least based on memory we can label it Category 2 to the extent it may still correspond to a "disappointing object" in the mind of someone else.)

  

The point of this brief analysis is to highlight the extent to which we superimpose these categories and should be willing to discern them carefully, which in turn greatly affects the way we experience and respond to the objects. In truth, only Kohli is strictly Category 2 because his image rightly corresponds to an existing object Kohli in the world, based on memory. All other images and thoughts of this example even where based on memory are not referring to existing objects of the world. Should we become aggrieved by the thought of someone thinking bad of us for some action of the past? Or should we consider the thought as strictly our own mental projection and not relate it to the world? At least when there is no pramana or memory basis to correspond with a world object, we should deem it strictly as the mental object that it is, a projection that in no real way afflicts the Self that I am. It is Category 1.

  

Once we determine a mind-object is of Category 1, it is to our advantage or decision to not further our imaginations and karma based on it. For Vedantins at least, this should be naturally a part of their sadhana.

  

II\. The situation becomes more complicated when we have to consider Category 2 or 3 objects. Then there is some valid basis for considering that our mind-object represents a world-object truthfully and that we should do karma based on the projected mind-object. We cannot take an absolute stance on whether and how one should respond to a mind-projection that can be corresponded to a world-object. Given the self-identifications, it may be dharmic to desire and act in the world in accordance with the initial mind-projection. But at the same time, a lot of such action by people can also be deemed paradharma or *unnecessary*, so that the mind-object of Category 2 and 3 in such cases would be better responded to just as if it is Category 1. If such is the decision, then not only the object but also all thoughts suggesting further connection with world must also be reduced to what they are - only projections of mySelf, the unchanging Reality. The whole show must be subsumed into quietude.

  

A\) The image of Kohli we said corresponds to the cricketer Kohli in the world. Suppose alongside that thought comes the knowledge (based on memory) that he is presently playing a match and the match is shown on TV. This whole sequence of thoughts belong to Category 2 since they all can be corresponded to existing objects of the world.

  

At this point we are pushed by vasanas (also mental objects) to act upon our knowledge, turn on the TV and fulfill the desire to see the match. Do the vasanas reflect our svadharma or not that we follow through on them? As I observed in the original mail, the answer depends on the context of our self-identifications with Ishvara'smanifestation. For the professed Vedantin seeking to seek moksha/jnana, clearly these vasanas are not indicative of his svadharma and following through on them expends his time and energy in wasteful directions that he regrets later.

  

If I am able to discern them as paradharma mind-objects, then I have (from a practical standpoint) demoted the objects to being equivalent to Category 1. **At this point the sadhana once again is to nip such mind-objects at the bud by bringing in the awareness that they are only my projections and nothing apart from me.** This takes diligence, alertness and resilience since we are trying to neutralize deep-set vasanas through awareness of their unreality (beyond our self) and thereby to break the worn-down pathways of our habits.

  

B\) On the other hand, suppose we remember our landlord and alongside comes the memory of having to pay our rent. The mind-objects are category 2 but in this case we have to follow through on the subsequent desire to pay the rent. We need not exaggerate thoughts of fear or the landlord's anger, such can be relegated to Category 1; but nevertheless the svadharma of paying the rent will have to be fulfilled.

  

III\. Category 3 mind-objects are meant to refer to knowledge of world-objects obtained through valid pramanas adjunct to pratyaksha (like anumana) and shabda. When we see smoke and think of fire, the fire is projection in our mind but it corresponds to the knowledge of actual fire, that we know existed via the valid pramana of anumana. Likewise when we consider adrishta-vishaya like dharma, punarjanma, Ishvara, the nastika may deem all such thoughts as lacking correspondence with reality outside one's mind-world; whereas the astika knows through shabda pramana that these objects projected in the mind denote Ishvara'smanifest reality.

  

When properly known, the correspondence of Category 3 objects with the world is regarded as being free from doubt, and hence one may do karma based on them in accordance with svadharma. If we see smoke coming from a heap of leaves, then warning our child "there is fire there" is a dharmic response corresponding to realityin the sense-world and not merely our mind-world.

  

IV\. Finally, the fourth category is the proper sense-world. As per the traditional viewpoint, (when obstruction between the jiva's senses+mind and object is removed) the (subtle) sense organs of sight, smell, etc. followed by the mind establish immediate direct contact with the object; themind takes on the svarupa of the object as a vritti; Consciousness illuminates this vritti and shines as knowledge the object. Thedirect contact ensures that the object existing in the world coincideswith the vritti formed in the mind, and therefore the knowledge revealed in consciousness is of the object as it is manifest in the world. Thatis why the senses are said to be valid pramanas or means of knowledge. (This is in contrast with the theories of science which follow a representational stimulus-response model that precludes the pramanatvam of the senses. See the book "Natural Realism and Contact Theory of Perception" by Sri Chittaranjan Naik)

  

From a practical perspective however, the question of knowability of sense-objects is not the primary issue for most people; they take that for granted. When we see a chair, we assume the traditional standpoint that the eyes indeed have the power to present the chair as it exists and any further error is due to malfunction or obstruction. It would indeed be a problem if it was the other way around, where we have to somehow attach to each perception the belief it is representational only. For our purpose here, we should understand clearly what constitutes category 4 (and 3) in order to identify distinctly the further Category 1 & 2 overlays that are superimposed upon it in our mind-world and harassing us into action, much of which is otherwise invalid or unnecessary and best dealt with in manner described above (in I. and II.).
