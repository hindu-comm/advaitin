Namaskaram,

  

some further thoughts.

  

If A is the bad habit we want to overcome, I suggested for that purpose a rational enquiry into  

  

a\. the nature of the mind-objects

  

b\. the superimposing of mind-objects onto the world based on imagination, speculation and memory (Categories 1, 2), versus

  

c\. the knowing of world-objects (existing, at present) through valid pramanas, pratyaksha etc.and shabda (Categories 3, 4).

  

Then use this discriminating knowledge to sublate Category 1 and the paradharma elements of Category 2 as nothing but mental projections/forms of self, and thereby stop the process of further imagination and pursuit of karma on their basis.

  

In particular this is a jnana-based approach. "Jnana" tries to resolve the problem at its core by dissolving the ignorance that causes it.Jnana destroys that notional "reality" of A. The sadhana is to see through the unreality of the bondage rather than to see away from. If the rational enquiry is successful, then the shraddha is immediate and to the point of our concern. Then the sadhana is focused on following through on the conclusions honestly (which can be demanding),and the onus for that falls on us. All this may not be easy to achieve, but at least the intent is straightforward.

  

  

  

Alternatively, adualistic "bhakti"-based approach is to find an alternate focus B for our attention and blocking off all routes to A with B and forcing the mind to redirect and absorb in B.

  

Such "bhakti" shifts from the problem to something else in which we are able to independently stabilize. Temporarily, this allows the mind to be abstinent of A and become free of the compulsions to indulge in it.

  

However, there is a problem. The object B is a refuge from A but focusing on it does not mean we have conquered (our fear of/taste for) A. A is quarantined away so long as we are able to dwell in B; but it remains the same real monster opposed to our self in the hidden depths of our mind.

  

Ideally however, our committed pursuit of B will lead to a higher jnana of B that naturally eliminates the bondage of A.

  

Trouble is not quite this roundabout process but that it is likely to be abused by beginning "sadhakas" for the following two reasons:

  

1\. Their "bhakti" in B is prompted more by their desire for freedom from A. They may not have independent shraddha in B, and it may become a waning false show, losing the impetus to "know" B further, once they find some relief from A.

  

2\. Their desire for freedom from A (bad habits) may be more intertwined with their desire for success in some C (studies, job, etc.), so they subconsciously only want to use B ("Ishta devata") as a quick-acting lever for stopping A so that they can stabilize in C.

  

For the supposed religious sadhaka who brings in his "Ishta devata" as B, the above issues may matter eventually. This type of "bhakti" definitely can be helpful, but one has to be aware that it will be incomplete in the early stages; and one has to, after obtaining some freedom from A, work harder to obtain proper knowledge of B so that the bhakti can fructify.
