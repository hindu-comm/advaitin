More thoughts:

  

Recall the four-fold classification of the objects of our perception that I presented earlier.

  

Quote

  

  

1\. Objects that are projected in the mind and which cannot be corresponded with objects in the world through recourse to valid pramana.

  

2\. Objects that are projected in the mind but which can be corresponded to objects in the world by recourse to Memory.

  

3\. Objects that are projected in the mind but which can be corresponded to (knowledge of) objects in the world (known through valid pramanas that are adjuncts to pratyaksha, or through shabda). Like the thought of fire when smoke is seen.

  

4\. Objects that are known directly through pratyaksha pramana.

  

The first three categories constitute the mind-world for the jiva. The fourth is the sense-world.

  

Unquote

  

  

In the perception of these objects, the first fundamental question whose *right* answer must shine in our awareness is:

  

1.  Where is the existence of this object?

  

Suppose I am thinking of the big Gorilla King-Kong rampaging outside my house. Clearly, this King-Kong that I perceive is a mind-object existing only in my mind. But is this knowledge shining in my awareness as I witness this King-Kong (KK), or am I thinking of him as being really there outside my house?

  

\[Note: KK will denote below Category 1 imaginations that leave a mark on our psyche, not necessarily a thought of some other jiva like a gorilla. Even Category 2 imaginations are often accompanied by Category 1 attachments; KK can denote these attachments without negating the possible sense-world correspondence of the Category 2 objects.\]

  

  

There are two possibilities:

  

1.  Yes, I am fully conscious that King-Kong is only in my mind and I do
    not (really) superimpose his existence onto the sense-world. 2.  No, as I imagine King-Kong, there is a concurrent imagination that
    situates King-Kong in the world outside. That is, if my imagination
    is that King-Kong is outside my house, there is a “belief” (gross or
    subtle) during the course of the imagination that indeed he is
    outside my house.

  

Case 1 is similar to when we watch King-Kong in a movie theater. No matter how scary KK looks, we know he is make-believe and unreal so far as our “actual existence” is concerned. We never lose sight of the fact that our self is unaffected by the KK we are imagining in our mind.

  

Case 2 is similar to when we dream during sleep. Here, one of the concurrent mind-objects in our perception of KK is the thought that he actually is present in the sense-world, about to do good or bad. We find our mind pulled by this thought superimposing the mind-object onto the sense-world. In such a case, if KK appears to kill someone, our body-mind reacts as if it is really happening and we find ourselves shaking in fear. Etc.

  

In Case 1, we have full knowledge of the real situation, that KK is a mental projection having a lower order of reality to our self that is unaffected by whatever KK ‘does’ or whatever happens to him. In Case 2, the KK imagination assumes a separate reality-status equal to but opposed to our Self and his actions therefore are also imagined to affect us positively or negatively.

  

In much of our daily thinking, the Case 2 superimposition (of mind-objects onto sense-world) happens as a matter of course, at least during the period of our imagining. It may not be a complete blackout of the Case 1 awareness, but there will be a parallel indulgence or compulsion of Case 2 superimposition, to the effect that the body-mind transforms to reflect the imagination as if it is the real experience of the corresponding object in the sense-world. We desire, fear, get happy, angry, upset, enjoy or suffer the thought as if the event is happening in our presence right now.

  

Case 2 is a case of Ignorance, superimposition, lack of discrimination. It may be unnecessary at best and highly detrimental at worst. We are continuously exhausting ourselves looking at mind-objects that arise in our perception as if they are real sense-objects imposing upon us in the world. If it is a dream, we may come out and move on to the next. If it is a nightmare (or day-mare), then even after coming out, we are wondering whether KK really punched us in the stomach and we should henceforth not eat or drink as much! And the next time we step into the woods, our mind precedes us to the thought of KK being there.

  

Case 2 imaginations can thus have the snow-ball effect of creating and reiterating vasanas that de-root our consciousness from the stability of our Self and spiral us back into the world of such imaginations.

  

Basically, I am making the case that Case 2 or Category 1 objects in general are not our best buddies and we should make a concerted effort to pull back from them. Practice Discrimination of unreal and affirmation of real.

  

  

One way to do this as said before is to recognize the mind-objects as belonging to the mind only, and then finding a way to sublate them as only projections of self in the mind. (Or some alternate method to revert attention to the Self alone, where the reality of mind-objects becomes negated.)

  

But there is another very important practice that should also be exercised alongside. This has to do with affirmation of real, with regard to the sense-world. When the mind-object arises in consciousness and gets superimposed onto the sense-world, we have to pull ourselves out and turn the mind’s full attention to the sense-world as it really exists at this moment. Affirm diligently what actually is present - even if based on memory. King-Kong is simply not there. We are not countering Case 2 KK with some Case 1 ‘SS’; rather we are directly asking what is the present manifest sense-world as per our pramana-based knowledge, then firmly asserting to our perception that manifest existence alone - thus naturally eliminating the appearance of KK. The Case 2 mind-objects conjured up in our thoughts are not part of the manifest sense-world, hence they vanish as we focus on (and bring to our mental perception) that “real world”. Settle the mind in the real. (This should be part of general practice as part of training the mind, not only when afflicted by ‘bad’ thoughts.)

  

And if the Case 2 mind-objects still don’t seem to vanish or seem ready to spring forth again, we have to turn back at them and face them; call the bluff. Use the previously said method of confirming in our awareness that they are only mind-objects, only our projection, etc. (See earlier mails regarding this). It will be work and we have to be willing to do it, repeatedly. (In case we need to, when seemingly pushed to the edge of a cliff, we can also have Case 1 ‘SS’ stock mind-objects that can act as a quick deterrent or antidote of the Case 2 KK. But the purpose of such ‘SS’ should be to regroup ourselves so that we can battle using the more direct jnana-based methods.)

  

———————————-

  

What I have outlined above has in it a shift from Category 1 & 2 objects to Category 3 & 4, since the latter deal with the actual sense-world. Note that we have not yet dealt with Ishvara per se, the underlying non-dual Reality of all objects, the Consciousness appearing and identified in dualistic terms, as jiva, Jagat, mind-world, sense-world. We have to take recourse to shabda pramana to first learn that everything is He/I the Self of All; but the knowledge cannot take root in our consciousness while we are still harassed by the mind to the extent we generally are and our shraddha in Shastra is as limited as it initially is. So the above ideas are meant for general person, who is cognizant of the problems presented by the mind and is at least willing or wanting to tackle them through rational discriminative methods. If we obtain a requisite level of vivekam, confidence and self-control over our gross mental activities, we may find ourselves more inclined to shift to (or do alongside) the more systematic shabda/shastra oriented methods: karma yoga, bhakti yoga, jnana yoga. 

  

Below is from an earlier write-up that summarizes some of the methods given above:

  

Thoughts (including of past and future) are formsin the mind, projected \[illumined\] by the Self.

  

Practical: a. Separate the mind-world and the sense-world, and desist superimposing what is only mental projection onto the world. For this purpose, you can ask the question "What am I seeing?" Answer this through abidance in what the senses are directly revealing now, peeling off the addedthought & image interjections happening in the mind.

  

b\. Look upon the thoughts as the Self's (your own) forms of the mind (whose changes do not change You); and Look upon the Present manifest sense-world as the Self's (Ishvara's) projected form (whose changes do not change Him). (the same Mother whether She appears as Lakshmi or Kali.)
