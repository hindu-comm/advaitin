(This is continued from my post on ‘Brahmajñāna’ but I am giving it a separate subject heading to keep comments separate.)



Incidentally, our discussion began with my being informed that, nowhere in prasthāna traya does the term ‘Self-ignorance’ occur. I was initially surprised at this. (The topic does seem to be discussed quite extensively by Sureśvara in Naiṣkarmya Siddhi, for example.) But I rationalized this as follows:



Initially, we do not know who-we-really are. We do not have knowledge about our ‘Self’. We do not have ‘Self-knowledge’. Accordingly, since ‘ignorance’ is the term that we use to describe the fact that we are lacking knowledge about something, we say ‘colloquially’ that we have ‘Self-ignorance’. But we are only able to say this because, although we do not truly realize the nature of the Self, we all know what we mean when we talk about ‘Self’, even though we initially and erroneously
identify this with body or mind. So, although we do not have Self-knowledge, we are able to say that we have Self-ignorance.



We have ignorance about many things but, if we have never even conceived of something, we could not really say that we have ignorance about it. For example, we would never say that we had ‘black-hole ignorance’ if we knew absolutely nothing about astronomy, astrophysics etc. and had never come across the term. It is only when someone tells us about it that we (may) start to take an interest. Then we will begin with ‘black-hole ignorance’ and ultimately gain ‘black-hole knowledge’ (at least, to the extent that this is possible at present).



So it is always Self-knowledge that is relevant. Initially we do not have it, Then, with the aid of scriptures and guru, we may get it. All of this, of course, emphasizes the notion that there is only knowledge or lack of it. There is no real, existent thing called ignorance.
