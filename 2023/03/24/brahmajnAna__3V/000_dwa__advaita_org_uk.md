Being Advaitins, we speak about brahmajñāna and ātmajñāna all the time – it is what Advaita is for! The main topic of the second volume of my ‘Confusions in Advaita Vedānta’ is ‘Ignorance’. Consequently, I am talking about Self-knowledge and Self-ignorance all time.



I often run into problems when I want to find out exactly what Ṥaṅkara said about something, usually because I don’t believe what a translator or commentator has written. When this happens, I often seek help from a colleague who knows rather more Sanskrit than I do. (I can read Devanāgarī slowly and look up words in Monier-Williams. But I do not know grammar or much else.)



I have just had occasion to speak to someone recently (not sure whether he wishes to remain anonymous) about ‘Self-knowledge’. My understanding has always been that what this means, in the context of Advaita, is that the jīva does śravaṇa-manana and eventually realizes that ‘I am Brahman’ etc. I.e. the jīva gains Brahmajñāna. He or she had Self-ignorance and now has Self-knowledge.



But now I am told that the combination (samāsa) forms, brahmajñāna or ātmajñāna are in ṣaṣṭhī (genitive) case, and mean ‘the knowledge belonging to Brahman or ātman’ – Brahman’s knowledge! Surely we understand that ‘I, the jīva, am Brahman’; it is not that ‘Brahman gets the knowledge that Brahman is Brahman’??



Please could some Sanskrit scholar clarify this confusion.
