Hare Krishna



- At the risk of being garrulous let me share my understanding before
  you receive any scholarly reply 😊 



My understanding has always been that what this means, in the context of Advaita, is that the jīva does śravaṇa-manana and eventually realizes that ‘I am Brahman’ etc. I.e. the jīva gains Brahmajñāna. He or she had Self-ignorance and now has Self-knowledge.



- If the ignorance is really there as a solid thing it cannot go
  anywhere, jnana cannot create anything new or destroy anything that is
  already existing. The dawn of self-knowledge would give the knowledge
  that there was / is / never ever be ajnAna as such and what was /is /
  will ever be there is jnana only and my svarUpa is jnana only.



But now I am told that the combination (samāsa) forms, brahmajñāna or ātmajñāna are in ṣaṣṭhī (genitive) case, and mean ‘the knowledge belonging to Brahman or ātman’ – Brahman’s knowledge! Surely we understand that ‘I, the jīva, am Brahman’; it is not that ‘Brahman gets the knowledge that Brahman is Brahman’??



- There is a particular bhAshya bhAga which discusses this exclusively,
  I think it is in bruhadAraNyaka. Here prima facie raises the doubt :
  the knower knows himself is self contradictory since there is shruti
  you cannot know the knower of knowledge itself. For this objection
  siddhAnti answers : It is not contradiction to say that he is known
  this way and gives subsequent clarification with regard to this
  stand. Up.sa. also says something like this the witnessing self
  itself would be intuited by itself of the nature of intuition. kArika
  also talks about it somewhere.

Hari Hari Hari Bol!!!
