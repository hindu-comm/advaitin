Dear Subbuji,

  

I read one of the very small articles (or rather call it just a small paragraph) from the Kanchi Kamakoti Math, which is as follows:  

**Sri Abhinava Sankarendra Saraswati** :  
He was one of the greatest among the successors of Adi Sankara, so great indeed  
that he and his deeds are often confounded with those of the Great Bhagavatpada and  
his deeds. ***His life story has been described in detail in a work entitled the "Sankara  
Vilasa" by Vakpati Bhatta.*** His father was one Visvajit of Chindambaram. He was  
invested with Yajnopavita at the age of five and taught Vedas. He showed an  
extraordinary promise in dialectics and soon he became a formidable disputant on  
intricate questions in philosophy. He defeated Vakpati Bhatta, a great scholar of  
that time, in argument. He ascended the Savajna Pitha in Kashmir. It is said that he  
entered the Dattatreya cave in the Atreya Mountains in the Himalayas on Amavasya.

This para is self explanatory.

  

The book "Sankara Vilasa" by Vakpati Bhatta was on Abhinava Shankara, the navavatara (incarnation) of Adi Shankaracharya. But that book is not available now, not even with the Kanchi Kamakoti Math, which has been called the Svamatha (own matha) of Adi Shankara, in the BrihadSankaravijaya, written by none other than Shri Chitsukhacharya, the childhood mate and disciple of Adi Shankaracharya. Adi Shankara spent last five years of his life there at the Mokshapuri Kanchipuram at the feet of Mother Lalita. The Lalita Trishati is also the last bhashya written by Adi Shankra, but unfortunately none of the Shankara mathas seem to know this.  

  

Now that you find from the content-list that the book published under the title "Sankara Vilasa" by Palaniappan Pillai, has nothing to do with Abhinava Shankara, and that supports or rather establishes my suspicion that there had been some machination by some hostile forces, who want to suppress information about the details of the great Abhinava shankara, by publishing another book of the same name.  

  

There are somewhat similar unfortunate cases which occurred in relation to the Dwarka math also in the past, but we may talk about it on another occasion.  

  

Regards,

Sunil K Bhattacharjya
