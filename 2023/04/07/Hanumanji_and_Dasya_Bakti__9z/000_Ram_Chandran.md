Hanumanji and Dasya Bakti

Dasya (Service with total dedication without ego): Hanuman is the greatest example for this form of Bhakti. Ramakrishna Paramahamsa gave a beautiful example to illustrate Hanuman's Bhakti toward Shri Ram. The Lord once asked Hanuman, "What is your attitude toward men? How do you look upon, think of and worship me?" Hanuman replied, " O Rama, I am conscious of my body when I feel that I am this visible body and then I have the conviction that thou art the Lord and I am thy servant. Thou art to be served and I am the one to serve. When I am conscious of my self as the individual self, one with the mind, the intellect and the soul, I have the conviction that thou art the whole and I am thy part.
When again, I remain in samadhi, in the mood that I am the pure Self without all qualifying adjuncts, I have the conviction that I am also verily that which Thou art. Thou and I are one. There is no difference whatsoever."



The original verse in Hindi was fromRamcharitmanas by the Hindi poet Gosaway Tulsidasji. Most references state that the verse is from Ramcharitamanas.



**देहदृष्टयातूदासोअहम्,जीवदृष्ट्यात्वदंशकम्।**

**तत्त्वदृष्ट्यात्वमेवाह्म्,इतिमेंनिश्चितामति:।।**



Here when Bhagwaan Sri Ram asks his great devotee Hanumaanji that “Who are you?” The great intellect Hanumaanji replied with the above verse which when translated will be: “If I look from the perspective of this mortal body, I am your Sevak/Servent (दास). Further Hanumaan ji says, if we look from the prespective of eternal truth (तत्त्व), I am you. Because whatever is out there is all you, nothing else and this is very clear to me.

This validates why Hanumanji should be considered the foremost among intellectuals. In Sanatana dharma, the ultimate goal of a Jiva is to reach the level which Hanumaanji mentioned in the last verse. In the beginning Jiva is able to fulfil the demands of his/her body and at that time Jiva follows the path of “saadhna,” to slowly discover the inner-self. This will enable the Jiva to realize that Jiva is different from the body and wants to realize the Aatman. To know the Aatman is wisdom or Gyan. But this is not the ultimate destination Jiva further pursues to know that there is nothing else other than the Aatman. This is ParaVidhya or knowing the Ultimate Truth. Hanumaanji knows this eternal truth makes Him the Foremost among the Intellectuals!

The words of Hitopadesh seem to fit very well the qualities as exhibited by Hanumanji\>

“vidyA dadAti vinayaM, vinayAdyAti pAtratAM \|  
pAtratvAddhanamApnoti, dhanAddharmaM tataH sukhaM \|

True knowledge disciplines the mind, from discipline one develops the character, from character one gets prosperity, with prosperity one is able to do good deeds and from that one attains Bliss.
