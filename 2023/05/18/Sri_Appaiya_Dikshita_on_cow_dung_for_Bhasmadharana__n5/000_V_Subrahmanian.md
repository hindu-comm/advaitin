Srimad Appayya Dikshitendra's Explanation of the Role of Deshi Cows in the Tripundram We Wear on Our Foreheads

  

Pundram (forehead mark) worn by the followers of all the Sampradayas of Sanatana Dharma is vital in the course of Karmanushthanam and Upasana. The wearing of Pundram is a must for one to execute any Dharmic activity. The Shastras have described the sanctity of pundra-dharanam, raw materials required for different types of Pundras, method of preparation, and method of application on the forehead, torso and arms.

  

Acharyas of various Sampradayas have explained the Pundra-dharana-vidhi in great detail. As far as the wearing of Tripundram is concerned, Parashivavatara, Nityagnihotri, Advaitavidyacharya, Srimad Appayya Dikshitendra has explained the sanctity of Tripundra-dharanam in works such as Shivarchana-chandrika and Purvottara-mimamsa-vadanakshatramala (भस्मोद्धूलनत्रिपुण्ड्रयोः सकलब्रह्मविद्याङ्गत्वसमर्थनवादः).

  

Chapter 4 of Shivarchana-chandrika deals with Bhasma-dharana-vidhi or the method of wearing bhasma. He says that there are four categories of bhasma:

1\) Kalpa-bhasma (the first grade and the most superior bhasma),

2\) Anukalpa-bhasma (the second grade bhasma),

3\) Upkalpa-bhasma (the third grade bhasma) &

4\) Akalpa-bhasma (the fourth grade and the most inferior bhasma).

  

1\) Kalpa-bhasma (the first grade and the most superior bhasma):

Cow dung that is collected in a vessel before it falls on the ground, made into bolus, dried, burnt while chanting specific Vedic Mantras, and sieved to give fine particles of bhasma is called Kalpam.

  

2\) Anukalpa-bhasma (the second grade bhasma):

Cow dung that is collected from go-pracharana-bhumi (grazing land), dried, burnt while chanting specific Vedic Mantras, and sieved to give fine particles of bhasma is called Anukalpam.

  

3\) Upakalpa-bhasma (the third grade bhasma):

Cow dung that is collected from go-pracharana-bhumi (grazing land) or the cowshed, made into bolus by mixing with cow's urine, burnt while chanting specific Vedic Mantras, and sieved to give fine particles of bhasma is called Upakalpam.

  

4\) Akalpa-bhasma (the fourth grade and the most inferior bhasma):

Cow dung that is collected and burnt without using any Vaidika Mantra, and sieved to give fine particles of bhasma is called Akalpam. Bhasma that is collected from the yagashala or homakunda of an agnihotri-brahmana or any shrotriya-brahmana is also called Akalpam.

  

After studying this chapter I was wondering as to how we categorize the bhasma that we wear. The substance that we wear in the name of bhasma is terribly inferior to the Akalpa-bhasma or fourth grade bhasma mentioned by Dikshitendra here. The genuineness of bhasma-dharanam is no more.

  

And yes, as shishtacharam says, the dung of deshi bulls, male and female deshi calves and deshi heifers should never be used to prepare bhasma or vraatii used for all Vaidika Karmas. The dung of deshi cows that have given birth to calf and given milk for at least once in their lifetime should only be used for these purposes. Jersey cows, Holstein cows and cows that are a mixture of deshi and foreign breed of cattle are completely out of question.

  

Our Vedic life is a void without the deshi cows. They give us aihika-sukham (happiness in this lifetime) by giving milk, ghee, curd, and buttermilk that are required for nourishing our physical body. They also give us different grades of aamushmika-sukham (happiness in the life after). The highest aamushmika-sukham (mukti) that is achieved through Brahmavidya is dependent on the cows as well. That is what Dikshitendra explains in Chapter 22 of Purvottara-mimamsa-vadanakshatramala (भस्मोद्धूलनत्रिपुण्ड्रयोः सकलब्रह्मविद्याङ्गत्वसमर्थनवादः). Yes, Tripundra-dharanam when done strictly as per shastra-vidhanam will definitely bestow Brahmavidya when it is done with proper Karmanushthanam, Adhyayanam of the Veda & all the Shastras, Bhagavadupasanam, and strict observance of every form of Vaidika Achara. We have a very long way to go. It is a journey of multiple lifetimes.

  

Just like serving a shrotriya and brahma-nishtha guru is important to gain Brahma-jnanam, the service of cows is equally important to gain Brahma-jnanam. Deshi cows should be an integral part of our lives. The more we serve them with bhakti and shraddha, the more we give them a happier life, and the more we make use of their milk, milk products, gomutra and gomaya in our Vaidika sadhanas, the more we get shreyas.
