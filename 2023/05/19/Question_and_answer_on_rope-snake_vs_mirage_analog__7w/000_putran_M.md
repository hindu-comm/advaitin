Mail 1:

  

I had this doubt while reading this \[attached\] text of swami paramananada bharati called vedanta prabodha.If you could help me it would bemuch appreciated.

  

The second question and it's answer was the issue,i used to think of the appearance of jagat/prapancha in brahman similar to mirage example,since snake in the rope i thought to be the mistake of the observer due to impaired where we confuuse it to be snake.

  

Response 1:

  

If there is a “creation” to talk about, then the mirage description is good since even after knowledge of the unreality of water, we are talking about some sort of dualistic “as if water” appearance of the Air. Likewise, if after realization that Jagat is appearance of/in Brahman, we still think Jagat appears as jada different and separate from Chaitanya, then such a Jagat we would say is a mirage appearance that we know to be unreal.

  

The other viewpoint is that once you know what is seen is not water but only Air appearing in a certain shiny way, then you no longer see/know water there either but only Air in a certain appearance that is not separately identified in any way whatsoever. Why would you think “That is Air but it looks like water”? No, you would simply know “That is Air appearing to me in this manner.” No question of water any further. Likewise, once we realize sarvam-brahmam, there is no other thing to identify as if separately of Brahman and then to negate as unreal: we only see Brahman/Chaitanyam in all the appearance. So in this case, the notion of something different from, or a part of Brahman is all undone once we have knowledge that only Brahman IS.Whatever we see, we know to be Brahman. Whether we see Kali or Lakshmi, the devotee knows to be only the Divine Mother in different forms; even the forms are not separately talked about apart from the Mother - they only denote Her. Hence once the snake is realized to be unreal adhyasa, it is gone forever and we know only the Rope that always was and is.

  

The trouble is that in truth even after knowledge that all is Brahman, whoever we call jnani in fact talks about the world of duality. Take Krishna, Shankara, SSS, Ramana, whoever you please. They engage in vyavahara. Thus we have to admit that in any standpoint of discussion that we are involved in, a jnani (when identified by a body-mind) also has awareness of the vyavahara duality even though the duality we see is only Brahman to him. He sees only the Mother when we see Her as Kali and Lakshmi separately;**however**he also sees Her in two forms as presented by Her. That does not vanish in jnana because the “jnani” is himself part of the appearance of the Self in the vyavaharika standpoint.

  

The conclusion is that the jnani also if he describes his knowledge in words will have to deem Brahman as Ishvara = Brahman + Maya, the Consciousness/Self appearing as a Dreamer projecting the dream. We cannot avoid the standpoint of Dreamer-dream duality, only the ajnani believes the dream and the jnani acknowledges that Brahman appears as if “Dreamer-dream” or “seer-seeing-seen”.This rudimentary “Brahman+Maya” duality is non-negatable satyam in vyavaharika and asat in paramarthika standpoint of Brahman, hence it (or Maya and its effects jagat etc.) is mithya. 

  

  

Mail 2:

  

Thanks a lot. I wanted to know then,why would bhagavan bhashyakara use the example of rope and snake with Brahman and Prapancha ?

  

Brahman is nimittopadana karana for the jagat but is this the case with rope and snake?

  

  

Response 2:

  

Let's look at jagat, which has a simpler connotation. Now this jagat is typically understood to be a Jada vastu, something different from Chaitanyam.

  

But knowledge is that Brahman is the nimitta upadana karana of jagat. The effect cannot be fundamentally different from the cause, hence that which we call jagat is only an appearance of Brahman by recourse to its intrinsic Maya Shakti. That is, this notion of karana-karyam is itself only in the sense of Maya, as-if.

  

In realizing Jagat as only a nama-rupa denotation of Brahman, we have completely negated the original understanding that it denoted something different from Brahman. That jada denotation of jagat which is based in ignorance has vanished like the snake on the rope. In its place is the knowledge that it is only appearance or projection of Ishvara - because of which Brahman+maya is said to be karana.

  

In particular the snake refers to jagat as understood by an ajnani and when we speak of karana-karya, that has to be understood in terms of knowledge that Brahman appears as jagat.
