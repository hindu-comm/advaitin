Dear Putran ji,

  

Not sure if this point is already covered in the discussion: The mirage analogy has an advantage over the rope-snake analogy in that it continues to offer the phenomenon even after one has had the right knowledge that there is no real water there. The post-sublation/negation continued appearance of the water is useful in the Advaitic Jnani's post-world mithyAtva realization. It continues to appear because the perceiving body-mind complex has not yet disappeared. So, in the presence of the instruments the world-perception will continue, but with the knowledge to the Jnani that it is not real. Shankara gives yet another analogy: the perception of the dual-moon: here too the person knows that there are no two moons but only one but the appearance of the extra moon is due to a defect in his eye. So, even if an opponent or a seeker in doubt may question the validity of the rope-snake analogy, the above stated other two analgies are unassailable.
