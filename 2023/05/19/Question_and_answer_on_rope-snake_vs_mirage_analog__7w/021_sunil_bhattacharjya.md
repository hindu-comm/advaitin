Dear friends,

  

While any thorough discussion is generally welcome, one has to keep in mind that the statements in the Vedas and particularly in the upanishads are not to be literally interpreted. More and more discussions only complicate matters. We should not think that we can elucidate things better than Adi Shankara did or Lord Krishna did.  

  

Though Arjuna was very intelligent, the ParamBrahma Avatara Lord Krishna did not use the wordings like 'Jaganmithya' nor used the the 'Rope-snake analogy', and instead conveyed to Arjuna in the very second chapter of the Original Bhagavad Gita that that the Vyavaharika world is illusory through the verse:  

  

आदावन्ते च यन्नास्ति वर्तमानेऽपि तत्तथा ।  
वितथैः सदृशाः सन्तोऽवितथा इव लक्षिताः ॥

  

Lord Krishna told the same thing to Uddhava in the Uddhava Gita, using similar wordings. In fact Shri Gaudapadacharya also borrowed this verse from Lord Krishna and used it in the 2nd prakarana of his Mandukya karika, to emphasize that the world is illusory.  

  

My 2 cents
