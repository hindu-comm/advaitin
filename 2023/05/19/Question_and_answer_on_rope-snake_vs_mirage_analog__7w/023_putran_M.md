Reading this line triggered some thoughts and questions.

  

1\.

  

Take a wooden elephant doll.

  

When we see that ‘elephant' and call it elephant or say to a child 'how big the elephantis!’, or to a carpenter ‘Chop that elephant into 10 pieces and build a shelf’, at all points of time our usage of 'elephant' is only as a nama-rupa denotation of the reality wood. It is wood that appears as or projects nama-rupa 'elephant' and our ‘elephant’ always refers to the ‘wood'.

  

‘Elephant’ then is real in this context because it denotes the reality wood only.

  

Another example would be, whether we go to the temple in Tirupati or Trivandrum, we refer to the same deity Vishnu by different nama-rupa denotations corresponding to the appearance. If Vishnu is real, then so is “Venkateshwara” and so also is “Padmanabhaswamy” because they are simply different denotations of the same reality.

  

Likewise, if ‘world’ is a nama-rupa reference of the Reality Brahman, then saying ‘world is real’ is non-different from saying ‘Brahman is Real’.

  

2\.

  

HOWEVER, the fall-out of such a standpoint is indeed it posits dvaita with respect to the appearance of Brahman. Brahman being the non-dual Reality will then have to be known as the cause for this duality in appearance. If we say it is non-dual Vishnu who appears as both Venkateshwara and Padmanabhaswamy, then we have to affirm that Vishnu has an intrinsic maya-shakti because of which He appears in different nama-rupa. If we posit a world appearance of Brahman, then we posit in Brahman the Mayic power to appear as world and the dualities we see within it.

  

Why is this significant? Because it means that we are still talking of Brahman having maya-shakti as its intrinsic attribute by which it projects self as a world appearance. It is saguna in the most rudimentary sense. Thus, Ishvara = Brahman+Maya is the upadana nimitta karana for this world appearance. Such a dvaitic knowledge of Brahman is the truth in what advaitins call the vyavaharika standpoint.

  

3\.

  

The question we have to ask is whether this dvaita in terms of projection/appearance is sathyam of Brahman, or is there a so-called “paramarthika standpoint” in which Brahman is nirguna and beyond all dualities (appearance or otherwise)? If the world-appearance and the causal Maya attribute are negated/sublated in a higher paramarthika standpoint, then it makes sense to understand that such vyavaharika duality - whether Ishvara's appearance or jiva's ignorance - associated with Brahman is ultimately vivarta (apparent), mithya, anirvachaniya - ontologically neither real nor unreal, and Brahman as pure Consciousness is alone satya.

  

4.

  

The other perspective is to say that advaita doesnot imply a negation/sublation of the dualistic Brahman+Maya or of its being the nimitta upadana cause of world appearance. Instead it is only about negating the child’s ignorant thinking that the wooden-elephant is ‘alive’ or ‘not wood’ - that the child is to realize the truth: “No, no. all you see is wood in different nama-rupas”. This is the position initially examined in 1. above.The question in turn for this viewpoint is whether this makes the philosophy a sort of Visishtadvaita, since Maya is some sort of real attribute of Brahman and the dualistic world gets posited even if it be called ‘appearance’.
