Just a point.

  

If we see something in dim light, typically we have some doubt or wonder as to what we are seeing since we are not used to seeing objects in dim light. That is, our smruthi is lacking a proper reference to correspond with what we are seeing. Nor can the object we see in dim light be an exact match with the 'snake' in our smruthi because it is an incorrect identification even after we make the conclusion "Oh this is (or may/must be) a snake!" So it was our best guess but possibly we grab onto the thought of snakeas a possibility and then do theadhyasa thereafter. In particular, it does not seem correct to say that the cognition of snake happens at the time of first cognition.  

  

However, it is possible that the way the analogy is meant to be used is as you say it. That the identification of snake is equivalent to the incorrect knowledge of rope and it is present at the start of cognition of rope; and must be removed when correct knowledge is attained. However, the overall framework that I presented still makes sense: there is cognition of rope-in-dim-light, cognition of snake-thought, cognition of adhyasa thought linking the snake in mind to rope seen. This adhyasa-'thought' is anaadi (for the jiva has not attained jnana yet) and therefore it is the filter at the time of initial cognition itself (not as something that comes later). Rest as per my original reply.
