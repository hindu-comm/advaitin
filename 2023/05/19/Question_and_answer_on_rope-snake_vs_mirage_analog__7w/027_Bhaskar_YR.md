Brahman being the non-dual Reality will then have to be known as the cause for this duality in appearance. If we say it is non-dual Vishnu who appears as both Venkateshwara and Padmanabhaswamy, then we have to affirm that Vishnu has an intrinsic maya-shakti because of which He appears in different nama-rupa. If we posit a world appearance of Brahman, then we posit in Brahman the Mayic power to appear as world and the dualities we see within it.



Ø To uphold the Advaita doctrine as enshrined in shruti Acharya says there is no difference between mAya (shakti / power) and Ishwara/brahman ( shakta). And this power neither increases nor decreases in brahman as it is brahmAnanya. Shankara at various places establishes this.





The question we have to ask is whether this dvaita in terms of projection/appearance is sathyam of Brahman, or is there a so-called “paramarthika standpoint” in which Brahman is nirguna and beyond all dualities (appearance or otherwise)? If the world-appearance and the causal Maya attribute are negated/sublated in a higher paramarthika standpoint, then it makes sense to understand that such vyavaharika duality - whether Ishvara's appearance or jiva's ignorance - associated with Brahman is ultimately vivarta (apparent), mithya, anirvachaniya - ontologically neither real nor unreal, and Brahman as pure Consciousness is alone satya.



Ø Yes as you said brahman as pure consciousness is ALONE satya.
This is the ultimatum given by shruti.
