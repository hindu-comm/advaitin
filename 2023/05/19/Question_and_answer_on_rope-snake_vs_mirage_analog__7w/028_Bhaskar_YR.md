Just a point.



If we see something in dim light, typically we have some doubt or wonder as to what we are seeing since we are not used to seeing objects in dim light. That is, our smruthi is lacking a proper reference to correspond with what we are seeing. Nor can the object we see in dim light be an exact match with the 'snake' in our smruthi because it is an incorrect identification even after we make the conclusion "Oh this is (or may/must be) a snake!" So it was our best guess but possibly we grab onto the thought of snakeas a possibility and then do theadhyasa thereafter. In particular, it does not seem correct to say that the cognition of snake happens at the time of first cognition.



Ø The first cognition of snake happens only in vipareeta grahaNa (misconception) in this cognition the cognizer hardly takes all these aspects into consideration i.e. he is having a dim light to cognize a thing, he is having poor eye sight, there is a close resemblance between rope-snake hence there is possibility of seeing a thing wrongly etc.
But you are right in the ‘samshaya’ (doubt) he would consider all these things into consideration like illuminating light is so dull so better light required, how can a snake come-in when I covered the area very well with fence and just day before I took a piece of paper as currency my eye sight is poor so it is better to check with proper light / means to determine what is really there!! This type of behavior we can expect only in saMshaya jnana which is as you know not as harmful or devastating as mithyAjnAna is!!. Hence bhAshyakAra emphasized atasmin tadbuddhiH (adhyAsa) as avidyA is more harmful and which is the main cause for the rAga-dvesha etc. But yes, avidyA explained as agrahaNa, anyathAgrahaNa and saMshaya as well.
