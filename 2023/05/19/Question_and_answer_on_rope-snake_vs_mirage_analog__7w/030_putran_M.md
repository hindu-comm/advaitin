Namaskaram Bhaskar-ji,

  

I wrote this reply separately looking over all your mails together, referred to you in 3rd person at places.

  

---------

  

Bhaskar-ji’s assertions.

  

1.  Mail 1: “It is better to differentiate between Ishwara srushti and
    jeeva srushti”

  

2\. Mail 4: “ that is how it is, Ishvara cannot be jeeva. Jeeva has his own limitation and cannot be Ishvara.”

  

3\. Mail 2: “when there exists nothing apart from brahman, where is the question of dvaita \[in appearance of Brahman\]?… where is the room for any duality?

  

4\. Mail 2: “ to uphold Advaita… there is no difference between Maya (shakti) and Ishvara/Brahman (Shakta).

  

  

5\. Mail 2: “karyakara also karana rupa eva satyam… this jnana is not avastha nirbandhita to ‘literally’ experience the absolute absence of nama and pupa. It is badhita jnana that elephant is nothing but wood… the so-called anatma vastu was/is/not ever be there ‘apart’ from brahman.”

  

  

  

  

Statement 1 says “it is better” which means two possibilities are there but one is better than the other. Statement 2 negates this allowance by categorically stating that only one possibility can be entertained and that is Ishvara jiva bheda.

  

Now, the traditional position accepts this as the dvaitic standpoint of the ajnani. That I am a jiva and there is Ishvara, I am the cause for my Kalpana and Ishvara is the cause for maintaining and forwarding the Order in jagat according to the jivas’ karma. Ishvara in this context does not technically create jagat which also is anaadi as well. Jagat also in this context is jada.

  

Then the question is when we say Brahman is the second-less cause of the world, is this “brahman” only referring to this Ishvara who is different from this jiva and the statement is basically saying only that Ishvara is projecting and withdrawing a jada jagat in accordance to the activity of the jivas?

  

Moreover, is this now claiming that whenever we refer to maya of Brahman, it is only a reference to this Ishvara who is different from jiva and who is cause for some part of existence and not another?

  

  

Let’s keep these questions in mind and consider the Mandukya karika 2.11-13.

  

2.11: \[Opponent\] If all objects in both states \[dream and waking\] be unreal, who apprehends these objects and who indeed is their creator?

  

2.12: \[answer\] The self-effulgent Self imagines Itself through Itself by the power of its own Maya. The Self itself cognizes the objects. Such is the definite conclusion of Vedanta.

  

2.13: The Lord manifests diversely the mundane things in the mind. Turning the mind outward, He creates the well-defined things (as well as the undefined things). Thus does the Lord imagine.

  

  

These verses tell us that there is a unitary Self, the Lord, who imagines the things in the mind and the things of the world; who both imagines the objects and cognizes them; who does so via His unitary power of Maya.

  

This is not a negation of the Ishvara-jiva bheda standpoint spoken of earlier but informs us that that standpoint is sublated in a higher abheda standpoint where Brahman/Self (+ Maya) is truly the “second-less” cause of all duality - and that includes Ishvara, jiva, jagat, jiva kalpana and so on, all of which reduce to the status of ‘imagination. It is the non-dual Self in association with its power of Maya that “imagines” all objects, in the ‘mind world’ and the ‘sense world’.

  

However, the duality of the effect is reduced to a fundamental duality of Brahman+Maya.

  

Now, this depiction of Brahman in relation to the ishvara-jiva-jagat world, as the Self who imagines such a world through Maya, is presented by a jnani who \[Mail 4\] “knows his own true svarupa” and therefore who has “no avidya whatsoever”.

  

Wait a second!! If there is no duality \[in appearance\] (as per Mail 2), then wherefrom is this mention of imagination of objects in the utterances of a “jiva” with no avidya? Bhaskar-ji’s statements have this type of contradiction. The need to talk of imagination, of Maya and then to assert that Brahman and Maya are non-different (or Maya sublates in Brahman in paramarthika) - all that affirms that there is a duality in appearance for both the ajnani and the jnani except the jnani knows that even such duality is asat in the standpoint of knowledge/Reality. The ajnani believes that duality is not appearance but real; he doesn’t know of Brahman as the non-dual Reality that appears dual and as a consequence he thinks in terms of Ishvara-jiva-jagat bheda.

  

In point 5, Bhaskar-ji who said (in point 3) there is no duality even in appearance because of the truth of point 4 quickly reverts to telling why jnana is not about negating nama-rupa absolutely but about realizing the non-dual substratum Reality. Again a contradiction to his earlier statements. In fact, the nama-rupa denotations constitute the duality in appearance because the same wood is identified variously as elephant, chair, house and so on.

  

At this point, Bhaskar-ji says even though nama-rupa duality may be there, by no means is such identification something anatma apart from Brahman. But this is why it is **mithya**. From the paramarthika standpoint of knowledge (“no difference between shakti and shakta”, nirguna brahman), the nama-rupa imagination/appearance is not sat. Yet in the lower vyavaharika standpoint of even a jnani, the duality of nama-rupa persists and hence it is not asat (though jnani fully knows it is Brahman/Self who appears/imagines so and that the nama-rupa identifier cannot be identified as an entity apart from the substratum Brahman).

  

  

Finally, it is wrong to say that the viewpoint I present is meantto imply jagat is jiva parikalpita. If there is jiva, then jiva is different from Ishvara and so on. We agree with that, as the dualistic standpoint of ajnana. However the jnani is the intermediary who is identified in vyavaharika but who has jnana of the paramarthika satyam. He knows there is only non-dual Self that (in vyavaharika) is ishvara-jiva-jagat karana, i.e. that IJJ is all projection, appearance, imagination of Brahman+Maya (=Ishvara, in the ultimate sense possible from vyavaharika standpoint), and that from the absolute paramarthika standpoint, that same Self has not even the bheda of Brahman+Maya.
