Namaskaram Bhaskar-ji,

  

*Quote*

*  *

*Yes for him jagat is brahman (himself) but he (brahman) is not jagat,
he is himself without any visheshaNa. i.e. he is always nitya, shuddha, buddha, mukta svarUpa Atman.*

*  *

*Unquote*

  

This is why jagat (Brahman+viseshana) is mithya and anirvachaniya.

  

If jagat is Brahman-identified-with some attribute/upadhi called “viseshana” and Brahman itself is without any viseshana, then the viseshana is something ‘extra’ tagged onto Brahman that is not of the same order of reality as brahman. Brahman appears with viseshana in the lower vyavaharika standpoint and is without it in the higher paramarthika standpoint. That is why you can phrase the sentence as you did - saying jagat is (always) brahman but brahman is not jagat. The (duality of) viseshana is therefore mithya; its reducible to a unitary causal attribute (Maya), which also is mithya.

  

  

*Quote*

- *The jnAni’s status is not to stand in between ajnAni-prapancha And
  paramArtha-brahman, identification of jnAni in vyavahAra and his
  activities etc. is the perception of on-lookers not jnAni’s. His
  realization fetch him the knowledge he was / is / never be
  kartru-bhOktru. However, it has been clarified that jnAni-s senses
  would operate normally even after samyaK jnana just to drive home the
  point jnana does not immediately bring the physical death of the jnAni
  but he is ashareeri always.*

*Unquote*

  

“Shankara is a jnani; that monkey on the tree is not a jnani.” When we identify a jiva as a jnani and then tell “his realization fetches him”, we are talking about the Self in the context of manifest upadhis. Likewise, when you say “jnani’s senses”, you mean a jiva (having body, mind, senses etc.) that has some special type of knowledge about its Self. All this is within vyavaharika standpoint.

  

That jiva has cognition of duality (like everyone else, minus their ignorant adhyasa) even while it has paramarthika knowledge of non-dual Reality.

  

Now we can say “that jiva is no longer jiva from the standpoint of its knowledge”. That is true. However, so long as we identify a “jnani” in terms of body-mind and distinguish him from ajnanis, the Self identified in those upadhis has in that context both cognition of duality and the knowledge “aham brahmaasmi” etc. That is still in vyavaharika even if it be called the standpoint of the jnani.

  

This is nothing to frown about, because the jagat duality is mithya and not asat to begin with. It need not vanish in that standpoint where the jnani is distinctly identified. His knowledge of paramartha brahman (beyond all attribute, viseshana, duality) translates to the vyavaharika standpoint as “sarvam kalu idam brahma”, “tat tvam asi” and so on.

  

*Quote*

*  *

*What is this some part created and some other part not created by Ishwara?? Have I said anything like this?? If you are holding Ishwara srushti and jeeva srushti to argue this part business in creation then I am afraid you have not understood what jeeva srushti mean and what Ishwara srushti actually is there. Anyway, once again for the convenience for the avidyAvanta jeeva this jagat appears as apUrNa, asarvaM, abrahmam due to his anAdiranantO naisargikOdhyAsaH like seeing the snake in place of rope (avidyAkalpita) But Ishwara srushti, jagat is not like that it is brahman only. See mundaka upanishat 2.2.11.
brahmaivedaM amrutaM purastAt.*

*  *

*Unquote*

  

Anything cognized is srishti of the Lord/Self and He alone is the cognizer. Rope in world, snake in jiva mind or thought of rope being snake (i.e. adhyasa in jiva mind) - all are part of the ‘world’ for which Brahman is the ‘second-less’ cause. The ajnani, who regards ishvara-jiva bheda to be real, can call something jiva kalpana and something else Ishvara srishti, can say one thing is due to (or indicative of) avidya and another due to Maya. But for the jnani, it is the Self+Maya that projects/manifests/appears/imagines ALL ‘srishti’ - both what appears in the sense world and what plays out in the Jiva’s mind. This is the import of the cited Mandukya karika verses and others that follow them, as I understand.

  

The difference between jiva kalpana and Ishvara srishti is that jiva kalpana (the adhyasa part) gets eliminated within the vyavaharika standpoint itself when the jiva attains jnana. The jnani’s body-mind upadhis are cleared of the ajnana based identification. Ishvara srishti (jagat) however remains as an object of cognition because it is satya in the vyavaharika standpoint (and asat only in paramarthika), and the jnani in this standpoint knows it to be the projection/appearance/imagination of/by Self due to or in conjunction with Maya.

  

Now is it necessary for this jnani to say “No, no. It is not my kalpana. I am only a jiva. It is Ishvara srishti!!”??? The jnani can very well consider all cognition to be his own srishti (parikalpana) since He knows himself as the Self of All, including of “ishvara”. If He speaks in terms of this knowledge, like Krishna did, it can appear to the ajnani as if he as a mere jiva claims to be the cause for world. For the jnani however, the “jiva" and “ishvara” are simply nama-rupa denotations for Brahman(+Maya), himself. He can therefore say the jiva creates the world like you and I can go to a movie and say King-Kong jumped up that building - where KK denotes the Light that appears as-if the movie. He can also speak in 3rd person and say the All is imagination/projection/appearance of the non-dual Lord.
