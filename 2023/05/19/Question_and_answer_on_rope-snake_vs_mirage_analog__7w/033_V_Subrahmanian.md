When Bhashyakara says the creation by Brahman is buddhiparikalpita, he does not mean that there is nothing else apart from such an imagination: There does exist Brahman the source of such a creation.

  

In this verse of Manisha Panchakam Shankara says:

  

brahmaivāhamidaṃ jagacca sakalaṃ cinmātravistāritaṃ  
*sarvaṃ caitadavidyayā triguṇayā’śeṣaṃ mayā kalpitam \|*  
itthaṃ yasya dṛḍhā matiḥ sukhatare nitye pare nirmale  

cāṇḍālo’stu sa tu dvijo’stu gururityeṣā manīṣā mama \|\| 2 \|\|

  

 “I am Brahman (pure consciousness). It is pure consciousness that appears in this universe. *All this is only something conjured up by me because of avidya (nescience) which is composed of the three gunas (sattva, rajas and tamas)”.* One who has attained this definite realization about Brahman which is bliss itself, eternal, supreme and pure, is my Guru, whether he is an outcaste or a Brahmana.   

  

Shankara very clearly says that from the Jnani's point of view, the world is an imagination (of the Jnani/observer of the universe) caused by avidya that is of three guna-s. This is exactly what Shankara means by *buddhiparikalpitena*.

  

Even Sri SSS who has translated this text says that the Jnani sees the world that is adhyaropa; he does not say that it is physically 'there':



[https://adhyatmaprakasha.org/php/bookreader/templates/book.php?type=kannada&book_id=077B&pagenum=2b0007#page/8/mode/1up](https://adhyatmaprakasha.org/php/bookreader/templates/book.php?type=kannada&book_id=077B&pagenum=2b0007#page/8/mode/1up)  

  

In the Brihadaranyaka 1.4.10 bhashya, Shankara while discussing the locus of Avidya, finally says: we have to accept this Avidya to be of Brahman alone since avidya requires a chetana, sentient, entity for it to be located. There is no chetana entity other than Brahman, Shankara reasons. This is because the mantra itself says: ब्रह्म वा इदमग्र आसीत् ....Even before Brahman realized itself as Brahman, it was Brahman in truth. Thus the Upanishad itself is providing the ground for the thesis: Brahman is the locus of avidya.

  

Hence Shankara is consistent in admitting avidya to Brahman, the sentient.
