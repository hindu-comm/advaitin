Namaskaram Bhaskar-ji,

  

Some thoughts and counter-opinions below. You can give your side on it. I am going to try and take a break unless I have something new to offer to the discussion or am otherwise impelled to continue. Have given my basicunderstanding or way of thinking on these topics already.

  

Quote

  

Ø   *What is mithyA and what is anirvachaneeya here that we have to clarify first. Seeing the snake in rope is mithyA, seeing the dviteeya Chandra in place of one Chandra is mithyA. In short, it is that which will be realized as not existent by the means of right knowledge is ‘mithyA’. Snake is mithya / not existent after the realization of right knowledge i.e. rope. But as you agreed jagat ‘as such’ is equally seen by both jnAni and ajnAni as well. Hence it is said seeing the ‘snake’ in rope is karaNa dOsha whereas jagat (rope) is Ishwara srushti.
Therefore it is wrong to say that jagat in front of us which is available for our (both jnAni and ajnAni) transaction is ‘mithya’ like snake on the rope.*

  

Unquote

  

From the standpoint of vyavaharika, prathibasika is mithya. From the standpoint of knowledge of paramarthika, both vyavaharika (~ seen is jagat) and prathibasika (~ seen is jiva kalpana) are known as mithya - because all such duality based on the seer-seen divide are negated/non-existent (like the snake) in the absolute non-dual standpoint of Brahman. The seer-seen divide is not a real viseshanaof Brahman (pure Consciousness); such a dualistic division in Consciousness can best be known to the ‘seer’ as the projection or imagination of that Consciousness itself, due to or in conjunction with Maya-shakti. The viseshana that characterizes Brahman as jagat (both vyavaharika and prathibasika), the viseshana that exists only in the context of the seer-seen divide *imagined* on/by Brahman, is not intrinsic reality of Brahman nor is it absolutely unreal (for it is imagined “by the Lord”). It is therefore mithya.

  

Quote

  

*Hence shankara said mAya is anirvachaneeya but avidyA is nirvachaneeya and it has been explained as agrahaNa, anyathaagrahaNa, saMshaya etc. And it can be noted that after the dawn of jnana this anirvachaneeyatvaM goes for the jnAni and realizes that jagat is nothing but brahman and there exists nothing but brahman. Hence strictly speaking it is NOT brahman + visheshaNa, it is brahman’s visheshaNa which he himself appearing as jagat. *

  

Unquote

  

This is a valid point. If Brahman had such viseshana as its reality and that is the only right knowledge of Brahman, then we have to assume Brahman’s visheshana as satya and not mithya. Recall however I was commenting on your statement: “Yes for him jagat is brahman (himself) but he (brahman) is not jagat, **he is himself without any visheshaNa. i.e. he is always nitya, shuddha, buddha, mukta svarUpa Atman.**”

  

So, now you are saying this “nitya shuddha … “ Brahman “without any visheshana” has the visheshana that he himself appears as jagat.

  

For me, this is a contradiction. You are mixing the paramarthika standpoint and the vyavaharika standpoint. Brahman is known within the vyavaharika standpoint as having the visheshana called Maya-shakti by which he appears as jagat etc. whereas the paramarthika standpoint of Brahman has no such association with or as one with a power that makes the non-dual appear dual. Why not? Because Consciousness/Self is undivided in paramarthika whereas it begins with the division (of seer-seen, dreamer-dream) in vyavaharika. All talk of Maya, jagat, and Maya/jagat being non-different from Brahman, of sarvam and then sarvam kalu idam brahma - all of that is in vyavaharika only.

  

Quote

  

*As I said above the word mithyA does not suit to the jagat for which brahman is the upAdAna and nimitta kAraNa.*

  

Unquote

  

Here I would make the point that brahman is upadana and nimitta karana for both the jagat you speak of and of the jagat of the Jiva’s kalpana.

  

Quote

  

*Shankara gives example of chArudatta and insists HE IS Charudatta only nothing else when he is folding limbs or sometimes stretching his limbs.*

  

Unquote

  

Yes. All are varying nama-rupa denotations of the same reality. However, note that even when you say these things, you are affirming the variations in your awareness of the nama-rupa appearance of that Charudatta. This duality in appearance is a reality of the vyavaharika standpoint.

  

Quote

  

*And please note when we are saying jagat is brahman’s vishesha darshana we are not saying there is a ‘gap’ in seeing pot, jug, plate of clay. We are saying pot, jug, plate the ‘socalled’ ‘gap’ among nAma rUpa too brahman only (mrut sAmAnya only) So visheshaNa does not mean there is somewhere nirvikAri nirvishesha brahman who is appearing as ‘visheshaNa’ in jagat. The ultimatum of kArya-kAraNa ananyatvaM is there is no vishesha as such ‘apart’ from brahman and brahman in ALL pervasive and ALL inclusive.*

  

Unquote

  

I am not saying there is a gap. Everything is brahman. The ‘everything” includes the nama-rupa identifiers. Pot is brahman, water is brahman. The form of pot is brahman, the notion of shape, the word “pot” is brahman, etc. Like everything whatsoever that appears in a movie is nothing but Light. All this we agree with. But the paramarthika standpoint of this knowledge/reality has no place for separate identifications such as pot, water, and so on. We would not be saying “jnani also sees jagat. Jagat is only brahman” and so on, unless we are affirming simultaneously a duality in appearance and cognition, where we know pot and water are brahman but appear as different manifestations of brahman.

  

The duality in appearance, of seer-seen divide, of jagat, all belong to the lower vyavaharika standpoint (status: “imagination”) where only such visheshana can even be objects for cognition and discussion. In the higher paramarthika standpoint the reality alone stands affirmed and all this duality (“jagat visheshana of brahman”) is non-existent.

  

No one says the nirvikari nirvishesha brahman appears as visheshana in jagat. When we speak of jagat, Brahman is always known as Brahman+Maya that appears as jagat and hence the karana. Yes the jnani knows karana-karya ananyatvam and hence knows of the paramarthika standpoint of Self (nirguna etc.); but he himself is in the vyavaharika seer-seen standpoint where he does see duality of jagat etc. The paramarthika standpoint is not to be regarded as an avoiding of the “fuller” knowledge of vyavaharika but that it is the higher, satya standpoint of Brahman - that it is nirguna, nirvishesha etc.

  

Quote

  

*Now the question is how the jnAni sees the world!!?? Is the jagat for the jnAni mithyA darshana like snake in the rope or satya’s vishesha darshana like seeing the pot holding the clay jnana. That would determine the nature of jagat in the drushti of the jnAni. And shruti /bhAshya explains jnAni would have samyak darshana, Atmaikatva darshana, kArya-kAraNa ananyatva darshana, bedha buddhi rahita ekatva darshana.
And in this he would not separate himself and say I am Atman and what I am seeing is mithyA and I am sAkshi to this mithyA darshana!!?? That would again duality and this dvaita bhAva conspicuous by its absence in Atmaikatva darshana.*

**

Unquote

  

Consider the question. Two different entities “jnani” and “world” are identified and then their relation asked. That places us squarely in vyavaharika standpoint. This is analogous to the question, “How does King-Kong see the building?” In the movie. Its an absurd gibberish question from the standpoint of the non-dual Light. There is only Light, no King-Kong, appearance or whatever. But for the KK in the movie, there are two possible answers:

  

1.  The building is made of bricks and I can climb on it 2.  The building and myself are only the Light that I am, appearing so.

  

If you talk of KK and building and ask how KK sees the building, then don’t complain at the “appearing so” in the end of the second answer. The duality in appearance of KK and building, *as if* distinct entities, remains in the cognition of the KK in the movie even if it has full awareness that all is Light and Light is all. But that said, the whole rigamarole is vivarta mithya nonsense from the standpoint of that paramarthika knowledge itself, because when Light is the non-dual reality, wherefore this question of what KK thought of the building? It makes sense only in the movie for KK who knows the truth and for his friends who don’t. That is fine. The movie is the vyavaharika standpoint and it has an honest answer (the second one), realizing which KK is liberated "even while living" (i.e. within vyavaharika).
