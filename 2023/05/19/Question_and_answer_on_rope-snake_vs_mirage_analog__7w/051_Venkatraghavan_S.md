Namaste Subbuji

  

The term charama vRtti refers to AtmasAkshAtkAra and not the last thought before death of the body. In the beginning of this chapter, the siddhikAra had defined avidyAnivRtti asचरमवृत्त्युपलक्षितस्यात्मनोऽज्ञानहानिरूपत्वात् - ajnAna hAni (=avidyAnivRtti) is the Atma which is upalakshita by the charamavRtti. The finality denoted by the term 'charama' refers to there being no concept of time upon the cessation of avidyA through Atma sAkshAtkAra, not to the final thought before death.

  

The reason I believe so is for the following:

  

In the course of arguing against this definition, the opponent saysन च वृत्त्युपलक्षित आत्मा जीवन्मुक्तावप्यस्तीति तदापि मोक्षापत्तिः; - the opponent is saying, if the advaitin defines avidyAnivRtti as charamavRtti upalakshita Atma, that exists even in jIvanmukti, which will mean that jIvanmukti ends up being moksha too.

  

The siddhikAra does not deny the opponent's contention that the charamavRtti refers to AtmasAkshAtkAra. He does not say that the charamavRtti instead refers to the final thought before death. Nor does he say jIvanmukti is not moksha.

  

Rather, he concedes the assumption behind the opponent's question (that charamavRtti in fact refers to the sAkshAtkAra enjoyed by the jivanmukta), and says (जीवन्मुक्तौ)मुक्तिमात्रापादनस्येष्टत्वात् - it is desirable to us to have such an implication - that jIvanmukti is moksha too.

  

If that is the case, how is videhamukti also mukti (if the first AtmasAkshAtkAra upalakshita Atma is mukti, what about the Atma after the body is dropped - is that charamavRtti upalakshita too)?

  

He then says, if there is no problem with jIvanmukti being mukti, there is certainly no problem with videhamukti being mukti, because the charamavRtti upalakshitatvam for the Atma is equally applicable to videhamukti also. परममुक्तेश्चरमसाक्षात्कारोपलक्षितात्मस्वरूपत्वेन तदापादकाभावात् - ie this is not a defect forparamamukti, ie videhamukti, because the Atma at the end of a jnAni's death is also charama (tattva) sAkshAtkAra upalakshita only - once he gets the jnAna he is a jnAni.

  

To explain, if a crow was a upalakshaNa for Devadatta's house today, ie if I have identified Devadatta's house by recollecting the crow I saw there earlier today, that is sufficient for me to identify the house as Devadatta's house tomorrow also. There is no need to postulate a requirement for me to see the crow flying from his tomorrow also, in order for me to identify Devadatta's house tomorrow. The upalakshitatvam stays the same, whether today or tomorrow. The same applies for jIvanmukti (JM) and videhamukti (VM) then.

  

If there is no difference between JM and VM on account of the upalakshitatvam, how to account for the difference between JM and VM in general? The opponent says because the advaitin's scheme would imply no difference between the two, in order to create a difference, why don't you instead say that the Atma upalakshitatvam refers to upalakshitatvam by the last second of life or by the last life breath, be mukti? (जीवन्मुक्तिप्रयोजकवृत्त्यपेक्षया परममुक्तिप्रयोजकवृत्तौ आनन्दाभिव्यक्तिगतविशेषाभावे चरमक्षणेन चरमश्वासेन वा उपलक्षित आत्मा मुक्तिरिति किं न स्यादिति).

  

To which the siddhikAra says, the difference is the presence of vikshepa due to prArabdha in the former and the absence of vikshepa in the latter (प्रारब्धकर्मप्रयुक्तविक्षेपाविक्षेपाभ्यामभिव्यक्तिविशेषस्याङ्गीकारात्). Pls note there is a missing आ-कार in विक्षेपाविक्षेपाभ्याम् in advaitashArada.

  

The bhAShyakAra too admits to this much difference in the sUtrabhAShya.

  

I concede that the usage of the term charama vRtti may ve confusing and can lead one to think that there is a requirement for a final sAkshatkAravRtti before death in the siddhikAra's scheme, but I don't think that is the case. It wouldn't make logical sense.

  

Coming back to the original contention, I continue to believe there is no difference of substance between the siddhikAra and bhAShyakAra.

  

Regards

Venkatraghavan
