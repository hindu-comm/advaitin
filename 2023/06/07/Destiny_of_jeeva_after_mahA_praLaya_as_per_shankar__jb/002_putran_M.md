I do not debate about Ishvara being the only cause of srishti. This issue is more about who is Ishvara and what is srishti. There is the (lower) vyavaharika standpoint (based in ignorance) in which there is a jiva and there is a corresponding Ishvara in relation to the jiva - with their roles being different (one is karmi, doer of adhyasa; other is phaladaata, cause of srishti, etc.). The (higher) vyavaharika standpoint based in knowledge of the non-dual Self is that there is naught else but Ishvara and so everything that becomes an object of dualistic identification (seer, seen, self, not-self, whatever you can objectify by thought or word) is imagination/appearance/denotation of that Ishvara (Brahman+Maya). Therefore He is the second less upadana-nimitta karana for all jiva-jagat-ishvara manifest srishti and everything whatsoever identified within it. All this talk of jiva, its ajnana/adhyasa/imagination, “due to avidya”, then of a different ishvara, ishvara srishti “due to Maya” - ALL of it is Him appearing as-if. Srishti is of Isvhara and not jiva because jiva is itself an apparent projection of that Ishvara, a part of that srishti that is His imagination. The jiva-ishvara-jagat bheda of the lower vyavaharika standpoint is Mithya: it is perceived as-if existent and real but known by the jnani to be an apparition. The jnani therefore ‘sees’ the Self in All and All as appearance of Self.

  

In the paramarthika standpoint of Brahman, the duality even as an appearance is non-existent. The transcendental Self is beyond the reach of words and thoughts. If we try and describe it in terms derived from our vyavaharika standpoint, we will either say things that don’t make sense (like barren woman’s son) or we will “best-fit” the absolute non-dual truth into our relative experience and come out with a conditioned knowledge that is truth in vyavaharika and yet is not the paramarthika satya. We think we are describing the Sun as it is when in fact we are describing a reflection in the pot of our mind. [https://m.facebook.com/knowswamiji/photos/a.351920988226526/1058968167521801/](https://m.facebook.com/knowswamiji/photos/a.351920988226526/1058968167521801/) The clay-pot analogy is verily of this kind where in affirming the non-duality of clay we must implicitly accept an essential property of clay to make itself appear in forms.Similarly if the dream jiva relates to the non-dual nirguna Brahman, the pure Consciousness that contradicts its experience of division in Consciousness, it will have to project that non-dual tattva within its duality-affirming standpoint and the end-reflection is Ishvara. Not at all asat but mithya with reference to the paramarthika standpoint.

  

This conditioning of the non-dual reality is precisely what the higher vyavaharika standpoint of knowledge is, where all the duality and causality imagined in jiva-jagat is reduced to a simple duality/causality of Brahman in conjunction with Maya (i.e. some power that can make-appear a manifold world). This is not about shakti and shaktimaandenoting the same Reality but that the description forces a duality in that Reality of upadana + nimitta, cause & effect, where the effect of many remains affirmed even while the substratum cause is non-dual. The dreamer-dream duality in Brahman is affirmed because the dream is cognized and the non-dual Self of that dream is also realized as truth - thus the Self is the Dreamer who appears as if the dream. And from there we say something that is gibberish in this dream standpoint - the paramarthika satya - that no, no, even the dreamer-dream duality is superimposition/imagination/projection on the non-dual substratum Self in which standpoint such a divided description is non-existent/incoherent/meaningless (like barren woman’s son). It is logically untenable as it leads to infinite regress (for now the Self is Dreamer of that Dreamer-dream duality and so on. Or we simply say the ishvara-jiva-jagat appearance is a fact of Maya rather than trying to link Maya with paramartha Brahman and calling Him Ishvara of the appearance.) That is why the paramarthika standpoint is beyond words and thought if you try and grasp it through words and thoughts founded in duality, you are back to dreamer-dream, seer-seen, Brahman-Maya, Ishvara-jagat. It is our best-fit description but the realization transcends such simple duality even.

  

Now, personally, I don't mind accepting the higher vyavaharika standpoint as the final reality. Naturally it is more aligned and relatable given our identification as jivas. If we can still establish the necessary separation from the dvaita schools and ward off their objections, then we likelywould classify as advaita and represent correctly (most if not all) the core advaitic statements of the sruthi and bhashya. Trouble comes when you insist on proving the traditional interpretations (jagat mithya etc.) as wrong or deviant from Shankara. I don't know how correctly the above represents the traditional understanding but it is my resolution of this conflict where if necessary one can also work with the traditional nomenclature and stronger assertions regarding brahma satya, jagan mithya etc.
