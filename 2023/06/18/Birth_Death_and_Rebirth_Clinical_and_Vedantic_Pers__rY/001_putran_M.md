Namaskaram,

  

Very nice presentation.

  

Lecture is generally about karma theory that Prof K. Subrahmanian-jipresents as a hypothesis that provides a satisfactory explanation for observation. I think from the standpointof pramanas, this is called "arthapatti" (postulation) which is regarded as a valid means of knowledge in suitable situations. However I don't think it equates to just another hypothesis that can explain something beyond direct perception. The conditions that define the event or object we wish to explain have to be such that there can be only one satisfactory "hypothesis" that answers the questions involved. If we can frame 10 possible hypotheses that will work, then our picking one of them arbitrarily will not constitute arthapatti - because arthapatti has to result in 'knowledge' when applied properly where it is applicable and not in speculation. He gets into 'trouble' in this sort of presentation when a questioner (1:27:00) challenges him at the end about "probability theory" being another suitable hypothesis. There was not enough time to have a deep discussion on this, but the swami may have given a brief rejoinder in his ending comments. Anyway the question from the purvapakshin gives the astika the opportunity to contemplate and rethink the basis of his positions.
