\[Quote From the wikipedia\]

The notion of avidyā and its relationship to Brahman creates a crucial philosophical issue within Advaita Vedānta thought: how can avidyā appear in Brahman, since Brahman is pure consciousness?^([\[170\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta#cite_note-211))For Shankara,*avidya*is a perceptual or psychological error.^([\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMayeda199278-121))According to Satchidanandendra Saraswati, for Shankara "*avidya*is only a technical name to denote the natural tendency of the human mind that is engaged in the act of superimposition."^([\[171\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurthi2009160-212))The later tradition diverged from Shankara by turning*avidya*into a metaphysical principle, namely*mulavidya*or "root ignorance," a metaphysical substance which is the "primal material cause of the universe (*upadana*)," thereby setting aside Shankara's 'Unevolved Name-and-Form' as the explanation for the existence of materiality.^([\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMayeda199278-121)[\[172\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurthi2009149-213))According to Mayeda, "\[i\]n order to save monism, they characterized*avidya*as indefinable as real or unreal (*sadasadbhyam anirvacanya*), belonging neither to the category of being nor to that of non-being."^([\[96\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMayeda199278-121))In the 20th century, this theory of*mulavidya*became a point of strong contention among Advaita Vedantins, with[Satchidanandendra Saraswati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satchidanandendra_Saraswati "Satchidanandendra Saraswati")arguing that Padmapada and Prakasatman had misconstrued Shanakara's stance.^([\[173\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurthi2009150-214))

Shankara did not give a 'location' of*avidya*, giving precedence to the removal of ignorance.^([\[174\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDoherty2005209-210-215)[\[note 34\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta#cite_note-216))Sengaku Mayeda writes, in his commentary and translation of[Adi Shankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adi_Shankara "Adi
        Shankara")'s Upadesasahasri:

> Certainly the most crucial problem which Sankara left for his
> followers is that of avidyā. If the concept is logically analysed, it
> would lead the Vedanta philosophy toward dualism or nihilism and
> uproot its fundamental
> position.^([\[175\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMayeda199282-217))

The later Advaita-tradition diverged from Shankara, trying to determinate a locus of*avidya*,^([\[176\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPotter20067-8-218))with the Bhamati-school locating*avidya*in the*jiva*c.q.*prakriti*, while the Vivarana-school locates it in Brahman.^([\[177\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMurthi2009155%E2%80%93156-219)[\[176\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advaita_Vedanta#cite_note-FOOTNOTEPotter20067-8-218)  
)

> > In the Bhashyam for the mantra 7: naantaHprajnam, etc. Shankara
> > says: the three states of waking, dream and sleep, that formed the
> > subject matter of the first six mantras, is unreal, apAramArthikam,
> > product of avidya, like the rope-snake, and is of the nature of
> > seed-sprout (seed (cause) = deep sleep, and the sprout (effect) are
> > the waking and dream. Shankara holds all the three, including the
> > sleep state, to be endowed with Avidya (tattva agrahanam):
> >
> >   
> >
> > तस्यापरमार्थरूपमविद्याकृतं रज्जुसर्पादिसममुक्तं पादत्रयलक्षणं बीजाङ्कुरस्थानीयम्।
> > अथेदानीमबीजात्मकं परमार्थस्वरूपं रज्जुस्थानीयं सर्पादिस्थानीयोक्तस्थानत्रयनिराकरणेनाह
> > — नान्तःप्रज्ञमित्यादिना।
> >
> >   
> >
> > Contrasting with the three states (which is actually the entire
> > samsara anubhava, the jagat), the Turiya, Chaturtham, is
> > paaramaarthika, akin to the rope, which is taught as negating the
> > snake-like pAda traya.
> >
> >   
> >
> > In that very bhashya, while the mantra negates the sushupti state:
> > na prajnAnaghanam, Shankara says: the state of sushupti is akin to
> > seed, devoid of discrimination, aviveka svarupa (in the waking and
> > dream the distinctions, vivikta, are perceived but not in deep
> > sleep):   
> >
> >   
> >
> > नप्रज्ञानघनमिति सुषुप्तावस्थाप्रतिषेधः, बीजभावाविवेकस्वरूपत्वात्
> >
> >   
> >
> > Thus, in the above statements, Shankara accepts avidya in deep
