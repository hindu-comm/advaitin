Namaskaram,

  

Yes, if we understand manas generally, it includes the buddhi. But the word intelligence ultimately connotes the manifesting/knowing nature of Consciousness. So it can be distinguished from the more jada associations in the mind.

  

Katha Upanishad I.iii.10: The sense-objects are higher than the sense, and the mind is higher than the sense-objects; but the intellect is higher than the mind, and the Great Soul is higher than the intellect.

  

Sh.Bh: "...By the word manah is indicated the elements in their rudimentary subtle form (tanmaatras) which are the material cause of the mind, for they are the originators of volition and conjecture. As compared with even the mind, the intellect is higher - subtler, more pervasive, and is their inner self. By the word buddhih, is denoted the tanmaatraswhich are the source of determination etc. As compared with the intellect, the Great Soul (is higher); it is atma, because it is the innermost principle of the intelligence of all beings, and it is great, because it is the most pervasive of all. The principle called Hiranyagarbha, which was born before all, from the Unmanifested (Maya), and which consists of both intelligence and activity, is called the Great Soul that is higher than the intellect."

  

I wrote the below some years back, on the way I understood the chariot analogy. (Here by buddhi, manas etc. is meant the Self identified in its manifestation as buddhi, mind etc.) Its for general picture only, take if it helps.

  

Quote

  

This Life isDharmaKshetram, the battlefield where we fight to upholdsvadharmaagainst forces determined to make us forsake it  

  

Svadharmais The Law/Nature of your being, and Being is The Self identified with limitations

  

The Limitations are body (deha) \<-\>chariot; mind (manas) \<-\> horses; intellect (buddhi) \<-\> charioteer; and World (jagat) \<-\> path of travel.

  

Generally, the body has itsdharma, and so long as the mind is deeply corresponding to it and the buddhi also identifies Self with it, thedharmaof the buddhi is to have strong shraddha (~faith rooted in experience) 1. in its own capacity as the determining faculty, 2. in the body'ssvadharmaand 3. to ensure that the mind acts on the body and reacts in accordance with thatdharma. 

  

The mind'ssvadharmais to listen to the buddhi; but in general it is a fickle entity. So the world will tend to affect the body, which in turn will impact the mind. It is the great responsibility of the buddhi that it should not forsake the shraddha inDharmaof the total being on the basis of how the world pushes the body or how the mind tends to react. It has to remain the uncompromising axle no matter the opposition, and even if the body-mind becomes crippled in the process due to momentary mistakes in judgment of the buddhi, the buddhi has to keep fighting on. Because it is the ultimate deciding faculty in the individual, and all powers of change operate through it.

  

...

  

Now, there is another level of operation, where the buddhi must transcend operating in terms of body-mind. When its shraddha that Self is body becomes fundamentally challenged, then the path of travel changes. (We know here that this challenge is a good one, accepted by Veda; hence when it happens, we should not fight against but try to see if we can align with it.) Thedharmaof one's being then shifts from the body level and turns inward. At this point, the fight also must change. The world will still impact the body and the body impact the mind. But the buddhi's fight is no longer merely a fight fordharmaat this level; it is in conjunction with that higher level of awareness which transcends that level. At that stage, everything becomes interesting. We want to work first towards getting there.

  

Unquote
