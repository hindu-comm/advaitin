I agree that it is a valuable reference for both but an indispensable guide for the teacher more than anything else. To support this position, I lean on the evidence for the method of teaching unfolded in the Upadeshasahari, in which Shankara suggests nowhere that one should study the Bhashya-s to know the Self. Other similar, but less dense, texts such as the Vakyavrtti and Tattvopadesha also support the view, though implicitly, that the Bhashya-s are not necessary to know the Self.



Ø I agree with this. bhAshya study is not mandatory for the self realization. When shAstra said shwetaketu realized through uddaalaka by tattvamasi vAkya, when arjuna realized through geetopadesha by Lord etc. the role of shAstra bhAshya/commentary hardly mentioned. So a brahmanishTa jnAni or a vEdavida shrOtreeya can directly give the upadesha of the shAstra vAkya to his student. If the student is capable enough to grasp this it is well and good for the shAstra vAkya janita jnana. But, if mere shravaNa is not enough then that student needs mananaM of the shAstra vAkya and here comes the help of bhAshya to do manana in an effective sAmpradAyik way. Here the guru, sampradaya teacher, who is also erstwhile shishya who did the mananam with his guru through bhAshya, takes the help of sampradaya paramAchArya bhAshya / explanation / commertaries to explain the intricacies of shAstra to his shishya within the boundaries of sampradaya. So mananaM based on shAstra vAkya and shAstra vAkya vimarsha based on brahmavAdins observations (here in our tradition shankara bhAshya) is not completely out of scope in Advaita jnana pradhAna sAdhana. Atleast this is how I see the importance of bhAshya in guru-shishya sampradaya, upadesha and sAdhana.
