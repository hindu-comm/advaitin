\> There is no such rule that can be proven and has been considered as a pUrvapakSha in many bhASha-TIkA contexts. Since it is based on pure tarka, the counterexample to this is tArkika's prAgabhAva which is beginningless but has an end, that is ghaTaprAgabhAva is anAdi but antavat on production of ghaTa.  
  

\> Why should one examine what is not there? The very reason it can be inquired into is because it has some difference from non-existent/ asat. That is why shAstra says that vichAraM na sahate, it doesn't stand scrutiny/ inquiry.  

  

One can examine what is not there, because of thisprAgabhAva. You know the meaning ofprAgabhAva, right? That which does not exist but might be yet. "Prag" "abhava", meaning prior not-exists but seem to exist due to effects.

Like Rope and Snake. Like Silver and mother-of-pearl. Like Water in Mirage.

Can we say that Snake exists in Rope? or Silver in mother-of-pearl? or Water in Mirage?

It may look like exist, but upon examination, it is found to be nonexistent even beginning/prior. So, even if one really does not exist before, the appearance can be examined/questioned.

  

Actually, in my understanding, we think/feel/seem to be Ignorance and Bondage and Moksha exists, and so say 'aroused' or 'beginningless' and comes to an end due to 'moksha', but upon examination of Ignorance like examining Snake, it ends up in "Knowledge of Atman" - like ends up in Rope.

Which is Ignorant/Bondage/attain Moksha? Atma/Brahman? Atman is ever free and never can be in bondage - so no topic of Moksha to it. Not that it is Paramarthika, even in Vyavaharika there is no Bondage/Moksha, as Atman remains only as Witness everywhere in all beings.
