Namaskaram,

  

Perhaps the Space vs pot-space analogy is useful for understanding this. In advaita, any sort of pot-space identification is only of Space identified with upadhis. From that standpoint of identification, there is a duality of individual pot-spaces and distinctly of the all-pervading Space as well. The individual jivatma and the supreme paramatma (Ishvara) are only non-dual Brahman (atma) identified differently in the upadhi-based vyavaharika standpoint. Such dualistic identifications are not untruth in that standpoint but become known as mithya when sublated in a higher standpoint. For example, that a person has a "mother" and "this is her" will be true in the standpoint of body identification and yet the relationship will not have validity in the standpoint of the atma identified with the (transmigrating) subtle body. The bheda of this kind is accepted at one level and negated at another. Ishvara Himself can be understood in different ways in contrast to the jiva's identification with 'its' upadhis. Sri Krishna is guiding the jiva that sees Him and others as different atmas to the knowledge that He (Self) is the non-dual ksetrajna(Space) appearing as if different (pot-spaces) in the kshetras. In the paramarthika standpoint, all such divisions superimposed on Self are absent with reference to Brahman.
