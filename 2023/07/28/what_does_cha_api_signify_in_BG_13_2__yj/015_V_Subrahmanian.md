I think if Bhagavan is to be taken as Kshetra too, then that would go against the seminal theme and teaching of this chapter: to see kasetram as anaatmaa and claim the Kshetrajna alone as the only real Tattva and oneself. The entire chapter positions the two as opposed to each other. Just like the opeisentece of the adhyasta bhashya.

  

Also the last verse in this chapter says explicitly as per the bhashya also that the kshetram is to be rendered mithya, abhavagamanam, once the discrimination between the Kshetra and the Kshetrajna is accomplished.

  

In fact this last verse is a mirror verse of BG 2.16 where too the clear distinction between the satyam and mithya is done. So how could Bhagavan teach that he is to be understood as the kshetram as well?

  

At the most we will have to say in that interpretation that Brahman is kshetram on baadhaayaam saamaanaadhikaranyam.
