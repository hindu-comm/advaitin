That is fine.

  

We can also accept that the same verse can have multiple levels of meaning and apply sensibly for the different standpoints, of knowledge and ignorance.

  

If the ajnani considers himself to be a jivatma (having identified self with the subtle body), then 13.1 affirms to him that he is a knower of the ksetram and 13.2 teaches that Ishvara (who is different from him) is also a knower, who is present in all the fields.

  

The advaitin need not take a stance that such a viewpoint is absolutely false. It is vyavaharika satyam. But for the jnani, those very words of Krishna are consistent with and pointing to the deeper advaita tattva regarding the non-dual Self. The significanceof Acharya's bhashya comes to the fore in this regard because it illumines the inner real import of Krishna's teachings, that can otherwise easily be missed by the rest of us (who are entrenched in jiva-bhava in general).

  

An advaitin can also translate 13.2 like this: "The (non-dual) Field-Knower in all the fields \[is manifest/appears as you (when identified with body-mind kshetra, as the jiva or karmi) *and*\] is *also* Me (when identified with Maya, as the Lord or karmaphaladaata)".
