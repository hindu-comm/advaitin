I decided to write down some thoughts on myunderstanding. It will not convince you even remotely and may be convoluted, but best I could do for now.

  

1\. Pratyaksha reveals the same bheda-darshana for both jnani and ajnani. The knowledge of jagat as obtained through pratyaksha is Truth in the pratyaksha standpoint; however it can get sublated in the higher or more comprehensive knowledge obtained via another pramana. So thebheda-filled jagat that both the ajnani and the jnani*see* is *known* by the jnani as (nama-rupa appearance of) Ishvara. Note: the jnani does not become incapable of discerning what pratyaksha reveals as jagat; that apparentexistence is an object of his knowledge as well. Because he knows exactly the limits of that knowledge in that standpoint, he can interact with ajnanis at their level; and yet his own knowledge of Brahman allows him to see Jagat in the non-dual light.

  

The iron door and the wood chair are different in substance and qualities in so far as "substance" and "qualities" are defined in the context of pratyaksha standpoint. However we realize that the duality shown by pratyaksha is only manifest appearance when we know of substance in the deeper substratum sense. In fact this duality is realized as being non-existent in the substratum standpoint. Thus the bheda darshana is realized as being mithya (not asat nor sat) in the light of abheda jnana.

  

2\. Those who espouse "mithya" are also aligned to the mulaavidya= maya position. For them, ultimately there is no fundamental difference like maya srishti vs avidya srishti. Whatever is seen is Ishvara darshana (or His appearance/projection via maya=avidya). Jagat in any standpoint is sat in the context of that standpoint, an appearance of Self in the context of identification with corresponding upadhis - sat when known properly via the pramanas relevant to that standpoint. (Of course, it can also be known incorrectly due to ajnana (standpoint of ajnani) - like seeing snake for rope is ajnana in pratyaksha because it can be known correctly by shining light and removing obstruction within pratyaksha context itself). But this same jagat can be realized as being mithya whenone has knowledge of a higher or more comprehensive standpoint. In particular, the jnaniregards the Ishvara-srishti bheda and any name-form based jagat to be mithya in the light of that nondual knowledge of Brahman.

  

Paramarthika standpoint is beyond the realm or reach of words. If we try to talk about it, we will be affirming bheda darshana and abheda jnana and return to sarva-atma Ishvara (Brahman+Maya). Talking of "what does jnani see" etc belongs in vyavaharika. The jnani'sstandpoint is what I called the higher vyavaharika standpoint because it sublates jagat by his paramartha jnana as naughtelse than Ishvara, due to which he has sarvatmabhava etc. - though he himself is identified within vyavaharika only.

  

3\. Jagan mithya resolves in Brahma satyam where the duality basedjagat is realized as an appearance of/on non-dual Brahman - like the world of pot-spaces is non-dual Space appearing manifold.
