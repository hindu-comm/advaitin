Namaskaram,

  

A clarification.

  
  

>   
> Jagat in any standpoint is sat in the context of that standpoint, an
> appearance of Self in the context of identification with corresponding
> upadhis - sat when known properly via the pramanas relevant to that
> standpoint. (Of course, it can also be known incorrectly due to ajnana
> (standpoint of ajnani) - like seeing snake for rope is ajnana in
> pratyaksha because it can be known correctly by shining light and
> removing obstruction within pratyaksha context itself). But this same
> jagat can be realized as being mithya whenone has knowledge of a
> higher or more comprehensive standpoint. In particular, the
> jnaniregards the Ishvara-srishti bheda and any name-form based jagat
> to be mithya in the light of that nondual knowledge of Brahman.

  

Consider the rope-snake example.

  

It is true that in the vyavaharika standpoint the sarpatvam is completely asat in the rajju. The sarpa-imagination is a consequence of ajnana and adhyasa (of a prathibasika cognition in the mind onto the object cognized via pratyaksha). So it seems that thesarpa is sat in the "lower" pratibhasika standpoint and negated in the "higher" vyavaharika standpoint. Therefore it should be considered mithya.

  

But this does not equate to mithya in my understanding/usage of the term. In this example, we are not talking about two valid standpoints of knowledge, even if one be apparent (lower) and the other absolute (higher). Instead we are distinguishing false knowledge and true knowledge.

  

The sarpatvamattributed to the rope is not an appearance that is mithya (not categorizable as sat or asat). It is false knowledge regarding the rope. It is not something that is non-negatable in one valid standpoint (hence not asat) but negated/absent/non-existent in a higher standpoint (hence not sat). One who has knowledge of the rope knows clearly (whether he continues to see it as snake or not) that snakeness is asat and the ropesnake is negated absolutely for that jnani.

  

This is different from the case of jagat in general.

  

jagat is the object of cognition in the vyavaharika plane,revealed by valid pramanas such as pratyaksha. At the same time, jagat (or any framework of dualitic identification of Brahman) is asat in paramarthika standpoint where duality has no place whatsoever. Because of the dichotomy of two valid standpoints (the apparent and the real), we understand that jagat has the status of appearance, nama-rupa identification, or projection, imagination (of and by that very Consciousness, Ishvara, maya) - is mithya, anirvachaniya but in particular, NOT false knowledge or asat. It is Ishvara (Brahman+Maya) who appears as the All. The paramarthika standpoint does not absolutely negate the vyavaharika for the jnani in it because the duality is pre-affirmed in vyavaharika where only the Self is identified with upadhis. Yes he sees the All as Brahman and Brahman as All, but knows that in the higher standpoint, even such a verbal acknowledgement of Brahman vs a nama-rupa All and the consequent Ishvara-srishti/jagat duality is total nonsense.
