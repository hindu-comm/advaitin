This is true within the vyavaharika standpoint. Not when we talking of different standpoints of knowledge regarding the same entity and describing one from within the other.

  

Example: the hare-with-horn is true in the dream through pratyaksha for the dreamer. The dreamer is told of the waking state where the hare-with-horn is asat. For the waker, there is no such thing as hare-with-horn - it is gibberish,nonsense, non-existence. The knower in the dream-standpoint, he realizes that the hare-with-horn cognition in the dream is a nama-rupa imagination/appearance of the Self that is absolutely non-existent in the waking standpoint. That is his "waking up" within the context of the dream. The question of hare-with-horn is completely inapplicable in the waking standpoint. (Here the "waking standpoint" should not be understood as "jnani's", it refers to the paramarthika standpoint where the duality of dreamer vs hare-with-horn and the object hare-with-horn are equally asat. The jnani in the dream has paramartha jnana.)

  

The moment any pot is identified, it is the Self is identified with that upadhi of pot and that identification of Self is the pot-space. The standpoint of nondual Space has no place for pots and potspaces - nor for "there are pots-appearance but no potspaces". But in the standpoint of potspaces, it is possible to have knowledge of the standpoint of undivided Space: that jnana translates/projects to the potspace standpoint as sarvam Space, aham Space etc. This is not false knowledge; it is the true Knowledge of Reality in that standpoint. The paramartha jnani thus knows himself to be the All and All in him, but it does not mean the duality is lost to him (nor that pratyaksha gets falsified in its applicable standpoint): rather he says it is nama-rupa, appearance, mere speech, mithya, maya, anirvachaniya, when one seeks the answer for "How?" and try connecting the Space standpoint to the potspace standpoint. (If you don't like this, then we can also say "jnani merges in that Space standpoint in samadhi" - but I don't want to debate that again :)
