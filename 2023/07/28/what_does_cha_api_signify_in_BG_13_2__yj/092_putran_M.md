Above is a bit misleading. What I meant is that in vyavaharika, a person's seeing of snake in rope would be strictly a case of ajnana/adhyasa and not because rope-snake is mithya in this standpoint. Pratyaksha pramana can eliminate snake cognition.

  

If in spite of seeing the rope and knowing snake is not there in that place, an imagination of snake in place of rope persists due to vasanas, then perhaps we can say that snake is mithya object in his *mind standpoint* with reference to the actual object in question - it cannot be negated in the mind and yet the cognizer knows the snake attribution is asat in the world.

  

My thought: Admittedly the use of this rope-snake example for conveying concept of mithya has to be done carefully, otherwise people can start scrutinizing its usage out of its scope. Keep also in mind that the snake imagination can be said to also have a separate locus or substratum than the rope, at least in part (like memory or mind objects). Such is not the case with jagat: Brahman is the only substratum. So as with all analogies, there are bound to be limits in their being exact-match in this type of enquiry. We have to use them prudently.
