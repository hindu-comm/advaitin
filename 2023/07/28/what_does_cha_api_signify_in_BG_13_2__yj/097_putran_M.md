Whether one sees wave or foam, one sees and knows only water. Agreed. The point is that at the level of cognition, there are various identifications of water in the context of upadhis and a world of vyavahara continues based on that. That is pot-space identification of Space, it happens even while we have knowledge of "Space alone is".

  

At the level of cognition, does the dual identification of water as wave vs foam imply a semblance of division in water for the jnani? My understanding is No at the level of knowledge but yes at the level of cognition. He has knowledge of sarvam and the implicit duality therein, but at the same time knows only water appearing as wave and foam. A division is superimposed onto water by virtue of having distinct upadhi based identifications of it.

  

Now I don't mean to create horror by that last sentence. Look, this analogy is supposed to tell us how. We are all jnanis when we visit the Ocean. There is a semblance of division in water as here and there, as wave and foam, as big and small, as wave and Ocean, and so on. We know fully well whatever we see is only water but yet it appears divided and manifold in appearance, and we identify it variously in corresponding distinct nama-rupa terms ("mere speech"). The knowledge of duality and division at the level of cognition does not conflict with the knowledge of non-duality at the level of adhishtanam.

  

The jnani-wave belongs to the vyavaharika standpoint where such divisions and duality as seer and seen, Water and Ocean, Ocean and wave, wave and foam are not asat. He knows of the paramarthika "Brahman is the All" standpoint wherein all such division and duality is asat. Therefore he knows them to be not sat even while they appear real in his standpoint. All of that duality is realised as an appearance, mithya with reference to the higher standpoint.

  

I think of "Brahman is the All" as a reference to the paramarthika standpoint. A jnani can know of it but his knowledge in the vyavaharika standpoint where he is identified translates as "All is Brahman". The All is not negated absolutely but known as appearance - either due to Maya or of Ishvara = Brahman+Maya.
