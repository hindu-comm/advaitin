The usage of the expression "Brahman is the All" to denote or to point to nirguna brahman can be justified based on Brihadaranyaka Up. bhashya I.4.10. We know it is the truth, certainly the knowledge of the jnani "aham brahmaasmi" ~ "I am the All"; the question is whether the expression befits as a description of nirguna Brahman or the paramarthika standpoint.

  

My assimilation: In general, the word All is understood by delineationof parts or individual identities that together constitute the All. The world of Cognition (the All) has the distinguishing of wave and foam, the jnani-wave knows the all as expression or projection of Self and non-different from Self. Or there is scope for a duality of seer-seeing/doing-seen as three aspects of All. When we say "Brahman is the All", by putting Brahman up front the message is thatthe seer-seeing-seen triple is collapsed in the non-dual Brahman beyond all scope for dualistic identification (even as "mere speech").

  

Whether we like such a verbal expression as a sense-giving pointer to unconditioned Brahman may be dependent on how our minds are conditioned. And my mind oscillates on such things.

  

The bhashya for this verse is of independent interest with regard to our discussions. Others can read and understand.
