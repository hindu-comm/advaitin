Namaskaram,  
  
I try to explain my understanding based on an example.  

  

>   
>
> My assimilation: In general, the word All is understood by
> delineationof parts or individual identities that together constitute
> the All. The world of Cognition (the All) has the distinguishing of
> wave and foam, the jnani-wave knows the all as expression or
> projection of Self and non-different from Self. Or there is scope for
> a duality of seer-seeing/doing-seen as three aspects of All. When we
> say "Brahman is the All", by putting Brahman up front the message is
> thatthe seer-seeing-seen triple is collapsed in the non-dual Brahman
> beyond all scope for dualistic identification (even as "mere speech").

  

The Chair is known in multiple ways. For instance,

  

1\. An entity that is the subject of manifold cognitions. Each cognition has its distinct existence in the plane of cognition, in Ishvara's knowledge. And yet we know the chair when we have any particular cognition of it.

  

2\. As having parts like chair-leg, chair-seat and so on. Each part has its own objective existence and the chair as their total also has its. Yet when we see the chair-leg, we see the chair. When we know the chair, we know the chair-leg.

  

Any particular or individual identification has distinct identity or satta in the vyavaharika standpoint within the set of **all** identities. The qualified chair in which the qualification is an object for identification is also in this plane.

  

The paramarthika "Chair-All" is the nondual Identity-Center that subsumes all individual identities of the vyavaharika standpoint and leaves no room for individual identification either. This may not be a strong enough statement. My position is that when we talk of the vyavaharika "all" alongside the Chair-All and the former as denoting or as non-different from the Chair-All, we are projecting and translating knowledge of Chair-All within vyavaharika standpoint where we also implicitly posit its "all" by way of dualistic identification. Then the Chair-All is realised as the One who appears or imagines the all and/or the vyavaharika all is realised as mithya. "Jnani knows only Chair-All but sees as this nama-rupa or that." Perfectly fine, that's right knowledge in vyavaharika standpoint where he also is identified. But.. The vyavaharika all is simply asat in the Chair-All standpoint, no dilly-dallying about it.

  

Of course I may be in some fanciful incoherent lala land in trying to be too strict in drawing the line. But that is the present understanding that is basis for some of the arguments put forth. (Am writing an essay based on above perspective, will post if it is completed satisfactorily.)
