Hare Krishna



Any particular or individual identification has distinct identity or satta in the vyavaharika standpoint within the set of **all** identities. The qualified chair in which the qualification is an object for identification is also in this plane.



Ø Yes, individual identification and individual shape and name would serve the definite purpose in all practical ways. Example, legs give the support, seat to rest our back, handles to rest our hand etc. Ring, bracelet, necklace, bangles all serves different purposes in wearing but what we are wearing is ‘gold’ only in different names and forms.



The paramarthika "Chair-All" is the nondual Identity-Center that subsumes all individual identities of the vyavaharika standpoint and leaves no room for individual identification either.



- In paramArthika it is just chair and nothing but chair. Just for
  conveying this truth we just add prefix or suffix ‘all’. It is just
  like this, instead of pointing out this is chair’s leg, chair’s seat,
  chair’s handle, chair’s back rest etc. we simply look at the chair and
  say it is ‘the chair’.



This may not be a strong enough statement. My position is that when we talk of the vyavaharika "all" alongside the Chair-All and the former as denoting or as non-different from the Chair-All, we are projecting and translating knowledge of Chair-All within vyavaharika standpoint where we also implicitly posit its "all" by way of dualistic identification. Then the Chair-All is realised as the One who appears or imagines the all and/or the vyavaharika all is realised as mithya. "Jnani knows only Chair-All but sees as this nama-rupa or that." Perfectly fine, that's right knowledge in vyavaharika standpoint where he also is identified. But.. The vyavaharika all is simply asat in the Chair-All standpoint, no dilly-dallying about it.



Ø I think we have already discussed there is pAramArthika and we cannot define it, yatO vAcho etc. and vyAvahArica ‘all’ cannot stand independently apart from chair. AND paramArtha jnana is NOT vyavahAra abhAva (absence) jnana but vyavahAra bAdhita (sublation) jnana and what is bAdhita here is our qualified cognition of chair and as a result realization of ‘chair’ in totality. (paripUrNa, avyabhichArita jnana of the ‘chair’).



Of course I may be in some fanciful incoherent lala land in trying to be too strict in drawing the line. But that is the present understanding that is basis for some of the arguments put forth. (Am writing an essay based on above perspective, will post if it is completed satisfactorily.)



Ø IMO the stage for the jeeva’s vyavahAra (cognizing the chair through individual parts) is Ishwara srushti (the wood / chair) and for that Ishwara srushti the upAdAna and nimitta kAraNa is brahman only and nothing but brahman. Because it was ONE and ONLY which was/is/ever existing without any second.
