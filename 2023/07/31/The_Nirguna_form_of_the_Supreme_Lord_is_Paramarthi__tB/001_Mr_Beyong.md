"Therefore, the mind should be established in Brahman alone, which is the Pure Consciousness, without seeking any external aid. What is different from this mere Consciousness is the illusory world."  
Different is not synonymous with separate, therefore the so-called illusory world is actually a superimposition of form/name in Brahman. That is why it says: "there exists nothing other than Brahman."

  
"Therefore, if samsara, bondage, is to be destroyed, one should focus the mind on the witness that is the Self." 

  
For how long? This "focus the mind on the witness that is the self" being only provisional, in most cases it becomes a practice for life, without the symptoms of understanding appearing. Why? Because from the beginning, one agrees to the practice with the intention of destroying something that he considers separate from Being when in reality it is only different. Different, for the simple reason that it is seen as other than the Being when in reality if there is only Being alone. Jagat is the Being appearing, as if dressed with the koshas, upadis, name, form, functions, etc.  
  
What would "destroy samsara" mean? If everything is Brahman as the Mandukya Upanishad verse 2 says, who could exclude samsara or jagat from it? We need then to find another meaning or implication for the term "destroy". In my understanding and based on the input of the scriptures, this can only mean the eradication of preconceptions, that we have about such samasara as something separate from Being. It is not necessary to destroy the world, or isolating ourselves, from it. It has nothing necessarily to do either with constantly mentally repeating to yourself, 'I am Brahman ... I am Brahman, or ... "The world is Brahman ... "The world is Brahman". if not simply, "see" the manifestation for what it really is, an erroneous preconception that the assimilation of the pointings of the scriptures help us to eradicate Once all this is assimilated, all phenomena should be seen as pointing to the Self instead of being a veil for the Self.
