Namaskaram Kalyan-ji and Sanskrit scholars,

  
  

>   
> Likewise the swami has translated anirvachaniya as the standard
> definition of mithya (I think this is in accordance with tradition)
> but this will be denied by opponents.

  

I read the anirvachaniya in तत्त्वान्यत्वाभ्यामनिर्वचनीये and assumed the swami was translating anirvachaniya as "non-determinable as either real or unreal" which is practically definition for mithya.

  

However I could not properly parse and hence skimmed past the front part of that phrase: "tattvaanyatvabhyaam". Can someone say if the following is correct?

  

Tattva can mean real, as even in tattvavada of Madhvacharya. So now it seems likely this part means "with/from/for real and unreal" and the swami only translated anirvachaniya for "non-determinable" - which seems proper. If correct, I don't see any sort of stretch in the mithya translation that he gave for the full phrase.

  

Of course the word iva in the sentence can be abused as anyone pleases, but (if above correct) the straightforward interpretation of the bhashya here would allow that nama-rupa basis of jagat is mithya.
