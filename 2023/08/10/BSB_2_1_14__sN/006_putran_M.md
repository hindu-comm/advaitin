> Pg 356: "Brahman becomes subject to all kinds of (phenomenal) actions
> like transformation, on account of the differences of aspects,
> constitutedbyname and form, which remain either differentiated or
> non-differentiated, which cannot be determined either as real or
> unreal, and which are imagined through ignorance. In Its real aspect
> Brahman remains unchang- ed and beyond all phenomenal actions. And
> since the differences of name and form, brought about by ignorance,
> are ushered into being through mere speech, the partlessness of
> Brahman is not violated. Besides, this text about transformation is
> not meant to establish transformation as a fact, for no fruit is seen
> to result from such a knowledge. But this is meant to establish the
> fact that all this is in essence one with Brahman that is beyond all
> phenomenal processes; for some fruit is seen to result from such a
> realization."

  

Sanskrit for above translation is below (2.1.27):

  

अविद्याकल्पितेनचनामरूपलक्षणेनरूपभेदेनव्याकृताव्याकृतात्मकेन
