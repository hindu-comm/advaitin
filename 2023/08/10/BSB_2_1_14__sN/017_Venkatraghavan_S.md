Shankaracharya in different places uses nAmarUpa slightly differently. Sometimes he refers to nAmarUpa to refer to manifest names and forms, vyakta nAma rUpa. Sometimes he refers to nAmarUpa to refer to unmanifest names and forms, avyakta nAma rUpa.

  

Where Shankara talks of nAmarUpa as vyaktanAmarUpa, there nAmarUpa is a kArya, and therefore has avidyA as its upAdAna kAraNa.

  

Where nAmarUpa is referred to by Shankaracharya as avyaktanAmarUpa, like in the case of BSB 2.1.14 (सर्वज्ञस्येश्वरस्यात्मभूते इवाविद्याकल्पिते नामरूपे तत्त्वान्यत्वाभ्यामनिर्वचनीये संसारप्रपञ्चबीजभूते सर्वज्ञस्येश्वरस्य मायाशक्तिः प्रकृतिरिति च श्रुतिस्मृत्योरभिलप्येते), such a nAmarUpa is avidyA itself.

  

There is no tautology here in saying that such a nAmarUpa is avidyAkalpita, because here the intent is in saying the avidyA itself is avidyAkalpita, ie adhyastha, ie such an avidyA is also mithyA only. Here avidyAkalpitatva refers to avidyA as the nimittakAraNa for the adhyAsa. It is important in this context for avidyA's mithyAtvam to be stated in the bhAShyakAra, because it is in response to a question how the same Brahman can be spoken of as one, undivided consciousness and at the same time be the creator of the universe (कूटस्थब्रह्मात्मवादिन एकत्वैकान्त्यात् ईशित्रीशितव्याभावे ईश्वरकारणप्रतिज्ञाविरोध इति चेत्).

  

This is very different from the adhyAsabhAShya. You had said that the phrase mithyAjnAna meant adhyAsa itself. So I had asked what purpose is served by saying adhyAsa is a result of adhyAsa (mithyAjnAna-nimittah).

If the intent is in saying that this adhyAsa is mithyA, he has already said it by the use of the word avivekena.
