> Shankaracharya in different places uses nAmarUpa slightly differently.
> Sometimes he refers to nAmarUpa to refer to manifest names and forms,
> vyakta nAma rUpa. Sometimes he refers to nAmarUpa to refer to
> unmanifest names and forms, avyakta nAma rUpa.
>
>   
>
> Where Shankara talks of nAmarUpa as vyaktanAmarUpa, there nAmarUpa is
> a kArya, and therefore has avidyA as its upAdAna kAraNa.
>
>   
>
> Where nAmarUpa is referred to by Shankaracharya as avyaktanAmarUpa,
> like in the case of BSB 2.1.14 (सर्वज्ञस्येश्वरस्यात्मभूते इवाविद्याकल्पिते नामरूपे
> तत्त्वान्यत्वाभ्यामनिर्वचनीये संसारप्रपञ्चबीजभूते सर्वज्ञस्येश्वरस्य मायाशक्तिः प्रकृतिरिति
> च श्रुतिस्मृत्योरभिलप्येते), such a nAmarUpa is avidyA itself.
>
>   
>
> There is no tautology here in saying that such a nAmarUpa is
> avidyAkalpita, because here the intent is in saying the avidyA itself
> is avidyAkalpita, ie adhyastha, ie such an avidyA is also mithyA only.

  

  

I think Sureshwaracharya'sverypopular [Br.Up.Bh](http://Br.Up.Bh). vartika verse:

  

तत्त्वमस्यादिवाक्योत्थसंयग्धीजन्ममात्रतः ।

*अविद्या सह कार्येण नासीदस्ति भविष्यति ॥*  

  

The Tattvamasi, etc. passages give rise to that knowledge which dispels the avidya, along with its effects, which is non-existent in all the three periods of time.

  

Here avidya (kAraNa) which had caused samsara (kaarya) gets sublated upon aparoksha jnana born of mahavakya. Since the non-existence of avidya in all three periods of time is admitted, like the adhyasta sarpa, avidya is mithya, adhyasta.
