I want to know for future reference: what is your 1. translation and 2. interpretation for the full phrase तत्त्वान्यत्वाभ्यामनिर्वचनीये ? Translation by Swami Gambhirananda is "non-determinable as either real or unreal".



Ø Clearing old mails 😊 I think I have shared my view points with Sri Kalyan Prabhuji on ‘mAya’ which is tattva or otherwise and how it is anirvachaneeya. Just thought of little elaboration on the same.



Ø As you know we don’t equate avidyA with mAya like vyAkhyAnakAra-s.
So our interpretation of the ‘tattvAnyatvAbhyAmanirvachaneeya’ is different from vyAkhya. vyAkhyAna says mUlAvidyA which is also avidyA, mAya, mUla prakruti, avyAkruta etc. is anirvachaneeya. But adhyAsavAdins say mAya is adhyasta (superimposed on brahman) when it is viewed from absolute standpoint of brahman. In that sense mAya is avidyAkruta, avidyAtmaka, avidyApratyupasthApita etc. here avidyA itself is adhyAsa and due to this adhyAsa we are suffering from katrutva and bhOktrutva without realizing the true svarUpa of avidyAkruta / avidyAkalpita nAma rUpa. In this sense, mAya / nAma rUpa is false appearance. Let us take the standard example rajju-sarpa, when I don’t know the real nature of the rope (jnAnAbhAva) I see (anyathAgrahaNa) either snake, garland, gO mUtra dhAra etc. And during this realm of avidyA I really feel that there is indeed a snake. For me the snake appears ‘as if’ it is there really. This false appearance of snake is called as mAya and this mAya appearance is due to lack of right knowledge of rope or misconceiving the rope as something else (mithyAjnAna). In this light shankara at various places says mAya/nAma rUpa is avidyAkalpita, avidyAkrita, avidyAkArya etc. Because avidyA is a subjective defect (defect of the mind, please refer Sri Subbu prabhuji’s shared video of sarvapriyaananda with regard to avidyA and mOksha) and mAya is ‘as if’ false objective appearance due to this ignorance. I have added ‘as if’ here since Sri SSS not so particular about ‘vastu rUpa arthAdhyAsa’ as his emphasization only on jnAnAdhyAsa. So wherever shankara talks about mAya / saMsAra / nAma rUpa/ vyakta jagat etc. it is in this light only i.e. “ seeing the snake in place of rope” due to wrong knowledge of rope ( atasmin tad buddhiH). BUT JAGAT WHAT WE ARE TALKING ABOUT AS BRAHMAN IS NOT LIKE A SNAKE ON THE ROPE, bhashyakAra clarifies this in chAndOgya shruti bhAshya.



Ø And from another view point / aspect, mAya is also called vyakta and avyaktAtmaka ( manifested names and forms and unmanifested seed/shakti form). For this bhAshyakAra uses Ishwara shakti which is brahmAbhinna (not different from brahman). And from this view point, the brahman himself appears in the form of universe. And he will be having the avyAkruta jagat in seed form before creation and makes it vyAkruta for the jeeva-s. Here point to be noted is avyAkruta is explained as akshara, avidita, asat ( asadvA edamagra Asit says shruti) which is parabrahman itself says somewhere bhAshyakAra and for the vyAkruta jagat brahman is abhinna nimittOpadAna kAraNa. So if we are seeing jagat it is the ‘vishesha darshana’ of that parabrahman only nothing else because HE is upAdAna, nimitta as well as antaryAmi of this vyakta jagat. (see the spider example in mundaka, pravesha shruti in taittireeya). And as I have been insisting when vedAnta teaching us by accepting the beeja shakti and manifested form of the universe it describes the kAraNa kArya respectively to drive home the point that brahman’s secondless nature of ekatvaM and not kArya-kAraNa prakriya per se.



Ø And mAya is anirvachaneeya, as it is difficult to determine whether it is tattva (reality) or anyatva, means having independent existence of its own. In the vyAkhyAna it is explained as satasat vilakshaNa, sat (being) or as not being (asat / anAtma) but it is slightly differently used in bhAshya hence it is not tattva and attatva but it is tattva and anyatva as explained above. If we take the rajju-sarpa example, the snake appearance is not really the substratum of the reality (rope) and at the same time the snake does not have independent existence of its own apart from rope. This does not mean there is an anirvachaneeya sarpa in the rope but anirvachaneeya khyAtivAdins advocated this theory by saying there was an anirvachaneeya sarpa in the rope or silver in the nacre. Look at shankara bhAshya, while explaining anirvachaneeya he gives the example of foam, waves, bubbles which are not the same as water but at the same time not different from water. This example is more are less resembling with (a) kArya ( pot, jar etc. ) is not different from kAraNa (clay) but (b) kAraNa (clay) is different from kArya and NOT kArya (pot, jar etc.). In (a) both the effect and the cause are together and we are also aware that the cause clay itself is appearing in the form of the effect, pot /jar etc. and we know that the transactions are possible only in the effect, but not in the cause. Therefore, a deterministic description of the effect as ‘only one with the cause’ or as ‘different from it’ is not possible. For example, the transactions like holding water, etc cannot be done in the clay, but quite possible in the pot. If we view the pot from the point of transaction it is upAdhi, but no transaction is possible in the clay as such. Therefore, it cannot be described unambiguously whether the pot is clay only or is different from it. This ambiguity is called anirvachaneeya.



It is already getting very lengthy I must stop here. In short, mAya is used differently at different context in PTB and the concept of mAya needs to be understood as per that context only without disturbing the ultimate siddhAnta of Advaita as elucidated by bhAshyakAra in his PTB.
Sometimes it is avidyAkalpita, sometimes it is vyaktAvyaktaatmaka jagat, it is at some places anirvachaneeya, it is as brahmAnanya and it is also Ishwara shakti. For each and every aspect of mAya we have the bhAshya support.
