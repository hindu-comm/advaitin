Hi Bhaskar-ji,



Regarding your first point, it is imaginative (to say the least) to suggest that Ṥaṅkara, having gained j~nAna, would continue to interact with the imagined jIva-s in what used to be his created

world. This reminds me of the story I used in a book years ago of the man who dreamed he encountered a friend who had sustained a serious accident. The he woke up. Would he then telephone for an ambulance to come to save his friend?



But, regardless of that, if we assume that Ṥaṅkara was the only person in the history of the universe to become enlightened, are we all still part of the world that he imagined? Despite the fact that his body-mind died 1200 years ago (longer for some members of the group)? Or, if we assume that one or two others have also been enlightened down the ages, how do these various imaginings interact? Or does every j~nAnI imagine exactly the same world? I rather think that such ideas are contravening Occam’s Razor (to say the least!).



Finally, are you imaging me or am I imagining you?



Best wishes,

Dennis



(Apologies for not addressing other points. The first I didn’t understand (I’m afraid my immediate Sanskrit understanding is still very limited) and the second did not seem relevant to my points.)
