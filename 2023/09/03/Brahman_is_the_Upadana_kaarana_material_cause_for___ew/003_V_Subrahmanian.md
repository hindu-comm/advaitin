> I have these long-standing questions.

>   
>
> Is brahman the material cause of the world? Or is mAyA the material
> cause of the world?
>
> Both view points exist?  

  

Advaitins hold Brahman to be vivarta upadana karana and Maya to be the parinami upadana karana. That maya does not have ultimate reality is also admitted.

> 
>
>   
>
> Further, what does the word "material cause" signify? If, say, a piece
> of paper is taken and investigated materially to the deepest level,
> will it be observed to be same as brahman? If not, in what sense is
> brahman the material cause of the paper? Or brahman has made an
> illusory transformation as the paper? But this is not exactly the same
> as clay-pot, is it?

  

The Chandogya 6th chapter teaches: The clay products are unreal by themselves and only the material clay in them is real. So, as clay alone the clay products are real and as pot, saucer, etc. they are unreal. Also the effect has no existence apart from the cause, there is cause effect identity is also taught there itself. It also teaches the elements five or three emerge from Brahman and hence they are non-diff from Brahman. That way at every stage this cause-effect non-diff is taught there itself.

  

Paper is a product of Prithvi element and hence non diff from prithivi. Prithivi is non-diff from its cause water and thus going back to Brahman, paper is non-diff from its ultimate cause Brahman.

  

So in two ways we can arrive at paper-Brahman non-diff. Through the series of causes and through vivarta upadana: like rope appearing as snake, Brahman alone appears as paper. Paper minus name and form is Brahman.
