This is a wrong interpretation/understanding of that BSB dialogue. While it is true that as per the Gaudapada karika: no creation, no dissolution, none bound, none aspiring for liberation and none liberated - which is the ultimate standpoint, which is what is implied by the above BSB dialogue, the very purpose of the Brahmasutras, as Shankara declares in the very beginning, is to 'dispel that ignorance that has wrought all disaster'.

  

Also, the hypothetical opponent who thinks avidya is a second entity, thereby resulting in advaita hAni, this is a misconception on the part of that opponent, arising out of his non comprehension of Advaita. That ignorance is not a real entity like Brahman, when alone it could be counted as asecond. It is mithyA itself: mithyA cha tat ajnAnam cha mithyAjnAnam. Sureshwara says; once:the enlightenment dawns, avidya, along with its products/effects, is known to be 'was not, is not and will not be.' It is therefore ultimately non-existent in all three periods of time, yet enabling experiencing its effects: anirvachaniya, sad-asat-vilakshanam.
