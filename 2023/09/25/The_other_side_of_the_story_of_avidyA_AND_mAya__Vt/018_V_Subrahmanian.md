That Brahman is actually deluded is not the siddhAnta. It is always 'as though deluded'. This is perhaps the best instance that clinches the issue:

  

प्राणादिभिरनन्तैस्तु भावैरेतैर्विकल्पितः।  
मायैषा तस्य देवस्य ययायं मोहितः स्वयम् ॥ १९ ॥

  

यदि आत्मैक एवेति निश्चयः, कथं प्राणादिभिरनन्तैर्भावैरेतैः संसारलक्षणैर्विकल्पित इति ? *उच्यते शृणु — मायैषा तस्यात्मनो देवस्य*। यथा मायाविना विहिता माया गगनमतिविमलं कुसुमितैः सपलाशैस्तरुभिराकीर्णमिव करोति, तथा इयमपि देवस्य माया, *यया अयं स्वयमपि मोहित**इव**मोहितो भवति*।[‘मम माया दुरत्यया’ (भ. गी. ७। १४)](https://advaitasharada.sringeri.net/display/bhashya/Gita?page=7&id=BG_C07_V14&hl=%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%AE%20%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE%20%E0%A4%A6%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE)इत्युक्तम् ॥

  

Atma, called 'Deva' by Gaudapadacharya, 2,19 is as though deluded by its/his own Maya. And Shankara cites the Gita verse which is actually Bhagavan saying, 'My Maya is extremely difficult to transcend.' So, it is so clear here that for Shankara, it is the same Mayashakti (which makes Ishwara omnipotent, omniscient, etc.) is the one that also deludes himself to be the jiva.

  

In common Advaitic parlance, as Sri Bhaskar avers, Maya is reserved for Ishwara and avidyA for jiva. But here we have a very patent instance where Shankara applies the word Maya, that too citing Bhagavan, to the deluding power, avidya, of the jiva. Again 'Vasudeva' appears as though deluded as well.
