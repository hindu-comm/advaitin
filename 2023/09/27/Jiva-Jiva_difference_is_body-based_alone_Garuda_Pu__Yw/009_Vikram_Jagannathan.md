Namaskaram Shri Suresh ji,

  

It seems to me that you are looking at this from a non-Advaitasampradhayam perspective (potentially Visishtadvaita). My apologies if not. Advaita'svyavaharika perspective already accommodates all other vedantic schools as well as all distinctions, includingVarnashrama dharma. The paramarthika satya - Brahman - is already & always existing in & through all vyavaharika experiences as well. Bheda and abheda are already inherently reconciled within Advaita system, without the need for a bridge (ghataka).

  

Please allow me to share further comments:

  

1\. Though it is generally accepted & said in commonplace that vyavahara drishti corresponds to ajnanis, if one has to be accurate, there is a subtle difference between the vyavaharika perspective of an ajnani, jnani and Bhagavan's avatara. An ajnani believes vyavaharika is ultimately real and perceives pluralitydue to prarabdha karma. A jnani understands vyavaharika is relatively real and perceives plurality due to prarabdha karma. Bhagavan's avatara understands vyavaharika isrelatively real and perceives pluralitydue to His sankalpa. Though there is the perception of plurality (vyavahara drishti) amongst all three, it is incorrect to conclude that they are identical, meaning they are all ajnanionly. These, again, are only vyavaharika perspective. The paramarthika perspective is always one Brahman-Atman alone, and non-dual.

  

2\. Lord Shri Krishna as an ajnani: It is accepted that even the avatara of Bhagavan is only in the vyavaharika perspective. Meaning, the very conception of Bhagavan's avatara as Lord Shri Krishna (as son of Shri Vasudeva), His teaching to Shri Arjuna, etc. is all in vyavaharika perspective. In this sense, it is not that this Lord Shri Krishna is the paramarthika Satya, but it is only the svarupa of Lord Shri Krishna, as Brahman / Atman, who is the paramarthika Satya. People have historically misunderstood this subtle point of Advaita and have inaccurately come to the conclusionthat following Advaita logic Lord Shri Krishna is also an ajnani, since He perceives pluralityand teaches Shri Arjuna! The accurate statement is that Lord Shri Krishna is a Bhagavan-avatara in vyavaharika, since He perceives plurality and teaches Shri Arjuna; which is accepted by Advaita.  

  

3\. A clarification on a potential misunderstanding: If jivas are multiple, what is the status of their atma svarupa? Is it multiple as well? Is it distinct from Brahman svarupa? Non-Advaita sampradhayamsmostly believes in the multiplicity of jivas, their corresponding atma svarupas (jivatmans) and their individual distinction from Brahman svarupa. But per Advaita, one Brahman alone is the atma svarupa of all perceived jivas. Therefore jivas existing in their atma svarupa only means realization of the one eternal immutable non-dual Brahman as their very Atman / Self. There is no multiplicity of atma svarupa of jivas, and there is no distinction between this atma svarupa and Brahman svarupa.

  

4\. On "vayam" & identify with universe: There is no direct identification between the microcosmic / individual and macrocosmic / universal (except perhaps through DSV, which can be tabled aside for this discussion). Jiva (microcosmic) and Isvara (macrocosmic) are considered to be eternally distinct in Advaita. Though space is one, the pot-space itself is not identical to room-space. Similarly, it is only the underlying Atman-Brahman that brings in the identity between Jiva & Isvara. Jiva vs Isvara per-se are always distinct. This also explains the nature of oneness between oneself and the entire universe.

  

5\. Your mention of the word 'Sarvatma' needs to be clarified. It doesn't imply "all atmans" but only implies "atman of all". The former posits multiplicity of atmans and the latter the underlying unity.

  

6\. On paratantra satya: The bheda between atman & anatman is said to exist only in the Vyavaharika perspective. The one Consciousness entity appears to be conscious of itself (as atman) as well as others (anatman). But there is no bheda whatsoever in paramarthika satya. If so, what does your term - bhedatmaka paratantra satya - mean? Does it mean the existenceof this bheda-satya in vyavaharika realm? If so, this is already accepted and accommodated within the vyavaharika realm of Advaita. Jiva is accepted to be subservient to the independent Isvara. Does it exist in paramarthika perspective as well? If so, this is completely against the fundamentals of Advaita philosophy and thus the proposition is invalid.

  

with humble prostrations,

Vikram
