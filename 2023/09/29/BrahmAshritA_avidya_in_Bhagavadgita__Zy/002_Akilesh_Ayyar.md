An interesting debate.

  

I see several positions that can be taken up here and all have justifications:

  

1.  Maya and avidya are the same, because it is quite possible for
    something to be horrible from one perspective and beautiful from
    another. Take for example a movie about war and injustice. The
    audience watches it and for a while forgets that it is a movie. For
    those two hours they are the soldiers suffering and dying. In that
    forgetting they experience pain and horror — all the problems that
    ignorance poses. But from another perspective this effect is created
    by the director, actors, writers, etc. It is an artistic effect. It
    is their Maya alone that has created the absorption of the audience
    in the movie — and it is beautiful.

  

2.  Maya and avidya are different, because avidya does not really exist.
    When it is investigated, it mysteriously vanishes. Indeed, when
    investigated it turns out never to have existed in the first
    place.  
      
    One way of looking at this is that, as Ramana Maharshi put it
    "Ajnana has two aspects: avarana (veiling) and vikshepa
    (multiplicity). Of these, avarana (veiling) denotes the veil hiding
    the Truth. That prevails in sleep. Multiplicity (vikshepa) is
    activity in different times. This gives rise to diversity and
    prevails in waking and dream states (jagrat and svapna). If the
    veil, i.e., avarana is lifted, the Truth is perceived. It is lifted
    for a Jnani and so his karana sarira (causal body) ceases to exist.
    Vikshepa alone continues for him. Even so, it is not the same for a
    Jnani as it is for an ajnani.”  
      
    So Maya could be said to be what generates vikshepa whereas avidya
    is purely the veiling.

  

3.  This debate is really irrelevant because, as Maharshi put it, "The
    answers will be according to the capacity of the seeker. It is said
    in the second chapter of Gita that no one is born or dies: but in
    the fourth chapter Sri Krishna says that numerous incarnations of
    His and of Arjuna had taken place, all known to Him but not to
    Arjuna. Which of these statements is true? Both statements are true,
    but from different standpoints. Now a question is raised: How can
    jiva rise up from the Self? I must answer. Only know Your Real
    Being, then you will not raise this question."
