Dear Bhaskar ji,

  

Even though you say 'it has been explained so many times....' the undeniable fact remains that the Chandogya Upanishad itself is saying:

  

vAchArambhaNam vikAro nAmadheyam, mRttikEtyEva satyam. The upanishad itself denies the unreality of the vikAra (jagat) as a separate existent entity, the pot (jagat). How do we know this? The Upanishad goes on to explain and reply: the clay (Brahman) alone is real. Hereby the Upanishad is teaching: the clay-pot example is non-different from the rope-snake example. If that were not the case, the Upanishad would be wrong in declaring: mRttikEtyeva satyam, by using EvakAra for clay only. It has already denied reality to the pot bythe word 'vAchArambhaNam nAmadheyam'.. In fact the Upanishad does not even recognize a thing called pot; it denotes the pot by simply
'vAchArambhaNam nAmadheyam.' The Upanishad sees the pot as simply a name and not a substance. It holds that the substance in the pot is clay alone.

  

Thus, the Upanishad has made the rajju sarpa analogy inalienable from the clay-pot analogy. Hence alone Shankara uses the rajju sarpa analogy in all such places. Otherwise Shankara will have to be faulted for using the rajju sarpa example in this shruti.

  

regards

  

subbu
