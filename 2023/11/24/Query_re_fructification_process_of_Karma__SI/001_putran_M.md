Namaskaram brahmaasmi-ji,

  

I recall the prayer of Kunti devi to Sri Krishna: "O Krishna, I desire that we face more troubles so that we can always remember You and thus be freed from the cycle of birth and death." (Here is a random person on the net giving her instructive elaboration:[https://medium.com/@catanvijoshi/the-story-of-queen-kuntis-prayers-251e3cf82913](https://medium.com/@catanvijoshi/the-story-of-queen-kuntis-prayers-251e3cf82913)

  

Moral of the story: Difficulties in life are not always negative. They can be opportunities for growth, reminding us of our inner strength and bringing us closer to the divine. Instead of fearing challenges, we should embrace them as a means to deepen our spiritual journey and connection with God.)

  

There is much truth in Kunti's prayer. Because when the pain of life, based on false identifications of self, is there, it is so much 'easier' or natural to actually contemplate advaita or have Ishvara dhyana. But when we have the option to forget or avoid the pain of our Ignorance, by diving into our loukika movie-worlds, we would be hard-pressed to return to the kurukshetra for the higher purpose of human life. So, yes, the suffering thatis karmaphala has to be transmuted into persistent contemplation ofHim,the Self.

  

Keep in mind that the blissful mother Kali has two sides, one pleasing to the sight and the other terrible. Both are Her manifest appearance and it is our job to discover Her, the unchangeable Self, in every such form that She presents in our consciousness. Can you just bear witness to the harassing mind and its memories andresolve them all as Her manifest appearance in Consciousness? When we forget the non-dual Self, we get caught in the whirlpool of duality, identifying one form/manifestation as preferable to another and working ourselves mad to achieve that end. Then wefeel miserable when She rewards us a result we don't want.

  

Also, run through your mind positive thoughts to counter the depressing thoughts: "I am perfect, I am poornam, already complete. I am Sat-Chit-Ananda. I am Satyam-Jnanam-Anantam Brahma. I am blissful. How can I be otherwise!" "What I am seeing is only the blissful form of the Mother. How beautiful, wonderful." Just practice worshipping that form as She appears and refuse getting pulled into feelings of misery. If the form is too difficult to behold, turn back to the formless Self within or to another benign form of the Lord toget back your footing. Hold yourself from action or reaction based on the onslaught of such thoughts, just be a witness and persist in connecting back to the Self/Her/Him, while affirming positive feelings in yourself that you would associate to a jnani. Of course, in the midst of all this, there will be the regular "duties" that you must take care of.

  

I had written some of the thoughts with regard to dealing with the mind a few months back, motivated by a simpler context but having useful general info. Attaching this; see from sec IV. Some of it may help at this stage for you, given that pain is pushing you to search for solutions.
