Namaskar:



The fructification of any Karma is a natural phenomenon and I don’t believe that we need any justification through any Shastra. This does not mean that Shastras did not discuss this important topic directly or indirectly. We can recognize the fructification process on many of our day to day activities. When the yogurt culture is mixed with a warm milk, after few hours, we notice the milk becomes Yogurt! Also when we put a seed in the garden, it germinates into a plant or tree after a specified time. We also understand when the milk doesn’t become Yogurt when we mixed the Yogurt, the reasons for the failure such as cold weather or the quality of the culture. Similarly, all seeds do not germinate and some fails due to quality of the seed, soil and water!



The issue of fructification arises mostly because of our ignorance and that forces us to learn and enhance our knowledge to erase the ignorance. Let me explain what Bhagavad Gita states regarding the fructification of Karma. If we can fully understand the famous verse of Gita on Karma Yoga, Verse 47 of Chapter2:



This one of the most often quoted verses of Gita on Karma Yoga which has inspired many persons. Several Acharyas and authors of Book of Gita have also interpreted this verse to get the full essence of this important verse of Gita. Both Pujya Chinmayanandaji and Pujya Dayananda Saraswati have brought new insights on the understanding of this verse. Those who have heard or read either one of their presentations were able to appreciate the essence of Karma Yoga. Here is my attempt to share my understandings of the essence with all of you. If you notice carefully that Karma Yoga provides the correct platform to understand the role Prarabdha and Purushartha whenever we undertake any Karma.

karmaNi eva adhikaaraste maa phaleshu gadaachana  
maa karma phala hetuH bhuH maa sanghaH astu akarmaNi  
  
We only have the "right" to conduct the action and certainly we have no control over the results of the action. We should avoid using the "results" as the motivating force of our action and free our attachment to inaction. The facts will become crisp and clear if and only if we make efforts to understand this verse carefully. We are part of the Nature and we should understand our rights' and our ‘limitations.' Nature has given us the skill and power to conduct an action of our choice. We can use our skills to set up the goals for the action and should proceed to apply all our energy and skill to accomplish those goals. Our intelligence, energy and knowledge do not give us the authority over the results of the action. This human limitation is also a fact of life and we have no choice other than to accept the outcome. The results can be more, equal, less or even opposite to our expectations (goals). The results of any action are influenced both by Prarabdha and Purushartha.   
  
Any expression of expectation (prejudging the results) before completing an action develops an attitude that binds the action to the results. Consequently, the doer changes the focus from action to the result (as Arjun did before fighting the war). In other words, our Purushartha gets diminished, diverts our full attention. This diversion makes us to be inactive, ineffective and confused. Lord Krishna in Bhagavad Gita emphasizes to Arjun that he has no rights (authority) over the consequences. Implicitly the Lord points out that the Lord only has the authority over the results!

The views of Karma Yoga expressed in this verse are certainly no different from what is emphasized in modern management institutions.
Also, successful political leaders, scientists and sports’ coaches also emphasize the same views. But the message of Gita is much more profound and precise. According to Gita, the humans have the "rights" on their action (Karma) but ‘right' comes along with greater responsibility to maintain Dharma. The doer has the freedom over his/her Karma but he or she is expected to follow the Dharma established by his/her own mind.
It is very difficult (impossible) to describe all the noble things that both these stalwarts of Vedanta emphasized during their discourses and writings.

If we pursue to remember this verse at least once a day or once a week, we will likely be protected from anxieties and excitements. Pujya Gurudev used to advice all of us to Plug our EAR - E stands for Excitement for the Present; A for Anxiety about the Future and R for the Regrets of the Past. Learn from the Past and Plan for the Future, but Do not Torture the Mind with Past Regrets or Future Anxieties. Act in the Present with a Serene Mind. Be a Cool Dude!

Indirectly, this verse reminds us that the fructification is natural and it happens according to the efforts employed and the fruit is determined not on the basis of our expectation! We know the fact that any Karma (action) that we undertake includes actors other than us and the cooperation and level of support from other participants. The subtle message is that we alone are decision maker but any Karma happens because of the decisions made by known and unknown. participants. In conclusion we have only the authority to perform a Karma but the fructification happens with the level of cooperation and support by other known and unknown participants!
