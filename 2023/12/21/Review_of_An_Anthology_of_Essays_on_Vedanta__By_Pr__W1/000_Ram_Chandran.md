Review of “An Anthology of Essays on Vedanta.” By Prof. V. Krishnamurthy

During my India visit, my nephew, a good friend of Profvk gave me the book, “The Essentials of Hinduism,” by Prof V Krishnamurthy (Profvk) and that was my first exposure. His style of expression with precise description of concepts impressed me a lot. Profvk during 1998 joined a Vedantic mailing list forum with the focus on advaita philosophy as postulated by Adi Sankaracharya. This forum was initiated by me and Profvk was a co-moderator along with many scholars well versed in Vedanta. Since 1998 I have been continued to participate my discussions with Profvk through the mailing list. Besides, I have read many books of Profvk on both the religious and the philosophical aspects of Hindu religion and culture. I was fortunate to have the opportunity to review quite a few of his books. I am proud to declare that I was quite impressed by his scholarship and the clarity of his explanations.

This book, “On the Anthology of Essays on Vedanta” can be considered as a sequel to his the book “A Primer of Spirituality: 108 Questions & Answers in Hinduism.” I believe that these essays provide detailed explanations to some of the questions that often asked about spirituality by the truth seekers. The book is organized using the step ladder approach where in order to reach the top of the leader; one has to go step by step. One of the major issues raised by westerners is mostly because of their misunderstanding of the symbolism behind Idol (Icon) Worship. Profvk has provided one of the most satisfactory explanations of the relevance of Idol worship in Hinduism. He points out profoundly that “we worship God in the Idol and not Idol as God!” As a second step Profvk states that God can’t be described by words; the eyes cannot see Him; the ears cannot hear Him; He can’t be indicated or related to things that we know; He is taller than the tallest and shorter than the shortest; He is present in both actions and inactions; and He is beyond the mind, body and intellect! The next of the steps to figure out the differences between the absolute and non-absolute schools as the means to find out the Oneness! To get an in depth understanding of the Oneness, it becomes necessary to know Jiva and Jivathma; Saguna and Nirguna Brahman and most importantly the vision of Equanimity through an understanding of Practical non-Duality. Profvk’s love for the youngsters of today is fully reflected through the Vedanta Sopanam in 27 Shlokas. In his Forward to this last section of Part I of the book, Dr. Aravinda Rao, a well-known Sanskrit scholar summarizes with the statement: “This is a time-tested spiritual menu which was administered to all kids born in the generation of Profvk.” He further points out that Profvk is highly passionate and concerned about the emotional and spiritual training of young minds. In this last section, Profvk provides the flavor from a wide variety of verses taken from hitopadesam, Vairagya Satakam, Bhagavatam, Rigveda and from Upanishads. 



The part 2 of the book with the title, “Bits of Heard Wisdom” consists of Sections A and B. Section A focuses on the works of Adi Sankaracharya and Section B summarizes the speeches of Advaita Vedanta Scholars and Acharyas of Vedanta Institutions such as Ramakrishna Mission, Chinmaya Mission, Arsha Vidhya Gurukulam and Institutes of higher learning. Profvk also provides the You Tube videos of all the presentations in this section. The second part is a large collection of summaries of talks in the Global Festival of Oneness 2021 organized by Advaita Academy in honor of the memory of Acharya Bhagavatpada Sankara. Most of us who couldn’t attend the month long conference get the opportunity to hear the speeches and the summaries provided by Profvk will allow us to get better understanding of advaita philosophy. On the basis of my reading, I have the following recommendations for interested readers:

Upasana and its importance to a Mumuksu; Panchakosa Prakriya;Brahmasutra Catuhssutri: An Introduction; Adhyasa Bhashya- A Jewel by Sankara; Karya Karana Vada; Sruit vs Anubhava Pramana in Advaita; Sankara’s Teachings on Jivamukti; Understanding Dharma in the Light of Advaita Philosophy; Need of Vedanta in Everyone’s Life; Bhagavadgita: Self-Management – Success Redefined; Science and Vedanta; In Pursuit of Happiness – Insights from Advaita Vedanta; Significance of Vedanta in Daily Life; The Logic of Advaita Vedanta

On the basis of my reading of this book and the hidden treasures that I found in both parts 1 and 2, I strongly recommend to both the scholars and also to students who want to strengthen their knowledge of Vedanta Philosophy.
