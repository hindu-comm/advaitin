Obviously the author is infinitely better at translating Sanskrit than I am so that his version must presumably be OK. The problem with most (all?) translations is that the person doing them has a prior ‘presumed-understanding’ of the content and therefore likely meaning. They translate according to that understanding, and not necessarily according to what is literally said. The other problem here is that Vivekachudamani was probably not written by Ṥaṅkara.



Having said that, my comment is that the translation, whilst (probably) linguistically accurate, is not a true expression of Ṥaṅkara’s understanding. The ONLY path to mokSha is via Self-knowledge. SamAdhi is an experience, so that the usually understood (Yoga) meaning of the word cannot be directly relevant to liberation other than as a preparatory practice to prepare the mind for shravaNa. It appears in the VC in the long section devoted to nididhyAsana, where that is understood as the ‘cementing’ of the understanding gained from shravaNa-manana. And that may be done by revisiting the knowledge acquired, by reading, writing, teaching or whatever. It is needed because the preliminary sādhana catuṣṭaya sampatti was incomplete. Accordingly, the samAdhi functions as before – stilling mind, opening attention etc. – but now having the effect of enabling the ‘consolidation’ of what has already been learned. Mentioning vairAgya as well implies that the whole of sādhana catuṣṭaya sampatti should be revisited and ‘completed’.
