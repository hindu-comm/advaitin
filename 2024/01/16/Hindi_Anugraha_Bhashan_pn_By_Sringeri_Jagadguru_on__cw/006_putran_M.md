My Reply:

  

I have not been following the internet/SM for the related news sufficiently to know of these attacks on the mathas. Only saw Sri Jayanarayan-ji's recent post informing of this. I know the puri acharya had objected and now also see the objections of the jyotir matha. I assume they would be correct as per the shastras governing the temples, but that matters only to Hindus who respect the orthodox traditions and the sampradaya acharyas. "Children must submit to be taught", unfortunately most of our frivolous people are disconnected from the meticulous seriousness of religion and its traditions, think their loose ideas on dharma/bhakti are sufficient everywhere and the rest of it is superfluous baggage. They now know - thanks to Modi - there is something called pranapratishtathat formally legitimizes the temple, but the further details that the shankaracharya shows concern about seem unimportant and obstructive to the greater good. So they decide the shankaracharyas are complicating the religion, being archaic, not allowing hindu unity, and so on, and that they are false gurus who pretend to represent sanatana dharma for all Hindus. In the background is the fact that the traditional mathas are still the votaries of varnashrama (caste), which modern Hindus want to do away with. Every Hindu has become the judge and reformer of the "flawed" religion he has inherited.

  

Now the task for the acharyas and Hindu leaders is indeed to guide such a group of people during this momentous occasion for sanatana dharma, when Sri Rama's temple is being re-born. The puri/jyotir acharyas are right to point out the mistakes and not take part; there must be some who take that clear stance on Dharma and refuse to allowcompromises. That needs to register with the people, whether they can accept now or not. And yet (given the overall situation) the sringeri acharya is also right to support from the distance and encourage the general Hindu to participate in the inevitable function. The people's focus on the bhakti andshraddha in Rama, what really makes sense to them, that's also at stake. One is looking at the Tree and the other is looking at the Forest. Their evaluation and teaching of dharma can be aimed at different objectives and can both have validity.
