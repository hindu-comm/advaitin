My preface for this email:

  

In advaita, there is an important yet simple analogy of Gold-Ring. The Gold denotes the unchanging Reality, the higher Self, the Existence-Consciousness substratum, that never becomes greater or lesser, is ever stable, ever full/complete/infinite. We are this nondual Self, and if we stay established in this Knowledge, we remain at peace and "live happily", even as we take on a Ring-identity in the relative plane and participate in the Ring-bangle show.

  

The Ring denotes the plane of name-form identifications of this Gold (Self). It is ever changing, non-constant, and associated with dualities of good-bad, high-low, right-wrong, preferable-not preferable, and so on. The ajnani is caught head-over-heel in an unstable temporal fluctuating Ring identity, and is always struggling to preserve or perfect its shape in this whirlpool of relative existence.

  

From the standpoint of (Knowledge of) Gold, everything of the relative plane is only Gold, hence even notions of change and action are non-existent in the higher standpoint of the Self.

  

Janaka who is a jnani but also a king in our world (the 'father' of Sita) expresses in a few instructive verses how one lives happily (i.e. free of fear, unhappiness, doubt) when established in the jnana of the Self.

  

(This is from RKM publication of Ashtavakra Samhita, translated and with extensive notes by Swami Nityaswarupananda. I included the notes for only the third verse.)

Janaka said:

  

The tranquility which is born of the consciousness that there is nothing but the Self is rare even for one who wears but the loin cloth. Therefore, giving up renunciation and acceptance, I live happily.

  

There is trouble of the body here, trouble of the tongue there, and trouble of the mind elsewhere. Having renounced these, in life's supreme goal I live happily.

  

Fully realizing that nothing\* whatsoever is really done by the Self, I do\*\* whatever\*\*\* presents itself to be done and so I live happily.

  

\*: because whatever is done is done by the body, mind, and senses accompanied by the ego. The Self is beyond all these.

  

\*\*: being devoid of the ego and being unattached. The body and mind work, the Self remains unattached.

  

\*\*\*: as a result of past actions which are responsible for the present body.

  

The yogins who are attached to the body insist upon action or inaction. Owing to the absence of association or disassociation, I live happily.

  

No good or evil accrues to me as a result of staying, going, or sleeping. So whether I stay, go, or sleep, I live happily.

  

I do not lose by sleeping nor gain by striving. So, giving up thoughts of loss or elation, I live happily.

  

Observing again and again the inconstancy of pleasure and pain, I have renounced good and evil, and I live happily.
