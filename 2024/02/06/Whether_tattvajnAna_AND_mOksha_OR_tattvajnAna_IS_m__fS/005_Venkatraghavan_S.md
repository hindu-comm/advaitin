No, there is no gap between tattvajnAna and moksha, but there can be a gap between moksha and jivanmukti.

  

So what is the difference between moksha and jIvanmukti?

  

Moksha is vasthusthiti - it is the nature of the thing, i.e. the svabhAva of Brahman (brahmabhAvashcha mokshah, the bhAShyakAra says) and tattvajnAna reveals that svabhAva to the jnAni.

  

Whereas, jIvanmukti is vasthusthiti + mano avasthA - ie it is the state of the mukta jnAni who has attained absolute peace of mind through vAsanA kshaya and manonAsha.

  

One can be a jnAni without jIvanmukti also. That being the case, if one has jnAna, he realises that mukti is his svarUpa, but he may not have jIvanmukti.

  

For jIvanmukti, he needs tattvajnAna + vAsana kshaya + manonAsha.

There is no gap between tattvajnAna and moksha, but there may be a gap between tattvajnAna and jIvanmukti.

  

In practice, as the attainment of tattvajnAna needs chitta shuddhi to a certain extent - if that chitta is significantly pure, it is possible that with tattvajnAna itself, vAsanA kshaya and manonAsha happens.

> If yes, jnAni can be called ‘krutakrutya’??

He is kRtakRtya because the attainment or non attainment of jIvanmukti really makes no difference to the jnAni, because he is Brahman. The anAtma that he formerly associated with, may benefit from jIvanmukti, but he knows that He, Brahman, has no use for jIvanmukti. There is nothing that Brahman can gain, or lose, from the attainment or non-attainment of jIvanmukti.

  

> how shAstra / veda vAkya can be considered as ‘final pramANa’ in
> brahma jignAsa when it is just quiding us to the further sAdhana??

Because jijnAsa, meaning jnAtum icChA, the desire to know, is satisfied
when Brahman is known through shAstra pramANa. It is antya pramANa, because in reality there is no pramANa prameya vyavahAra for Brahman.

  

So what is actually doing any sAdhana post jnAna? Nothing in reality - guNAh guNeShu vartante iti mattvA na sajjate as the gItA says. The mind and the indriyas are engaging in vAsanA kshaya and manonAsha, but the jnAni is not doing anything in reality.

Yes, it is possible that even after tattvajnAna, the mind can get affected by certain events around it, but in reality it does not bother the jnAni, because he is Brahman.

  

moksha is not something that can be qualified by an adjective like "only" - it is the highest puruShArtha after all. There is also no permanent loss of mental peace - it is just the nature of anAtma to be subject to change and affected by pUrva vAsanA. That is true for the jnAni's anAtma, as it is for the ajnAni's - the difference is that the jnAni's anAtma is more refined than the ajnAni's.

  

vAsanA kshaya and manonAsha are simply techniques for managing the anAtma till the end of prArabdha.

  

It is for this reason that there is a type of sannyAsa called vidvatsannyAsa - there was no need for yAjnavalkya to walk out of his home - he was already a jnAni, a mukta puruSha. He did it to focus on jnAna niShThA.

  

If there was nothing for the anAtma to do post jnAna, why have a category of sannyAsa called vidvatsannyAsa at all? It is a time devoted to the management of anAtma, not for the attainment of jnAna or moksha - because the former (jnAna) has already been attained and the latter (moksha) is simply the vidvatsannyAsi's nature.
