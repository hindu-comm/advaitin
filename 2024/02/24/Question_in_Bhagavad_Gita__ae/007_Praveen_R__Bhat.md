Namaste Kalyanji,

  

Since you're thinking in English first, i have you an option. Unto and to don't have the same meanings at all times, eg offering our surrendering unto Ishvara, where one is saluting, but giving something away to someone, where the recipient is thankful. The cases are different. To doesn't always mean dvitIyA, sometimes it means ShaShTI also. saptamI also translates as towards, whereas one may think dvitIyA as towards and to might seem similar.

  

The source of fear takes panchamI. chorAt bhayaH translates as fear of the thief. Will we say of is a ShaShTI then!

  

It takes time to start thinking only in Sanskrit cases. Maybe you already knew this. I just shared how i learnt differences and use them in my teaching. If it helps, well and good. Else, i suggest you to try to use English or other language phrases/prepositions that mark a difference in Sanskrit cases.
