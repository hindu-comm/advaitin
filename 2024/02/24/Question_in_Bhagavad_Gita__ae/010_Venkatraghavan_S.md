Namaste Subbuji,

  

As you say, Shankaracharya comments on the word संन्यस्य as निक्षिप्य in 3.20. Interestingly, here also he comments on the word आधाय as निक्षिप्य.

  

ब्रह्मणि ईश्वरे आधाय निक्षिप्य ‘तदर्थं कर्म करोमि’ इति भृत्य इव स्वाम्यर्थं सर्वाणि कर्माणि मोक्षेऽपि फले सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा करोति यः सर्वकर्माणि.

  

The क्षिप् root with नि prefix takes a few meanings ([https://kosha.sanskrit.today/word/sa/%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B7%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%8D?q=%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B7%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%8D&scope=kosha](https://kosha.sanskrit.today/word/sa/%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B7%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%8D?q=%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B7%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%8D&scope=kosha)

  

नि throw down, place, put, apply;

deposit, grant, deliver, entrust, appoint (2 acc. or acc. of pers. &

loc. of th.);

leave, abandon, give up.

  

In English we use the word 'into' when something has been dropped into something - which can be used both in a saptamI (hence the 'in') and dvitIya (and the 'to') sense.

  

Here, the meaning of the saptamI with this root can mean dropping / giving up / renouncing all karma-s into Ishvara, like in the verse 3.20 मयि सर्वाणि कर्माणि संन्यस्य.

  

The object of the act of renunciation is karma, and that takes a dvitIya meaning. The locus of the act of renunciation (ie the renunciation is into Ishvara) is saptamI.

  

As you mention, there too Shankaracharya uses the word संन्यस्य to mean निक्षिप्य only -मयि वासुदेवे परमेश्वरे सर्वज्ञे सर्वात्मनि सर्वाणि कर्माणि संन्यस्य निक्षिप्य he says.

  

Thus the आधाय of verse 5.10 = संन्यस्य of verse 3.20 = निक्षिप्य, and consequently we have the saptamI in ब्रह्मणि of 5.10 and मयि of 3.20. I think in the cases, what is meant is giving up / renouncing the karma-s into Ishvara.
