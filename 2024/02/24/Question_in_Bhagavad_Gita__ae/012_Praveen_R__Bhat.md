I assumed English because your post translated phrases in English. Hindi is definitely much better, but what is obvious to you as panchamI with चोर से डर, may not be to other Hindi speakers as ShaShThI with चोर का डर (not karmaNi), just like in English or Sanskrit! Or what you think as परमात्मा को समर्पित करें maybe परमात्मा में समर्पित करे

for others.  
  
There are just too many case meanings that we get contextually as we study more and more. eg, saptamI translates as in/ on(adhikaraNe), at a time (kAla), at a place (desha), when (sati), w.r.t. (viShaye), among (nirdhAraNe), object of action (nimitta) and more....or anything else in chhAndasa/ ArSha prayoga.

  

There are many ways to learn; I suggested justone. And the reason is because I have seen costudents and students struggling with "why not this case?" more than learning "oh, the case carries this meaning as well". I prefer the latter. They leave more language saMskAras than translation saMskAras.  
  
Best wishes.
