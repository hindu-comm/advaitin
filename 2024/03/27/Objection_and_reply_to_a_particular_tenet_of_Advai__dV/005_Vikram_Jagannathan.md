Namaskaram Shri Suresh ji,  
  
Please correct me if I am missing your intent..

  
  
\>\> I agree with your explanation, but based on the shAstra vAkya of sarvam khalvidam Brahma - is it correct to equate the essential Self with everything - including Guru-Shastra-Shishya?   
  

Yes, it is correct to equate the essential Self, our own Self, with everything including guru-shastra-shishya. But this doesn't then mean guru is directly equated to shastra or to shishya. This is just as saying that the ocean, waves, foam, bubblesetc. are all equated with essential water; but doesn't then mean the ocean is directly equated to a wave etc. The sense of the equation is the underlying identity and not the perceived distinctions within.

  
  
\>\> Because even though the Self is unrelated to anything, every relation is rooted and attributable to THAT only! Entire jagat including the Shastra has no independent existence apart from THAT.  
  

Just as when a rope is misapprehended as a snake, the snake in its entirety is rooted and attributable to that rope alone. But still the rope is ever unrelated to the snake!  
While it is correct to say that the misapprehended snake does not exist independent of the rope, Advaita questions whether the snake truly even dependently exists!

When a lump of clay is molded into a certain shape, we call it a pot; but from the clay's perspective, it has always been a clay and there is nothing really called a 'lump' or a 'pot' that is related to the clay itself.  
While the independent existence of the entire jagat, including the shastra, is questioned by other Vedanta sampradhayams. Advaita alone questions even the truth of the 'dependent existence' of the entire jagat & shastra!  
Other sampradhayams take the dependent existence of the jagat as real and exists, but Advaita says that even this dependent existence of the jagat is mithya and does not truly exists.

  
  
\>\> In that case MithyAtva would be the apparent "separate existence of all the many name/forms" which would eventually merge in the non-dual Self! Not sure if I am straying away or losing focus :)  
  

A small correction here. As explained above, in Advaita it is not that the 'separate existence' (or 'independent existence') of jagat alone is mithya; even the 'dependent existence' of the jagat is mithya only.  
Chaitanya (Self / Atman / Brahman) alone truly exists; and in this Chaitanya there is the manifestation of vasanas in the form of antahkarana. While the antahkarana is manifested, the entire jagat is cognized. This cognition is dependent on the Chaitanya alone. But when the antahkarana/vasanas is sublated or transcended, it is not that the jagat merges / vanishes and one would just see some sort of hazy white cloud everywhere around. In the absence of antahkarana/vasanas there is no more experience or perception at all.

  
  
\>\> Long time ago I remember having asked the same question and at that time Sri Subbuji had mentioned that "TAT" pada viveka will not arise without shAstra. I would like to understand more on that.  
  

Of course Shri Subbu ji is correct and I will defer to him for more clarification. I believe the key here is 'viveka' or the true discriminative understanding of the 'TAT' pada's svarupa versus experienced distinctive attributes.

  
  
\>\> Interestingly, Swami Krishnanandaji actually says -  
\>\> "That historical circumstances required Acharya Sankara to consider Scripture as the final authority would not preclude the great logical acumen which is demonstrated in his writings, which can stand independently as a supreme philosophical system, even without any reference to Scripture."  
  

While I do not consider myself qualified to comment on swamiji's statement, I am sure there is more to it (or a deeper significance) that rationalizes with Advaita philosophy of Shastra being the independent and sole means regarding knowledge of Brahman. It could be related to the particular question that compares philosophy that "accepts versus not accept" scriptural testimony as a pramana. But please keep in mind that in Advaita even this shastra is sublated on aparokshanubhuti.
