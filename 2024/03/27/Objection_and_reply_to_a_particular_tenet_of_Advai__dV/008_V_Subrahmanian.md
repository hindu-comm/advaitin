Dear Ananda

  

Thanks for the Brihadaranyaka reference. In my understanding, the case of 'finding a lost animal by following its foot markings' is not similar to the case that has been discussed in the article. In the analogy, the footprints are not mithya; they are a definite clue to the path the animal has traversed. In the present case the entire exercise involving teacher, student, teaching, etc. is ultimately not absolutely real. I do not see a difference between the technical meaning of 'mithya' and 'relatively real' and 'unreal' What is carefully distinguished is the analogy of 'hare's horn, sky flower' type of 'asat' or 'atyanta asat' or 'non-existent' objects. That is the reason Advaita holds the rope-snake type as 'sadasad vilakshanam, mithya/anirvachaniyam'. The 'teacher, teaching, etc.' case is that of sadasad vilakshanam.
