Namaskaram Suresh-ji,

  

If you see a snake where there is rope, the shastra telling "This is a rope" or giving instructions "Go turn on the light, look closely, poke it with a stick ..." - all that is meaningful to you.

  

But if you already realize there is rope only, you don't need the shastra to tell you anything and its instructions are irrelevant to you. They may even appear nonsensical or fanciful because they seem to imagine a problem that is nonexistent (in your awareness).

  

Stating line 2 above as a fact does not mean line 1 is invalid or undermined for those who see snake and are suffering because of it. In the same way, however advaitins speak of NB/SB or jnana/moksha, that should not be construed as negating the relevance of shastra for the ignorant.

  

People here argue over what mithyatva means from some technical or theoretical angle; you are mixing it up with speculated practical outcomes that cannot become reason to decide what *should be* true or not.
