Ok. What is the point? You know that advaitins distinguish mithya from non-existence, asat, unreal. They say mithya is an ontological category that is neither sat nor asat. (I mean many - not including Bhaskar-ji and co.)

  



> It only means "separate existence"
>
> while in reality jiva/Ishwara/shAstra exist in such a way that the
> Self alone is.

  

It means duality of nama-rupa. It means the fundamental divide in consciousness of seer and seen, self and not-self, enjoyer and joy, as Ishvara and His appearance, as jiva shastra jagat. If you say the Self alone is and still affirm jiva, shastra, Ishvara in distinct terms, then you are in vyavaharika standpoint that is the realm for words and thought, and where you think of Brahman at least in terms of a fundamental duality: Existence + Consciousness, Self + manifest appearance. This standpoint is mithya because thisdivide is known to be asat (inapplicable for consideration) in the paramarthika standpoint and yet it is the truth in vyavaharika.

> sense of separation and

But, we are not debating about the condition of the ajnani. For the jnani, there is no *true* sense of separation either. That is the point. Whatever sense the ajnani thinks is real, for the jnani if at all it comes into awareness, it is sublated as mithya or only an appearance of Ishvara or Self - the separation lacks fundamental or substantial reality.

The terms useful, useless and goal are inapplicable for the jnani whose Will you said is merged withIshvara's. We cannot assign our injunctions upon the jivan-mukta. He has nothing to gain or avoid, under or beyond the sun, because everything every which way we point to is Brahman only. They are ournitya karma to do and not to avoid; a jnani may appear to do it or not but whatever we see him doing is in accordance with dharma, and*from our standpoint*is for loka hita and Ishvara arpana- for heknows no separate self apart from the Self of All.

That is the right perspective for an ajnani who identifies self with body-mind and thinks he is a part of Ishvara'suniversal form. He does not realize the non-duality of Self and hence thinks in terms of the duality.

  

For such an ajnani, of course there is a duality of dharma vs adharma. I already said this: The duality of dharma vs adharma belongs to this standpoint. It is a relative reality, corresponding to ignorance of the non-duality of Self. Dharma leads the jiva towards knowledge of Brahman and adharma leads away.
