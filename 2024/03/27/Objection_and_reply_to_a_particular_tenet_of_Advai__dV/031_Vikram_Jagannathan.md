Namaskaram Shri Suresh ji,

  

Shri Putran ji has covered the points in great & accurate detail. Let me just add three more points from Advaita perspective:  
  
1. ‘You’ are already Nirguna Brahman. There is no ‘attainment’ of Nirguna Brahman or anything else whatsoever different from what ‘you’ already always are. You just don’t realize it due to your ignorance of yourself. No amount of karma can remove ignorance or change what you currently are here and now. This is because karma only operates withinthe realm of ignorance. Only knowledge of yourself can remove the ignorance of yourself. Knowledge is gained per the directions of Shastra, under the guidance of a guru, with Isvara's grace and blessings. Karma and upasana, in their entirety with dharma, prepares the ground in you for the genesis of this knowledge.

  
2. As mentioned several posts ago, it is important to note that the mithyatva of Shastra is only truly realized by a jivanmukta. For others, the Shastra is as real as the bondage that the Shastra directs us to overcome. After realization, it is perfectly acceptable for the Shastra to be mithya just as the bondage it successfully guided us to overcome. In other words, the reality & utility of the Shastra is closely tied to our sense of bondage and genuineness to overcome. It is understandable for an ignorant person to question, criticize and even deny the Advaita mithyatva of all objects. The intent of our (ignorant) understanding & discussions about mithyatva is only to build the intellectual shraddha, viveka and vairagya. By definition, mithya appears as real under ignorance. For the ignorant, that entity which is otherwise termed as mithya is definitely real as experienced, and no two ways about it! But that’s just ignorance at play and doesn’t require the definition and understanding of the term ‘mithyatva’ to be changed. A commonly used fantastic example to illustrate this is the utility of the pole during pole vaulting.

  
3. This also answers your question on the expected defense of Shastra from antagonistic questions. There are several responses from our perspective, here is one distinct counter questioning response - Do you know who ‘you’ are? Do you believe you are the individual entity comprising the desires, thoughts, knowledge, antahkarana, senses, body (or a subset of these)? Are you an infinitesimally small entity interacting with family and people in this vast universe? Do you believe that there is some misery, sorrow, fear, bondage in the current state of affairs and that you are looking for some sort of permanent freedom from these? Then the Shastras, as explained by your guru, are your only means for achieving what you are looking for. They are as real and relevant as your concerns and expectations. You don’t believe in Shastras or our sampradhayam, fine, be it so. But then also temper your expectation on immediate and direct realization of permanent freedom from your genuine concerns! On the other hand, if you truly have no concerns whatsoever and are absolutely & unconditionally happy, it is perfectly fine that the Shastras do not have any more utility value for you!

  

prostrations,

Vikram
