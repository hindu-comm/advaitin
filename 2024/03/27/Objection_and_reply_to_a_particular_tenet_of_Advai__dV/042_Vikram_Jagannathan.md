Namaskaram Shri Suresh ji,  
  
\<\<  
But ultimately all differences gets reconciled in Consciousness/Self/Brahman. This includes all vedAntic view points like Advaita/Dvaita/V.Advaita  
\>\>  
  
Agreed, but there is a fundamental difference between the three sampradhayams.  
Per Dvaita, in absolute reality there is sajatiya bheda (within distinct jivas and jagat) and vijatiya bheda (between jiva and jagat).  
Per Visishtadvaita, in absolute reality there is no sajatiya bheda or vijatiya bheda; but there is svagata bheda (internal distinction between Brahman and His prakaras of chit and achit).  
Per Advaita, in absolute reality there is no sajatiya bheda or vijatiya bheda or svagata bheda. But encompassing the other two schools, all 3 bhedas are accepted in vyavaharika sense.  
  
All philosophical differences between these schools originate from this fundamental distinction.  
  
I would like to bring your attention to a brief overview I wrote couple of months ago - [https://archive.org/details/reflections-on-the-three-foundational-tenets-of-advaita/](https://archive.org/details/reflections-on-the-three-foundational-tenets-of-advaita/)

Namaskaram Shri Bhaskar ji,  
  
Skipping your comments on mithya, anirvachaniya, rajju-sarpa...

  
  
\<\<  
First of all we have to realize that brahma jnana is NOT vyavahAra abhAva jnana but IT IS vyavahARa badhita jnana.  
\>\>  
  

1\. No; this understanding is incorrect. Brahma jnana (knowledge of true nature of Brahman) is independent of vyavahara jnana and is neither vyavahara abhava jnana nor vyavahara badhita jnana.  
2. For illustration, Sun and sunlight is neither amavasya nor purnima. Whether the moonlight is seen or not seen has no bearing on the knowledge and direct perception of sunlight.  
3. Brahma jnana is the svarupa jnana or just svarupa or pure Jnana or pure Chaitanya.  
4. Whereas, vyavahara jnana is antahkarana vritti jnana. Even the akhandakara vritti jnana is only an aspect of vritti jnana.  
5. Vritti jnana or vyavahara jnana manifests only when antahkarana functions, but svarupa jnana is One and ever constant Chaitanya.  
6. When antahkarana is subdued, there is no more vritti jnana or vyavahara jnana and that is simply termed as 'vyavahara abhava jnana' (truly speaking, there is no such thing).  
7. An ignorant person is associated with the individual BMI and believes in the satyatva of the vyavahara jnana. Such an empirical natural knowledge is just called 'vyavahara jnana'.  
8. A jnani, though seen to be associated with an individual BMI, is one who has the direct immediate realization (aparokshanubhuti) of svarupa jnana. This is sadyomukti.  
9. At this point, there are two possibilities from the perspective of the BMI erstwhile associated with the jnani; please note this is not from the jnani's perspective, but only the erstwhile BMI's perspective  
10. A) Either the BMI & underlying vasanas are completely sublated or B) under the influence of vasanas / prarabdha Karma, the BMI continues to function in the mere presence of Chaitanya.  
11. In the first case, the erstwhile physical body is dropped dead and the subtle & causal bodies are just dissolved. This is videhamukti.  
12. At this point there is no more any vritti jnana or vyavahara jnana whatsoever. Clearly 'vyavahara badhita jnana' is not applicable and even the term 'vyavahara abhava jnana' doesn't make any sense. There is just nothing termed 'vyavahara'. It is svarupa jnana or Chaitanya alone.  
13. In the second case, the erstwhile BMI continues to function under the influence of vasanas / prarabdha Karma. The functioning BMI manifests vritti jnana and perceives the material world around. This is vyavahara jnana. This is jivanmukti.  
14. But at this point, due to realization of the true nature of svarupa jnana, there is no more satyatva observed in the vyavahara jnana. This vyavahara jnana has been falsified (badhita). The objects of the vritti jnana is realized as mithya; and the vritti jnana itself is realized as mithya. The vyavahara jnana is now mithyAjnana (term deliberately used in itrans). This is also called 'vyavahara badhita jnana' to differentiate from the 'vyavahara jnana' of the ignorant.  
15. Therefore, 'vyavahara abhava jnana' and 'vyavahara badhita jnana' are both only in respect to functioning BMI or 'vyavahara' and Brahma jnana or Chaitanya has no dependence on this.

  
  
\<\<  
For vyavahAra abhAva we have to literally ‘smash’ the existing world or we have to wait till mahA pralaya.  
\>\>  
  

Not necessary. There is vyavahara abhava as soon as the BMI is sublated or transcended through whatever means.

  
  
\<\<  
Seeing the bheda is not avidyA having the satyatva buddhi in bheda AkAra is avidyA clarifies bhAshyakAra somewhere  
\>\>  
  

This too is inaccurate. Perception of something 'else' (bheda) itself is avidya. Neha nanasti kinchana. Satyatva buddhi in bheda is the ajnana of ajnani. This definitely is avidya. Mithyatva buddhi in bheda is the badhita jnana of a jnani. Even the badhita jnana (which, as seen above, is only an antahkarana vritti jnana) is a particular manifestation of antahkarana and hence a product of avidya alone. However, this avidya is distinct from that of an ajnani, as this avidya doesn't result in additional Karma or samsara (like a roasted seed). In that sense one may say that this is not the binding avidya. But this is never the true jnana (svarupa jnana / Brahma jnana) but only an after-effect of true jnana.

  
  
\<\<  
Bheda (changing) is quite natural to this asatya jagat but for this upAdAna and nimitta is that parabrahman only  
\>\>  
  

I am skipping your comment on 'asatya' jagat, but just calling out that in Sutra Bhashya 2.1.22, Bhagavan Bhashyakara explicitly states that the "creatorship of Brahman is also removed on the genesis of knowledge of non-difference". The karanatvam of this 'asatya' jagat is also only mithya and an effect of avidya. Conceding vyavahara & bheda, the rest of your comments and scriptural references are applicable.
