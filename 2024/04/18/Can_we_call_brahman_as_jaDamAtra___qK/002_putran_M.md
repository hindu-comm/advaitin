Namaskaram,

  

My understanding:

  

Advaita also calls Brahman as Ishvara who is Consciousness of the jiva-jagat world of objects. This divided perception of Brahman - as Seer-seen - is what makes sense to us. "Pure consciousness" is not something that will make mental sense to us because we are operating the mind within a standpoint where Brahman can only be known as Ishvara. Pure consciousness is like undivided space whereas our mental consciousness corresponds to the divided cognition of space, as the pot-spaces (jiva-jagat) and (for the jnanis) as the Space (Ishvara) that appears as if the pot-spaces (that are only denotations of Space). That is the limit of the mind. Why does the jnani see all as Ishvara the Self? Because, the jnani realizes the absolute non-duality of Space in the substratum standpoint (beyond the reach of words and mind); therefore he has realized that the division in the context of mind and perception is apparent (mithya) and a superimposition on "pure Consciousness". He therefore "sees" that Self in all things (including his own witness-self) and all in the Self (Ishvara).
