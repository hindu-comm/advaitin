Namaste Lokesh ji,

  

There are a few assumptions in your email - I don't understand their basis. Perhaps you can clarify.

  

1\) Why does something that is beyond comprehension have to be non-existent? Is there a rule that everything existent must be known?

2\) Why does something that is beyond comprehension be without meaning? Are you saying that the word "Brahman" has no corresponding object that is denoted by it, or are you saying that the word "Brahman" is devoid of meaning, nonsensical - nothing is comprehended when one hears it? There is a difference between the two views - the phrase "hare's horn" has no corresponding object, but the phrase is not nonsensical. One can understand what it means.

3\) You say that Brahman is beyond comprehension because its comprehension requires theinert. Your description was that Brahman and the inert are anyonyasiddha, interdependent. Do you mean that the existence of Brahman requires the existence of inert objects? Or do you mean the comprehension of Brahman requires the existence of inert objects?

4\) If the former, in your view, are you saying that because there are no objects in deep sleep - the entity that is "you", ceases to be, and a new "you"emerges when waking up?  

5\) If the latter, what is the entity that is the seer of nothingness in deep sleep, and the void between thoughts, when waking? Is one not aware of the existence of that seer of nothingness?

  

Kind regards,

Venkatraghavan
