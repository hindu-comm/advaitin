नमस्ते Venkatraghavan जी

  

You have posed intriguing questions. I will try to answer them to the best of my abilities.

  

\> 1) Why does something that is beyond comprehension have to be non-existent? Is there a rule that everything existent must be known?  
  

Firstly, "comprehension" is an ambiguous term. There can be two meanings of it - one that is subjective, and the other objective one. Subjective comprehensibility refers to one's limitation in understanding a given expression. Objective comprehensibility on the other hand is expression's own contradictory nature that makes it incomprehensible to anyone trying to understand it logically.

  

When I say वन्ध्यासुत I refer to objective comprehensibility, an inherent contradiction that makes it impossible for this expression to have any meaning and hence it is meaningless. वन्ध्यासुत means child of a childless woman. No matter how intelligent one is, one never would be able to apprehend the meaning of वन्ध्यासुत. Another example would be चतुर्भुजत्रिकोण (triangle with four sides). A triangle has three sides, so the expression चतुर्भुजत्रिकोण is meaningless.

  

Therefore, I say वन्ध्यासुत or चतुर्भुजत्रिकोण cannot be existent, for they have no objective comprehensible meaning. Similarly, the expression चिन्मात्र (pure consciousness) is equally incomprehensible because चित् means perception/consciousness (चेतति इति चित्) and when you say चिन्मात्र ie consciousness without an object, it is an incomprehensible expression for consciousness has no meaning if there's nothing to be conscious about. Arguing for pure consciousness is like arguing for sight or seer without anything to be seen.

  

I hope this clarifies my position.

  

\> 2) Why does something that is beyond comprehension be without meaning?

  

By the term "meaning", I do not refer to the mere outer apprehensibility of an expression but rather it's integral meaning. For example, expressions like "liquid without liquidity", and "A exists and doesn't exist at the same time", are contradictory and lack any coherent meaning. In that sense they are meaningless.

  

\> Are you saying that the word "Brahman" has no corresponding object that is denoted by it, or are you saying that the word "Brahman" is devoid of meaning

  

No. I intend to analyze the expression चिन्मात्र and not ब्रह्मन् in this post.

  

\> 3) You say that Brahman is beyond comprehension because its comprehension requires theinert. Your description was that Brahman and the inert are anyonyasiddha...

  

I make no comments on Brahman. Let's keep the same terminology to avoid any confusion. The discussion is about interdependability of चेतन (consciousness) and जड (inert). I propose that one doesn't exist without the other. चेतन (consciousness) has no meaning without जडता (inert) and जड (inert) has no meaning without चेतनता (consciousness).

  

\> 4) If the former, in your view, are you saying that because there are no objects in deep sleep - the entity that is "you", ceases to be, and a new "you"emerges when waking up?

  

I make no such claim about deep sleep. I am making a simple statement that "**consciousness has no meaning if there's nothing to be consciousness about, therefore the expression चिन्मात्र seems meaningless to me.**"

  

If you want to refute my position using the above deep sleep argument, you are welcome to put forward this as an argument. Then I can try answering it.

  

\> 5) If the latter, what is the entity that is the seer of nothingness in deep sleep, and the void between thoughts, when waking? Is one not aware of the existence of that seer of nothingness?

  

If according to you, there's nothing in deep sleep, then why are you looking for a seer? Indeed you see something in nothingness and contradict yourself.
