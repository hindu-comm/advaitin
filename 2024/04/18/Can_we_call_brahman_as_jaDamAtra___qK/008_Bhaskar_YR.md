Therefore, I say वन्ध्यासुत or चतुर्भुजत्रिकोण cannot be existent, for they have no objective comprehensible meaning. Similarly, the expression चिन्मात्र (pure consciousness) is equally incomprehensible because चित् means perception/consciousness (चेतति इति चित्) and when you say चिन्मात्र ie consciousness without an object, it is an incomprehensible expression for consciousness has no meaning if there's nothing to be conscious about. Arguing for pure consciousness is like arguing for sight or seer without anything to be seen.



I hope this clarifies my position.



praNAms Sri Lokesh prabhuji

In the meantime most of the times we should not go by the literal meaning of words when doing the siddhAnta nirNaya..the lakshyArtha needs to be understood as we should agree that shruti is trying to explain us which is infact inexplicable/incomprehensible.
Your arguments / observations sounds like to say brahman is satyaM, jnAnam and anataM brahma there should exists something like asatyaM, ajnAnam and sAntaM otherwise these are all meaningless!! Na…that is not the way it should be understood atleast in my opinion. Let us take mAndUkya mantra where shruti explains IT is neither antaH prajnA, nor bahirprajnA, nor prajnAna ghana etc. does it mean consciousness is like Gagana Kusuma or Chaturbhuja trikONa!!?? No I don’t think so. All these efforts put forth by shruti to convey the tattva which is beyond the reach of vAk, mana…(yato vAcho nivartante aprApya manasa saha).
bhAshyakAra somewhere says there is no problem (chyuti) to the seer (sAkshi) even though there is nothing to ‘see’. He always remains as HE IS. I think the term ‘chinmAtra’ should be understood like this contextually without overreading it literally.
