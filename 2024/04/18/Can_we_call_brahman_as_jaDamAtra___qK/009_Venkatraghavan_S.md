> \> 1) Why does something that is beyond comprehension have to be
> non-existent? Is there a rule that everything existent must be
> known?  
>   
> Firstly, "comprehension" is an ambiguous term. There can be two
> meanings of it - one that is subjective, and the other objective one.
> Subjective comprehensibility refers to one's limitation in
> understanding a given expression.



Comprehensibility refers to one's limitation in understanding? Perhaps you mean incomprehensibility. Otherwise, what you have defined is the opposite of the meaningof the word.

> Objective comprehensibility on the other hand is expression's own
> contradictory nature that makes it incomprehensible to anyone trying
> to understand it logically.
>
>   
>
> When I say वन्ध्यासुत I refer to objective comprehensibility, an inherent
> contradiction that makes it impossible for this expression to have any
> meaning and hence it is meaningless.

Each of the words vandhyA and suta are known, the meaning of the phrase is translated as the son of a barren woman. So perhaps when you say something is incomprehensible objectively, you mean that it is inherently contradictory. That is fine. My point is that the words themselves and the phrase itself conveys a meaning. That meaning may be contradictory, but one cannot say that the phrase has no meaning.

  

So the question I have for you is - when you say that cinmAtra has no meaning, are you saying it is inherently contradictory, or that the word is unintelligible? You don't understand the meaning - like the word pyrtxqgl.



> वन्ध्यासुत means child of a childless woman. No matter how intelligent
> one is, one never would be able to apprehend the meaning of वन्ध्यासुत.
> Another example would be चतुर्भुजत्रिकोण (triangle with four sides). A
> triangle has three sides, so the expression चतुर्भुजत्रिकोण is
> meaningless.



>   
>
> Therefore, I say वन्ध्यासुत or चतुर्भुजत्रिकोण cannot be existent, for they
> have no objective comprehensible meaning. Similarly, the expression
> चिन्मात्र (pure consciousness) is equally incomprehensible because चित्
> means perception/consciousness (चेतति इति चित्) and when you say चिन्मात्र
> ie consciousness without an object, it is an incomprehensible
> expression for consciousness has no meaning if there's nothing to be
> conscious about. Arguing for pure consciousness is like arguing for
> sight or seer without anything to be seen.



Not quite, when you argue that there is nothing. Are you saying not knowing that there is nothing? Or knowing that is nothing? If you are arguing that there is nothing, not knowing that there is nothing, then you contradict yourself and commonexperience. If you know there is nothing, who is it that knows?

> meaning?

> By the term "meaning", I do not refer to the mere outer
> apprehensibility of an expression but rather it's integral meaning.

> For example, expressions like "liquid without liquidity", and "A

> any coherent meaning. In that sense they are meaningless.

>
> \> Are you saying that the word "Brahman" has no corresponding object
> that is denoted by it, or are you saying that the word "Brahman" is
> devoid of meaning
>
>   
>
> No. I intend to analyze the expression चिन्मात्र and not ब्रह्मन् in this
> post.
>
>   

I see, I onlyused the word Brahman because the subject of this email said "Can we call ब्रह्मन् as जडमात्र". Never mind, we can stick with cinmAtra, or anything else you prefer. It does not matter what word is being discussed, the question is to whom does the discussion appear.



> I make no comments on Brahman. Let's keep the same terminology to
> avoid any confusion. The discussion is about interdependability of चेतन
> (consciousness) and जड (inert). I propose that one doesn't exist
> without the other. चेतन (consciousness) has no meaning without जडता
> (inert) and जड (inert) has no meaning without चेतनता (consciousness).
>
>   
>
> \> 4) If the former, in your view, are you saying that because there
> are no objects in deep sleep - the entity that is "you", ceases to be,
> and a new "you"emerges when waking up?
>
>   
>
> I make no such claim about deep sleep. I am making a simple statement
> that "**consciousness has no meaning if there's nothing to be
> consciousness about, therefore the expression चिन्मात्र seems
> meaningless to me.**"
>
>   
>
> If you want to refute my position using the above deep sleep argument,
> you are welcome to put forward this as an argument. Then I can try
> answering it.



I am not seeking to refute your position, I am merely asking whether based on the above postulate, is your conclusion that you do not exist in deep sleep?

>   
>
> \> 5) If the latter, what is the entity that is the seer of
> nothingness in deep sleep, and the void between thoughts, when waking?
> Is one not aware of the existence of that seer of nothingness?
>
>   
>
> If according to you, there's nothing in deep sleep, then why are you
> looking for a seer? Indeed you see something in nothingness and
> contradict yourself.
>
>   

I am not looking for a seer. Nor did I say that there is nothing in deep sleep. I asked, what is the entity by which there is the cognition of nothingness? If there was no seer, then who cognised nothingness, to recall it on waking up?

  

The charge of contradiction would be valid if I had said "if there is nothing there hasto be something", but I didn't make such a claim.

  

Theonly claim I'm making in this email is that to know that there is "no thing", there has to be something that knows it. If you don't agree, perhaps you can explain why.
