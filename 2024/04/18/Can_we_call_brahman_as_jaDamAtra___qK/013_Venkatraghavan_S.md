Let us take these two positions for further analysis:

  

> \> ...is your conclusion that you do not exist in deep sleep?
>
>   
>
> Depends on what you identify as "you". If you identify the person
> देवदत्त as a collection of impressions, then देवदत्त exists during deep
> sleep because those impressions give rise to an almost similar person
> that you identify as देवदत्त on the following day. If you identify the
> person देवदत्त as consciousness, then देवदत्त doesn't exist during deep
> sleep because there's nothing to be conscious about.
>
>   

  

and

  

> 
>
> \> Theonly claim I'm making in this email is that to know that there
> is "no thing", there has to be something that knows it. If you don't
> agree, perhaps you can explain why.
>
>   
>
> "To know that there is \*no thing\*" means "absence of knowing
> anything". Therefore, there's no requirement for a knower here. So, I
> do not agree that there has to be someone that knows it.

  

I see. How do you know that there was the absence of cognitions in deep sleep?

  

1\) If you say that it was based on the recollection "I slept well, I didn't know anything then" upon waking up, that recollection is the very basis for there being a knower in deep sleep. A memory is recalling a prior experience. For that there has to be a prior experience - the experience of the absence of cognitions, in your hypothesis. If that is the case, there has to be a prior experiencer,a knower.

  

2\) If you say that is based on the inference - "If there had been cognitions in deep sleep, I would have known it. I did not know anything, therefore there were no cognitions in deep sleep" and "because there were no cognitions in deep sleep, there was no consciousness".

  

a) Then all you are doing is conflating cognitions with consciousness - because you are using the same logic to infer the absence of consciousness and the absence of cognitions. Let me explain - There can be no perception of the absence of cognitions in deep sleep, because there is no perceiver in deepsleep according to you. Therefore, in the present, you are inferring the absence of cognitions in deep sleep, based on the lack of memory of those cognitions. However, you are using the same lack of memory, to also imply that consciousness is absent.

  

Essentially, you are conflating cognitions with consciousness - if all you are seeking to prove is the absence of cognitions in sleep, then it is a case of *siddhasAdhanam* - you are proving something that is not in dispute, and moreover, such a proof does not prove the absence of cinmAtra, because the cinmAtra that we talk ofis an entity independent and separate to cognitions. Your inference is also a tautology - the absence of cognitions implies the absence of consciousness, which is nothing but the absence of cognitions. It is saying A is A.

  

b\) Such an inference also fails because you are making that inference in the waking state about what was happening in deep sleep, i.e., an inference about a past event. The only way you can make an inference about a past event in the present, is if there is some present recollection of the experience of the past event, and there was a common knower of the two. If the past had been experienced, we go back to there have being a knower - same as 1) above.

  

c\) Let us leave that aside. The basis of the inference itself is flawed. The inference - if there had been a cognition, I would have known it - is making the absence of knowledge in the present of past cognitions, a proof for absence of cognitions in the past.

  

For that proof to hold good, every cognition that one has had in the past, one should be able to recall later. It is only then, that the inability to recall later can be a hetu, a proof, for the absence of the occurrence of past cognition. However, the inability to recall is not always necessarily because of the absence of prior experience - There are many experiences that one has had, that one has simply forgotten.

  

Therefore, there is no reason to conclude that, *as a* *rule*,every cognition that one possesses, one recalls later. That being the case, the hetu "If there had been cognitions in deep sleep, I would have known it" suffers from the flaw of *vyabhichAra*, because even if there had been cognitions and consciousness, it is possible not to remember those cognitions later.

  

d\) Further, the inference is made on the basis that everything is known by consciousness (If there had been thoughts, consciousness would have known it). However, as stated above, you yourself admit that impressions were present in sleep, why did consciousness not know those impressions in deep sleep?

  

If you say consciousness did not know it, because consciousness was not present then - according to you, consciousness and the inert are anyonyasiddha - if the inert is present, consciousness is also present. If that is the case, as there is an object (impressions) in deep sleep, why is consciousness also not present, in accordance with your postulate? Are you now introducing a rider to your postulate - presence of consciousness requires the presence of objects, with the exception of impressions? Or that impressions are not objects of consciousness?
