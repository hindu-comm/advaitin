\> Essentially, you are conflating cognitions with consciousness...

  

Yes, you are correct. I'm using the word consciousness for चेतनम् which is the same as ज्ञानम् ie cognition according to me.

  

I've mentioned above

  

\>चित् = चेतनम्means perception/consciousness (चेततिइतिचित्)

  

I have defined चित्/चेतनम् as चेतति इति चेतनम् . This is my definition of consciousness and it is the same one that is used in the scriptures.

  

If you disagree with this definition of चित् or चेतन I provided above, please let me know what is चित् according to you and how it is different from ज्ञानं (knowledge/cognition/perception).

  

Panini too defines the धातु (root word) चित् to mean "to know" -

  

चितीँ संज्ञाने
([https://ashtadhyayi.com/dhatu/01.0039](https://ashtadhyayi.com/dhatu/01.0039))

  

Also, Acharyas define चिन्मात्र as ज्ञानमात्र (just knowledge). This further supports that ज्ञान (cognition) and चित् (consciousness) are the same thing.

  

If you disagree, please let me know.

  

Also, I don't think we need to go into the mechanics of deep sleep, memory, or waking to understand my assertion that consciousness cannot exist without "an object to be conscious about". It's a simple logical fact.

  

All that I'm saying is that look, there are three things -

  

1\. A conscious entity, (चित्त)

2\. consciousness, (चेतन)

3\. an object to be conscious about. (चेत्य)  

  

Unless you disprove the claim that consciousness is the same as knowledge, I can map these three entities to the below entities for the sake of simplicity -

  

1\. Knower (ज्ञानी)

2\. Knowledge (ज्ञानं)

3\. Known (ज्ञेयं)

  

Now, AV says that "Knowner only" is the ultimate reality or "Knowledge only" (ज्ञानमात्र) is the ultimate reality. I do not agree with this for the simple reason that **Knowner, Knowledge, and Known are interdependent**. You cannot have one without the other two. You cannot have a *seer* without something to be *seen*. Similarly, you cannot have a *sight* without any *seer* or *seen*. Similarly existence of "mere consciousness" or "mere knowledge" is not possible.

  

We do not need to dive deep into deep sleep or anything else to realize the simple fact I'm presenting above. I hope you see that by definition, the expression "pure consciousness" (चिन्मात्र) or "pure knowledge" (ज्ञानमात्र) i.e. "knowledge without object" (अज्ञेयज्ञानम्) is contradictory.
