However, this does not mean that vRttijnAnam is chaitanyam. Rather, the chaitanya of advaita is self-evident consciousness distinct from the vRtti-s. The primary meaning of the word jnAnam is chaitanyam, its meaning as cognitions (vRtti) is a secondary / figurative one.  



So far, I have not provided any quotations from the scriptures, or from the AchArya-s because it was not clear whether you attributed prAmANya to Vedic scriptures. However, scriptures are quoted above and it is alleged that the AchArya-s take a different view, I am providing quotations that clarify the true intent of the siddhAnta.

  

When AchArya-s define cinmAtra as jnAnamAtra, they do not mean vRttijnAnam, as you seem to assume. In fact, the vivaraNAchArya says, अन्तःकरणवृत्तौ ज्ञानत्वोपचारात् - mental cognitions are figuratively referred to as jnAna. The word "jnAna" in its primary sense refers to Atma chaitanya. jnAna in a figurative sense is used to refer to antahkaraNa vRtti.

  

Whereas, Atma chaitanya is spoken of in the upaniShads in terms like na jaayate mriyate vaa vipashcit / kadAcit (Katha / Bhagavat Gita), satyam jnAnam anantam (Taittiriya), yatsaakshaat aparokshaat (Brihadaranyaka), manaso manah (Kena) etc. If jnAnam meant a finite vRtti as you allege, its description as na jaayate mriyate, anantam, manaso manah etc would be untenable.

  

As Shankaracharya says in the taittirIya bhAShya to the word jnAnam
-ज्ञानं ज्ञप्तिः अवबोधः, - भावसाधनो ज्ञानशब्दः - न तु ज्ञानकर्तृ, ब्रह्मविशेषणत्वात्सत्यानन्ताभ्यां सह । न हि सत्यता अनन्तता च ज्ञानकर्तृत्वे सत्युपपद्येते । ज्ञानकर्तृत्वेन हि विक्रियमाणं कथं सत्यं भवेत् , अनन्तं च ?

The sub-commentator, Anandagiri clarifies -भावसाधन इति । भावव्युत्पत्तिकः । क्रियासामान्यं यद्यप्यन्यत्र भाव उच्यते तथाऽप्यत्र निर्विशेषं चिन्मात्रं भावव्युत्पत्त्या लक्ष्यते सत्यादिशब्दसन्निधानादिति द्रष्टव्यम् ।

  

Therefore, there is an unchanging, self-evident principle postulated by the scriptures, that is the witness of the mind, but which the mind does not witness (yanmanasA na manute, yenAhurmano matam).

  

Why then is vRtti referred to as jnAna in the world? The siddhAnta is that this is because of the tAdAtmya adhyAsa (superimposition of identity) between chaitanyam and vRtti. As a result of this mistaken identity, the jnAnatva of caitanya appears in the vRtti, and the birth and end of the vRtti gives the impression that caitanya is arising and dying, however in reality this chaitanya jnAna is neither born nor does it die.
