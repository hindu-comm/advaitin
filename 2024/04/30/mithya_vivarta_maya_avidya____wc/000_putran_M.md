Quote

  

At the same time, theappearance is also not absolutely non-existent or unreal as it is experienced "as if real". That is why we say it is vivarta, a sort of projection/appearance without real change in the substratum,*due to maya*. Other ways of referring to this include "it is mithya/anirvachaniya, neither real nor unreal"; "it is adhyasa, due to superimposition on the Reality - one thing mistaken for another"; "it is a product of ignorance or ajnana". A famous analogy often given is "A rope mistakenly taken to be a snake". A superimposition due to ignorance that upon removal (of the defect/obstruction) results in right Knowledge; but until there is realization, the apparent snake cannot be negated in absolute terms. After knowledge, the question does not arise in oneself; but where it is raised, one calls it maya or ajnana and points to the real. But each has to realize himself and dissolve the "self-world-God" triple in the 'vision' of the Self.

  

Unquote
