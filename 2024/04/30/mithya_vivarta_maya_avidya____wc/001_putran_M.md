Namaskaram,

  

Let me give an example of where this sort of confusion shows up. (presenting from one viewpoint of advaita below, given that our forum has at least a couple on these topics).

  

Consider this passage:

  

Quote

  

Chandogya Upanishad VI.8.4

  

Shankaracharya bhashya: "... understand Sat which is one without a second and is the supreme Reality; mulam, as the root. **That Sat on which are superimposed due to ignorance, all these transformations that have**

**speech only as the basis, and indeed, are unreal like the appearance ofsnake etc. on a rope -, That is the root of the universe.** "

  

Unquote

  

The "superimposed due to ignorance."

  

How is this to be understood? Part of the issue is translation. I assumed ignorance is translation for "ajnana" of a jiva-ajnani. Checking the sringeri website, the Sanskrit word in the bhashya is avidya.

  

Are we to understand avidya here as an ajnani's ajnana, that is absent in both a jnani and in Ishvara? In that case, do they not see or identify through speech these transformations? The jnani - if we identify him in aBMI-endowed person - knows the difference between nama-rupas and their relations in our world. He will not eat a rock, for instance. Yet he knowsin all the Self and Self in all.

  

The point is, the transformations that have speech only as a basis remain as a mithya world in which transactions continue for the jnani as well. He may not differentiate (the duality as real) as we do but if asked about it, he will say (imu) that only the real (atma) is there that *appears* manifold. He will say the world we see (including himself) is mithya, an appearance due to Ishvara'smaya; adhyasa on Sat; and our assuming otherwise is ajnana.

  

BUT the world is not merely a product of ignorance (ajnana) that goes away from cognition upon attainment of jnana. The duality remains in the jnani's cognition/awareness and a world of transactions based on that duality remains in cognition; however*that too is known to be adhyasa on Sat* - therefore it is mithya so far as the jnani is concerned.

  

Thus we have (mithya) adhyasa on Sat that is **not** contingent on ignorance, and the jnani can relate to it and interact based on it though he knows only Sat in and through that apparent duality.

  

This conundrum can perhaps be avoided if avidya is interpreted in its root sense as just maya=mula-avidya. Then the adhyasa being referred to is non-different from the projection or cognition of Ishvara (even if it be via a jiva manifestation) - for He alone is there who is cause for this superimposition as well. This brings the status of world to mithya - that is asat in paramarthika and yet appears in the cognition of a jnani for whom it is denotation of Self. (Some place in Taittiriya bhashya translation, *denote* is distinguished from *indicate*, but I am not seeing a big difference here, may have to do with translation.)

  

But the Upanishad above does not seem to be making this general statement and seems only to refer to the ajnani's superimposition due to ignorance. The passage appears not to give "the full picture" by bypassing the mithyatvam of the transformations apart from what is superimposed by the ajnani and can be correctlyrealized in jnana.

  

Ch Up. VI.2.3

  

Bhashya: “Since it is Existence itself that is perceived otherwise through the duality of different forms, therefore, there is no non-existence of anything anywhere. That is what we say. As the Nyaya school, after assuming that a thing is different from existence, says again that it has no existence before its birth and after its destruction - it is not assumed by us in that way, at anytime or anywhere, that any word or any thing denoted by the word can be there differently from Existence. But all words and all things that are spoken of with the idea of their being different from Existence, are Existence \[Sat\] only, just as in the world a rope itself is spoken of as a snake; or as a lump and pot etc. are referred to with the words lump, pot,

etc. under the idea that they are different from earth. But **just as the word and idea of a snake cease for one who has the discriminating knowledge about the rope**, and as the words and ideas of pot etc. cease for one who has the discriminating knowledge about the earth, **similarly words and ideas with regardto all other transformation cease for those people who have the discriminating knowledge about Existence.”**

———————————————

  

Once again, this passage is difficult because what does it mean to say words cease for one who has knowledge? It must mean that the duality of referents implied by the words ceases to have *real* meaning because of the realization of the non-dual Sat pointed to by all of them.

  

Whether I call Her Durga or Kali or Parvathi, so long as I know only the same Mother in all namarupas, the duality is superfluous, not in my mind even. Yet the duality remains in appearance and there is a world (an Order, in terms of attire, worship etc) that corresponds with that duality, which the jnani can also align to by "his actions". If someone asks about the "three deities", the jnani says that this duality is apparent only, mithya, due to maya and it is the same Mother in all such forms. Belief in that duality being real is ajnana.
