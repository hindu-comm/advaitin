Namaskaram Shri Putran ji,  
  
Sharing a couple of quick thoughts. This is my current understanding and definitely requires more of my self-reflection for its crystallization. I am not citing direct scriptural references as this is more of my understanding.  
  
The term avidya, or ignorance, could imply different conditions on different context. It seems to me that they are all merely different aspects of a fundamental ignorance which is merely the lack of knowledge of the true nature of Chaitanya. This fundamental lack of correct knowledge brings about the various misunderstandings. The different terms you had mentioned are mithya, vivarta, anirvachaniya, adhyasa, maya, avidya and ajnana. In addition, scriptures typically use the following terms as well to designate avidya (or something similar to it - prakruti, avyakta, karma.  
  
To validate this understanding, just by considering the original SBG shlokas, we fine that Bhagavan Shri Krishna explains knowledge in and as its various aspects. To explain, knowledge in Chapter 2 is defined as the knowledge of the true nature of the Self / Atman / Brahman / Bhagavan. This knowledge of the Self brings about the distinction between the nature of the Self and that of the non-Self. This is the knowledge of the kshetrajna-kshetra or purusha-prakruti. This distinction will help clearly understand the nature of action, agentship, renunciation, and consequently giving up the fruits of action. This is also defined as knowledge, as this clear understanding is possible only on the knowledge of distinction between the Self and the non-Self. This distinction also results in the knowledge of immanence and all-pervasiveness of the Self / Atman / Brahman. Consequently, there is the attitude of sameness towards all other entities and with the various alternating experience of dualities / pairs. This attitude of sameness is also called knowledge as its genesis is from the knowledge of the true nature of the Self.  
  
Some of these points I have included in a brief write-up
-[https://archive.org/details/reflections-on-avidya](https://archive.org/details/reflections-on-avidya)  
  
Avidya as Adhyasa:  
Similarly, the fundamental avidya is that the jiva does not realize its own true nature as pure Chaitanya. Not realizing the true nature as SCA has brought about the sense of incompleteness and that has resulted in desires. These desires are latent as Karma / vasanas and manifest as the antahkarana. This fundamental ignorance has now brought about the adhyasa between the antahkarana (in its manifest and latent sense) and its delimited Chaitanya. Therefore it is said that adhyasa is caused by ignorance. Quoting from Adhyasa Bhashya, this adhyasa is “mithyajnana nimitta”.  
  
Avidya as ajnana:  
At the superficial level, since the fundamental avidya is the lack of correct jnana, avidya is said to be ajnana. This is said to be the avarana shakti of avidya, the power to obscure knowledge. One definition of ignorance is the absence of knowledge. Per this definition, there is no more any avidya or ajnana whatsoever for a jivan-mukta jnani; since the continuation of any lack-of-knowledge is illogical after the dawn of full knowledge. This aspect of avidya as pure ajnana could lend to its nature as jnana-abhava-rupa; since knowledge is known to only remove its own absence and not create or destroy another entity.  
  
Avidya as Karma, avyakta, prakruti:  
Now, desires are brought about by this fundamental ignorance. The Virat Purusha feeling alone or separate or hungry is a sign of the sense of incompleteness and corresponding desire to ‘become’ complete. Because Karma / vasanas are latent forms of desires, they are rightfully equated as avidya too. In the individual sense these are just individualized Karma / vasanas. But on the collective sense, these are called as avyakta (since they are latent unmanifest), prakruti (since like on the individual manifestations of antahakrana, sharira etc., there is the collective manifestation of the entire sukshma and sthula jagat) etc.. As Karma, traces of avidya (prarabdha Karma) is said to persist in vyavahara for a jivan-mukta jnani. This aspect of avidya is said to be the vikshepa, or the power of projection. This aspect could also lend to avidya’s nature as bhava-rupa; since there can be no manifestation from mere non-existence. The fact that the entire Jagat is a manifestation from avidya implies avidya also has a positive aspect. But it is critical to remember that this perspective of continued avidya is only from vyavaharika perspective, particularly from the standpoint of other ignorant people for whom distinctions appear as real.  
  
Avidya as mithya:  
One of the definitions of Mithya (3rd definition in Advaita Siddhi) is its sublatability by knowledge. Since, clearly, avidya or ajnana is overcome by knowledge or jnana, avidya is categorized as mithya.  
  
Avidya as vivarta:  
It is accepted that avidya or ajnana is for the Chaitanya aspect alone as the achit cannot have, by definition, any jnana or ajnana. It is also seen that due to avidya there has risen desires, Karma, antahkarana, sharira etc. Now the question is whether this avidya has a real impact on this Chaitanya aspect or is it only an imaginary impact? Meaning, has there been a real transformation of the Chaitanya into the above entities or is this only an imaginary transformation? Chaitanya, per scriptures, is nitya shuddha buddha mukta svarupa. Consequently the changes are only imaginary or vivarta. Hence avidya and its impact is only vivarta.  
  
Avidya as anirvachaniya:  
The fact that avidya and its impact is only vivarta on Chaitanya, its ontological status is neither as real as Chaitanya nor completely unreal as sky-flower. Vivarta, albeit imaginary, is still an experienced change. Hence the ontological status of avidya and its impact, from the perspective of its experience, is anirvachaniya.  
  
Avidya as Maya:  
Maya is explained in the Vedas as the power through which One appeared as the Many. The situation that avidya has resulted in Chaitanya appearing as the manifold creation results in the avidya - Maya equivalence. Maya is said to be collective Karma / vasanas / prakruti / avyakta whereas avidya is typically identified as the individualized aspect of this Maya. As we all know, there is the recent ongoing debate whether Maya and avidya are the same or different. My personal opinion is that these are merely two aspects of the same fundamental paradoxical concept relevant only in vyavakarika. One should just not miss the forest for the trees.  
  
On a completelypersonal thought - it is funny to me when we (including me) break our heads to exactly understand the true nature of avidya, and hope that that exact specific nature remains unchanging throughout time, when it is clear that avidya by definition escapes an exact understanding and our intent is actually to overcome it any whichever way we perceive / understand it!!  
  
Comments and corrections are most welcome - this goes without saying.
