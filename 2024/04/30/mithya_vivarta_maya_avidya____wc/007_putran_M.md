Did you mean: "Non-duality is not about "It is the same Mother in all such forms""? Duality starts with the identification of different namarupas ("forms").

The "All such forms are non-different from I" is advaita tattva. But expressing what non-difference means is where the Mother comes. Non-duality is to be realized as truth - as you put it. However, when we try to put non-duality in the same bucket in which duality has been affirmed, then our words fall short, or theSelf is known in that dualistic context as "Ishvara, Mother, I, etc.*appearing in/as all such forms*".

  

After reading your mails, I am a bit more comfortable with what I had originally written: "After knowledge, the question does not arise in oneself; but where it is raised, one calls it maya \[mithya\] or ajnana and points to the real. But each has to realize himself and dissolve the "self-world-God" triple in the 'vision' of the Self."

  

The question does not arise because the jnani (in every cognition) has "undifferentiated" awareness of non-dual Self-All alone. When you have knowledge of Brahman, you do not differentiate because you realize all (including namarupa) as Self.

  

But just as Ishvara can come out of the causal state and manifest differentiation, so also (where the question is raised) in the jnani awareness shifts from the paramarthika standpoint to the vyavaharika and thus he can relate to the fact of difference in the world of cognitions. And this world that the ajnani takes to be real, the jnani knows is mithya because he knows the Undifferentiated. But the expression of that knowledge in the context of this world (in vyavaharika standpoint) will (or at least can rightly) be as Ishvara, Mother appearing/manifesting manifold nama-rupa (cognitions).

  

  

When you say "all such forms", you have already affirmed difference and duality. Each form has its own unique identity (defined by its limiting adjunct) that makes it different from every other form. There is no question of saying the forms are non-different from one another. By definition, they are different.

  

So when we go further and say that though two forms themselves are different from each other, they both are non-different from I, then in some way, the forms have to be pointers to I. So that we are identifying a non-dual reality (I or Mother) appearing as if dual in those forms.

  

  

  

Consider: "This Self,which it is the aim of all scriptures to teach, on which differences of agent, action and result have been **superimposed by** **primordial ignorance**, which is the cause of the whole universe, **of which name and form consist as they pass from the undifferentiated to the differentiated state**, like foam, an impurity, appearing from limpid water, and which is **distinct** from that name and form**,** being intrinsicallypure, enlightened and free by nature-this Self, while manifesting undifferentiated name and form, **which are a part of****It,**has entered into these bodiesfrom Hiranyagarbha down to a clump of grass, which are the support of the results of people's actions, and are characterised by hunger etc." BUB 1.4.7.

  

This "Self" when described as above, I have been calling "Ishvara" in my past writings. He is associated with "primordial ignorance" or "Maya" through which He projects/appears as namarupa duality. A**distinct**ion (duality) is maintained between the Self and namarupa (like dreamer vs dream) so long as we are talking of Ishvara. If we wish to account for duality in our standpoint of differentiation, we are constrained to speak of Brahman as Ishvara only, and to posit namarupa duality as a (maya) part of Him, whether differentiated (manifest) or not.

  

But Advaita says even this differentiation is adhyasa on the Undifferentiated Self. These are two standpoints of knowledge of Brahman, but because the causal state (like deep sleep) has no differentiation, it is naturally a pointer to the paramarthika standpoint. We know the causal state of Ishvara is not considered intrinsically Undifferentiated because (imu) the bhashya itself states that the duality in potential form is a part of Ishvara even prior to manifestation - only it is undifferentiated in His awareness. That is how the causal state has to be understood from within the vyavaharika standpoint. But from within itself (Ishvara's nirvikalpa samadhi?), the causal state is awareness/abidance as Self in the satya paramarthika standpoint. Knowing this, the jnani relates to manifest differentiation of vyavaharika as being mithya adhyasa maya, or Mother's appearance in consciousness.
