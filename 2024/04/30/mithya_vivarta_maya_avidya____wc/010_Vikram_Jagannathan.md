What do you think it means to say mithya gets "sublated" by knowledge? We have the rope-snake and mirage analogies. Do they both constitute mithya and in the same sense, or are they different types of mithya?  

\]\]  
  
Sublation, in direct terms, means invalidation or falsification. The sublated object no more creates any direct sense of like / dislike in us. There is no more misunderstandingof what that object is; although whether the object continues to be perceived as something different or not,is a different, potentially less impactful, point. I know that there are different types of adhyasa such as svarupa-adhyasa, samsarga-adhyasa etc. There have been a few discussions in the past (both in Google groups as well as in Advaita List) and there are considerably better scholars in our group who would be able to state this better. I am not well versed in this either. However, one thing is clear that there is a distinction between the adhyasa in rope-snake versus mirage situations. In the former, the mithya object snake is no longer perceived after knowledge; but in the latter, the mithya object mirage continues to be perceived. This implies that there is a distinction in the type / categorization of mithya in these two examples. But in both the cases, there is no more any utilityderived from the mithya object; they are as good as a burntrope or a roasted seed. However, in my personal opinion, these distinctions are trees for the forest. There are some common characteristics, which might suffice as part of Advaita sadhana. Will list them in a bit.

How are we to understand this "neither sat nor asat" definition of mithya, and why is jagat referred to as mithya (I think) by this definition? As you know, the word itself seems wrong for some in our group, at least some of these definitions; since you seem ok with the term, you can perhaps give your present understandings on this term and that will be food for further contemplation for members including myself.  

\]\]  
  
There are some common characteristics of a mithya experience:  
1. Every mithya experience has a real (relatively and finally absolutely) substratum.  
2. Every mithya experience is initially considered as a real (relatively) and a valid experience until there arises a knowledge of otherwise (badhita jnana).  
3. With the dawn of this knowledge, the earlier experience is rendered false or invalid. In other words, the earlier experience is falsified or invalidated by the new knowledge. This is referred to as sublation by knowledge. The object of the earlier experience is now categorized as mithya.  
4. As long as the cognizer and the memory of earlier cognition persists, the previous misunderstood experience also persists as a vivid memory, along with its then impact, although a falsified experience now  
5. Now, in mithya / adhyasa, the perception of an object as something else is the result of various factors.  
6. Until correct knowledge, there is the perception of an object as something else and there is also a misunderstanding that the object is actually something else. For example, Object-A is perceived as Object-B and also understood only as Object-B.  
7. This is complete and proper adhyasa / avidya / ignorance.  
8. With the dawn of true knowledge, the misunderstanding is completely removed.  
9. It is observed that sometimes the cause of mis-perception is also removed  
10. With this, there is no more perception of the object as Object-B.  
11. Consequently there is no more any relationship with the perception of the object as Object-B  
12. But in other times, the cause of mis-perception continues to persist, even after dawn of true knowledge, until its cause is eradicated. In other words, the object continues to be perceived as Object-B, although it is now clear that Object-A alone appears as Object-B.  
13. Even then, the continued perception of Object-B does not bind the cognizer with a sense of attachment (raga) or repulsion (dvesha) towards the object and merely appears as a burnt rope  
14. The fact that the misunderstanding is removed, the earlier experience is rendered as false & invalid, irrespective of whether the mis-perception continues or not.  
15. In this sense, even if a jnani continues to perceive the jagat, there is nothing more for the jnani to derive out of the jagat. There maybe a usefulness for other ignorants, but for a jnani, this experience of jagat is a falsified experience - hence mithya.  
  
This is about Mithya in general. Particularly, regarding sat-asat-vilskshana is indeed the 1st definition of Mithya in AS. Kindly referring to the earlier references for details. My personal concise understanding is that a mithya entity was indeed experienced as true / valid earlier; hence cannot be asat. At the same time, it has been rendered false with the current knowledge; hence cannot be sat. Consequently, as long as I continue to perceive / only remember the earlier experience, that object is categorized as mithya.

A reasonable point of debate here is whether the jnani's awareness should be stated as shifting from one standpoint of knowledge to another, or whether that too is adhyasa of an ajnani only, or whether the jnani, when identified, already is in vyavaharika though having paramartha jnana. What we see however is that the jnani, when identified by a BMI, knows the world of namarupas and relates accordingly, knows the condition of ajnani and teaches us on Brahman. And the standpoint (jagat) corresponding to this type of differentiating knowledge is vyavaharika, which therefore that same jnani will teach to be mithya.

Yes, in agreement with you. My current take on this is as follows:  
  
A simple 2x2 matrix of one reality’s perspective with respect to a reality awareness. This is split for an ajnani and a jnani separately.  
  
Ajnani’s  
Vyavaharika perspective of Vyavahara - Real  
Vyavaharika perspective of Paramartha - Theoretically Real  
Paramarthika perspective of Vyavahara - Theoretically Unreal  
Paramarthika perspective of Paramartha - Theoretically Real  
  
Jnani’s  
Vyavaharika perspective of Vyavahara - Assumed Mithya  
Vyavaharika perspective of Paramartha - Assumed Real  
Paramarthika perspective of Vyavahara - Unreal  
Paramarthika perspective of Paramartha - Real  
  
For an ajnani, aspects related to Paramartha or Paramarthika perspective are only theoretical. It is at most an intellectual understanding, but lacking aparokshanubhuti.  
  
For a jnani, the assumption is that a jnani has a Vyavaharika perspective apart from Paramarthika. This assumption is from an ajnani’s perspective, who is trying to explain the characteristics of a jnani!! But how to we (ajnani) know what exactly is jnani’s awareness of reality? An ajnani sees a jnani’s BMI continuing to interact with the world and hence assumes that the jnani should be perceiving Vyavahara in some capacity. To me, this is arthapatti and I don’t see a problem believing this from an ajnani’s perspective. This is essentially required for the guru-shishya parampara for jnana upadesha.  
  
But the fact is that, a jnani's so called perception of Vyavahara is only from an ajnani’s standpoint. This is because Vyavahara implies duality / plurality and a jnani’s perception of Vyavahara implies some type of existence of Vyavahara from Paramarthika Jnana perspective. This goes against fundamental Advaita that there is absolutely no plurality (either in real or mithya sense) whatsoever in Brahman. Pure Brahman is Paramartha & Paramarthika perspective.
