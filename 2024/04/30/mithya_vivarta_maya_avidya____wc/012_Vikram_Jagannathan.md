Yes, that is correct.

  

From the perspective of certain scripturesand certain verbal testimonies of realized masters, when there is no more any sense of plurality whatsoever, the understanding is that even the memories of earlier experiences are non-existent. Consequentlythere is nothing else apart / distinct from Brahman that can be realized as mithya. The object of context is thus asat from Brahman's perspective.

  

Meanwhile, in the example of Object-B, as you mentioned, the assumption is that in vyavaharika the cognizer and memory persists as mentioned in \#4 "As long as the cognizer and the memory of earlier cognition persists, the previous misunderstood experience also persists as a vivid memory, along with its then impact, although a falsified experience now."

  

Based on my understandingof Advaita siddhanta, the snake-object and the snake-experience, after realization of the rope, is always designated as mithya and never as asat. I do not believe the problem is open-ended for people to consider it as either mithya or asat. Clearly in this example, the snake-object and the corresponding snake-experience both simply vanish on realizing the rope. But their memories are persisting. With reference to the earlier experience we say that "the snake I had seen earlier is \_\_\_\_\_\_\_". In this sentence the blank cannot be 'real' since there is an apposition between 'had seen' and 'is'; the snake 'was' real but 'is' not real. Similarly the blank cannot be 'unreal' since there would be a conflict in the sentence; 'had seen' cannot go along with 'unreal'. Consequently the only word that can fit in is 'mithya'; and the valid sentence is "the snake I had seen earlier is mithya". This simple example demonstrates mithya is sadasat-vilakshana.

  

Hope this clarifies.🙏🙏
