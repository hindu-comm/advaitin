Namaste Putran Ji,

You are correct. Quote is from BUB 1-4-7 and not from BUB 4-1-4. Sorry for the error.

Reg // I don't see how that is equivalent to avidya in Ch U bhashya I cited //,

Context in both BUB (1-4-7) and Ch Up (6-8-4) quoted by you is Origination of Creation at PraLaya. 6-8-4 uses the term सन्मूलम् (sanmUlam) for जगतो मूलम् (jagato mUlam) and states that this includes अविद्या (avidyA) while 1-4-7 uses the word तद्धेदं तर्ह्यव्याकृतमासीत् (taddhedaM tarhyavyAkRRitamAsIt). Context being same, I understand the two terms अविद्या (avidyA) as used in 8-1-4 and अव्याकृत(avyAkRRita) as used in 1-4-7 as equivalent.

Reg // However, when we try to put non-duality in the same bucket in which duality has been affirmed, then our words fall short, or theSelf is known in that dualistic context as "Ishvara, Mother, I, etc.*appearing in/as all such forms*" //,

On Realization, no need \*\* to put non-duality in the same bucket in which duality has been affirmed, then our words fall short \*\*. That is only prior to Realization.

Reg // Did you mean: "Non-duality is not about "It is the same Mother in all such forms""? Duality starts with the identification of different namarupas ("forms") //,

No. Even if the understanding is "It is the same Mother in all such forms", since they are understood as different from \*\*I\*\*, it is duality only. That is what I meant. Duality starts with identification of \*myself\* (jIvAtma) as different from pratyagAtma, the Supreme Self. Reference \*\* ‘उदरमन्तरंकुरुते [अथ तस्य भयं भवति’ (तै. उ. २। ७। १)](https://advaitasharada.sringeri.net/display/bhashya/Taitiriya?page=2&id=T_C02_S07_V01&hl=%E0%A4%85%E0%A4%A5%20%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%20%E0%A4%AD%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%82%20%E0%A4%AD%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%BF)
(‘udaramantaraM kurute’ ‘atha tasya bhayaM bhavati’ (tai. u. 2 \| 7 \| 1) \*\*.

I stop here. But feel compelled to refer to your observation in the earlier post, copied below.

// But the Upanishad above does not seem to be making this general statement and seems only to refer to the ajnani's superimposition due to ignorance. The passage appears not to give "the full picture" by bypassing the mithyatvam of the transformations apart from what is superimposed by the ajnani and can be correctlyrealized in jnana //.

My understanding is it is not a limitation of the Upanishad or Bhashya. It is a limitation on our understanding of the same. It only means further study is needed. I hope you do not misunderstand this as a criticism. But only take it as a suggestion.
