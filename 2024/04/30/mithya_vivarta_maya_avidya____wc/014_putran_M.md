Namaskaram Vikram-ji,

  

This is not to contradict the siddhanta but just to present my logic. First of all, I think there may be room for the question of whether a (differentiated) jagat is mithya or asat for a jnani, because afterall it is presumably some jnani who told us "jagan mithya". So let me just take it as "brahman perspective".

  

The differentiation in jagat is satya in the context of pratyaksha pramana and it is asat in the Brahman's perspective. Thus, it is sat in one standpoint of knowledge and asatin another. Now the ajnani who formally accepts shabda pramana (but doesn't have proper aparokshanubuti), he will say that though duality is true under his primary means of knowledge (pratyaksha), it is ultimately an apparition on Sat. Therefore jagat is mithya (neither sat nor asat) for that person,because thoughit is sat under the pramana he identifies with as primary means of knowledge,he knows it is asat in the context of another accepted pramana.  

  

With the snake, the knower has realized completely its non-existence. His recollection of past belief does not affect his present knowledge that that belief was entirely a figment of his imagination, i.e. adhyasa. Past ajnana should not change the status of his present knowledge of what the entity is and is not. So, it doesn't make sense that he should call the snake tobe mithya simply because he had earlier seen it as snake. That is still asat in his knowledge standpoint where he is presently.

  

So, then is it wrong to call it mithya? Not necessarily, if we understand the standpoint properly. The snake is not mithya because it was seen thus earlier but because it was imagined thus earlier. The rope was not merely seen before directly and solely, but through the obstruction of both dull light and a vasana laden mind. That gives the standpoint of the earlier vision in which context the imagination had validity. It is the standpoint of knowledge ("ignorance") filtered through the obstruction limiting adjuncts, and that "pramana" revealed/superimposed snake-imagination onto rope in the observer. When the obstructions are removed and we attain knowledge of rope, we proritise the unobstructed knowledge of rope as the satya and the superimposed past knowledge of "snake" as being mithya - having a relative validity in the context of a temporal pramana.

  

Reflecting back, the same is true for nama-rupa jagat. Consciousness is conditioned through the filter of maya or avidya, that superimposes nama-rupa jagat onto Brahman. The divided knowledge is not asat because it is valid in the context of this filter: normal pratyaksha operates in this realm as well. But the unobstructed knowledge of the true Entity is what we are seeking, and once we realize that, the earlier filtered knowledge is relegated to ignorance - whether we continue to see through that filter or not. But technically, it is mithya because there is a vyavaharika standpoint of knowledge - provided by maya and in which sense-based pratyaksha is pramana - in which such a divided vision of Brahman is satya.
