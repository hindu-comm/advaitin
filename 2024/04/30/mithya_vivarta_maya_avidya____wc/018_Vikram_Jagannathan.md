Namaskaram Shri Putran ji,

  

\[\[

  
This is not to contradict the siddhanta but just to present my logic. First of all, I think there may be room for the question of whether a (differentiated) jagat is mithya or asat for a jnani, because afterall it is presumably some jnani who told us "jagan mithya". So let me just take it as "brahman perspective".  

\]\]  
  
Similarly, presenting my understanding of our siddhanta: I agree with you. There is indeed the possibility for us to understand that for a jnani the jagat is asat or the jagat is mithya. For a jnani who has instructed us “jagan mithya”, the jagat is indeed mithya. This can also be taken as Brahman perspective, where Brahman refers to Saguna Brahman (Isvara) perceiving the mithya jagat. But this is only in vyavaharika sense. It is also possible that a jnani who has realized the absolute supreme as the Nirguna Brahman, there is no more any awareness of any distinctions (as jagat or even memories of jagat); from such a jnani’s perspective - as what we say - jagat is asat. Of course such a jnani is not going to say that the jagat is asat! It is just our understanding.  
  
\[\[

  
The differentiation in jagat is satya in the context of pratyaksha pramana and it is asat in the Brahman's perspective. Thus, it is sat in one standpoint of knowledge and asatin another.  

\]\]  
  
Correct. Jagat is sat for ajnani and asat for Nirguna Brahman. Just to mention, for a jnani who continues to perceive the jagat, and for Isvara, the jagat is already mithya.  
  
\[\[

  
Now the ajnani who formally accepts shabda pramana (but doesn't have proper aparokshanubuti), he will say that though duality is true under his primary means of knowledge (pratyaksha), it is ultimately an apparition on Sat. Therefore jagat is mithya (neither sat nor asat) for that person,because thoughit is sat under the pramana he identifies with as primary means of knowledge,he knows it is asat in the context of another accepted pramana.  

\]\]  
  
I have a question here - is a mere acceptance and intellectual understanding of shabda, particularly with respect of jagan-mithya, constitutes as valid prama originating from shabda pramana? Or does it particularly require aparokshanubhuti which then is the true prama for Brahma-satyam-jagan-mithya shabda? I was under the impression of the latter, but happy to stand corrected. However, with your logical approach, I agree with you that the intellectual understanding will arrive at jagat being mithya.  
  
\[\[

  
With the snake, the knower has realized completely its non-existence. His recollection of past belief does not affect his present knowledge that that belief was entirely a figment of his imagination, i.e. adhyasa.  

\]\]  
  
A very small correction - as Shri Chandramouli ji had pointed to me earlier (in another email), the snake was not merely a figment of imagination, but indeed an actual modification of avidya on the rope locus. But I get your point.  
  
\[\[

  
Past ajnana should not change the status of his present knowledge of what the entity is and is not. So, it doesn't make sense that he should call the snake tobe mithya simply because he had earlier seen it as snake. That is still asat in his knowledge standpoint where he is presently.  

\]\]  
  
True, the snake ‘is’ asat now. But the same snake ‘was’ sat earlier. Holistically the snake is thus mithya. The fact that an asat snake was perceived as sat earlier gives it the category of being mithya. Had the person not got the knowledge of the rope, the snake would have continued being a sat entity for him! Similarly, one who placed the rope there, the snake will always be asat for him! The situation of mithya only comes to one who was ignorant and now has the knowledge. Perhaps this was just a digression and we are already aligned :)  
  
\[\[

  
So, then is it wrong to call it mithya? Not necessarily, if we understand the standpoint properly. The snake is not mithya because it was seen thus earlier but because it was imagined thus earlier.  

\]\]  
  
I humbly disagree here; well, kinda disagreeing… the point that the snake was ‘imagined’ implies that the snake was only seen earlier but no longer seen now. An essential requirement for mithyatva, per the 2nd definition in AS, is that “the absence of an object in all three periods of time, in the very location in which it appears, is mithya”. There was a perception of a snake which was actually non-existent in the locus of its appearance, namely the rope. This alone makes it mithya. A mere imagination, which I mean as a mere remembrance or recollection, of a snake seen elsewhere is not categorized as mithya. Again, I believe I understand your intent that the snake is not mithya not because it was just seen, but because it did not actually exist but still was perceived (all condensed in the word ‘imagined’); but just wanted to point out for absolute clarity.  
  
\[\[

  
The rope was not merely seen before directly and solely, but through the obstruction of both dull light and a vasana laden mind. That gives the standpoint of the earlier vision in which context the imagination had validity. It is the standpoint of knowledge ("ignorance") filtered through the obstruction limiting adjuncts, and that "pramana" revealed/superimposed snake-imagination onto rope in the observer. When the obstructions are removed and we attain knowledge of rope, we proritise the unobstructed knowledge of rope as the satya and the superimposed past knowledge of "snake" as being mithya - having a relative validity in the context of a temporal pramana.  

\]\]  
  
Agreed.  
  
\[\[

  
Reflecting back, the same is true for nama-rupa jagat. Consciousness is conditioned through the filter of maya or avidya, that superimposes nama-rupa jagat onto Brahman. The divided knowledge is not asat because it is valid in the context of this filter: normal pratyaksha operates in this realm as well. But the unobstructed knowledge of the true Entity is what we are seeking, and once we realize that, the earlier filtered knowledge is relegated to ignorance - whether we continue to see through that filter or not. But technically, it is mithya because there is a vyavaharika standpoint of knowledge - provided by maya and in which sense-based pratyaksha is pramana - in which such a divided vision of Brahman is satya.  

\]\]  
  
This assumes that the inner-instrument (antahkarana) that operates as the instrument of knowledge, its understanding as well as its memory repository, alongside the concept of time, continues to operate even after the realization. Only then can it be said that “the earlier filtered knowledge is relegated to ignorance”. This assumption implies we are talking about the scenario wherein a jnani continues to perceive jagat. Logically, said jagat is then mithya and not asat.  
  
Also, checking for a possible typo - did you intend “in which such a \* undivided \* (instead of divided) vision of Brahman is satya”?  
  

prostrations,

Vikram
