Yes, technically "mere acceptance" does not amount to prama. Most of us (or at least myself) can attest to that. However, one can have sufficient shraddha in shabda that the shraddha transfers to the knowledge provided by that shabda. I want to just run my thoughts on this.

  

If the usage is of avidya being synonymous with Ishvara in the "undifferentiated avyakkrita" sense (if I understood that BUB section somewhat correctly), then ok.

  

I personally am more comfortable with relating to this kind of avidya not as an entity unto itself or identical to the Self, but as an adjunct associated with the Self. a la "Brahman is the cause for the universe". "Existence is conscious, being the agent of visualization". So we say the Self+Maya/avidyais cause for nama-rupa projection. The cause for all creation is the Self as Ishvara or as identified in His manifestations (jiva, ishvara). So, saying that the snake in question is the jiva's imagination is not a contradiction to it being the projection of Self+avidya; we can say that the imagination happens in the mind already enveloping the rope and therefore the locus for the imagined snake is still (almost) the rope.  

  

But the subtlety is different if the rope is regarded as the Sat in ChUp and avidya is distinguished as the cause that superimposes namarupa onto Sat. We have to ask the purpose for this language. It cannot mean a duality of Sat and avidya like the purusha and pradhana of sankhya. It has to mean that the Sat here is denoting Nirguna Brahman of the paramarthika standpoint, completely devoid of inherent duality. And avidya makes it appear saguna by superimposing on Sat the namarupa duality. It is actually denoting the causal link between paramarthika and vyavaharika standpoints of Brahman, through which one in vyavaharika reaches out to the paramarthika. In the realm of words. For a "best approx" understanding that maintains the nirguna status of Brahman.

  

Logically it is not tenable in advaita that this avidya or its namarupa "modification" is both regarded as satya and yet distinct from Sat, because then we would devolve to a form of Sankhya.

  

So this avidya=Maya being referred to here is anirvachaniya.

  

This is how I described this usage for Maya in my Two Perfections file:

  

Quote

  

In order to refer to the non-dual Self, we have to explain duality-based Ishvara as also an appearance of (or adhyasa on) Self. Thus once again, the non-dual Self has to be associated with Maya, through which power It appears as if Ishvara. Thus this Ishvara-appearance has Ishvara again as cause. And so on. We slip into an infinite regress where we can never escape duality and Maya and reach non-duality.

  

Our conclusion is that Maya is a well-defined causal reality in Ishvara=Self+Maya so long as we restrict attention to the Play of duality enacted by Him. But when we seek to understand Maya as the causal place-holder (in Ishvara=Brahman+Maya) linking the paramarthika and vyavaharika standpoints - for why nirguna Brahman appears saguna - then its status changes from satya to mithya, anirvachaniya. For it is sat here and asat there, and also seems like a bridge in the middle that is indeterminable upon scrutiny. Thus, we look upon not only Maya but also its effect which is the entire world of duality as being mithya - that which cannot be said to be sat or asat.

My logic is like this:



Is the rope always there? Yes. Satya.

  

Is the real-snake ever there? No. Asat. There was never a perception of a snake there.It was always a rope there, that appearsto meas snakedue to obstructed vision and furtherimaginations viaavidya.

  

Is the imagined snake there? Yes and No. Theimagined snake appears as if there so long as I imagine it and is gone when I cease to imagine so.

  

SO what is mithya here at the locus of the rope? The real snake or the imagined snake? It is the imagined snake only that \*appears\* to be there, but which is really not there either because the rope alone is there. The real locus of the imagination is the mind (avidya) that is enveloping the rope during perception and hence giving the appearance of both the snake and that the snake is where the rope is. The vritti corresponding to the snake-part of my perception has no correspondence in the locus of the rope,but rope+snake-part is known as "real snake" due to further ignorance of reality.

  

I know this is a roundabout striving to conform with this definition. But technically, the imagined snake is projected/modified by Consciousness/avidya in the mind that is enveloping the rope and therefore \*appears\* to be at the locus of the rope (during the period of imagination), but was in truth absent there at all points of time. So this snake-appearance is mithya adhyasa, due to avidya, as if at the locus of the rope.

We can always add the disclaimer that this is from the viewpoint of an ajnani who sees his identified jnani talking to him about satya,mithya, ajnana. We can say the jnani's will or exercise of shakti is merged with Ishvara'sandis aligned to the unobstructed flow of Dharma (His Order).
