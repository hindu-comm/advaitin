Namaskaram Chandramouli-ji,

  

> Reg // If I say "It is the same Gold in ring-form, bangle-form,
> necklace-form", that is essentially stating "ornaments \[gold
> identified distinctly according to form-appearance\] do not lose their
> svarupa of being gold". You had however objected when I said "it is
> the same Mother in all such forms". I don't see the point of such
> scrutiny on that sentence when you are comfortable with this gold
> analogy //,

My point is that the Gold analogy and Mother analogy are equivalent. One does not separate the malleability from the Gold and one **does not** separate shakti from the Mother; and say "Malleability does this, Gold is substance" or "Shakti does this, and Shiva is svarupa of Shakti". In that sense the analogies are parallel.

  



> What I have stated is the Upanishadic concept of how the diverse
> Creation of inert objects, both Gross and Subtle, evolves through
> transformation (pariNAma) from a single material source (upAdAna
> kAraNa) which itself does not lose its svarUpa in the process. Other
> DarshanAs like nyAya, sAnkhya etc have an entirely different view. The
> analogy is not mine. It is that of the Upanishad.

  

I say Mother=Shiva+shakti. You say Mother = Shiva+Shakti.

  

I say Mother appears/projects as namarupa through her intrinsic shakti.

  

You say, it is not Mother who appears but Shakti that manifests/projects namarupa (onto Shiva). That Shakti and its manifestations have Shiva as unchanging svarupa.

  

The middle-ground would be to say that both of these are just adhyaropa and it is a matter of preference based on what allows each person best to relate to the non-dual tattva.

  

But this can be a point of debate as well. For instance, I usually take (or at least communicate) the stronger position that Ishvara=Shiva+shakti is the satya in vyavaharika (even if ultimately an adhyaropa) whereas that the other Shiva+Shakti is more akin to adhyaropa (even in the context of vyavaharika) in order to indicate the pararmarthikatattva/standpoint of Shiva. And in the latter "adhyaropa", logicallythe Shakti in question is anirvachaniya - but this maynotbe necessary to highlight (if the construct is already admitted as adhyaropa for the sake of ajnani).
