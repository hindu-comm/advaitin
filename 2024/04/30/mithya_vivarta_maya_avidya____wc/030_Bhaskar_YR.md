I had quoted from the Chandogya bhashya earlier:



Chandogya Upanishad VI.8.4



Shankaracharya bhashya: "That Sat on which are superimposed due to ignorance \[*avidya*\], all these transformations that have

speech only as the basis, and indeed, are unreal like the appearance ofsnake etc. on a rope -, That is the root of the universe.****"



Ch Up. VI.2.3



Bhashya: “ Butjust as the word and idea of a snake cease for one who has the discriminating knowledge about the rope, and as the words and ideas of pot etc. **cease** for one who has the discriminating knowledge about the earth,similarly words and ideas with regardto all other transformation cease for those people who have the discriminating knowledge about Existence.**”**



Ø Infact both are exquisitely talking about adhyArOpa (superimposition) and apavAda (subsequent recession) what shAstra’s adhyArOpa is not due to avidyA but it is for teaching purpose and what ajnAni does the adhyArOpa (superimposition) on the jagat is due to his avidyA. Anyway, here is Sri SSS’s view points on two types of adhyArOpa :



- //quote// superimposition like not-self on self is of two different
  varieties. One due to innate avidyA of the common man and the other a
  deliberate device employed by shAstra. Of these two, the ordinary
  human procedure due to avidyA (adhyAsa) is the source of all evil in
  life while deliberate superimposition employed by the scriptures for
  the purpose of teaching is a purely a conventional device aiming at
  ‘enlightening’ the intellect of the enquiring student.//unquote//.



- Sri SSS explains this further by quoting two technical terms from the
  kArika saMvrutti and Kalpita saMvrutti. Here saMvrutti means
  concealing, the common and natural view of the ordinary men under the
  influence of avidyA. upAdhi drushti or parichinna drushti. And
  Kalpita-saMvrutti means the procedure adopted by the scriptures or
  the Acharya as a device for teaching the means of knowing the truth.
  The above shruti statements consisting of both i.e. saMvrutti and
  Kalpita saMvrutti. The kArya-KaraNa prakriya, paNcha kOsha prakriya,
  avasthAtraya prakriya, srushti prakriya, brahman becoming the
  efficient and material cause etc. etc. comes under Kalpita saMvrutti
  by shAstra to ultimately drive home the point that brahman is
  nirvikAri and nirvishesha. In this context shAstra would also helps
  the sAdhaka to eradicate the ‘saMvrutti’ also which is avidyAkruta.
  So from the saMvrutti view point (empirical standpoint) all this is
  certainly born, transformed (eternally changing) so in the region of
  avidyA there is nothing that can be considered to be eternal. It is
  because of this reason only I argued that when the Shabda (veda) is
  eternal the shabdArtha ( the jagat too eternal) if at all there is
  denying both should be denied and only kUtasthA brahman to be accepted
  as ‘nitya’. Anyway when the same is viewed from the pAramArthika
  drushti ‘everything’ is ONE entity (ekaM eva adviteeyaM) and
  considered as they are (yathArtha jnAna) as the unborn self and in
  that view point there neither anAtma vastu nor exists anything that is
  destructible.
- So, what has been seen through avidyA drushti will be eradicated
  completely without any trace after the dawn of jnAna. And what has
  been ‘actually’ there will be realized as a homogenous entity. Snake
  in the rope is saMvrutti due to jeeva’s avidyA. suvarNAbharaNa,
  mrutsAmAnya-mrudghata, ghatAkAsha-mahAkAsha, kArya-kAraNa ananyatvaM
  is Kalpita saMvrutti by shAstra to teach the secondless brahman. I
  have said this multiple times when I tried to convey kArya is nothing
  but kAraNa but kAraNa is not kArya.



Specifically for your or the SSS school's interpretation of these passages: According to you, is the avidya being mentioned strictly that of the ajnani



- Yes, if you want to debate this then you will have to first accept
  brahman has the avidyA even before the creation and got himself into
  the act of creation!! We should not forget that these vaidika loukika
  vyavahAra in general and pramAtru-pramANa-prameya triad in particular
  are within the sphere of adhyAsa (avidyA).



and the transformations being mentioned notwhat constitutes*visesha*darshana in general (that jnanis also have?)



- As said above the talks about transformations etc. are Kalpita
  saMvrutti which shAstra itself does for the teaching purpose and it is
  for the definite purpose and more importantly shAstra adhyArOpita
  ‘avidyA’.



but visesha-visesha darshana in the ajnanis alone - which will *literally* cease on attaining discriminating knowledge of Existence?



- What literally cease on attaining the ‘viveka’ is bhrAnti jnAna
  (mithyA jnAna, mithyA darshana, anyathAjnAna, vipareeta or
  misconception). The word ‘snake’ the ‘knowledge of snake’ literally
  cease to exist after the dawn of rope knowledge.
- At this stage we have to be very careful to understand that what is
  that which is dropped (literally ceased) after the dawn of viveka.
  The common man (ajnAni) before understanding the intrinsic nature of
  Brahman, was looking at the world from the transactional view only
  (saMvrutti / parichinna / upAdhi drushti). Never once did he see it
  from the transcendental view (i.e. brahman is the abhinna
  nimittOpadAna kAraNa). After studying the shAstra after hearing the
  shrOtreeya and brahma nishTa guru upadesha, he develops the
  transcendental view about the same world and then the wrong
  impressions he had about Brahman/jagat previously get dropped. The
  misconception that the transactions / transformation are carried out
  by brahman, the mAyAshakti as limiting adjunct etc. are gone. Though
  he continues to perceive with the physical eye the transactions and
  transformations of the world (ghatAkAsha, patAkAsha etc. or ring,
  bangle, necklace etc.), his firm understanding that none of it exists
  in Brahman (mrutsAmAnya – mahAkAsha) Itself and none of these details
  (nAma rUpa) exists independently apart from IT- is never shaken.
  Kindly note it is not ‘bheda sahishNu advaita’ it is bheda bAdhita
  Samyak or Atmaikatva jnAna.
