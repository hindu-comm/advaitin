Regarding the three points that you found noteworthy in Sri Satyan Sharma-ji’s presentation, which I agree with, here is the way I interpret the relevant shastra texts:

  

1.Reading of the Vedas is allowed for everyone including shudras and women because reading is done from written / printed texts and a written / printed text is not Veda. Veda is essentially sound, sabda. When it is put down in writing as a written text, that written text is not Veda in the primary sense but is Veda only in a secondary sense since it is a written representation of the Veda and is not the Veda (sabda) itself. The prohibition of learning Veda is primarily a prohibition with regard to the chanting of the Veda (in its primary sound form) which includes the pronunciation of the Vedic mantra along with its phonetic and metrical forms without which it is, strictly speaking, not the Vedic mantra at all.

  

2.This also explains why shudras and women have access to the Vedic sentences found in the Itihasas and Puranas, including the Gayatri mantra. However, chanting of the same requires learning the mantras from a Vedic teacher along with the learning of the two Vedangas, siksha and chanda; but such learning requires the student to have first undergone the upanayana rite. So, the chanting of mantras found in the Itihasas and Puranas along with the proper svaras, etc, are not allowed for shudras and strees even though they may have access to reading them.

  

3.Let me now come to the main point, whether shudras are allowed to hear the Vedas or they are debarred even from hearing them.

  

Before I proceed ahead, I would like to point to a method that is sometimes used in the shastras to exhort one towards performing an action or to dissuade one from doing it. It is the adoption of***hyperbolic praise***or***hyperbolic reproof***with respect to an action. It may be broadly called*arthavada*. For example, in the Purva Mimamsa sutras it is pointed out that the expressions “the trees performed the sacrifice” or “the cows sat at the sacrifice” are meant to praise the sacrifice so that one learns of its excellence (since even the trees and cows desire its result) and thereby to exhort one to perform it. Likewise, in Shankara’s BSB, the Acharya states that the excommunication that is spoken of with respect to a sannyasi who gets involved with a woman is not an injunction to be followed since the act is not a*mahapataka*(unless it happens with a guru’s wife) and can be offset by a prayaschita and that the intention behind all the talk of excommunication is to strongly dissuade the sannyasa from doing it.

  

The case of the one or two stray sentences found in the shastras with respect to shudras being debarred from hearing the Vedas is of the same nature; these sentences are arthavada and their main purpose is to dissuade the shudra from taking up formal study of the Vedas. Why do I say so? Because there is no direct prohibition in the shastra which states that a shudra should not hear the Veda. The statement cited in the BSB by Shankara is not a prohibition to the hearing of the Veda by a shudra but is a description of the kind of punishment that should be meted out to a shudra who hears the Veda. This is not a prohibition addressed to a shudra but appears in the form of an injunction addressed to others. But where is the primary prohibition addressed to the shudra that he should not hear the Vedas? In the absence of the primary prohibition, the statement of punishment is to be understood as arthavada to dissuade a shudra from taking up the study of Veda. Similarly, the statement that the Veda should not be recited in the vicinity of a shudra because he is like a walking cemetery is to be again treated as arthavada. The expression “walking cemetery” is meant to convey the idea that the shudra is unclean and impure like a cemetery (for even a visit to the cemetery requires one to take a bath to cleanse oneself from the impurity one gathers from the visit) but then if a shudra is by nature always impure how does he become eligible for brahmavidya through alternate means such as the study of itihasa and purana? For one cannot become eligible for brahmavidya unless one has stainless mental purity (chitta-shuddi); and it is admitted in the shastra that a shudra may be eligible for brahmavidya. If a shudra is unclean and impure like a ‘walking cemetery’, then the very possibility of a shudra becoming eligible for brahmavidya shouldn’t exist. But it does, which leads us to believe that these statements, such as*pouring molten lead into the ears of a shudra who hears the Veda*and the*shudra being alike a walking cemetery in whose vicinity the Veda should not be chanted*, etc, are all arthavada whose primary purpose is to prevent the shudra from taking up study of the Veda or to dissuade a brahmana from teaching the Veda to him. The whole idea is to use the hyperbole that the shudra should not even hear the Veda so that the question of him learning the Veda, or of a brahmana teaching him the Veda, should not arise. And this is exactly the way Shankara uses these quotes (which are taken from the Gautama dharma smriti and Vashishta dharma smriti) by stating that when a shudra should not even hear the Veda as per these statements, the question of him studying it does not arise. One may note that Shankara is not dealing with the question of whether the shudra can hear the Veda; the Acharya is dealing with the question whether the shudra can undertake the study of Veda and he uses these hyperbolic statements to infer that when they debar a shudra from even hearing the Veda, the question of him studying the Veda does not arise. The conclusion is that a shudra is eligible for brahma vidya but his prarabda karma is such that in this life as a shudra he is not eligible for adopting the path of pursuing it through the learning of the Vedas. 

  

There is another reason why these statements are to be regarded as arthavada. An injunction or prohibition pertains to an action that is performed through the karmendriyas. But hearing is not an action; it is a function of the jnanendriya, ear, and this function pertains to a cognition and not an action. So, how can there be an injunction aimed at prohibiting one from hearing something when hearing is a cognition that happens by the mere presence of the object being in one’s vicinity? It may be said that the basic prohibition here is that the shudra should avoid being in places where the Veda is being chanted. But this does not make sense because the vocation of a shudra is to serve, and the most excellent service he can perform, as per the shastras, is to serve a brahmana. And the very same shastras prescribe so many Vedic rites to be performed by a brahmana every day that it would be impossible for one who is in the service of a brahmana to not hear Vedic chanting while he is in his service. If a shudra is to avoid hearing the Veda being chanted, then it would be best for him to avoid doing service to a brahmana; but the shastras say that the service of a brahmana is the best service that a shudra can offer. This seeming contradiction in the shastras can be avoided only if these statements (that a shudra should not hear the Veda, that the Veda should not be chanted in the vicinity of a shudra, etc.) are treated as arthavada and not injunctions / prohibitions pertaining to the hearing of the Veda.
