Yet, if the jnani is Self identified with a particular BMI (to say "jnani sees the world"), then the cognitions pertaining to the BMI are in that jnani'sconsciousness and not elsewhere.



Suppose the hand comes in contact with fire. The jnani removes it at once and treats the burnt fingers appropriately. The pain or hurt experienced by the body due to the fire is registered in the local mind of the jnani, the jnana of which triggers the (dharmic response) iccha-kriya to remove the hand at once.



If there is no dehabimana, why does the jnani feel compelled to react to the body's experiences just as you and I do? It seems the jnani can be controlled by affecting 'his' body-mind through things associated with pain and pleasure.



praNAms Sri Putran prabhuji
