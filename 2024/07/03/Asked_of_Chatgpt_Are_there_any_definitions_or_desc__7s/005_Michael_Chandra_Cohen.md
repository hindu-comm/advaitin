In the context of \*\*Adhyasa Bhashya\*\* (a commentary on the concept of superimposition or adhyasa), the question of whether to split the term \*\*mithyājnāna\*\* as \*\*mithyā + ajñāna\*\* or \*\*mithyā + jñāna\*\* is crucial for understanding the nature of ignorance (avidya) in Advaita Vedanta.  
  
\### \*\*Understanding the Splits\*\*  
  
1. \*\*mithyā + ajñāna\*\*:  
 - \*\*mithyā\*\*: This means "false" or "illusory." It refers to something that is not real or not ultimately true but appears to have some form of existence.  
 - \*\*ajñāna\*\*: This means "ignorance" or "lack of knowledge." It denotes a lack of true knowledge about reality.  
  
 \*\*mithyā + ajñāna\*\* would imply that the ignorance (ajñāna) itself is false or illusory (mithyā). In other words, it characterizes ignorance as being of a false nature, implying that ignorance itself is an illusion or a mistaken perception.  
  
2. \*\*mithyā + jñāna\*\*:  
 - \*\*mithyā\*\*: Again, this means "false" or "illusory."  
 - \*\*jñāna\*\*: This means "knowledge" or "awareness."  
  
 \*\*mithyā + jñāna\*\* would suggest "false knowledge" or "illusory knowledge," implying that the knowledge we have is not true or correct.  
  
\### \*\*Adhyasa Bhashya Perspective\*\*  
  
\*\*Adhyasa Bhashya\*\* is a commentary by Adi Shankaracharya that explains the concept of adhyasa, or superimposition. According to Shankaracharya's explanation:  
  
- \*\*Adhyasa (Superimposition)\*\*: Adhyasa is the error of attributing properties of one thing to another. It is the mistaken perception of the world as separate from Brahman.  
  
- \*\*Mithyājnāna\*\*: In this context, mithyājnāna is used to describe the nature of ignorance. Shankaracharya uses the term to denote the kind of knowledge that is not true. This indicates that ignorance (avidya) itself is not just a lack of knowledge but a false or mistaken form of knowledge that leads to erroneous perceptions and superimpositions.  
  
\*\*Which Split is More Appropriate?\*\*  
  
\*\*mithyā + ajñāna\*\* is more appropriate in this context. Here’s why:  
  
- \*\*Nature of Ignorance\*\*: In Advaita Vedanta, ignorance (ajñāna) is not merely a passive lack of knowledge but is characterized as a mistaken perception or a false view of reality. This makes it \*\*mithyā\*\*, or illusory.  
  
- \*\*Superimposition\*\*: The concept of adhyasa involves the projection of false attributes onto Brahman. Thus, the ignorance that causes this projection is itself seen as false or illusory. Therefore, \*\*mithyā + ajñāna\*\* effectively captures the idea that the ignorance responsible for superimposition is not merely a lack of true knowledge but is a form of mistaken or false knowledge.  
  
- \*\*Alignment with Shankara’s Commentary\*\*: Shankaracharya’s analysis in Adhyasa Bhashya supports this view by explaining that ignorance leads to the illusion of duality and multiplicity. Ignorance is described as an active, erroneous force that causes the superimposition of unreal attributes onto the real Brahman.  
  
\### \*\*Conclusion\*\*  
  
In the context of the \*\*Adhyasa Bhashya\*\* and traditional Advaita Vedanta, \*\*mithyā + ajñāna\*\* is the more appropriate split for understanding the nature of ignorance. This split emphasizes that ignorance (ajñāna) is itself a form of false or illusory knowledge (mithyā), aligning with Shankaracharya’s explanation of how ignorance functions as a mistaken perception that leads to the superimposition of false attributes onto the ultimate reality, Brahman.
