Namaskaram,



> 
>
> Also, from bhashya to mandukya karika I.16: This one, **the j:vah**,
> the transmigrating individual soul; that is suptah, asleep; while
> seeing in both the (waking and dream) states such dreams as 'This is
> my father', 'This is my son', 'This is my grandson', 'This is my
> field', 'These are my animals', 'I am their master', 'I am happy,
> miserable', 'I am despoiled by this one, and I have gained through
> this one', and so on, **under the influence of dream that is but
> Maya** whose activity has no beginning and **which has the two facets
> of non-perception of Reality or the causal state, and false perception
> of Reality.**
>
>   

  

This is from Swami Gambhirananda'stranslation. However, looking at the sringeri site ([https://advaitasharada.sringeri.net/display/bhashya/Mandukya#MK_C01](https://advaitasharada.sringeri.net/display/bhashya/Mandukya#MK_C01)), some of this translation may be contested. For instance, theending part above on the two facets of Maya is only stated in the text as "स्थानद्वयेऽपि पश्यन्सुप्तः" So, this is translated as the two types or facets of sleep in seeing, and one has to infer further that this is with reference to non-perception and false perception. Will this interpretation be objected to by some groups of advaitins?

  

Also, the reference to Maya comes not immediately but a little earlier among other things "अन्यथाग्रहणलक्षणेन चानादिकालप्रवृत्तेन मायालक्षणेन स्वापेन"

  

So, the centralizing of the two facets of "sleep in seeing" as belonging to Maya may not be direct translation and also objected to by those opposed to such a viewpoint.

  

Swami Nikhilananda also translates similarly: "One who is called the*Jīva*1, the individual soul, (whose characteristic is to be) subject2to the law of transmigration, sleeping3under the influence of*Māyā*which is active from time without4beginning and which has the double characteristics of non-apprehending (on account of its being of the nature of the cause) and mis-apprehending Reality"

  

  

But given my reading of the Sanskrit and all the debate in our forum over the nuances of Maya, can members with knowledge in Sanskrit comment on the original bhashya and the Swami's translation?
