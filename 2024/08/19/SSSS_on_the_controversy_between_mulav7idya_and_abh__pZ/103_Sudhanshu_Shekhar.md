// He insists that if avidyA is considered as \*\*absence of knowledge\*\* , there is no distinction between avidyA and ajnAna//.  
  

Ok. Since SSS ji does consider avidyA as "absence of knowledge", for him, then avidyA would be identical with ajnAna. That is a reasonable position I feel.  
  
// यद्यपि ज्ञानाभावसंशयविपर्ययज्ञानानां त्रयाणामपि विद्यात भिन्नत्वात् वस्तुतत्त्वावरकत्वाविशेषत्वेन विद्योदयसमसमयमेव विनाशाच्च समानमविद्याशब्दार्हत्वम् व्यपदिश्यन्ते च तेऽपि क्वचिदज्ञानाविद्यादिशब्दैः, तथापीह वेदान्तदर्शने यथोक्तं मिथ्याज्ञानमेव विशिष्य परिभाष्यते अविद्येति ।//

  

Here also, SSS ji is saying that jnAna-abhAva, samshaya-jnAna and viparIta-jnAna are all eligible to be referred by ajnAna as well as avidyA. Still, he feels that in VedAnta, avidyA is defined as mithyA-jnAna alone. So, it appears that he does restrict the scope of avidyA to mithyA-jnAna. But it does not seem to indicate that he distinguishes ajnAna and avidyA. He may as well mean that both ajnAna or avidyA are defined to be restricted to mithyA-jnAna alone.

  

So, I think it can be held that as per SSS ji, ajnAna is synonym of avidyA. Anyway, I have also written a separate post seeking clarity on basic issues from SSS ji's followers. Let us see if we get some clarity.
