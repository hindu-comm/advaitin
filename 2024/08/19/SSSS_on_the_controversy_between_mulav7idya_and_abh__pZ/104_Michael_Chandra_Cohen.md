Case \#2: Disagreement on only the interpretation of veda.

The case is different, however, when there is agreement about the authority of Vedic revelation, and the dispute is only about its interpretation. Here the decision has to be made according to the methods of the Vedic exegesis by appeal to the group of criteria (śruti, liṇga,vākya, prakaraņa, sthāna and samākhyā) - including 'indirect implication' (linga), 'context' (prakarana) and so on, and the group including 'treatment of all matter between an opening passage and a closing recapitulation as constituting one topic' and so on.

This was the rule accepted by the ritualists in ancient times, though it was modified by Śri Śaṃkara.

The latter wrote, "The way Śruti provides the means of knowledge in the deliberation in righteous acts (dharmajijñāsa) is not the same way as in deliberation on Brahman (brahmajijñāsa). In the latter, Śruti as well as intuition (anubhava) come together as means of knowledge. This is so because the knowledge of Brahman culminates in intuition, and (unlike the part of the Veda dealing with commands and prohibitions) has an already existing reality for its object' ([B.S.Bh.](https://l.facebook.com/l.php?u=http%3A%2F%2FB.S.Bh%2F%3Ffbclid%3DIwZXh0bgNhZW0CMTAAAR3jwSMWCXeyTtJFy8Ai7xmZdTL_ut4aq-smGNTtGu1sfAd9fEZGXTjBY9M_aem_fylkXghSx5I4qZV9yrBVgw&h=AT3bJhX7wUTsUgZbbqVgqcMplYexeJxdO2bjWGFqtqAvCktx8S8uujRlnssU_70gxodVKEk_onJeeFuNZ51OFG_xRpe3G8SknkBqBzQkX34GP77wcDgbvKy3L1rFvhYsc3qh_BeVUEYaObdSlg&__tn__=R%5D-R&c%5B0%5D=AT0R_hgG9iKOf4Q0xh9pJdLExpIS7DBNTTH85DNa0BFZOijrCIJHJ68DpQzAsvm0ga5gy65w8uPoGmVhgo0FRdZLc5vGYQlu0IoEKfkBYgNrfy9EjeLtt_uFzmnRCoO_2ZOs5gkEFlG0tIPZwL8IZ-I7LeOBwnkqptj7g6ykq4TyTc6-2y8nRj5kPgMuorg)1.1.2)

Case \#3: Disagreement about meaning related to metaphysical inquiry.

But when the object is “not merely to interpret the meaning of the Vedic texts but to attain to knowledge of their content in immediate intuition (anubhava), as is the case in purely metaphysical enquiry into the true nature of the Self”, then reference to immediate experience should come before the exposition of the texts, since the enquiry has to culminate in immediate experience. In the case of heaven and other entities whose existence can only be known through the Veda, immediate experience cannot arise from experiences pertaining to the body in this present life. But it is not right to say that it follows in the same way that the personal experiences of one still alive cannot lead to knowledge of the true nature of his own Self, as the claim is not warrantable on the evidence. As Śri Śaṃkara says, 'In the case of action, the reward, such as heaven, is not immediately evident, and there can be doubt as to whether it will come or not. But the result of knowledge of the Absolute is immediately evident, for the Veda speaks of the Absolute which is immediately and directly evident" (Bṛ.U. 3.4.1) and teaches "that thou art" (Ch.U. 6.8.7) as an already accomplished fact ([B.S.Bh.](https://l.facebook.com/l.php?u=http%3A%2F%2FB.S.Bh%2F%3Ffbclid%3DIwZXh0bgNhZW0CMTAAAR2O7BDYv_Vz3NfXkCIt-YiF-r5dWi2Ohp7HfaPKa2uHtqB3gaEvslRxteY_aem_LX9RxlSPk2gkOdEGGguejA&h=AT3bJhX7wUTsUgZbbqVgqcMplYexeJxdO2bjWGFqtqAvCktx8S8uujRlnssU_70gxodVKEk_onJeeFuNZ51OFG_xRpe3G8SknkBqBzQkX34GP77wcDgbvKy3L1rFvhYsc3qh_BeVUEYaObdSlg&__tn__=R%5D-R&c%5B0%5D=AT0R_hgG9iKOf4Q0xh9pJdLExpIS7DBNTTH85DNa0BFZOijrCIJHJ68DpQzAsvm0ga5gy65w8uPoGmVhgo0FRdZLc5vGYQlu0IoEKfkBYgNrfy9EjeLtt_uFzmnRCoO_2ZOs5gkEFlG0tIPZwL8IZ-I7LeOBwnkqptj7g6ykq4TyTc6-2y8nRj5kPgMuorg)3.3.32,Ś.S.B. 6.233, M.V. p. 210, 447, 573)
