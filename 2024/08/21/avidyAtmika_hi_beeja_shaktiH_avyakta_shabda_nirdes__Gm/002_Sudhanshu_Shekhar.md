Namaste Bhaskar ji.

> As per our understanding both avidyA and mAya are not synonymous hence
> Atmika means coupling only.

So, सदात्मक ब्रह्म means sat coupled with Brahman? And चिदात्मक विषयी means chit coupled with vishayI?

  

Brahman is sat-mAtra. It is chit-mAtra.

  

BhAshya says -- Atmano mAyA avidyA. Further, avidyA hi avyaktam. Therefore, avidyA-Atmak refers to avidyA-mAtra on the lines of bhAshya usage of chidAtmaka and sadAtmaka. Any other interpretation is violative of usage by BhAshya.

  

> At some places ahamkara explained as avidyA which is coupled with
> avyakta / avyAkruta. For example : ahaMkAraH eti avidyA saMyuktaM
> avyaktaM. So in our book mAya can never ever be paryAya pada for
> avidyA.

The coupling is of ahamkAra-vAsanA and avyakta and not of avidyA and avyakta. BhAshya theek se padhiye. The issue in Gita 7.4 is that -- by the words mentioned in shloka, some other entity is meant. Like by the word bhUmi, pratihivI tanmAtrA is meant, by the word Manas, ahamkAra is meant. In this context, BhAshyakAra says that by the word ahamkAra, avyakta is meant.

  

How and why?

  

Just as poison + food is referred as poison, similarly ahamkAra-vAsanA (poison) + avyakta (food) is referred as ahamkAra (poison). From where has avidyA come into picture? If avidyA were to be the poison, then avidyA+avyakta would be referred by avidyA and not by ahamkAra.

  

So, bhAshya refers to avidyAtmaka avyakta by the word "avidyA-samyuktam-avyaktam" as is the case everywhere else. You can check Anandagiri TIkA in this regard.

  

Poison refers to ahamkAra-vAsanA and not avidyA. That is clear from bhAshya and also common sense.

  

Saying avidyA is different from avyakta is violative of BSB 1.4.3 which says - avidyA hi avyaktam.

> At some places bhAshyakAra the word avyakta being identified with
> avidyA when applied to jeeva and same avyakta identified as ‘mAya’
> when applied to hiraNyagarbha this is the glaring bifurcation between
> two different aspects.

These are lame interpretations violative of BhAshya. BhAshya is clear -- Atmao mAyA avidyA. Period. No interpretation of jIva/HiraNyagarbha is required. That is just to perpetuate a wrong kalpaNa violative of direct words of BhAshya and vArtika.

> Is bhAshyakAra making the fault of such extreme self contradiction in
> the same sentence?? One is applied as mAya and another is applied as
> avidyA with respect to jeeva and hiraNyagarbha respectively??

  

There is no contradiction as demonstrated. avidyA= mAyA=avyakta=nAma-rUpa-bIja.

> Somewhere I had read these two words avidyA and prakruti have entirely
> different root and cannot be equated at any stretch of imagination.

Sir, where is the need to stretch when bhAshya itself says clearly - Atmano mAyA avidyA. You are violating bhAshya terribly by stretching the imagination.

  

>  According to shankara vedAnta Ishwara’s mAya and jeeva’s avidyA is
> settled issue. Jagat is effect of mAya (hence mAya is also called
> jagadbeeja) which is same for any sane person whereas adhyAsa is
> effect of avidyA (jnAnAbhAva) which exists only in ajnAni in his
> antaHkaraNa.

Sir ji. VedAnta 101 says that Ishwara-jIva bheda itself is avidyA-krita. And you seek to differentiate Ishwara's mAyA and jIva's avidyA?

  

There can be vyapadesha of jIva's avidyA and Ishwara's mAyA depending on preponderance of sattva. That is not an issue. They are same stuff.

  

>  mAya is food and avidyA is poison. Without knowing this we are
> talking about vishAnna as visha only. There is absolutely no worthy
> purpose served by equating mAya with avidyA in shankaraadvaita.

  

Violative of bhAshya. The bhAshya does not say avidyA as poison. It says ahamkAra-vAsanA as poison. BhAshya theek se padhiye. If avidyA were to be poison, then avidyA+avyakta would be referred by avidyA and not ahamkAra, whose topic is discussed in 7.4. This is basic.
