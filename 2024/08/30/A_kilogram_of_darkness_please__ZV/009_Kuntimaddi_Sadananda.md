Jaishankarji - PraNAms

  

Just some food for thought. Time is defined as a gap between two sequential observations. It can be modified as a gap between two sequential experiences - For that Mind is required. Hence during the deep sleep state, one does not experience time.

  

Similarly, space is defined as a gap between two simultaneous observations - in essence, we need two points. Nature has provided two eyes and two ears and we have stereographic observation via senses. (Now a days one of my ears is not working!)

  

During the deep sleep state, we have no experience of space and time - since the mind is inactive. One conclusion is without the mind active one cannot perceive! space or time.

  

Also of interest is one cannot see! light also - one can only see or recognize the reflected light from the object. In the night we can see the full moon since sunlight falling on the moon is getting reflected. We cannot see the sunlight that exits between moon and say Mars if there is no object to reflect it.

  

Similarly one cannot recognize the light of consciousness that is ever present - only when it gets reflected by the subtle mind it can be recognized - only by the reflection process mind appears as a local conscious entity just like the moon becoming a local luminous entity by reflecting sunlight.

  

I agree the Vedic statements to be understood on their right - as it says - aatmanaH aakaashaH sambhuutaH -- etc.

  

In the discussion of bhaava-abhaava aspects, the need of the mind for observation is required.

  

Anyway, all the discussions are taking at the mental level and most of these are beyond my mind.

  

My 2C.

  

Hari Om!
