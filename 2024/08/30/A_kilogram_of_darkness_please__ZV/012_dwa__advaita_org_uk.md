Dear Sudhanshu-ji and Jaishankar-ji,



There is a very real danger in discussions such as these of missing the point of Advaita. It is a teaching method, obviously in vyavahāra, to bring our minds to the realization of the truth of non-duality. In reality, there are no seekers seeking Self-knowledge and no teachers transmitting it; indeed, no creation at all.



I believe that the original (scriptural) prakriyā-s and Śaṅkara’s bhāṣya-s use everyday (at the time) understanding of what can be seen and reasoned upon to bring about this realization. They do not require complex logic or modern scientific understanding to cut through the examples that they use. I believe that many post-Śaṅkara writers, and lots of modern-day academics, attempt to do precisely this. To my mind, it only serves to reduce the likelihood of a seeker understanding the message and nothing at all to ‘clarify’ it.



I think we can all appreciate that darkness is simply an absence of light. We can bring a torch into a cave but the only way that we can bring darkness into a room is by removing the source of the light. Ancients appreciated this without any special knowledge, even though we now know about photons and electromagnetic radiation.



I don’t accept the equivalence of waking and dream states so I do not find that argument helpful. (They are certainly similar – Māṇḍūkya kārikā bhāṣya 2.4 – but also different – Brahmasūtra bhāṣya 2.2.29.) I’m afraid that the theory of eka-jīva-vāda has never done much for me, although vācārambhaṇa śruti has clear teaching value. I talk about EJV in [https://www.advaita-vision.org/the-devils-teaching-part-1/](https://www.advaita-vision.org/the-devils-teaching-part-1/) if you are interested in my views.

One thing I would ask: You say that whatever we see in dream is “avidyA-kArya and hence triguNAtmaka”. I understand this to mean that you are saying that dream is the ‘effect of ignorance’ and is therefore a real thing? But doesn’t this pre-suppose that ignorance is a real thing that can be the ‘cause’ of anything? Where do you believe that a scripture or Śaṅkara makes the statement that dreams are ‘caused by’ ignorance?



Regarding science and dark matter, it is an interesting idea that darkness might actually be dark matter, but I suspect that an astrophysicist would fall over laughing! ChatGPT’s summary is that: “The idea that darkness "consists of" dark matter misunderstands both concepts. Darkness is about light's presence or absence, while dark matter is about unseen mass that influences gravity. The two are unrelated phenomena in the context of current scientific understanding.”



It is true that there is a danger of modern scientific understanding presenting an obstacle to understanding traditional Advaita but one cannot ignore or reject basic knowledge of worldly things. For example, we can no longer accept the literal notion of the mind being ‘contained’ in the heart. Another example is that the scriptures speak of insects being ‘svedaja’, meaning ‘born from sweat or moisture’. Examples such as these were used by the ancients to point the listener towards an understanding of the non-dual truth. They may well have believed them at the time but their relevance was that they functioned well as metaphors. It was never necessary that they were literally true. And now we know that some of them are not.



I suggest that darkness and ignorance being existent entities fall into this category. Useful (maybe) as teaching tropes but certainly not literally true.
