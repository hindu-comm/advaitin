Namaste Dennis ji.

  
I will retain the focus on bhAvarUpatva of tamas and hence ignore general points made by you.

  
  
\> I think we can all appreciate that darkness is simply an absence of light. We can bring a torch into a cave but the only way that we can bring darkness into a room is by removing the source of the light. Ancients appreciated this without any special knowledge, even though we now know about photons and electromagnetic radiation.  
  

Ancients went into great detail about what is "absence", which I see lacking in your presentation. You are using the word "absence" but not defining what it is. Please define what is "absence".

  

Without prejudice to my claim of bhAvarUpatva of darkness, let me for the time being go along with your claim of tamas being prakAsha-abhAva.  
  
Tell me, how is prakAsha-abhAva not bhAvarUpa! Shankara proves in ghaTa-bhAshya (BrihadAraNyak 1.2.1) that vishesha-abhAva such as pot-abhAva is bhAvarUpa. Here is the logic adduced by him: अपि च, चतुर्विधानामभावानाम् , घटस्येतरेतराभावो घटादन्यो ष्टः — यथा घटाभावः पटादिरेव, न घटस्वरूपमेव । न च घटाभावः सन्पटः अभावात्मकः ; किं तर्हि ? भावरूप एव । एवं घटस्य प्राक्प्रध्वंसात्यन्ताभावानामपि घटादन्यत्वं स्यात् , घटेन व्यपदिश्यमानत्वात् , घटस्येतरेतराभाववत् ; तथैव भावात्मकताभावानाम् ।  
  
So, even if you hold that darkness is prakAsha-abhAva, you will have to accept that darkness is triguNAtmaka bhAvAtmaka avidyA-kArya.  
  
You are mixing nyAya concept of vieshsha-abhAva in VedAnta. Advaita VedAnta does not accept nyAya concept of abhAva as demonstrated by BhAshyakAra above in BrihaDaraNyak 1.2.1.  
  
If you appreciate vedAntic concept of vishesha-abhAva, you will see the fruitlessness of your insistence on darkness being absence-of-light. It still remains triguNAtmaka avidyA-kArya as per ghaTa-bhAshya.

  
  
\> I don’t accept the equivalence of waking and dream states so I do not find that argument helpful. (They are certainly similar – Māṇḍūkya kārikā bhāṣya 2.4 – but also different – Brahmasūtra bhāṣya 2.2.29.)  
  

Then you have violated Aitareya Shruti 1.3.12 - trayah swapnAh, and the bhAshya thereupon -त्रयः स्वप्ना जाग्रत्स्वप्नसुषुप्त्याख्याः । ननु जागरितं प्रबोधरूपत्वान्न स्वप्नः । नैवम् ; स्वप्न एव । कथम् ? परमार्थस्वात्मप्रबोधाभावात् स्वप्नवदसद्वस्तुदर्शनाच्च ।-which posits absolute identity of waking and dream. This is also some great common sense and Phi 101 which posits identity of waking and dream on account of your very inability to distinguish a dream and waking. You cannot prove that you are not dreaming right now. So, your insistence on waking being different from dream is born out of untenable assumptions which have been contradicted by bhAshya.

  

The postulation of difference of waking and dream elsewhere in BSB is for manda-adhikArI, for whom creation is mentioned, as is explained by bhAshyakAra elsewhere. You are mistaking the postulation-for-manda-adhikArI as the advaita-siddhAnta.

  
\> One thing I would ask: You say that whatever we see in dream is “avidyA-kArya and hence triguNAtmaka”. I understand this to mean that you are saying that dream is the ‘effect of ignorance’ and is therefore a real thing?  
  

On the contrary, being avidyA-kArya, both dream and waking are illusory and hence not real.

  
  
\> But doesn’t this pre-suppose that ignorance is a real thing that can be the ‘cause’ of anything? Where do you believe that a scripture or Śaṅkara makes the statement that dreams are ‘caused by’ ignorance?  
  

Unreal ignorance is the cause of unreal avidyA-kArya. "Both waking and dream are avidyA-kArya" is repeatedly spoken in bhAshya at every step.  
  
Reality of ignorance is not needed for it being a cause. It is bhAva-abhAva-vilakshaNa, and due to sAjAtya with avidyA-kArya i.e. sat-vilakshaNatA, it is logical to postulate it being the cause of avidyA-kArya.
