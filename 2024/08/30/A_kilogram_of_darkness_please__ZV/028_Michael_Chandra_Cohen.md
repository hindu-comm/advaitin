Preceding the para cited below:

(Objection:)-What has been destroyed is not itself again born. Thus, like the pradhvamsabhava, non- existence of a thing, known as destruction, moksha is eternal and is yet produced.

(Answer:) No.

Cite - Para translated with SSSS ji notes.

Because moksha is positive (bhāvarūpa) \[1\].

To say that pradhvamsābhāva, non-existence (abhāva) of a thing known as destruction, is produced is only a verbal quibble (vikalpa \[2\]), inasmuch as nothing specific can be predicated (viśeșa) of abhāva. Abhāva (non-existence) is indeed only the pratiyogi of bhāva. How so? Just as bhāva, though one and the same throughout, is distinguished by cloth, pot, and so on, -e.g., we speak of the existence of a cloth, the existence of a pot (ghaṭabhāvaḥ paṭabhāva), and so on,-so also, though abhava or non-existence is in itself devoid of all distinctions, yet it is spoken of as different and in association with different acts or qualities (kriyā guṇa) as though it were a substance etc. \[3\] Abhāva (non-existence) cannot, indeed, co-exist with attributes as the blue lotus co-exists with its attributes. If it were possessed of attributes, then it would come under the category of bhava or being \[4\].

———

SSSS notes:

\[1\] The mokșa that is abiding in Self is not an abhāva like pradhvamsābhāva.

\[2\] Only a verbal quibble, not an entity.

\[3\] See BGBh 18.45. BSBh 2.2.26

\[4\] The sense is the attributes are fit only for bhāva.
