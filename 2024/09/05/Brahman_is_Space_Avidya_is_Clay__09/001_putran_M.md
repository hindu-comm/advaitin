1\.

  

When we identify the pots (pot-spaces), the "samanya" (the universal) is Clay and the visesha (particular/special) is the pot.

  

Clay is Ishvara who imagines/projects/appears as the pot-world.

  

Clay is Maya (jnana-iccha-kriya-shakti) and Space+Clay is Ishvara.

  

Clay is Avidya (causal Ignorance) that obstructs & causes superimposition of the pot-world onto Space.

  

Space is the samanya-adhishtana (absolute substratum Reality) - that is true in all standpoints, whereas Clay is the visesha-adhishtana (relative, contextually existent) that is coeval to the standpoint of the pot-world - and this pot-world and its samanya Clay are mithya.

  

Mithya (or anirvachaniya) is an ontological category of existence admitted in Advaita, that is different from absolute Existence (Sat=Brahman) and from absolute non-existence (like "son of barren woman"). Basically it is all that can be characterized as imagination or appearance, because they are true/existing in some relative cognitive standpoint but are negated in the absolute standpoint of the substratum. The standard analogy is the snake superimposed on rope. The rope denotes the truth, Reality, always, whereas the snake is mithya, being true with reference to the mind projecting it but gone in the realization of the rope - note the rope never goes or comes whether the snake is imagined or not. \[In this example\], the samanya "Clay" for the imagined \[snake\] isthe Mind. Alternately, mithya are all objects of knowledge that are Brahman conditioned with some limiting adjunct - like unbounded Space conditioned by a pot-shape makes appear the pot-space that we then identify as if separately.

  

  

2.

  

The Light, the Self, is constant and unchanging, and is satya even when outward consciousness is not manifest (as in sleep). So no question of turning off the light. It is a matter of explaining the cognitions of variation, change and limits that appear in the Light; and the explanation is that something else (i.e. darkness ~ avidya/maya) is obstructing and causing the appearance of duality in that non-dual Light. This explanation is valid in the very standpoint in which the cognized effects are posited. However while the Light is affirmed as the adhishtanam Sat (substratum Reality) in all standpoints of knowledge, this darkness is asat in the standpoint of pure Light, hence the darkness and its projected effect, the differentiated world, are posited only as mithya, appearance, superimposition.

  

For anotherpracticalanalogy: imagine a tiger chasing and you running in fear. However, all you are seeing are vrittis (think "wrinkles") in the mind. Here the mind is the causal substrate - the darkness or prism that obstructs and causes projection of unreal tiger. It must be affirmed as the intermediary linking you the pure Consciousness to the tiger duality.

  

So, at the individual level, all thought-creation is "nothing but mind"; and at the cosmic level, the perceived world of duality is "only maya/avidya".Being 'mindful' of this reductive principle detaches us from the flux of samsara and our imaginations, because we shift attention to the fact of modification at the homogeneous causal level where the 'movie' effect loses its reality.

  

Thus Self is divided into seer, the intermediate darkness (avidya) or seeing-power (maya), and seen; but this division is itself mithya imagination, negated in the absolute standpoint of You - and hence the components as identified in the context of this divided standpoint of knowledge are also mithya - not asat for they are present objects of knowledge and not sat either for they are absent in the standpoint of Sat.
