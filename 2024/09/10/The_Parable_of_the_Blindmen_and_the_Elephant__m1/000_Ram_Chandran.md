We the discussants are aware about our limitations we all want to be careful before asserting our own conclusions. Though we all accept that “Brahman alone knows the Brahman,” this does not mean that we shouldn’t discuss and share what we perceive. We do enjoy the freedom to develop our own framework of thought to provide our vision of what we believe. Any such framework (model) requires a set of assumptions to help us to postulate what we believe using a set of notions (definitions and assumptions). Such notions require validation without contradicting already established facts. Logical reasoning is applied to validate our framework on a given context. We do have so many frameworks of thoughts which are available in books, research articles and in the scriptures. Advaita applies the principle of ‘neti, neti’ to discard all contradicting or unaccepted notions to establish the final vision. The path of truth finding may be comparable to the well-known parable of the blind men and the elephant!

Several versions of this parable of blind men and the elephant are found in Buddhist, Hindu and Jain texts which discuss the limits of perception and the importance of knowing the Truth nothing but the Truth! The parable has several variations, but broadly the story can be summarized as follows:

A group of blind men heard about an unknown animal, called an elephant which had been brought to the town. None of those blindmen were aware of its shape and form. Out of curiosity. they said: "We must inspect and know it by touch, of which we are capable". So, they wanted to touch the animal to get a feel about what they found. The first person, whose hand landed on the trunk, said, "This animal is like a thick snake". Another one whose hand reached its ear thought it seemed like a kind of fan. The third person whose hand was upon its leg, said, the elephant felt like a pillar or a tree-trunk. The blind man who placed his hand upon its side said the elephant, "is a wall". Another who felt its tail, described it as a rope. The last felt its tusk, stating the elephant is that which is hard like a spear.

Soon the blind men discovered their disagreements and suspect the others to be not telling the truth! The parables in different versions fundamentally recognized the flaws and came to the conclusion that any conclusive resolution requires the comprehension of the full elephant. What we discussants of Advaita Philosophy need to recognize that we are all partially correct and partially wrong. While one's subjective experience is true, it may not be the totality of truth.
