> Namaste Subuji,
>
> Could you please explain how Brahman/Atman being sadasad vilakshanam
> aligns with
>
> satyaṁ-jñānam-anantaṁ-Brahma of Taittirīya upaniṣad 2- 1-1 , and with
> Shankara Bhashya for BG 2.16 ?

  

Namaste.

  

The Satyam of the Taittiriya Upanishad is the svarupa lakshanam of Brahman. This Satyam is unchanging and eternal. The sat and asat in thesadasad vilakshanamapplied to Brahman refer to the worldly existence and the absence thereof pertaining to created objects (objects perceived). As explained in the article, a pot gains existence upon being produced and loses the same when it is destroyed. The BG and Bhashya 13.12 where Brahman is stated to be expressible neither as sat nor as asat have for the purport this worldly sat and asat. Brahman is distinct from this sat and asat.

  

In the Bhashya for 2.16 it is taught that the Sat (Brahman) never goes out of existence. The existence of objects is actually this Existence.
When objects come into existence they are said to exist and before that they can't be said to exist. Upon destruction the objects are no longer said to exist. But the Existence that is Brahman upon which the objects are superimposed, does not go out of existence. That is the purport of the BG 2.16 and the Bhashya.

  

The Brahman taught in BG 13.12 is stated to be distinct from the pot-existence, sat, and pot-non-existence, asat. Thereby Brahman is 'sadasad vilakshanam.' This is not to be confused with the Sat, Existence of Brahman of the TaittiryaUpanishad.

  

Trust the above is clear enough.
