Namaste Lokesh ji.

  

// It is said that अतीन्द्रिय means अगोचरत्व but I haven't seen such usage before. Both words mean different things.//  

  

How a word is being used by the speaker is to be understood by the context in which he has used the word.

  

BhAshyakAra Himself mentioned that prior to using this logic of atIndriyatvam. So, by the context, anyone can infer that atIndriya here includes vAk-agocharatvam.अननुरूपमुक्तं न सत्तन्नासदुच्यते इति। न? अनुरूपमेव उक्तम्। कथम् सर्वासु हि उपनिषत्सु ज्ञेयं ब्रह्म नेति नेति (बृह0 उ0 4।4।22) अस्थूलमनणु (बृह0 उ0 3।3।8) इत्यादिविशेषप्रतिषेधेनैव निर्दिश्यते? न इदं तत् इति? ***वाचः अगोचरत्वात्***।।

  

Now, it can be the claim of none that atIndriyatvam cannot include vAk-agocharatvam, on the basis of popular usage. I will simply ask - do you agree that vAk is an indriya? If yes, then how doesn't it necessarily follow that atIndriyatvam must have vAk-gocharatvam. Just because in normal parlance it is not intended does not imply that the usage is incorrect. You have to understand the vivakshA of speaker.  
  

//Secondly, even if we accept that meaning, how is the main objection resolved that is "given Brahman is neither सत् nor असत्, how is it ज्ञेयम्"?//  

  

What is the problem? BhAshyakAra has given three reasons that despite being jneya, it cannot be the vishaya of any words. Such is the very nature of the jneya:

- अद्वयत्वात्
- अविषयत्वात्
- आत्मत्वाच्च

If something is non-dual, the very self and non-object, then you cannot positively describe it by any word. You can indicate it negatively as not this, not this. To know it is to be it.

  

Regards.
