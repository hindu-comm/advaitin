Namaskaram Bhaskar-ji,

  

In my understanding, the paramarthika standpoint is not a sleep/samadhi state where duality is simply not cognized though existing in some sense or other. Namarupa is asat in paramarthika; guna and vyavahara are abhava/asat/impossible/unavailable for consideration in Nirguna Brahman. This jnanam of Nirguna Brahman if projected within the vyavaharika standpoint will have to be vyavahara-badhita and not vyavahara-abhava - because the jnana itself must be conditioned to this standpoint where vyavahara (i.e. duality in some sense) is not asat. What this means is, the vyavahara-badhita jnana of our standpoint is itself realized as a pointer to vyavahara-abhava of nirguna brahman.

  

Then, the followup question is: Do we consider the vyavaharika standpoint (and its satya, involving a duality of namarupa-denotations and Ishvara the All as adhishtanam and cause) and the paramarthika standpoint (and its satya, that is nirguna, turiya Brahman) as distinct but equally real: two sides of the same coin, one notinterfering and diminishing the knowledge of other in any fundamental manner?

  

Or is turiya the satya and namarupa-associated Ishvara the mithya? Does knowledge of nirguna Brahman demote the vyavaharika standpoint to the status of imagination, adhyasa whose cause Maya is anirvachaniya? The bAdhitam should be such that it does not leave any room to add a clause "not abhava"; if you are tempted to do the latter, then the right clause is "mithya".

  

I accept this latteranswer as the siddhanta. Not sure how much it affects the practical side of things. I would guess most mumukshus do mananam focused on or channeled through Ishvara-tattva, that is natural to the vyavaharika standpoint. Even MS was said to be krishna bhakta, though he was the explicator for mithya.
