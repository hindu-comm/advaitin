Dear Subbu-ji,



Apologies for continuing this but much of my concern at present in my writing is about use of language. I believe that much of the confusion amongst seekers today is the result of ambiguity and error in the way that teachers communicate. E.g. even respected commentators can translate scriptures or bhAShya-s according to their prior misunderstanding rather than according to what was literally said in the original.



Regarding the word ‘fact’, one of the explanations given in Chambers dictionary does indeed correspond to yours: “Anassertionof thetruth”. But I have never come across this definition before, and I believe it has been added because many people do indeed use it (wrongly) in that way. Wordweb gives four meanings only, along with examples:



- A piece of information about circumstances that exist or events that
  have occurred, e.g. “first you must collect all the facts of the case"
- A statement or assertion of verified information about something that
  is the case or has happened, e.g. "he supported his argument with an
  impressive array of facts"
- An event known to have happened or something known to have existed,
  e.g. "your fears have no basis in fact"; "how much of the story is
  fact and how much fiction is hard to tell"
- A concept whose truth can be proved, e.g. scientific hypotheses are
  not facts"



Wictionary points out that “all facts are true, and anything not true is not a fact.” And Wikipedia adds “Generally speaking, facts are independent of[belief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belief "Belief")and of[knowledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge "Knowledge")and[opinion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion "Opinion").” In philosophy, “A fact can be defined as something that is the case.”



There is no doubt a lot more that could be said upon the subject but your last sentence implies that you actually agree anyway, since presumably both Brahman and shunya cannot both be ‘the fact’.
