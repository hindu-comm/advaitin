Namaste Dennis ji

You may be interested in the following if you wish to go down your particular line of thinking, viz., that Advaita is an interpretation whereas facts are "verifiable", as you put it.

According to the prevailing philosophy today, viz., post-modernism, there are no facts at all, there are only interpretations.

Dr. Gad Saad shares this conversation in "The Parasitic Mind" (2020).

Here's the exact quote:(\*Page 137-138\*:) "...I recall a conversation that I had with the girlfriend of one of my PhD students, who was a postmodernist. I asked her, 'Do you believe that the sun rises in the east?' She responded, 'Well, that's just a social construct.' I asked her to clarify, and she said, 'Yes, it's just a way of thinking about the world. Someone else might see it differently. Maybe to them, it's a dancing horse in the sky.' I replied, 'But what about the objective reality of the sun's movement?' She retorted, 'There is no objective reality.'"

"This conversation is not an outlier. It represents the quintessential postmodern perspective, wherein all truth claims are subservient to one’s subjective experiences and cultural narratives."

Dr. Saad discusses this conversation as an example of postmodernism's relativistic approach to truth, where objective reality is denied. In other words, there are no facts at all, only interpretations. This is the dominant philosophy in the western world today.

But such an idea that Advaita is an "interpretation" and not a fact is fraught with the same logical flaws that afflict the strange doctrine of post-modernism.
