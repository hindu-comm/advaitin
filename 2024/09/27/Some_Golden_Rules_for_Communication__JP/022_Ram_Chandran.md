Namaskar:

The purpose of my posting the message with the title, “Some Golden Rules for Communication” was to illustrate how we can all benefit by following our commonsense and by knowing our limitations. Srik Kalyan picked this quote, "There are no facts, only interpretations!" and raised with the question, “Are there no facts?” Quotations are often used at some contexts and they need to be understood on the basis of purpose for which they are used. All facts that are collected and used for empirical analysis for validation are always subject to interpretation. Facts are objective information that can be verified, while interpretation is the process of analyzing and making sense of those facts.Interpretation can help explain the significance of a fact, or provide a conclusion or reason for it.If I say that it is raining outside, though it may be a fact but farmers may like to know how much was the rain fall! The quotation was to illustrate the point that facts can always be further explorable. The fact that the sun rises in the east and sets in the west can be interpreted to explain why clock hands move in the same direction ("clockwise".) We can all interpret the same Vedantic facts in various ways because our background and perspectives are different. 

We also need to distinguish between verifiable and unverifiable facts.
For example, “I am Brahman” is an unverifiable fact because we neither know “Who am I?.” or “Who is the Brahman?” Some can say that Advaita is a system of concepts without any empirical foundational data. Another example for a tautological statement is, “my house is next to the post office.” This is certainly a fact but this will not help anyone to find either my house or the post office. With a map (information) if I locate my house then we can verify that my house is next to the post office.

Sri Kalyan made the assertion: “However, in this list, it would be absurd to treat advaita as an interpretation only, and not as a fact.” I am rather puzzled because, if all of us accept that “Advaita is the Truth” then what is there for us to discuss? We all try to interpret Advaita based on each of what we perceived and understood. The debate between Sankara and Mandala Mishra was to establish who is right based on the interpretation of the scriptures by those scholars!

In short, let us focus our attention to Advaita rather than debating on the validity of the quotation,
