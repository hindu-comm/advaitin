Namaskar:

I want to thank Putran Ji for posting the following 3 interesting and thought-provoking questions on Hindu Gods:

Do you think hindu gods are always moral?

Are they real or metaphorical?

If they're real, why do we need millions of gods?



It was my pleasure to read the balanced answers with reasonable explanations provided by Aravinda Ji. I fully agree with his assertion that we do owe explanations to youngsters with genuine doubts created because of our failure to clarify. By posing and answering the above questions, we can make them to understand the role of religion for a meaningful life.

Our Moral values constantly changing because they depend on context, time, environment and individuals. Are we qualified to say whether God is Moral or immoral? Anytime we make judgement on others we always ended up crossing the line from evaluating to cataloging thereby we become judgmental.The wisdom that gain from Gita enable us to consider Gods are superior to human beings and they are purer and as such we have no means to judge their character. The distinction between Good and Bad or right and wrong depends on circumstances and what is good at one time can become bad at another time. We may have to accept the fact that we have no means to understand why God always remain moral!

The Gita states that God exists beyond time in the spiritual world, and that nothing can exist without God. According to Gita, God as all-pervasive, infinite, and omnipotent. Gita states that God is the true friend, support, and refuge for all living beings. God is present everywhere, not just in his supreme abode.The Gita also affirms that God is attainable through unalloyed devotion.Further it asserts there is only one God, who is the sole object of worship.

Our Hindu ancestors employed a rich palette of metaphors to explain their experience of the Divine. Metaphors are used to comprehend an abstract object. When we say that ‘Life is Journey, we want to convey that our life experience is equivalent to taking some journey. When our ancestors called Varuna as rain-God, they conveyed God was the cause for rain! Metaphors may be used to explain the role of god and it does not imply that God is metaphorical!

Does Hinduism claim that there are millions of Gods in this universe? The answer is certainly No! It is a misconception that millions of Gods are present in Hinduism. As pointed out by Aravinda Ji, the main reason for this misconception is due to the single word Koti with two meanings – the one means 10 million and the other means types. The 33 Koti represents 33 types of Gods. There are 12 Adityas (Personified deities) 11 Rudras, 8 Vasus (deities of material elements) plus two Ashvins. These 33 types of Hindu deities is much larger than other religions but ultimately Hindus believe in one and only one God, the consciousness or the all-pervading Brahman. In particular Advaita philosophy postulates that all seekers look for the God (consciousness) which is ever present within! Hindus accept both the believers and non-believers of God. Similarly, Hindus accept both the worshippers of icons of Gods as well as those who focus their mind only on “Nirguna Brahman” who has no form or name! The followers of Hinduism or more correctly, the Sanatan Dharma, do not follow any specific path. The ultimate goal is to reach the top of the mountain and that can be achieved by taking a journey using many paths and means. This may explain why the seekers read Puranas or Upanishads or Bhagavad Gita. In summary, the Sanatana Dharma believes that “one shoe doesn’t fit all.” We can conclude that Hinduism does not believe in millions of gods and that is a misconception.
