Namaste Subbu ji

It's notable that, as you shared, Advaita is the ultimate truth proclaimed by \*texts\* of the Buddist and Jaina canon, as per skAnda purANam. The later \*followers\* may not agree of course. But that's secondary.

  

बुद्धागमानां सर्वेषां तथैवार्हागमस्य च ।

यक्षगन्धर्वसिद्धादिनिर्मितस्यागमस्य च ॥ ७०॥

  

If a shrotriya studies Buddhist works, he would easily see that fact of Advaita even in these nAstika texts. Rest of the verses contrary to Advaita would be contextualised by him as arthavAda, gauNa or figurative usage etc.

  

The mithyAtvaM (absence of independent existence) is explained saying "aniccA sabba sankhAra" and even anAtta (there is no objective self) can be explained differently than saying "there is no Self.

  

The point is that any text is only as advaitic as the person teaching it and so, many (though not necessarily all) religious and spiritual texts in the world can be suitably placed by a shrotriya within the framework or karma and jnAnam. Conversely Madhvacharya sees dvaita even in mANDukya upanishad because shrotriyatvaM was conspicuously absent.

  

shrotriyatvaM is the key.

  

Om

Raghav
