> Does anyone have scriptural references on the effectiveness or
> non-effectiveness of *prayer*— as opposed to mantras or rituals per
> se — in achieving material (not purely spiritual) ends?

  

We have the extremely popular Vishnu Sahasra Nama which, in the prelude and the epilogue, says amply about the efficacy of chanting this, effectively praying. One can see any book on VSNfor this. So is about the Shiva Sahasra nama. Shankaracharya's all stotras have the phala shruti at the end. These are all not vedic mantras nor rituals. Pure vaachikakarma: prayer. The phala mentioned covers the entire range of the four purusharthas.

  

From certain stories in the Mahabharata, etc. there are also cases where prayers, accompanied by penance or not, have not answered the praying person's longing. Often the reason is: the person has not the sufficient punya or pratibandhaka prarabdha. In the Kanaka dhara stotram Shankara prays: Even if you, Lakshmi, think these people (the householder couple) are loaded with poorva paapam, by your grace clear all that and shower your mercy on them. Then the Mother rained golden gooseberries.

  

People recite the Skanda shasthikavacham, kOLaru padhikam, etc Tamil prayers and attain phalam. Soundarya lahari is said to confer specific phalam for reciting specific shlokas.

  

Many stotras are for loukika, material, fruits. Like roga nivaranam, arogya prapti, dhana, udyoga prapti, bhumi, etc. Jyotishyas too specify certain stotras for certain pariharas. These are not spiritual.
