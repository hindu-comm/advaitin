Namaste Akhilesh ji

You wrote -

  

  

"But then what you say about Lakshmi suggests that there is a power of God to bypass prior karma, something that allows for non-linear and non-systemic results."

  

Thats where there is a certain confusion in what you wrote. Because, efforts like chanting the Vishnu sahasranama or even regional language prayers like the Hanuman chalisa are themselves as "non-linear" and "non-systemic" as it can get.

  

Because Prayer is nothing but an English word for adRShta-generating karma. adRShTa is the invisible effect of a karma which leads to the it's subsequent fructification. This occurs only because of being governed by Ishvara, who is both the Maker and Material of the universe. All prayer can be parsed/deconstructed as various forms of karma. Vocalized prayers are called vAcikam karmas. Ritual prayers are called kAyikam karma. And the silent mental repetition/holding on earnestly to the suitable prayerful thought/vRtti of \*any" prayer is maanasam karma. The efficacy of non-vedic prayers like Vishnu sahasranama and of non-sanskrit prayers like the Hanuman Chalisa or Tiruppavai (Tamil) is attested by shiShtas, vedic scholars and Shankaracharyas.

  

Besides, the "power of God" cannot be arbitrarily dispensed randomly without any trigger/cause/context/rhyme or reason. That would be utterly nihilistic and incompatible with the worldview of the Vedas.

  

Therefore, the "logic of prayer" as explained in the Kena Upanishad Shankara bhAShya is that, prayer is the trigger/cause/method that leads to a non-linear, non-systemic response mediated by Ishvara. Prayers work only because all that exists is the intelligent Being, viz., Ishvara. Prayer is nothing but a karma that triggers that response from the Cosmos, called Ishvara's Grace. The logic is the same for both vedic and non-vedic prayers.

  

Ishvara "bypasses" karma not randomly but only when his grace is invoked by us performing other countervailing karmas a.k.a. "prayers", whether kAyikam, vAcikam or maanasam and in one or the other language. Even so-called "silent" prayers implicitly employ language in the firm of the intention/resolve/sankalpa.

  

The methods of how to do these prayers should be either vedic, Agamic or veda-compatible regional language prayers as attested by Acharyas, shiShTas etc.

  

  

Notwithstanding the fact that certain of the vedic prayers and rituals are said to directly give rise to results, it does not imply prayers work due to some mechanical slot-machine kind of mechanism. No doubt this wrong impression may arise due to the literalism characteristic of vedic prayer. Yet, as SrI Ramana puts it, "karturAjnayA prApyate phalam". Prayers, vedic and non-vedic, don't work like a mechanistic slot machine where my karma is piled up and results ensure. This Intelligent-Being mediated process by which prayer produces effects is explained in some more detail in the Kena bhAShya. The role of shraddhA, bhakti is implicit and not regarded as redundant - because that amounts to the pUrva mImAmsa view of inert karma producing results by itself.

  

Do note, that this devotion, shraddhA in ancient times sometimes had a different flavour than the mediaeval vaiShNava/shaiva expressions of overtly emotional devotion. For example, Sri KumarIla Bhatta had so much devotion to the Vedas as a divine expression, that he was ready to put his life on the line and jump off a cliff due to shraddhA in the prAmANyam of the Vedas. So we need not dismiss the seemingly more prosaic and seemingly literalistic/ritualistic use of vedic mantras to fulfill a desire, as just karma added to the pile. The devotion in vedic ritual was the Kumarila-type of devotion where they could put their life on the line due to devotion to Vedas mAtA.

  

Coming back to whether one should have a pure mind etc., the answer as I understand, is that, the efficacy of prayer is not contingent on having sattvik intentions. Hiranyakashipu "prayed" with intensity to Brahma and got his wish and later on "preyed" on the three worlds! But do note that his tamasic nature was completely set aside temporarily during the time of that austerity he performed and he lived the life of an severely austere hermit for years of tapas. That temporary clause or requirement of some or the other discipline/austerity is non-negotiable.
