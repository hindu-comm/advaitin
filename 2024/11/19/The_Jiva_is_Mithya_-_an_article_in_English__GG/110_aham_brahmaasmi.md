Respected Vidwans,

  

Thank you so much for this lively discussion. I couldn't help but share some of my thoughts here, despite being an absolute beginner, to both shastra and sanskrit. I also wish to pose some questions to which any replies will be gratefully received.

  

***A) Thoughts:***  

  

1 ) Bandaru Vishwanath ji's points on ' understanding siddhanta in any language' and 'learning sanskrit to understand nuances of shastra', as also many otherpoints he made, have been very gratefully received by me. (sincere apologies if I have not quoted him quitecorrectly)

  

2\) I started learning Sanskrit well into adulthood. The internet is replete with many sanskrit lectures of different levels, available in plenty. Right from children's stories, kavyas, sahitya, all the way upto lecture series on vedanta granthas, vyakarana, nyaya, mimamsa,
and vakyarthasin vedanta, nyaya, vyakarana etc. I have an entire collection lined up, happy to share them with anyone as needed.

  

3\) Learning Sanskrit does not have to be an all-or-none approach, the more one can do, the better it is.

  

3 ) Based on my understanding of the language development process (any language), given my medical background (Paediatrics), wrt hearing and speech development in children, as also special circumstances in adults, I now tend to focus more on hearing and speaking in sanskrit, and to a much lesser extent on reading and writing. This method has reaped rich rewards.

  

4\) However, having studied devanagari script (in childhood) for Hindi and Marathi (other indian languages with the same script that is used for sanskrit), does help too. But those who do not have this gift, should not feel discouraged. It is never too late to start somewhere.
Knowing about the gentleman (retired CA) who learnt sanskrit post-retirement and then vedanta, was truly inspiring.

  

5\) The beauty and features of deva bhashaare beyond imagination. There is not a single day that even a beginner like me has not marvelled at the features, what to speak of experts !

7 s's of sanskrit's ability to convey shastram (in my opinion) : sAralyam, saulabhyam, sAnkshiptyam, saundaryam, spashTata, sahajatA, svAbhAvikatA (सारल्यम्‌, सौलभ्यम्‌, साङ्क्षिप्त्यम्‌, सौन्दर्यम्‌, स्पष्टता ) ( Ease, Convenience, Brevity, Beauty, Clarity, 'naturality', Spontaneity)

  

6\) Shifting from a vyakaran-based, reading-writing-based - study approach,to a listening-speaking -based- approach tosanskrit has helped me a lot. (in Samskrita Bharati -India's leading NGO doing stellar work in sanskrit promotion, some leading experts like Chamu Krishna Shastry ji, Dinesh Kamath ji, endorse this, and label it the 'immersion method'). This is just like an approach to our mother-tongue, and/ or to picking up any foreign language, based on exposure to it. For example, if I were to live in France, sooner or later, I would pick up some French just by exposure.

  

7\) Many sanskrit-courses (for secular adults like me ) are built around vyakaran, which I personally find rather unsuitable. It is like putting the horse before the cart. Language comes first and then vyakaran.
Many sincere students learn by thismethod, and then tend to focus only on the vyakaran, rather than getting the tatparya-grahanam (getting the central message) even during vedanta studies. This then tends to A- set an example for other students, who may not be as discerning. B- seems to put other people off studying sanskrit. ( This is ***sincerely not a dosha-darshanam***, but my humble attempt to learn from other's mistakes, so that I make less of my own).

  

8\) I find the textbook - wise study of vyakaran, nyaya, mimamsa (VNM) quite difficult. So I have lined up all the sources and done a bird's eye-view of them. When I study vedanta and come across certain VNM aspects, I then go and look those up in more detail. For ex. in shastrayonitvat, I would know where to look up the types of samasa in more detail. i.e I use VNM as reference sources for my vedanta study.

  

9\) Having said that, my medium of study is still predominantly in english, so I do study vedanta in english, although, my Jnana Guru uses sanskrit words as they are, so that helps.

  

10\) Those who have a long background in vedanta in english may be able to pick up the sanskrit vedanta lectures very easily, as they will be familiar with the technical terms and concepts anyway.

  

11\) Translating Sanskrit words to English etc works only to a limited extent. More often than not, the English translations are more foreign to me, especially when it comes to advanced granthas like advaita siddhi etc. For example, words like Philosophy, Epistemology, Hermeneutics etc. What can be conveyed in 3-4 words in Sanskrit will need 3-5 lines in English and still may not convey the idea with precision. (refer - point no.5 about 7 s's)

  

***My Questions :***

  

  

1\) I would be very grateful if Bandaru Vishwanath ji, or any others could please consider introducing me to sanskrit -speaking communities, especially where shastra is discussed. I regularly attended online groups for spoken sanskrit and that helped a lot. But now, I wish to use this for shastra rather than just conversational sanskrit.

  

2\) Any other advice to help with my studies.

  

with warm regards,

Sakshi
