>
> > For example, a cloth is pot-abhava and is bhavarupa.
>
>   
>
> True.
>
>   
>
> > But are not the horns of a hare also pot-abhava — and abhavarupa?
>
>   
>
> No.
>
>   
>
> Pot-abhAva is not nihswarUpa. Horns of hare are nihswarUpa.

  

How do we know what pot-abhava is or isn’t without examination? It just means “the absence of a pot.” Clearly “the horns of a hare” are incompatible with the presence of a pot. Thus they certainly qualify as the absence of a pot. So it would seem, based on that, that pot-abhava*could* be nihswarupa.

  

>   
>
> Pot-abhAva is avidyA-kArya. Horns of hare are not avidyA-kArya.

  

Again, why not? What then is the karya for the horns of a hare? In fact, even *more* than other things, it would seem that the karya for an erroneous idea like the horns of a hare would be — avidya!

  

>   
>
> Pot-abhAva is prameya and known through anupalabdhi-pramANa. Horns of
> hare are not known through any pramANa as they are not prameya.

  

If the horns of a hare are not prameya, then how do we know what they are or are not? For example, we *know*that they are nihswaroopa. Clearly this requires some pramana.
