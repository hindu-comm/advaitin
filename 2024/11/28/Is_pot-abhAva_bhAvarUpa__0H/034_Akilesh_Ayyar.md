In the future, if you could please give exact scriptural citation when quoting, that would be helpful.

In this case, that is, Br.U.B. 1.2.1, Sankara is giving a specific explanation in the context of trying to prove the prior existence of unseen things that nevertheless shall manifest in the future. That is, effects must exist in prior form in their causes.

  

He uses the example of a jar, and talks about its absence having to be something positive, something existent, prior to its manifestation, or there would be insuperable explanatory problems in its later manifestation.

  

Note that he’s referring to what the jar *before its manifestation* must be, and then reviews its possible negations, and concludes that whatever it must be before its manifestation, that must have some prior form and existence.

  

This is an *entirely different question* than asking “Of what may we say ’this is not the jar’”?

  

In fact, actually, in this very passage Sankara contemplates in passing the question we are debating. He writes: “…if the jar before its manifestation be an absolute nonentity like the proverbial horns of a hare, it cannot be connected either with its cause or with existence…”

  

Note that Sankara does *not*then write that the horns of a hare cannot be “jar-abhava” because that would violate some logical injunction; rather, it cannot be the *locus of the jar before its manifest existence*. That’s a very, very different question.
