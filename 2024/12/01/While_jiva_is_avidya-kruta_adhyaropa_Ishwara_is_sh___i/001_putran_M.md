Namaskaram,

  

Just to insert my thoughts (or as Sada-ji says, my 2 cents):

  

When there is cognition of and identification with duality (jiva-jagat) in any sense, then the knowledge of Brahman (pointed out as the adhishtanam by shastra) is Ishvara in the corresponding sense. If i have father and mother, karma and Dharma, then there is also my Lord of the Mountains governing His 'creation'. If all creation is akin to dream - wherein we still posit a flow, change and continuity, then He is the Dreamer of the dream, i.e. Ishvara = Brahman+mayashakti. If the All is reduced to namarupa denotations of Brahman, then that too is automatic identification of Brahman vis-a-vis a posited namarupa duality, hence as Ishvara. The highest realization of Brahman has no scope for association with duality, hence nor as Ishvara.
