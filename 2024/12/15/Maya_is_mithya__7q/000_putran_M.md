Namaskaram,

  

I started writing a response to Subbu-ji's post on BG 3.16, but it spun off to a separate collection of my thoughts which are noted in the points below.

  

-------------

  

  

1\. In Consciousness, there appears a duality of cognitions.

  

2\. This duality of cognitions is mapped in meaning to a world of duality, involving beings, objects and their relations, and Consciousness also is identified in this world as both its witness and its impeller.

  

3\. The question then is whether this world is a real part or feature of Consciousness=Brahman.

  

If the world-knowledge is based on the duality of cognitions in Consciousness, then would it not follow that there must be an intrinsic duality to Consciousness that is the basis for ‘seeing’ the duality of cognitions?

  

Indeed, the world is pratibimba and its bimba is the Maya of Ishvara. The duality of world is His iccha-kriya projection/cognition of His eternal Jnana. This means essentially that if we begin by positing duality as a fact of the cognition standpoint (vyavaharika), logically we end up positing duality (mayashakti) as being intrinsic to the unitary Consciousness that projects/imagines such a world.

  

4\. Does this mean Brahman = Ishvara =\> Saguna? Sruthi emphatically says No, Brahman is Nirguna. Then what about the duality of cognitions and the corresponding knowledge of Brahman as Ishvara?

  

It is all entirely a case of “imagination”, both world and its creator. This is a very subtle point because the easier position would be that the world is imagination of maya-associated Ishvara who however is the Real. The rope-snake analogy points to the rejection of this thesis because the rope can never be the real creator, but it is the real entity that is imagined as snake due to maya/avidya/adhyasa.

  

Likewise Brahman is the adhishtanam-Self for the imagination of Ishvara and ishvara-jiva-jagat. This is the basis for the mahavakyas like aham brahmaasmi and sarvam Brahma.

  

5\. Should we ask again whose is the imagination and would this not return us to Ishvara who we just now said was Himself a part of the imagination?

  

It is not that Rope=Ishvara/Dreamer and Snake=world/dream distinct from Dreamer. The Snake=Dream includes within it the Dreamer-dream duality in toto, the knower-knowing-known, the being vs non-being, the possibility of imagination and the imputed cause (maya/avidya) for the imagination. And yet by definition the imagination will point to a real cause being inherent in the adhishtanam, thereby returning to the incorrect proposition that Brahman/Rope=Ishvara.

  

Logically the problem has no solution in the binary sat vs asat framework. Maya/avidya and its pratibimba world cannot be categorised as either sat or asat. It is an appearance that has no existence apart from the Rope, no existence in the Rope, and yet cannot be said to not exist either. Therefore, Maya/avidya and likewise Ishvara/jagat are understood as of a distinct ontological category called mithya/anirvachaniya, that is vyavaharika satya in the cognition standpoint but ultimately a case of adhyasa on Brahman.
