Initial response had started off as below before it switched to present post:

  

Quote

  

Here is ~ the translation of Swami Gambhirananda.

  

"And further, that Knowable, though undivided, remaining the same in all beings, like space; appears to be existing; as divided; in all beings, because It is perceived as existing in the bodies themselves. And just as a rope is with regard to a snake that is falsely imagined, similarly that Knowable is the sustainer of all beings, since It sustains all during the period of their existence; the devourer, at the time of dissolution; the originator, at the time of creation."

  

.. The Knowable is Brahman, the undivided.

  

How is Brahman "known" in the context of ignorance (or alt., in the vyavaharika standpoint)? 1) As jivas/beings, jagat; the existence divided and identified in a world of objects and relations; 2) As Ishvara; the 'creator', 'preserver', 'destroyer' of jiva-jagat.

  

This divided "knowledge" of Brahman is ultimately a false imagination (or alt., has mithya validity). It is the snake superimposed on Rope. When the Rope is realized, the qualifications of snake (eyes, fangs; sustainer, etc.) vanish from awareness/real-consideration as the underlying non-dual Reality is 'known' "no matter which way we see It".
