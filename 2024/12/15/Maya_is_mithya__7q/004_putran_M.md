The Taittiriya Up Bhashya II.vi.1., in my reading, is presenting the knowledge of Brahman in the vyavaharika/cognition standpoint wherein the perception of namarupa duality has to be affirmed and accounted for. The understanding of Ishvara is in part corresponding to the nature of the questions asked with regard to duality.  

  

1\. Brahman is Sat, Existence, reality that underlies the apparent duality.

2\. Brahman is the cause for the namarupa projection/cognition.

3\. Brahman causes the world-projection by virtue of Its intrinsic capacity to desire (determine, act).

4\. The namarupa (and the desire that manifests them) has latent existence/identity/being in Brahman alone as there is no non-Self apart from Brahman. Yet Brahman does not consist of them. Brahman conditioned (through desire) with the namarupa upadhis is cognized as the empirical world.

  

(We find however Sri Ananda Giri reduce the namarupa etc. to modifications of Maya that also has identity with Brahman “(through superimposition)”.)

  

In my mind, point 4 is where the Advaitin is confronted with the problem of how to affirm Brahman is in truth Nirguna even while we are dealing with questions on namarupa duality. If we say namarupa is latent in Brahman, then is Maya a real vritti intrinsic to Brahman? Is it svarupa lakshana of Brahman? In the vyavaharika standpoint, that is how Brahman will appear. Yet advaita’s final reply is No: “Brahman does not consist of them” “Maya has identity with Brahman *through* *superimposition*.” Vyavaharika Satya is ultimately mithya because it is asat in the paramartha jnana of Nirguna Brahman.

  

Some parts of the Bhashya copied along with initial notes in \[…\].

  

\[Brahman=Sat =\> asti\]  

  

1\. Brahman is to be accepted as surely existing. ... existence is being first spoken of... what kind of satyam is meant in the assertion .. : "satyam jnanam anantam brahma" .. Brahman's truth is affirmed by speaking of Its existence; for it has been asserted that the existing is the true. Therefore, the very affirmation of existence amounts to an avowal of reality.

  

\[Brahman is the adhishtanam of all perceived namarupa. The fact of cognition implies the reality/existence of substratum of whatever is perceived and this is Brahman. Thus, if we ask "What?" underliesthenamarupa cognition, the answer is "Brahman".

  

Secondly, Brahman is understood as the "cause" for the perceived duality. That is, if we ask the question "Why?"namarupa appears, the answer again is "Brahman" is their cause that projects/cognizes them so.\]

  

2\. Brahman is the cause of space etc. ... Since all the products issuing from Brahman, such as space etc., are perceived... anything from which something is produced does exist... so Brahman does exist ... no effect is perceived in this world as having been produced from a nonentity. If such effects as name and form had originated from a nonentity, they should not have been perceived since they have no reality. But they are perceived. Hence Brahman exists. Should any effect originate from a nonentity, it should remain soaked in unreality even while being perceived. But facts point otherwise. Therefore Brahman exists.

  

\[Now, "How" is Brahman the cause for namarupa manifold appearance? Because the effect (namarupa) is "latent" in the cause (Brahman), non-different from the cause, and itsmanifestation is the iccha of Brahman.\]

  

3\. It is capable of desiring ... \[However,\] Brahman is independent... What then are these (desires of Brahman)? They are by nature truth and knowledge, and they are pure by virtue of their identity with Brahman.
Brahman is not impelled to action by them. But Brahman ordains them in accordance with the results of actions of the creatures. Therefore, Brahman has independence with regard to desires... And this follows also from the fact of Brahman's non-dependence on any other means \[accessories etc.\]... How do they exist then (in Brahman)? They are non-different from Itself.

  

Notes of Ananda Giri:

  

A. “Brahman, as reflected on Maya, is the material cause of the world, and It is possessed of desires that are the modifications of Maya. But these modifications are not distinguishable from truth and knowledge, since they are permeated by Consciousness that is not subject to ignorance etc…”

  

B. "Since Maya, as possessed of the impressions of desires, has identity with Brahman (through superimposition), the desires, that are the modifications of this Maya, have also identity with Brahman. Therefore, there is no need for a physical body, etc." (for making possible the existence of desires in Brahman, as it is in our case).

  

4\. Through the manifestation of namarupa that are latent in Itself. When name and form, existing latently in the Self, get manifested, they evolve into all the states by retaining their intrinsic nature as the self and remaining indistinguishable from Brahman in time and space. Then that evolution of name and form is the appearance of Brahman as many. ... Its finitude and plurality are just as in the case of (the delimitation and diversification of) space ... Hence the Self becomes multiple through these \[nameand form?\] alone. For no such subtle, disconnected and remote thing exists as a non-Self .. which is different from the Self and separated from It .. Therefore, it is because of the Self that name and form have their being in all circumstances, but Brahman does not consist of them. They are said to be essentially Brahman, since they cease to exist when Brahman is eliminated. And as conditioned by these two upadhis \[name and form?\], Brahman enters as a factor into all empirical dealings involving such words as knowledge, knower and knowable, as also their implications etc.
