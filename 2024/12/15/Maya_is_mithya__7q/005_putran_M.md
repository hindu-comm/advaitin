> The Taittiriya Up Bhashya II.vi.1., in my reading, is presenting the
> knowledge of Brahman in the vyavaharika/cognition standpoint wherein
> the perception of namarupa duality has to be affirmed and accounted
> for. The understanding of Ishvara is in part corresponding to the
> nature of the questions asked with regard to duality.  
>
>   

  

Shankaracharya in this bhashya has given important guidance not only in advaita siddhanta but also in sadhana. For our constant questions on namarupa duality, like What? Why? How?, we typically look for answers within the movie-context of jagat itself. That constitutes the madness of our samsara. The shastra completes our knowledge of the movie by pointing out ishvara as its God or Governer, thus we connect everything back to the One but in a dvaitic way.

  

The Bhashya however goes the next step and directs towards advaita: the answer to such questions at all levels is the same: Brahman. He will still be understood as Ishvara so long as we maintain the reference back to duality, but the namarupa no longer has any distinct reality that we can ponder independently on them or of them; it is all only Ishvara, His will, His appearance, Him. When our attention thus turns and fixes in Ishvara, as the answer to all questions, the questions fall away because their basis which is the belief in the duality also becomes sublated in Him. He alone remains, eventually no longer as even the cause or support of the "many", for the "many" is entirely sublated, but as the non-dual Self/Consciousness.

  

Basically, the acharya teaches the mumukshu-samsarin to keep turning our attention to Ishvara as the only Answer/Reality so that all imagination of a distinct or separate world is absorbed and annulled, and our consciousness is merged in Him.
