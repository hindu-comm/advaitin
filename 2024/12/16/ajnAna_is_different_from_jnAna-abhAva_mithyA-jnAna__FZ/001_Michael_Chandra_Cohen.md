### Summary

The entry discusses the **lakshaNa (definition)** of **ajnAna (ignorance)** as presented by Chitsukhacharya and defended in Advaita Vedanta, particularly in the Vivaraṇa school. The central claim is that **ajnAna is bhAvarUpa (a positive entity)**, distinct from **jnAna-abhAva (absence of knowledge)**, **mithyA-jnAna (false knowledge)**, and **mithyA-jnAna-samskAra (impressions leading to false knowledge)**. The following points are addressed:

1.  **Chitsukhacharya’s Definition**:

    - AjnAna is described as **anAdi (beginningless)** and
      **bhAvarUpa**, and it is sublated by **Atma-jnAna
      (self-knowledge)**.
    - Without such bhAvarUpa ajnAna, the practical experiences
      (vyavahAra) like "nAsti" (non-existence) and "na prakAshate"
      (non-illumination) about Brahman would not be possible.

2.  **Opponent's View**:

    - The opponent argues that ajnAna can be explained as:
      - **jnAna-abhAva** (absence of knowledge),
      - **mithyA-jnAna** (false knowledge), or
      - **mithyA-jnAna-samskAra** (false-knowledge impressions).
    - These alone can account for the non-illumination of Brahman and do
      not necessitate positing a bhAvarUpa ajnAna.

3.  **Rebuttal by Vivaraṇa**:

    - The non-illumination of Brahman in deep sleep (**sushupti**)
      cannot be due to:
      - **MithyA-jnAna**, as it is absent in sushupti.
      - **MithyA-jnAna-samskAra**, as such samskAras do not obstruct
        self-revealing Brahman.
      - **JnAna-abhAva**, since **swarUpa-jnAna (self-knowledge)** is
        eternal and cannot have an absence.
    - AjnAna must be **bhAvarUpa**, akin to an **AvaraNa (veiling
      force)**, which obstructs the realization of Brahman despite its
      self-luminosity.

4.  **Analogy and Support**:

    - Just as a blindfold (avidyA) leads to three effects—**agrahaNa
      (non-perception)**, **viparIta-jnAna (misperception)**, and
      **samshaya (doubt)**—bhAvarUpa ajnAna is required to explain the
      experiences of ignorance.

5.  **Conclusion**:

    - BhAvarUpa ajnAna is necessary and distinct from jnAna-abhAva,
      mithyA-jnAna, and its samskAra. This refutes the opponent's
      interpretation of ajnAna.

### Translation

Chitsukhacharya defines ajnAna as:

> 

> **अनादिभावरूपं यद्विज्ञानेन विलीयते। तदज्ञानमिति प्राज्ञा लक्षणं संप्रचक्षते॥**

> "That which is beginningless, of the nature of existence, and is
> dissolved by knowledge—this is defined as ajnAna by the wise."

Vivaraṇa elaborates:

> Without the presence of **bhAvarUpa ajnAna**, practical experiences
> such as "Brahman does not exist or is not illumined" are not possible.
> For instance, the obstruction caused by ajnAna is akin to the veiling
> of an object by darkness. Without this positive obstruction (AvaraNa),
> Brahman would naturally reveal itself as self-luminous.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Refutation

The arguments posited for bhAvarUpa ajnAna can be systematically refuted as follows:

1.  **Conceptual Ambiguity of BhAvarUpa AjnAna**:

    - Claiming ajnAna to be **bhAvarUpa (a positive entity)**
      contradicts the Advaita principle that Brahman is the sole reality
      and all else is mithyA (illusory). A **real, positive entity** of
      ajnAna would undermine the non-dual nature of Brahman.
    - If ajnAna is bhAvarUpa and real, how is it sublated (removed) by
      jnAna, which is mithyA in nature under Advaita?

2.  **Sushupti and AjnAna**:

    - The non-illumination of Brahman in deep sleep (**sushupti**) can
      be better explained as **jnAna-abhAva** (absence of cognition)
      rather than positing a new positive entity (bhAvarUpa ajnAna). The
      mind, being absent in sushupti, simply fails to recognize Brahman
      due to lack of mental activity.
    - The analogy of a blindfold fails because it assumes Brahman’s
      luminosity needs an external factor to manifest. However,
      self-luminous Brahman does not depend on anything else for
      illumination.

3.  **AvaraNa (Veiling) and Advaita**:

    - AvaraNa as a distinct entity goes against Advaita's ontological
      framework. AjnAna, if described as **anAdi-bhAvarUpa**, assumes an
      inexplicable nature (anirvachanIya) but gives it undue ontological
      status, which dilutes the non-dual premise of Advaita.

4.  **SamskAra and VyavahAra**:

    - The claim that samskAras cannot obstruct tattva (truth) is
      inconsistent with the theory of mental impressions in Advaita.
      SamskAras **do influence vyavahAra** and can explain Brahman’s
      non-illumination in the waking and dream states.
    - The Vivaraṇa's argument dismissing samskAras as non-obstructive
      lacks substantiation, as samskAras are known to influence
      perception and cognition.

5.  **Logical Issues in AjnAna as BhAvarUpa**:

    - If ajnAna is a positive entity (bhAvarUpa), then its relationship
      with Brahman must be clarified. Is it **an ontological reality**?
      If yes, Advaita collapses into duality. If it is **mithyA**, it
      cannot act as a substantive veil.

6.  **Gītā Reference Misinterpretation**:

    - The Gītā’s reference to avidyA being tamasika and leading to
      effects like agrahaNa or viparIta-jnAna does not necessitate
      bhAvarUpa ajnAna. These effects can arise from the absence of
      clarity (jnAna-abhAva) within the prakṛti-based framework.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Conclusion

The necessity of positing a **bhAvarUpa ajnAna** is logically untenable within Advaita Vedanta's framework. The non-illumination of Brahman in sushupti and vyavahAra can be adequately explained by **jnAna-abhAva**, samskAras, or mithyA-jnAna without requiring a distinct, positive entity. The opponent's simpler explanations are sufficient and do not introduce unnecessary ontological complications.
