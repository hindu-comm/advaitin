Shankara, in his prasthanatraya bhashyas, has explicitly advocated the physical puja of the Guru:  
  
We find the following three verses at the beginning of the Taittiriya Bhashyam, one of which is a direct namaskara to his Purvacharyas who have expounded the Upanishads:

  

यैरिमेगुरुभिःपूर्वंपदवाक्यप्रमाणतः।व्याख्याताःसर्ववेदान्तास्तान्नित्यंप्रणतोऽस्म्यहम्॥ २ ॥

At the end of the Mandukya bhashya he composes verses in veneration of his Guru and Paramaguru.

In the Mundaka bhashya explaining the mantra which says: the aspirants for worldly benefits and moksha have to venerate the Brahma Jnani, Shankara says: by prakakshina namaskara,etc.

At the end of the Prashnopanishad bhashya, while the aspirants are paying their respects to their Guru Pippalada for having given them the Brahmavidya, the upanishad says: tam archantaH, Shankara says: by submitting flowers at the feet of the Guru, etc.

So, Shankara is quite open to the Puja of the Acharya.

Interestingly we find similar, almost same, wordings in Shankara's opening and closing verses for the 17th chapter of the Upadeshasahasri.
