Namaskaram,

  

Very elevating talk of the essential preparatory role of yoga in Vedanta. The swami has a serene deep commanding voice. There were a couple of highlight drishtantas (on Sant Eknath and on the mother of a sick child) that sort of lifted us to "realize" what he meant byatmabhav. In the Q&A, the first question is on our standard objection to moksha being understood as a result of action. For the last question, his response emphasizing vairagya seemed directed to the questioner in particular; but if we replace the external things he mentions with the internal clutter that we hoard in our minds, then the message has a universal relevance: we have to identify and empty the.mind of the raaga-clutter, of identifications and thought patterns, that have not added "good value" to our life.

  

"Of all the things that you have accumulated in your life, what has added good value to your life? Can you identify that? And what has not added any value to your life, can you throw it away from your house? If you can do that, then that is Vairagya and that is *your* sadhana."
