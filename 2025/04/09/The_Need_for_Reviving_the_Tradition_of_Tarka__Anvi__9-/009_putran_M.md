Namaskaram Sunil-ji,

  

I can't say I fathom what all this entails and how Chitta-ji envisions this knowledge-revolution should happen. Perhaps our research institutions need tocommit their time and resources to understanding western thought, identifying where itsclaims are purvapaksha to our tradition, and then to uncompromisingly do tarka against such in order to re-establish our sampradaya knowledge on solid rational foundations. Similar to how shankara does in his bhashyas against the faulty ideas of his day. That will re-vitalize the shraddha in our people for our systems and knowledge, leading to a true astika renaissance.

  

There is a problem here. Some of the traditional knowledge that Chitta-ji wants tarka to resurrect expounds theories that contradict the basic tenets of modern science. Such as stimulus-response based perception, and maybe also Evolution, Relativity, particle physics, etc. Hey, we just believe these things nowadays; we have shraddha in the methods and proofs of science. And we live in a world of technology that is built on the foundations of these theories. That is pramana enough to evoke deep shraddha in us all. Nobody wants to be fighting science "in its domain" just to dogmatically uphold outdated theories of our own. We had debates in our forum as well on these topics where people wanted to argue why we should accept the scientific-views. That may seem sensible to most of us but afaiu not to Chitta-ji; and unfortunately he may not find an truly resonant audience among traditional scholars either because they too have adopted a position of compromise.

  

Why not just (formally) accept the adrishta things like punarjanma, based on shabda, but then bypass all these other vyavahara "howthe world works" topics where our tradition may contradictthe findings of science? Do they really matter for Vedanta?

  

Anyway this is a debate and mananam that needs to happen in scholarly circles. And it should involve people with the sort of convictions like Chitta-ji as well.

  

Now, for the question you actually asked, at the individual level, I think we should become more deliberate in our reasoning and decision-making process, that it is pramana-based and how so. So, we have to first of all teach ourselves what the pramanas are and how they are used. Then we have to do reasoning within the bounds of the accepted pramana-framework. And we have to dissuade ourselves from going off in speculative tangents independently of the pramanas.

  

If I see a cow, then that is valid knowledge due to the pratyaksha of eye-sense. If I see smoke and think fire is there, then I know that I am applying anumana based on the suitable vyapti. It may be I am mistaken and all that, due to unforeseenobstructions or defects; but my reasoning is clear and consistent. In the other direction, suppose I send this mail and think someone is angry at me after reading it. I have to be able to trace the reasoning, whether this cognition "he is angry" perceived in my mind is valid knowledge of the world (via proper application of anumana, upamana, etc.) or whether it reduces to speculation. And if the latter, then I have to stop building further castles in the air of my imagination!

  

thollmelukaalkizhu
