Namaste,

  

I do not participate much on this forum nowadays due to my other preoccupations but seeing that Sri Putran-ji has taken the trouble to bring some of the ideas I had expressed in the article on reviving the tradition of tarka to this forum, I feel I should add a few words to what he has said.

  

  

*I can't say I fathom what all this entails and how Chitta-ji envisions this knowledge-revolution should happen.*

  

I hope to give an indication of how it would unfold in my next book. But of course, this is not a one-man enterprise; it would need a whole community of scholars to be working on it.

  

Writing the book is taking longer than I expected because the scope of the book has increased from what it was envisaged to be when i started. I realized, after writing the first chapter, that most Indians, especially scholars from traditional circles, lack adequate exposure to Westerm philosophy, so I thought it would be a good idea to include a summary of modern philosophical thought (beginning with the modern era, i.e., Descartes, and ending with Post-Modernism) as one part of my book. Unfortunately, incorporating this summary (which has grown to almost 250 pages) has taken up more than 2 years of my time and I am still not through with it. I hope to complete this part in the next few months and then get on to the main part of the book.

  

  

*There is a problem here. Some of the traditional knowledge that Chitta-ji wants tarka to resurrect expounds theories that contradict the basic tenets of modern science. Such as stimulus-response based perception, and maybe also Evolution, Relativity, particle physics, etc. Hey, we just believe these things nowadays; we have shraddha in the methods and proofs of science. And we live in a world of technology that is built on the foundations of these theories. That is pramana enough to evoke deep shraddha in us all. Nobody wants to be fighting science "in its domain" just to dogmatically uphold outdated theories of our own. We had debates in our forum as well on these topics where people wanted to argue why we should accept the scientific-views.*

  

As part of the summary of Western philosophical thought, I have included a section on '*Philosophy of Science'*. I think this section will help to bring home to us the point that the foundations of science are not really as solid and firm as many of us think they are. I shall present this section, in two parts, in another thread on this forum. Part I deals with the problem of defining what science is and Part II deals with that aspect of philosiophy of science which would be of more interest to us namely the *realism verses anti-realism debate* in the field of philosophy of science.

  

  

*Now, for the question you actually asked, at the individual level, I think we should become more deliberate in our reasoning and decision-making process, that it is pramana-based and how so. So, we have to first of all teach ourselves what the pramanas are and how they are used. Then we have to do reasoning within the bounds of the accepted pramana-framework. And we have to dissuade ourselves from going off in speculative tangents independently of the pramanas.*



Yes, I fully agree.

  

Warm regards,

Chittaranjan
