Namaskar;

There isn't any precise (not even approximate) percentage of the Tamil Brahmin women wearing the Madisar (9 yard saree) on a regular basis.
The orthodoxy of a traditional family can not be determined on the basis of the dress code. The character of human personality is measured mostly on the basis of how the human interact in the society. Let us not try to measure the purity or nobility of a person on the basis of the dress he/she wears and unfortunately such judgements will lead us to unnecessary pitfalls. During religious ceremonies, festivals, events such as weddings, Seemanthams, Pujas, and last rites, brahmin women do wear Madisar. Even there is no universal custom to follow this routine and they are mostly done on voluntary basis. Madisar is certainly not a daily wear for most Brahmin families. But some orthodox families do maintain to wear Madisar during religious and spiritual activities.

The announcement from Kanchi Kamakoti Peetam declares that they are all set to appoint Duddu Satya Venkata Surya Subrahmanya Ganesha Sharma Dravidas its 71^(st)pontiff (acharya). He is set to be inducted into the mutt by the current seer, Vijayendra Saraswathi Shankaracharya Swamigal, at a function to be held in Kancheepuram on April 30. The new pontiff was raised by a Telugu brahmin family and provided all the necessary vedic studies and made him eligible to become the 71^(st) pontiff of Kanchi Sankara mutt. Let us stop probing the dresses of the parents or where they come from or what language they speak.

I am reminded of the saying, "Nadhi moolam, rishi moolam" that translates to "don't seek the origin of a river or a sage".It's a metaphor for the idea that one shouldn't delve too deeply into the origins or the past of something, but rather accept it at the current state.The saying suggests that focusing on origins can lead to unnecessary and incorrect judgement. We should appreciate the parents who give up their son to “Sanyasa” and to serve the universal community to keep and nourish the culture and traditions!
