Namaskaram,

  

That was intenseand packed, but a very excellent presentation by Nithin-ji. I was holding to a wrong impression that only in manushya janma one can strive and attain brahma-jnana and that devatas are temporary stations on account of karma, after which they must also return to human lives to strive for moksha. The talk clarifies that as a wrong understanding, although I am wondering how I came to such a misunderstanding or whether there is further nuance to this.

  

Nithin-ji also made it explicit that shankaracharya (and the smartha sampradaya) holds the manusmriti as pramana shastra that is aligned to the Vedas and not something less or changeable or flawed. If Veda is pratyaksha (from the words), the smritis are regarded as the anumana based on theVedas. We have to ask whether this interpretation can be analysed and justified explicitly, in terms of the definition and conditions for anumana. The other point that Nithin-ji mentioned in this context was that when there is the option between Veda vakya and smriti vakya, one may prefer and cite the Veda since it is direct pratyaksha and explicit but that does not mean the smriti's validity is questioned or diminished. (Of course, the general principle being that the smriti has to be understood without ending up as a contradiction to the Vedas.)

  

A cousin of mine argued that dharma is to be known only through ithihasa-purana(let me include smriti in general). That the Veda does not teach much of dharma and hence is not really the pramana for dharma. Now, this may not be formally accepted in the tradition but informally or as a way of arthavada for purana-ithihasa, that is the impression often spread. I think Sri Sundarkuman (in talk 2 on paramacharya) makes a brief statement along this line (will give the timestamp if I hear and find the place again); of course, he meant it in the right way. But the point is, my cousin or bhakti-oriented people will want the Ramayana and Mahabharatha to be given almost an independentimportance, and not derivationally subordinate to theVedas for all practical purposes. Push comes to shove, their argument will be "Smriti is pratyaksha and no way to establish it as anumana of Veda." So the siddhantin will need to delve further into how and why smriti is anumana.

  

Then of course the talk is just touching the surface of all the dirt being slung at manusmriti et al. The people are not worried about whether according to shankaracharya, moksha is available or not for those out ofashramas or women or shudras - what care they for moksha?, but that visesha dharma is not available and optional for all across the board; that certain karma-anushtanas are "withheld from 2/3 Hindu society". Moreover all the "evil quotes" of lead in ears and all that. Definitely since Nithin-ji hasdebated in SM, he must be used to dealing with all such criticism. Anyway this most engaging talk definitely can be followed up by a series by him and posted in Advaita-Academy. And we can buy and read his book as well.
