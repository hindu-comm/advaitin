> A cousin of mine argued that dharma is to be known only through
> ithihasa-purana(let me include smriti in general). That the Veda does
> not teach much of dharma and hence is not really the pramana for
> dharma. Now, this may not be formally accepted in the tradition but
> informally or as a way of arthavada for purana-ithihasa, that is the
> impression often spread. I think Sri Sundarkuman (in talk 2 on
> paramacharya) makes a brief statement along this line (will give the
> timestamp if I hear and find the place again); of course, he meant it
> in the right way.

  

I should not have inserted Sri Sundarkumar in there, without first finding the reference and quoting correctly. My recollection is that he said something along the lines that ithihasa-puranaare the means to understanding veda. So he did not try and delink or undermine the primary pramanatvam of veda in any sense. My reason for mentioning it was that it can be understood as saying that the ithihasa-puranaare the necessary and only means. And that can perhaps mislead people into thinking of a fundamental pramana-duality between sruthi and smriti.
