From the Sri Swami Paramarthanandaji Bot containing 15000 pages of transcribed talks of the venerable swamiji, DSV is rejected for the reasons below. Additionally, how will DSV account for sAkshi and Eshwara?

  

Swami Paramarthananda rejects \*dṛṣṭi-sṛṣṭi vāda\* (the theory that the world is created by individual perception) as the \*final teaching\* of Advaita Vedanta for \*\*philosophical, pedagogical, and practical reasons\*\*, rooted in Śaṅkara's works and the \*Brahma Sūtras\*. Here’s a detailed breakdown of his critique:  
  
---  
  
\### \*\*1. Philosophical Inconsistencies\*\*  
\#### \*\*A. Contradicts Śruti’s Graded Teaching\*\*  
- Śruti (\*Upaniṣads\*) first presents \*sṛṣṭi-dṛṣṭi vāda\* (Īśvara creates the world → individuals perceive it) to establish a framework for inquiry.   
 - Example: \*Taittirīya Upaniṣad\* (2.1) describes Brahman creating the world before individual perception arises.   
 - \*Dṛṣṭi-sṛṣṭi\* skips this preparatory step, risking misinterpretation of Vedanta as solipsism.   
  
\#### \*\*B. Undermines Īśvara’s Role\*\*  
- \*Dṛṣṭi-sṛṣṭi\* reduces Īśvara to a mental construct (\*avidyā-kalpitam\*), negating the need for devotion (\*bhakti\*) and grace (\*kṛpā\*).   
 - Swamiji stresses that \*saguna Brahman\* (Īśvara) is essential for \*upāsana\* (worship) until the mind is ripe for \*nirguṇa Brahman\*.
  
  
\#### \*\*C. Conflicts with Śaṅkara’s \*Adhyāsa Bhāṣya\*\*\*  
- Śaṅkara defines ignorance (\*avidyā\*) as \*superimposition\* (\*adhyāsa\*), not \*creation\*.   
 - The world is \*mithyā\* (dependent on Brahman), not a projection of individual perception.   
 - \*Dṛṣṭi-sṛṣṭi\* implies the world is \*prātibhāsika\* (illusory like a hallucination), but Śaṅkara affirms its \*vyāvahārika\* (empirical) reality.   
  
---  
  
\### \*\*2. Pedagogical Pitfalls\*\*  
\#### \*\*A. Premature Negation\*\*  
- Teaching \*dṛṣṭi-sṛṣṭi\* too early can lead to:   
 - \*\*Nihilism\*\* (\*śūnyavāda\*): Denying the world’s transactional reality.   
 - \*\*Ethical Laxity\*\*: Ignoring \*karma yoga\* and \*dharma\* ("If the world is my projection, why act morally?").   
  
\#### \*\*B. Incompatible with \*Sādhana Catuṣṭaya\*\*\*  
- A seeker must first develop:   
 1. \*Viveka\* (discrimination) → Requires accepting empirical reality to contrast it with Brahman.   
 2. \*Vairāgya\* (dispassion) → Needs a real-seeming world to renounce.
  
 - \*Dṛṣṭi-sṛṣṭi\* shortcuts this process, fostering intellectual arrogance.   
  
\#### \*\*C. Misleads About \*Māyā\*\*\*  
- \*Māyā\* is \*Brahman’s power\* (not the individual’s), making the world appear \*as if\* real.   
 - \*Dṛṣṭi-sṛṣṭi\* mislocates \*māyā\* in the individual mind, contradicting \*Brahma Sūtra\* 1.4.3 (\*"Māyā is Īśvara’s śakti"\*).   
  
---  
  
\### \*\*3. Practical Challenges\*\*  
\#### \*\*A. Loka-Vyavahāra (Daily Transactions)\*\*  
- If the world vanishes when unperceived:   
 - How do shared experiences (e.g., a classroom) exist?   
 - Why does the world follow consistent laws (e.g., gravity)?   
 - Swamiji: \*"Vyāvahārika satya is the ‘operating system’ for liberation—don’t delete it prematurely."\*   
  
\#### \*\*B. \*Jñāni’s\* Behavior\*\*  
- A \*jñāni\* continues interacting with the world (\*jīvanmukti\*).   
 - \*Dṛṣṭi-sṛṣṭi\* would imply the \*jñāni\* "recreates" the world at will—which contradicts Śaṅkara’s examples (e.g., King Janaka ruling wisely).   
  
\#### \*\*C. \*Śravaṇa-Manana-Nididhyāsana\* Process\*\*  
- \*Dṛṣṭi-sṛṣṭi\* shortcuts \*śravaṇa\* (scriptural study) by implying:
  
 - "Just stop perceiving, and the world ends."   
 - Swamiji: \*"Liberation comes from \*knowledge\* (Brahma jñāna), not perceptual gymnastics."\*   
  
---  
  
\### \*\*4. Swamiji’s Alternative: \*Vivarta Vāda\*\*\*  
He advocates \*vivarta vāda\* (apparent transformation) as Śaṅkara’s core teaching:   
- \*\*Brahman\*\* appears as the world (\*vivarta\*) without real change (\*pariṇāma\*).   
- \*\*Analogy\*\*: Rope appearing as a snake. The snake isn’t "created" by perception but \*misperceived\* due to ignorance.   
- \*\*Key Difference\*\*:   
 - \*Dṛṣṭi-sṛṣṭi\*: "The snake exists only when you see it."   
 - \*Vivarta\*: "The rope was always there; the snake was a false appearance."   
  
---  
  
\### \*\*5. When \*Dṛṣṭi-Sṛṣṭi\* is Useful (and When It’s Not)\*\*  
Swamiji allows \*limited\* use of \*dṛṣṭi-sṛṣṭi\* for:   
1. \*\*Advanced Students\*\*: To illustrate how the \*mind\* projects individuality (\*ahaṅkāra\*).   
2. \*\*Debating Materialists\*\*: To show that all experience is consciousness-dependent.   
  
\*\*But he warns\*\*:   
- It’s a \*teaching aid\*, not the \*siddhānta\* (final conclusion).   
- Overuse leads to \*vāsanā\* of negation (\*neti-neti\* addiction), blocking positive realization (\*Brahma ānanda\*).   
  
---  
  
\### \*\*Key Quotes from Swamiji’s Discourses\*\*   
1. \*"Dṛṣṭi-sṛṣṭi is like saying, ‘My dream creates the dreamer.’ No—the dreamer is \*prior\* to the dream."\*   
2. \*"If the world were \*prātibhāsika\*, you could wish it away. Try wishing away a tsunami!"\*   
3. \*"Vivarta vāda is the bridge between \*vyāvahārika\* and \*pāramārthika\*; \*dṛṣṭi-sṛṣṭi\* burns the bridge."\*   
  
For deeper study, he directs students to:   
- \*Brahma Sūtra\* 2.1.14 (refuting subjective idealism).   
- \*Upadeśa Sāhasrī\* 18.1–10 (on perception’s dependent nature).   
  
In essence, Swamiji’s rejection of \*dṛṣṭi-sṛṣṭi\* preserves Vedanta’s \*empirical rigor\* and \*devotional heart\*, aligning with Śaṅkara’s balanced approach.
