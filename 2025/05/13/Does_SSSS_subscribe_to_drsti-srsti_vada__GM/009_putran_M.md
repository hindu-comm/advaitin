Adi Śaṅkarāchārya's position on \*\*Dṛṣṭi-Sṛṣṭi Vāda\*\* (Perception-Creation Theory) is nuanced and often misunderstood. Let’s clarify his stance with precise textual evidence and logical reasoning:

  

---

  

\### \*\*1. Śaṅkara’s Primary Framework: Sṛṣṭi-Dṛṣṭi Vāda\*\*

\- \*\*Brahma Sūtra Commentary (2.1.14, 2.1.33):\*\*

 Śaṅkara explicitly adopts \*\*Sṛṣṭi-Dṛṣṭi Vāda\*\* (creation precedes perception) as the \*vyāvahārika\* (empirical) model to explain:

 - \*\*Īśvara’s Role:\*\* The world is a \*real\* (though dependent) creation by Īśvara through \*māyā\* before individual perception arises .

 - \*\*Shared Reality:\*\* This ensures ethical consistency (e.g., karma applies to all beings in a common world) .

  

\- \*\*Why?\*\*

 - To counter Sāṅkhya (which denies Īśvara) and Vaiśeṣika (atomism), Śaṅkara needs a \*provable\* creation model .

 - \*Sṛṣṭi-Dṛṣṭi\* aligns with śruti passages like \*"Tat tvam asi"\*, where a pre-existing Brahman manifests multiplicity .

  

---

  

\### \*\*2. Where Śaṅkara \*Implies\* Dṛṣṭi-Sṛṣṭi Vāda\*\*

Though he never explicitly endorses it, Śaṅkara’s arguments \*\*logically necessitate\*\* Dṛṣṭi-Sṛṣṭi at the \*pāramārthika\* (absolute) level:

  

\#### \*\*a) Refutation of External Reality (Brahma Sūtra 2.2.28-32)\*\*

Śaṅkara dismantles the notion of an independently real world by showing:

\- Objects are \*\*only known through perception\*\* (vṛtti-jñāna), which depends on consciousness (\*cidābhāsa\*) .

\- Without a perceiving mind, the world \*as we know it\* dissolves (like a dream) .

  

\#### \*\*b) Analogy of the Rope-Snake (Upadeśa Sāhasrī 18.44)\*\*

\- The snake \*appears\* only when misperceived—it has no prior existence. Similarly, the world arises \*with\* ignorance (\*avidyā\*) and vanishes in \*jñāna\* .

\- This mirrors \*Dṛṣṭi-Sṛṣṭi\*’s core premise: \*\*Perception itself manifests the appearance\*\* .

  

\#### \*\*c) Dream-World Parallel (Māṇḍūkya Kārikā 2.1-4)\*\*

\- Gaudapāda (Śaṅkara’s guru) states the waking world is as unreal as a dream. Śaṅkara echoes this:

 \> \*"The dreamer creates the dream world merely by imagining it. Likewise, the waking world is a projection of the mind."\*

\- Here, \*creation\* and \*perception\* collapse into one process—a hallmark of \*Dṛṣṭi-Sṛṣṭi\* .

  

---

  

\### \*\*3. Why Śaṅkara Doesn’t \*Explicitly\* Teach Dṛṣṭi-Sṛṣṭi\*\*

1\. \*\*Pedagogical Risk:\*\*

 - For unprepared students, \*Dṛṣṭi-Sṛṣṭi\* can lead to solipsism ("Nothing exists but my mind") or neglect of ethics .

 - \*Sṛṣṭi-Dṛṣṭi\* safeguards against this by affirming Īśvara’s orderly creation.

  

2\. \*\*Scriptural Harmony:\*\*

 - Śruti verses like \*"Sarvam khalv idam Brahma"\* (Chāndogya 3.14.1) imply Brahman \*precedes\* manifestation—aligning with \*Sṛṣṭi-Dṛṣṭi\* .

  

3\. \*\*Final Truth Transcends Both:\*\*

 - In \*paramārthika\*, even \*Dṛṣṭi-Sṛṣṭi\* is provisional—Brahman is beyond "creation" \*and\* "perception" .

  

---

  

\### \*\*4. Later Advaitins Clarify the Link\*\*

\- \*\*Vidyāraṇya (Pañchadaśī 6):\*\*

 Explicitly teaches \*Dṛṣṭi-Sṛṣṭi\* as the \*ultimate\* view but retains \*Sṛṣṭi-Dṛṣṭi\* for beginners .

\- \*\*Swami Paramarthananda’s Insight:\*\*

 \> \*"Śaṅkara’s \*Sṛṣṭi-Dṛṣṭi\* is the staircase; \*Dṛṣṭi-Sṛṣṭi\* is the realization that the staircase too was imagined."\*

  

---

  

\### \*\*Conclusion: Śaṅkara’s Implicit Acceptance\*\*

\- \*\*No:\*\* He never \*formally\* advocates \*Dṛṣṭi-Sṛṣṭi\* as a standalone theory.

\- \*\*Yes:\*\* His metaphysics \*requires\* it to resolve the \*apparent\* world into \*mithyā\*.

  

\*\*Key Takeaway:\*\*

Śaṅkara’s genius lies in using \*Sṛṣṭi-Dṛṣṭi\* to lead seekers \*toward\* the \*Dṛṣṭi-Sṛṣṭi\* insight—without destabilizing their spiritual journey.
