> Just to be sure, these terms are defined as, " pure consciousness
> (Brahman) conditioned by māyā" andpure consciousness (Brahman)
> reflected in avidyā (ignorance), respectively, by the Bot.

  

Upahita does not mean conditioned. Take from example face. When you put mirror in front of it, it becomes bimba. Nothing changes in face. But it becomes eligible to be called a bimba. This is what is upahita-chaitanya. It is shuddha chaitanya. But called as sAkshI/Ishwara from the frame of reference of avidyA/mirror.

  

> The idea of asanga chaitanya with an upadhi and actual ignorance
> thatcan be a reflecting medium doesn't make much sense to me.

  

Does the idea of buddhi as reflecting medium being the upAdhi of asanga chaitanya make sense to you? It has to. There are explicit statements in bhAshya for this.

  

And if that makes sense to you, then buddhi being avidyA-krita, avidyA-pratibimbita-chaitanya should also make sense. Nothing illogical or impractical.

  

> Are they elaborations upon the notions of mula and tula avidya?
> Instead both terms are sastrika superimpositions intended to dissuade
> notions of individuality and objective realism intended tobe
> ultimately withdrawn. Otherwise, what is the benefit of creating
> multiple avidyas? Do we find that in PTB or is it just another new
> theoretical elaboration?

  

MUlAvidyA is central to Advaita. Whenever Advaita says avidyA, it is mUlAvidyA.

  

The sum and substance is this -- whatever is seen, is illusory. The seer is non-illusory. Even I is illusory. The entity intended by the word I is non-illusory.
