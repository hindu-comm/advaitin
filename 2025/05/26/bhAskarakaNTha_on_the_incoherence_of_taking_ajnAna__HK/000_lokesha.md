नमः परमेश्वराय  
  
I want to share an interesting piece of text while going through the text चित्तानुबोधशास्त्र written by भास्करकण्ठ. This might be interesting to many.  
  
To those who do not know, Bhāskarakaṇṭha was an 18th-century ācārya and an expert in Abhinavagupta’s Paramādvaita Darśana as well as Vedānta Darśana. In his excellent work Cittānubodha, he affirms both Vedānta and Paramādvaita as representing the highest reality. He elucidates this through the concepts of Śiva–Śakti, and also through Brahman, which he describes as saccidānandasvarūpa.  
  
This integrative approach was possible because Bhāskarakaṇṭha was not only traditionally trained in Paramādvaita/Pratyabhijñā Darśana by his teachers, but also received formal training in Vedānta. In this work, he also offers sharp critiques of the Naiyāyikas, Bauddhas, and other systems, demonstrating keen philosophical insight.  
  
In my opinion, this is an exceptional work. The ācārya’s arguments are strikingly apt, practical, aesthetically refined, and intellectually captivating.  
  
Now, his remarks on Ajñāna as being opposite of jñāna, as opposed to being the absence of jñāna -  
  
ज्ञानं च परताज्ञानं सर्वभावेषु संस्मृतम्।  
न ज्ञानं नीलपीतादिज्ञानम् अज्ञानजृम्भितम्।।१७६  
  
jñāna (knowledge) is remembered as being other than ajñāna (ignorance) in all things.  
That jñāna is not the knowledge of blue, yellow, etc., which is a manifestation of ajñāna.  
  
विपरीतं बुधैर् ज्ञानम् अज्ञानमिति कीर्तितम्।  
नञो विपर्यये वृत्तेः स्वभावादेव नान्यतः।।१८७  
  
The wise declare that jñāna is the opposite of ajñāna.  
The negative particle नञ functions by way of reversal,...  
  
नीलादिकं हि चेत्यं स्यात् पूर्णचित्तत्वजृम्भणम्।  
अपूर्णनीलताज्ञानं तत्राज्ञानं प्रकीर्तितम्।।१७८  
  
The object such blue, etc., is a manifestation of the completeness of mind. (?)  
The incomplete jñāna of blueness is there declared to be ajñāna.  
  
ज्ञानाभावोsज्ञानमिति मूर्खाणां तु विकल्पना।  
ज्ञानाभावे हि केयं स्यान् नीलादेः पुरतः स्फुरा।।१७९  
  
The absence of jñāna (knowledge) is ajñāna (ignorance) — this is the imagining of fools.  
For if there is an absence of jñāna, what would be the manifestation of blue, etc., in front?  
  
शुक्तौ रजतविज्ञानम् अज्ञानं परिकीर्तितम्।  
ज्ञानाभावे तु का स्फूर्ती रजतस्य न विद्महे।।१८०  
  
The cognition of silver in a shell is declared to be ajñāna.  
But if there is an absence of jñāna, we do not know what the manifestation of silver would be.  
  
ज्ञानाभावेति वाक् चैषा व्याघातेनैव दूषिता।  
ज्ञानेन रहितो मूढो किं ब्रूयात् स शिलोपमः।।१८१  
  
This statement, "the absence of jñāna," is flawed by contradiction.  
What would a fool devoid of jñāna say? He is like a stone.  
  
ज्ञानी त्वज्ञानमेवैतन् न जानातीति किं वदेत्।  
जानन् वा ज्ञानयुक्तौsसौ कथमज्ञानवान् भवेत्।।१८२  
  
What knower say, that he does not know this ajñāna?  
Or, being possessed of ajñāna, he is jnani, how could he be ignorant?  
(Argument not clear here...)  
  
प्रकल्प्य कथनं यत् तत् पूर्वानुभवहेतुकम्।  
अज्ञानानुभवो योsयं स युक्तो नेति साधितम्।।१८३  
  
That which is stated by imagining is caused by previous experience.  
This experience of ajnana is not apt - this has been proven.  
  
विपरीतं ततो ज्ञानम् अज्ञानमिति युक्तिमत्।  
विपरीताख्यातिवादः सोsयमत्र प्रकीर्तितः।।१८४  
  
Therefore, it is reasonable that jñāna is the opposite of ajñāna.  
Here, this is declared to be the doctrine of erroneous ascription (विपरीताख्यातिवाद).

  

The above verses are from 14th chapter.
