Dear Kalyan ji,

  

Your concern is genuine. We have to explain this in this manner:  
  
In the Brihadaranyaka Upanishad is a passage:

  

तमेतंवेदानुवचनेन ब्राह्मणा विविदिषन्ति यज्ञेन दानेन तपसानाशकेन 4.4.22. This means: By means of Veda adhyayana, yajna, daana and by observing austerities and fasting one acquires relish to know the Self. For this Shankara comments:  
  
तपसा, तप इति अविशेषेण कृच्छ्रचान्द्रायणादिप्राप्तौ विशेषणम् — अनाशकेनेति ; कामानशनम् अनाशकम् , न तु भोजननिवृत्तिः ; भोजननिवृत्तौ म्रियत एव, न आत्मवेदनम्। Austerity means generally the observance of kRcchra chAndrAyaNa, etc. severe austeries. But in particular since non-eating, anAshaka, is mentioned, this means: avoiding overeating out of desire and **not completely avoiding food intake which will lead to death.** The Gita prescription you cite is to be seen in this light. ati sarvatra varjayet. extremes are to be avoided. Moderation is the norm.

  

So, in the context of nirjala ekadashi, since puranas say that and shiShTas observe that on special occasions (there are many Vaishnavas who strictly observe nirjala ekadashi on all ekadashi-s). But the thumb rule is: the individual's body conditions etc. are to be considered. There is what is yathAshakti nyAya in such prescriptions.

subbu
