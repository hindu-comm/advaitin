Thanks for your response and effort to dissuade. Here might be SSSS's reply to your doubt taken from Mandukya Rahasya Vivritt,  
  

Hence to accept the nature of the seed form as ‘Mulaavidyaa’ will be incongruous. For the present, it is enough and we will show more defects of this theory elaborately on another occasion.



**Locating the causal state in Brahman and dealing with its nature as revealed by words:**



 For the present it is important to take it that the nature of Atman is in the seed form of the universe which is conjured up by misconception i.e. Avidya and it is then called Praana and also as the cause of the universe. In the Shruti also it is said: “Praajna is the place of origin and the dissolution of all beings” (Mantra No.6), but when the Shruti wants to describe the real nature of Atman as absolute, in all the places it deliberately omits the causal nature i.e. having the seed form of the universe (which is conjured up by Avidya). The following utterances of the Shruti are in support of the above statement:

1) “He is superior to the (other) superior”, meaning He is superior to immutable (Avyaakrita) which is called superior ‘Akshara’. It is said here that having the seed form is conjured up by Avidya, which is called Akshara also and Self is ever devoid of all modifications (Mundaka 2-2).

2) “He is birthless, externally and internally” (Mundaka 2-1-2) Here it is meant that He has neither birth by any cause within Him nor by any external agent apart from Him. So He is ever birthless.

3) “Failing to reach which, words turn back along with the mind” (Taithiriya: 2-9-1). Here it is explained that words and the mind can only describe the relationship between the cause and the effect, but the Brahman is beyond the concepts of causation itself, and words and concepts (mind) are incapable of comprehending the real nature of Brahman. In this way the Shruti clearly negates causation in Brahman.

4) “Not this; not this” (Bri. 2-3-6). Here the visible, invisible and their impressions in Brahman are all negated, who is ever devoid of all mundane qualities like the cause and the effect.



In this way all Shrutis have concluded that Brahman is ever devoid of all adjuncts, by negating the seed form of the universe which is conjured up by Avidya, i.e. misconception, in Brahman. Therefore it is utterly wrong to consider that the cause and the effect are both real and that it is so taught by Shastra. In all the Upanishads first the causation is superimposed on Brahman to facilitate to know the real nature of Brahman, but after this objective is achieved, later the causation superimposed earlier is rescinded. This is called the method of rescission. Therefore the proficient traditionalists declare: “That which is devoid of all duality is described by the method of ‘Adhyaaropa-Apavaada’ i.e. by superimposition and rescission (See BG Bh: 13-13). The Shruti which has been quoted earlier by the opponent, namely, “That is infinite and this is infinite” etc. (Bri 5-1-1) does not denote that the cause and the effect (i.e. Brahman and the world) are real. On the other hand, the real meaning of this Shruti is as follows: Brahman who is supreme and ever devoid of all adjuncts, Himself is said to appear as the universe, which is manifested through names and forms, thereby giving room for a view that He appears to come within the scope of relativity (dualistic dealings). At the time of the appearance also He is ever infinite in His true nature. Hence it is said so in the Upanishads: ‘This is infinite’. ‘This’ means that the universe in its true nature as Brahman is indefinite and not in its differentiated form (in its manifested form) circumscribed by the limiting adjuncts. From the infinite Brahman the effect (universe) appears to emanate or emerge. Though Brahman appears as if He has taken the form of the universe (effect); even then in reality He does not give up his true nature because the effect is conjured up by ignorance (Avidya). Hence by the knowledge of Brahman if the apparent separateness (which is created by Avidya) has been annulled or falsified, then the infinite Brahman alone remains, like a lump of salt, as pure consciousness, homogeneous and absolute (see Bhashya about this Mantra Brih-5-1-1).



 In this way it is to be understood that the seed form of the universe is conjured up by Avidya. (the manifested universe is conjured up by Avidya and hence it is called an effect. From the standpoint of this manifested world the unmanifested seed form is also imagined as the cause and is considered as if it is related to the Self. Hence seed form is also conjured up by Avidya and the unmanifested seed form of the universe is called the cause of the manifested world. Owing to this adjunct of the seed form, Atman is called Praajna). This seed form is eradicated by knowledge of the Self and then the nature of Praajna will be cognized as ever devoid of the seed form and beyond all duality. Thus it is appropriate to say here that the nature of Praajna is unmanifested Self. Avyaakritaatma, from the standpoint of superimposed seed form of the universe, though by nature He is in this way called Praajna and cause for the universe, though by nature He is absolute. Using this reasoning Shankara states in his Bhashya: “That very entity that is called Praajna, will be spoken of separately as Turiya (fourth), who is free from causality, relation with body etc., and free from modes of waking etc.,”. And Gaudapada also says (Karika 1-10): The Lord eradicates all sorrows”. In these stanzas he confirms that the true nature of Praajna is Turiya. From the standpoint of superimposed seed form of the universe the very Turiya appears as Praajna. So there is no incongruity if Praajna is described as Avyaakritaatma or Praana.
