\### Article 131: June 10, 2025

\#### Chapter 38: The Process of the Three States – Deep Sleep (Sushupti)

We previously examined the cause of the universe as stated in the \*Brahma Sutras\*. Now, it is time to analyze the process of the three states (waking, dream, deep sleep) within it. All Upanishads unanimously declare that in deep sleep, the individual soul merges into the Supreme Self (\*Paramatman\*). No scripture teaches that ignorance (\*Avidya\*) exists in deep sleep. It is universally known that in deep sleep, one perceives nothing, hears nothing, and knows nothing. What causes this ignorance? Some modern followers of Shankaracharya claim that "affective ignorance" (\*Bhavavidya\*) envelops the soul then. But the scriptures state otherwise:

\> \*"Na tu taddvitīyamasti tato'nyadvibhaktaṁ yatpaśyet"\*

\> ("There exists nothing separate from the Self; if there were, one would perceive it").

The absence of duality (\*Dvitiya Abhava\*) is why nothing is perceived. Earlier, this was explained.

Now, let us see what the \*Brahma Sutras\* say about deep sleep. The fifth section (\*Adhikarana\*) begins by discussing the Vedic verse:

\> \*"Sadeva somyedamagra āsīt"\* (Chandogya Upanishad 6.2.1)

\> ("In the beginning, dear one, this was Existence alone").

Is this "Existence" (\*Sat\*) Brahman or primal matter (\*Pradhana\*)? The Sankhya school identifies \*Pradhana\* as \*Sat\*, from which intellect (\*Mahat\*), ego (\*Ahamkara\*), etc., emerge. Vedanta holds Brahman as \*Sat\*, from which elements (\*Bhutas\*), etc., arise. The text resolves:

\- Since "Existence deliberated" implies consciousness,

\- Scripture calls it the Self (\*Atman\*),

\- Liberation (\*Moksha\*) is granted to those established in the Self,

\- If \*Pradhana\* were meant, liberation methods would differ (but none are mentioned).



Thus, "Existence" is Brahman.



Next, \*Sutra 1.1.9\* ("\*Svāpyayāt\*") states:

\> "One is called \*Svapiti\* (sleeper) because he merges (\*Apyaya\*) into his own Existence."



The sentient soul (\*Jiva\*) cannot merge into insentient \*Pradhana\*. Hence, the \*Sat\* into which the soul merges in sleep must be Brahman. Shankaracharya’s commentary (\*Bhashya\*) clarifies:



\> \*"Sa upādhidvayoparame suṣuptāvasthāyam upādhikṛtaviśeṣābhāvāt svātmani pralīna iva"\*

\> ("When the two limiting adjuncts—gross and subtle bodies—cease, the soul in deep sleep, devoid of distinctions caused by adjuncts, merges into its own Self").



\*\*Crucial note:\*\* The \*Bhashya\* explicitly states \*no adjuncts exist in deep sleep\*. Later commentators wrongly claim ignorance (\*Avidya\*) persists as an adjunct, preventing full unity with Brahman. This contradicts the scriptures.

\### Article 132: June 11, 2025



The phrase "\*svātmani pralīna iva\*" ("merges into one’s own Self") is misinterpreted by some as "seeming merger, not actual." They argue:

\- \*Projecting power (Vikshepa Shakti)\* vanishes, so the soul \*seems\* merged;

\- \*Concealing power (Avarana Shakti)\* remains, preventing full merger.



This is incorrect. Shankaracharya unequivocally states in \*Brahma Sutra 1.1.9\*:

\> \*"Api ca na kadācit jīvasya brahmaṇā saṁpattirnāsti svārūpasyānapāyitvāt"\*

\> ("The soul’s unity with Brahman is eternal; its nature never ceases. Due to adjuncts in waking/dream, it \*appears\* distinct. In sleep, adjuncts subside, and it reverts to its true nature").



Thus, the soul does not "sometimes unite, sometimes not." \*\*There is no ignorance in deep sleep.\*\* The \*Bhashya\* further explains why no awareness exists then:



\> \*"Upādher upaśāntatvāt satyeva saṁpanno na vijānātīti yuktam"\*

\> ("Merged in true Brahman, with adjuncts destroyed, not knowing anything is logical").



\*\*Hence, there is no room for ignorance as a cause.\*\*



Now, consider \*Kaushitaki Upanishad\* (1.3–4): King Ajatashatru questions Brahmin Bālāki about a sleeping man:

\> \*"Kvaiṣa etad bālāke puruṣo'śayiṣṭa kva vā etadabhūt kuta etadāgāt?"\*

\> ("Where was this person in sleep? From where did he return?").



Bālāki cannot answer. The king explains:

\> "The person rests in the Self (\*Atman\*). From this Self, prana, deities, and worlds arise."



Shankaracharya’s commentary affirms:

\> \*"Suṣuptikāle ca pareṇa brahmaṇā jīva ekatāṁ gacchati"\*

\> ("In deep sleep, the soul attains oneness with Brahman. From Brahman, prana, etc., emerge—this is Vedanta’s doctrine").



\*\*Thus, the soul truly unites with Brahman in deep sleep.\*\*

\### Article 133: June 12, 2025



\*\*Objection:\*\* If the soul fully merges in Brahman during sleep, wouldn’t sleep itself liberate? Why does one return to samsara? A drop merging in the ocean cannot be retrieved!



\*\*Reply (Bhashya 3.2.9):\*\*

\> \*"Iha tu vidyate vivekakāraṇaṁ karma cāvidyā ceti vaiṣamyam"\*

\> ("Discrimination arises from karma and ignorance—absent in the ocean-drop analogy. Soul and Brahman are non-different").



Brahman appears as the soul due to adjuncts (\*Upadhis\*). Shankaracharya explains:

\> \*"Sadeva tūpādhisamparkāj jīva iti upacaryate"\*

\> ("Brahman is called \*Jiva\* due to association with adjuncts").



As long as the same adjunct persists (like a seed-sprout), the soul "sleeps" and "awakens." Some cite the term \*Avidya\* here, but:

\- \*Karma\* and \*Avidya\* are mentioned together;

\- \*Karma\* is absent in sleep (contradicting experience).

Thus, \*Avidya\* in this context \*\*does not imply its presence in deep sleep.\*\*

\### Article 134: June 13, 2025



\*\*Q:\*\* What is this adjunct (\*Upadhi\*) persisting in sleep?

\*\*A:\*\* The connection with intellect (\*Buddhi\*) is imagined due to false knowledge (\*Mithyajñana\*). Shankaracharya states:

\> \*"Paramārthatas tu na jīvo nāma buddhyupādhisambandhaparikalpitasvarūpavyatirekeṇāsti"\*

\> ("Ultimately, no soul exists apart from Brahman; the intellect-connection is illusory").



This connection persists \*potentially\* (\*Shaktya\*) in sleep/dissolution and re-manifests in waking/creation. Ignorance (\*Avidya\*) causes this appearance. Gaudapada’s \*Karika 1.16\* declares:

\> \*"Anādi māyayā supto yadā jīvaḥ prabudhyate \\

\> Ajamanidramasvapnam advaitaṁ budhyate tadā"\*

\> ("When the soul awakens from beginningless Maya-dream, it realizes: 'I am birthless, sleepless, dreamless, non-dual!'").



\*\*Thus, the intellect-connection belongs to beginningless Maya.\*\*



Sleep (\*Sushupti\*) and meditation (\*Samadhi\*) are identical in essence: both involve unity with Brahman. The difference lies only in the ignorant perspective.

\### Article 135: June 14, 2025



\*\*Final Synthesis:\*\*

The \*Chandogya Upanishad\* (6.8.1) states:

\> \*"Satā somya tadā saṁpanno bhavati"\*

\> ("He becomes united with Existence").



Shankaracharya explains this "union" (\*Sampatti\*):

\> \*"Buddhyupādhisthānaviśeṣayogād udbhūtasya viśeṣavijñānasyopādhyupaśame ya upaśamaḥ sa paramātmanā sambandha"\*

\> ("The cessation of special knowledge due to dormant adjuncts is \*figuratively\* called 'union with Brahman'—not literal connection").



\*\*Conclusion of Sushupti Analysis:\*\*

1\. \*\*No adjuncts\*\* (\*Upadhis\*) exist in deep sleep.

2\. The soul \*\*truly unites\*\* with Brahman.

3\. Sleep and \*Samadhi\* are \*\*essentially identical\*\*; differences exist only for the ignorant.

4\. \*\*Ignorance\*\* (\*Avidya\*) is \*\*not present\*\* in deep sleep per Shankaracharya’s original teaching.



\*\*Epilogue:\*\*

Sleep alone does not confer liberation. Animals sleep but gain no Self-knowledge. As Sri Chidanandavruta declares:

\> \*"You are already the Supreme Brahman!"\*

Liberation comes only through \*\*Self-knowledge\*\* (\*Atmajnana\*): realizing "I am the birthless, sleepless, non-dual Self beyond all states." This alone grants eternal bliss.

\*(End of Sushupti Analysis by SSSSji)\*
