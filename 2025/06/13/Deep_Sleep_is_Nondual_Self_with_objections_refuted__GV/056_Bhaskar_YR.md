| **Māyā as Upādhi** | Māyā is a **positive, beginningless, indefinable power** that **limits Brahman** and **produces** names/forms. | Māyā is **not a power**; it is **a conceptual error (bhrānti)** born of superimposition, not a real upādhi. |



praNAms Sri MCC prabhuji

Hare Krishna



mAya as per vivaraNa not different from avidyA, it is anirvachaneeya avidyA shakti that limits brahman. So here mAya can be used as synonym with avidyA and as per them brahman’s upAdhi is avidyA / mAya. Whereas as mAya as a separate thing from brahman is not accepted by Sri SSS in srushti prakriya, it is not brahmAbhinna to say that mAya is brahman’s upAdhi / shakti. It is a adhyAsa to think about mAyA as upAdhi to brahman to make the avyAkruta (unmanifest) as vyAkruta. In one of his works he observes that in shruti-s like chAdOgya, bruhadAraNyaka and other shruties teach the essential unity of the world with the non-dual Atman or brahman are appealed to. It follows that the apparent world as we are aware of is only the effect of mAyA and is ‘essentially identical with and has no ‘independent’ existence apart from brahman. The effect with qualities like sound etc. never exists ‘except’ in its essential forms as the cause whether now or before the creation. To strengthen this stand Sri SSS quotes araMbhaNAdhikaraNa bhAshya and highlights the bhAshyakAra’s conclusion : therefore it must be concluded that just as ghatAkAsha, patAkAsha and other apparent spaces are NOT other than the ONE mahAkAsha, for that appearances are of the nature of being perceived and vanishing, undefinable in their apparent nature, so also this manifold world of the experienced and experiences is NOT other than brahman in essence. And he finishes his observation with this note : the clarification of the concept of mAyA is to be found nowhere else except oin shankara’s exposition. Empirically speaking, mAyA is the causal potentiality of the world projected by avidyA or superimposition, it is but a special aspect of brahman which evolves itself into the world. But brahman in its true and real nature is above all causation.
