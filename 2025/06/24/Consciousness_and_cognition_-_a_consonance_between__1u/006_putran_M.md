Namaskaram Venkataraghavan-ji,

  

Thanks for the clear explanation of the usage of jnana here. You are right, I was bogged into the "figurative" understanding jnanam as the cognition of objects and how that reflects in the understanding of Ishvara; and not Jnanam as a fundamental reference to Brahman. But I think my questions in my reply to you were directed to asking about this fundamental usage as well.

  

Can you also explain why sometimes jnanam is said to be a pointer to Brahman whereas other times it is almost equated to Brahman? In our experience, Existence, Consciousness, Bliss seem to refer to three distinct facets of manifestation; so when they are all used to denote nirguna Brahman, we have to think of them as equivalent pointers to the Reality whose primary manifest appearance/cognition involves these three aspects. Yet in other places, they may seem to be taken as practically synonymous to Brahman or its fundamentalnature. Is such latter usage an arthavada? If not, and we say in one place "Brahman is Existence" and in another "Brahman is Consciousness" and in a third "Brahman is Ananda", that would seem to contradict the non-duality - because again, in vyavaharika, those words have different meanings.

  

If indeed they are to be known as equivalent pointers to Brahman and not literally Brahman, then we should know why such usage is justified: in what way does nirguna Brahman accommodate these pointers best? As I said to Bhaskar-ji, I can understand Existence because in my mind that is practically universally associated with all objects of cognition; but when it comes to bhaati or Consciousness, or Ananda/Priyam, these are not easily identifiable as universal and yet they are given the priority of being pointer to Brahman. Is shastra the only pramana for this, or how can tarka justify this preference for them?

  

thollmelukaalkizhu
