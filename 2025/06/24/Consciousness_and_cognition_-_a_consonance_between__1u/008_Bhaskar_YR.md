I used Ananda here which occurs in Taittiriya Up, likewise Sat in Ch Up. and Jnanam in Taittiriya Up. These are used as synonyms (?) as well as pointers.



Perhaps, Sat, Satyam and Anantam may have an equivalence "if and only if" which may justify their identification with Brahman or as Its pointer. But the others seem too caught in duality in vyavaharika that it is not obvious how to justify their equivalence to Brahman or as pointer.



Ø I think we had discussed this issue earlier as well, why the paramArtha tattva cannot be denoted by any (literally any words) words as it is something beyond the reach of speech and mind ( yato vAcho nivartante aprApya manasa saha). So that way, brahman cannot be described by any words, even satyaM, jnAnam etc. (neti neti). Hence in taittireeya bhAshya bhagavatpAda clarifies : brahman cannot be denoted by the epithet jnAnam. Nevertheless it is indicated (lakshyate) though not expressed by the word jnAnam denoting the semblance of consciousness which is really a modification of the mind. It is not directly denoted by that term jnAnam because brahman is devoid of any specific features.
Likewise ‘satyaM’ since brahman is by its very nature devoid of all specific features the satyaM really refers to the genus being inhering in external objects and when brahman is described as ‘satyaM’ it is only indicated by that term. But brahman is not actually expressed by the term ‘satyam’. IMO similarly we have to understand Sri Venkatraghavan prabhuji’s expression that jnAnaM-brahma (brahman is consciousness) aims at revealing that brahman is unlike in nature to such inert things as a pot. Since it is declared that brahman is the cause, it is liable to be taken to be an efficient factore like any ordinary thing, and a jada like clay. Hence ward of this supposition it is said brahman is consciousness (jnAnam brahma). I might be differing from Sri Venkat prabhuji here but this is how I would like to look at these terminologies.
