Namaskaram Sudhanshu-ji,

  

Thanks for the clarifications. Yes, I think I can follow for the most part although I have to make educated guesses for some of theSanskrit terms (which is fine here).

  

So every word can denote theadhishtanam Brahman or the visesha upadhi-conditioned Brahman (a thing among many things as available to cognition). Like rope and bangle both are Gold and hence one can use the word rope to refer to the Gold (lets say there is no specific word for the substratum, then all words can refer to it).

  

The question that follows is why do we prefer then to point to Brahman through these particular words if they too (like for all other words) have a visesha vyavaharika artha where they are "different" for what is denoted by other words. Is it not that the shastra is asking us to show preference to some words over others?

  

Now we may say the words in question, "sat", "chit", "ananda", satyam, jnanam, anantam are particularly different from "rope" etc., that their visesha artha in our world is peculiarly different (?) and therefore it makes sense to pick such words. Is that the argument here? It seems like this is your argument for sat and chit whereas you are giving a nuanced reasoning why it makes sense that the shastra uses ananda as well (although I wonder why one can then say equally that visesha-dukkha is pointer to brahman - again because it is only a matter of bypassing the anatma-vritti associated with any word? In that case why is only Ananda used as pointer?).

  

With Sat vs Chit. I can see that all objects we perceive are endowed with existence ("Sat") whereas all objects are illumined in our consciousness - but they themselves can be jada according to our buddhi. Right? Is this not a key difference.There is an i vs not-i differentiation (duality is real) when it comes to chit whereas i and not-i are linked to sat by virtue of their existence. We don't say i exist more or better than the rock that i see. Existence is common feature, hence it makes sense that it is a pointer to the adhishtanam. Whereas with chit, i am endowed with chit and that rock that is illumined by my chit is not endowed with chit, only with sat. That is the natural vyavaharika viewpoint.
