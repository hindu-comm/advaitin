I think I follow what you are getting at, except in theinterpretation of "meaning".

  

A word is a direct denotation of its meaning, no? The meaning for a word "Ring" is not just the gold but the gold when associated with a specific nama-rupa. That is an "entity" different from bangle because the rupa is different.

  

However, both rope and bangle can be used as indirect pointers to the same Gold, by bypassing the visesha upadhis that are part of the definitions for the words and highlighting the substratum/substance that is *also* included in the meaning.

  

Perhapswe don't think in terms of words (nama) as shabda alone but rather in terms of "nama-rupa" as a joint referent. The shabda/nama "Rope" has the rupa of Rope as part of its direct meaning; whereas the nama-rupa Rope can always refer to the Entity "Gold" that appears as Rope nama-rupa. The nama-rupas Rope and Bangle are two particular references to the same Entity Gold.

  

Likewise, the word/nama "jnanam" has a vyavaharika rupa/meaning, but it is the Entity Brahman that is being known through that nama-rupa and hence the word Jnanam can be considered synonymously to Brahman.

  

Next question will become: why then prefer certain nama-rupas if you say all nama-rupas are references/denotations/appearances of the same Entity? If you say "Jnanam Brahma", I can say "Ajnanam Brahma". If you say "Prajnanam Brahma", I can say "vyavaharam Brahma". Probably that is true by above argument, and yet shastra does not do this: it highlightscertain namas only in association with Brahman. Is it only for teaching purpose or is the 'rupa' of jnana somehow a direct connotation of Brahman?

> This brings us to your second question. Is the meaning of the words
> "satyam", "jnAnam" and "anantam" in the context of "satyam jnAnam
> anantam brahma" a facet of Brahman or is it Brahman itself? The answer
> Vedanta gives is that the meaning of satyam is jnAnam, is anantam, is

Yes, this is quite deep. So it is an "if and only if". If the word "meaning" itself has a technical definition, you can elaborate; or how you are using it.

  

Satyam is Truth, which as a noun can refer to anything that does not change or become falsified. If it is not falsifiable, then it has nothing real apart from it, hence is anantam and non-dual.

  

Now, is there some Thingthat is satyam-anantam? Is it Existence or Non-Existence? Nastika schools say there is no such thing and that fact of sunyata is the satyam-anantam. I think we require shastra here to clear the air and confirm our experience of existence in all things as derived from the Existence of the adhishtanam. Hence the Satyam is anantam is Sat.

  

I am able to get this far, but the Jnanam reference is a bit more confusing.



>   
>
> You say that this appears to contradict non-duality - I will attempt
> to articulate why I don't think it to be so.

  

More so, with regard to sat vs chit/jnanam vs Anandam. As I wrote above, satyam=anantam=Sat (using shastra for the last =) is easier to follow.

  

We have to understand Existence as nothing but pure Consciousness (that Sat and Chit are synonymous and not that one is the nature of the other), and the (jada) things that appear to exist in Consciousness is only due to a *vivarta* limiting of the anantam Consciousness. Of course, this brings in maya/avidya as the cause that is itself anirvachaneeya (hence not contradicting anantam).

  

But, I think perhaps shastra has to be pramana to equate Sat and Chit in the highest standpoint, and hence assure that maya/duality of perception is anirvachaneeya.

Ok. I understand this as: they are different cognitions of the same Adhishtanam, like seeing the same chair from different angles. Except the difference as seen is due to adhyasa and not because the adhishtanam itself possesses such differentiations. So, in this analogy, we experience fire as heat and light due to our ajnana-caused upadhis limiting Consciousness (the Entity 'fire') in these two manners.



> 
>
> Coming to satyam, jnAnam, anantam - this is something similar. Vedanta
> postulates that the only source of satyam (existence), jnAnam
> (consciousness), anantam (infiniteness) is Brahman. There is nothing
> else which is existent, or conscious or infinite.

  

Ok.

  

> So what differentiates satyam, from jnAnam from anantam? It is merely
> our experience of each, which is different. We experience the
> existence of a pot differently from the cognition of a flower, so we
> treat knowledge as something different to existence. But like in the
> case of fire and heat, the difference lies not in existence and
> consciousness, rather the difference lies in our medium of experience.
> We "see" the existence of a pot, and so we understand it to be a
> "thing". We know that we know, and so we think of consciousness as
> different to existence. We "feel" the happiness differently from how
> we "see" existence and think of the two to be different. We marvel at
> the vastness of the universe and guess its infinitude. In each case,
> existence, consciousness, bliss, infinitude are experienced to various
> degrees, differently. Thus we conclude that existence is different to
> consciousness, which is different to bliss, which is different to
> infinitude. We think of these as different because we experience them
> differently.

  

When we have cognition of a thing, we automatically affirm existence as its attribute. And we know that the cognizer also exists as part of this dynamic. So existence is common realization allaround inevery experience,though not necessarily with regard to the particular object of cognition (which can "cease to exist"). But consciousness is attributed by us to the cognizer and not always to the cognized. It is not anantam in our experience- there is a part of our world that we deduce has no consciousness.

  

But Vedanta says: this perceived existence and consciousness are two different knowledge of Sat=Chaitanya based on our different cognitions/experience. It is the same Brahman that is known in two different manners, due to our upadhis limiting the anantam Consciousness.

  

So, like we have heat and light which are two name-forms (in analogy) for the same Entity Fire, so also sat and chit are two name-forms for Sat=Chaitanya. It is pure Chaitanya that is the pure Existence in which the duality of sat vs chit appears (or is known as such). The adhishtanam for the objects and the seer of the objects is that ever-seeinganantam Consciousness alone, that'sees' them, hence that Chaitanya is the Existence realized in them (only it is imagined to be a jada-existence of a different nature).

> jnAnam anantam brahma" or "Anandam brahmeti vyajAnAt" we think of

So at the outer level, sat and chaitanyam are different indirect pointers to Brahman but at the inner true level, when we unpack their meaning with the knowledge from shastra, Sat is Satyam is Jnanam/Chit is anantam are equivalent "if and only if" direct denotations of Brahman. Therefore it is proper to say Satyam Brahma, Jnanam Brahma.
