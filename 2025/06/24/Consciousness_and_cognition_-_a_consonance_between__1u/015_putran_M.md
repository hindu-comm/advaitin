Namaskaram Venkataraghavan-ji,

  

I was writing how I was understanding and assimilating from what you had written. Some places it may seem like I was just independently giving my thoughts, but mostly it was trying to sort out the ideas. If you have any specific comments that can be useful, then please do let me know.

  

You are right, I took "denotation" as "denoter". I think I was primarily uncertain with the (bold) statement

  

"all words are indirect pointers, they cannot be direct denotations, **whereas the meaning of the words*is*the entity itself.** Therefore, when sometimes people refer to jnAnam as a pointer to Brahman, I believe they may be referring to the word "jnAnam". If they refer to the entity itself, then the entity jnAnam is not a pointer to Brahman. It is Brahman."

  

So, if jnanam has a literal meaning of Brahman+specific-upadhi, but if the upadhi association is due to avidya, then the Entity Jnanam being referred to by the word jnanam is Brahman only. Someone who knows this truth about the only Entity can certainly use the word in this manner. This is understandable. But this is not the same as saying the meaning of the word jnanam is the Entity itself. The meaning is the entity seen in conjunction with some upadhi: the word belongs within the realm of avidya. But if we use it in the generic sense to refer to the Entity, then we have to bypass the upadhi part of its denotation. That's why I was asking how you were using the word "meaning".

  

However, this usage applies for all words because though they all have some "Entity" meaning within the realm of avidya, the only Entity that is their Self is Brahman. So I don't think jnanam is special in this sense.

  

Later in your explanations, I think you made further points (or at least I was understanding this way) that the Jnanam or Consciousness is the Existence. So sat and chit are literally equivalent denotations equivalent to Brahman if understood properly. That is there is some intuitive way of realizing the merging of the meanings of these words in and as Brahman. It need not be logically but the shastra says it is the reality; and therefore, we can try and merge our logic and intuition with that knowledge. That is why they are not merely any pointers to Brahman but can be understood as specially denoting Brahman.

  

For the ring and gold, again the literal meaning of (Gold) ring is the Gold+specific ring-rupa. In the realm of avidya, this matters (rupa is real here, so we cannot bypassit to refer only to substance; the meaning/entity of word ring includes the form in question). But suppose the ring-rupaassociation is due to avidya and Gold alone is the entity being imagined as ring, then we can say that by Ring, we only refer to the gold and the knower can use the word Ring in this manner because he is not identifying with the avidya-based rupa. I think this is how you mean as well.
