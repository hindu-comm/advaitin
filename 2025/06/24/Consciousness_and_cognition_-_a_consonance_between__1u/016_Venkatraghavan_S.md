The first paragraph above is mine. The second is your response.

  

When I use the word "meaning", I use it in the sense of the Sanskrit "artha" - the entity denoted by the word.

  

I'm not saying the literal meaning (direct meaning / vAcyArtha) of the word jnAnam is shuddha Brahman (ie Brahman without upAdhi). I am saying the indirect meaning (lakshyArtha) of the word jnAnam is consciousness, which is Brahman itself.

  

So when I say

"If they refer to the entity itself, then the entity jnAnam is not a pointer to Brahman. It is Brahman.",

  

I am of course referring to the entity implied / pointed to by the word jnAnam, through lakshaNAvRtti, which is consciousness, which is Brahman itself.

  

  

> But if we use it in the generic sense to refer to the Entity, then we
> have to bypass the upadhi part of its denotation. That's why I was
> asking how you were using the word "meaning".
>
>   
>
> However, this usage applies for all words because though they all have
> some "Entity" meaning within the realm of avidya, the only Entity that
> is their Self is Brahman. So I don't think jnanam is special in this
> sense.

  

True, it is not special in that sense, because the essence of all things (ie all word meanings) is ultimately Brahman, and in that sense all word meanings have Brahman as their implied meaning. However, for there to be lakshaNA, verbal implication, there has to be some ostensible connection between the vAcyArtha and lakshyArtha, understandable by both the speaker and the listener, which enables the speaker to use the one word jnAnam and through it, refer to Brahman.

  

The word jnAnam is special because its *direct meaning* is "thought", and the awareness of thought is directly experienced by all thinking beings. That awareness/ consciousness is that by which ultimately all this is known (tasya bhAsa sarvamidam vibhAti). There is an ostensible link between thought and consciousness, which is not apparent between other word meanings and Brahman (ie there is no ostensible link between the word meaning of the word "pot" and consciousness, which happens to be commonly known), so the word jnAnam is special in that sense.
