Namaskaram Venkataraghavan-ji,

  

Thanks for the further clarifications. They make sense.

  

Only point I want to mention: For my mind, I am able to relate Sat or existence better as Brahman because it comes across as both satyam and anantam because I can associate existence immediately and present in all objects thatarise in "my" consciousness. For consciousness, I have to go through shastra in a more explicit manner since otherwise thought and consciousness both can appear limited in our experience or ascertainment of the cognized world. There is a place for jada in it and the reduction of the underlying consciousness to that of the seer apart from the seen. Whereas seen or seer, the notion of existence is pervasive.

  

Again this is the way my mind works. I am only saying that while Consciousness and thought is appealing to my intuition as well, it also appears contradicted in experience, and hence the shastra needs to be used at the start to know it as fundamental truth of Brahman (and then as you said that the true meaning of Sat and Chit are not different.)
