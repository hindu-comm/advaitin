Advaitins are often portrayed as “egoistic” due to misunderstandings of the tenets of Kevalādvaita. Many have even portrayed our ācāryas negatively. However, throughout the centuries, Advaita ācāryas have remained non-sectarian, regardless of others’ remarks.  
I would like to present a few excerpts from Ūthukkāḍu Veṅkaṭa Kavi’s and Tyāgarāja’s works.

*(For context: both were Smārtas; the former was also a Śrīvidyā upāsaka.)*

***Veṅkaṭa Kavi:***

The following excerpts are from his composition **Bhajanāmṛta Parānanda**:  
  
**bhāgavata santa caraṇa reṇum nirantaram vahāmyaham hari nija  ** *I ever carry on my head the dust of the feet of the holy Bhāgavata saints — dear to Hari.*

Later verse:**  **

**bhāgavata kathā paṭhana śravaṇa sadā smaraṇa bhajana guṇa kīrtana  
yathā kathita kṛṣṇa carita varṇanam tathānuguṇa prakaṭana vitaraṇa hari**

Reading the *Bhāgavata*, listening to it, constant remembrance, singing devotional songs, praising divine virtues;  
narrating and describing the life stories of Kṛṣṇa as spoken of in the scriptures, and in the same manner, revealing and distributing these sacred stories — O Hari!  
  
**nandeśa mataṅga vyāghrapāda sahasra phaṇa makuṭadhara tumburu  
caṇḍīśa mṛkaṇḍu suta munigaṇa santata nuta rajata śaila sthita śiva**

Nandeśa, Mataṅga, Vyāghrapāda, the thousand-hooded crowned serpent (likely referring to Patañjali), and Tumburu;  
Caṇḍīśa, Mṛkaṇḍu’s son (Markaṇḍeya), and the assembly of sages — who constantly offer praise to Śiva, who dwells on the Silver Mountain (*Rajataśaila*), i.e., Mount Kailāsa.  
  
**śiva hari śaravaṇabhava guha bhajana nirantara mālālaṅkṛta śobha  
vāgīśa śiva pāda hṛdaya suta maṇivācaka sundara ḍiṇḍima kavi-  
rāja madhurakavirāja rāmānuja kulaśekhara viṣṇucitta para-  
kāla purandara tulasīdāsa caraṇāravinda dhūli hari śiva guha**

Adorned with a garland of unbroken devotion to Śiva, Hari, and Śaravaṇabhava (Skanda); the hearts of the masters of speech (poets) are decorated by devotion to the feet of Śiva; beautiful are the jewel-like words of Maṇivācaka, Sundara, Ḍiṇḍima, and the poet-kings Madhurakavi, Rāmānuja, Kulaśekhara, Viṣṇucitta (Periyāḻvār), Parakāla, Purandara, and Tulasi Dāsa — who all remain as dust at the lotus feet of Hari, Śiva, and Guha respectively.

Following is the composition called **Alavadennalo**, where he praises the Śaiva Nāyaṉmārs of Tamil Nadu:  
  
“O Śiva! When do I obtain salvation by becoming one of your devotees’ devotees?  
You are kind enough to grant boons even to those who do not beg for them; you are the Supreme One. (Let me find) your lotus-like feet! I pray that I may lose this false, lowly life and obtain birth as one of the following saints, who are unequalled in their devotion to you: Śīrkāḻi manaṉ Śivapada magaṉ (Tirugñāna Sambandar), Tirunāvukkarasar, Maṇikkavācakar, Sundarar... Koṭceṅkaṇcōḻar, Kāḻaṟciṅkar, Karaikkāl Ammaiyār, Kaṇṇappar, Tirukkuripputtoṇḍar.”

***Tyāgarāja:***

In his composition **Endarō Mahānubhāvulu**, he praises countless saints — including those who worship the *Ṣaṇmata* deities:  
  
**bhāgavata rāmāyaṇa gīta (ādi)  
śruti śāstra purāṇapu  
marmamulanu śiv(ādi) ṣaṇ-matamula  
gūḍhamulanu muppadi mukkōṭi  
surāntarangamula bhāvambulanu  
erigi bhāva rāga layādi saukhyamu cē  
cirāyuvul kalgi niravadhi sukhātmulai  
tyāgarājāptulai na vārendarō  
  ** *There are countless great personages who — understanding the secrets of Śrīmad-Bhāgavataṁ, Rāmāyaṇa, Śrīmad-Bhagavad-Gītā, Vedas, sciences, epics, etc.; the secrets of the six methods (ṣaṇ-matamula) of formal worship like Śaiva and others; the inner states of the thirty-three crores of celestials; attaining very long life and being uninterrupted experiencers of bliss along with the joy of bhāva, rāga, and laya — have become the benefactors of this Tyāgarāja. I offer my salutations to all of them.*

In his composition **Giri­rāja Suta**, he praises Gaṇeśa as “the moon shining in the Śaiva Āgamas”:

> **gaṇa nātha parātpara śaṅkara-āgama  
> vāri nidhi rajanī-kara  
> phaṇi rāja kaṅkaṇa vighna  
> nivāraṇa śāmbhava śrī tyāgarāja nuta (giri)**

> *O Chief of the attendants (gaṇa) of Lord Śiva! O Supreme Lord –
> beyond everything (parāt-para)!  
> O Moon (rajanī-kara) shining in the ocean (vāri nidhi) of Śiva Āgamas
> (Śaṅkarāgama)!  
> O Lord who wears Śeṣa – king (rāja) of snakes (phaṇi) – as a bracelet
> (kaṅkaṇa)!  
> O Remover (nivāraṇa) of obstacles (vighna)! O Son of Lord Śiva
> (Śāmbhava)!  
> O Glorious (śrī) Lord praised (nuta) by this Tyāgarāja!  
> O Lord Vināyaka – Son of Pārvatī, daughter of Himavān – Lord of
> mountains!  
> O Merciful Lord!*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

In his *Prahalāda Bhakti Vijayam*, he directly addresses and praises Purandara Dāsa (Madhva Vaiṣṇava), Bhakta Rāmadāsu (Śrī Vaiṣṇava), Nārāyaṇa Tīrtha (Kevalādvaitin), Jñāneśvara (Nātha Śaiva), Tukārām, and many more.
