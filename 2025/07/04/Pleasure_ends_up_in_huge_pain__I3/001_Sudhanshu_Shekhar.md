Namaste Subbu ji.

  

Our default mode of working is to pursue pleasure and fear pain. We want to remain in perennial happiness and just don't want to have pain ever in our life.

  

To such a default mode of working, the above mentioned shloka does open a new facet. What you think of happiness, is actually generated from anAtmA and is hence bound to result in sorrow. Bhagvadgita also says the same -- whatever are the bhOga born out of contact, they are verily the seed of sorrow.\[ये हि संस्पर्शजा भोगा दुःखयोनय एव ते। आद्यन्तवन्तः कौन्तेय न तेषु रमते बुधः।।5.22।।\]

  

However, I would like to submit that this approach retains our abhorrence for pain. Keeping our abhorrence for pain intact, these shlOkAs seek to dissuade us from anAtmA-born-happiness.

  

However, on enquiry, when we appreciate the seen to be illusory, the distinction between this anAtmA-born-happiness and anAtmA-born-pain vanishes. So what if there is appearance of anAtmA-born-happiness! So what if it (seems to) results in hundred times pain!! Why to invite one, and why to abhor the other!

  

The approach changes and one sees the momentary appearance of both pleasure and pain. Knowing that they are mere appearances, without any cause. That appearances are just appearance. No need to link one with the other.

  

And, hence both pleasure and pain are ignored.

  

Regards.

Sudhanshu Shekhar.
