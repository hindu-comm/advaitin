Hare Krishna Bhaskar prabhu ji.

> Ø But this Upanishad also within your bhrAnti is it not??

  

Yes. They are within bhrAnti. But the object propounded by them is not bhrAnti. That is reality.

  

>  How does it matter for the bhrAntivAdi what one thing is acceptable
> and another is not acceptable within the SAME bhrAnti??

  

The assertion "tat tvam asi" is acceptable no matter it appears to be spoken by dream-charachter or movie-character or which character. The statement "tat tvam asi" is equally a bhrAnti. However, the object of this statement is not a bhrAnti. That is my own Self.

  

So, the mithyA statement "tat tvam asi" is acceptable to me, whereas another mithyA statement "atat tvam asi" is not acceptable to me, because the respective objects differ. The object of former-mithyA-statement is truth, whereas object of latter-mithyA-statement is illusory.

  

This is succinctly stated in Advaita Siddhi as -शास्त्रस्य प्रमामूलकत्वाभावप्रसङ्गादिति-चेन्न; प्रमामूलकत्वाभावेऽप्यबाधितविषयत्वेन शास्त्रप्रामाण्योपपत्तेरन्त्यपक्षाभ्युपगमात् ।

  

> I remember you said veda, acharya, Ishwara, tattva etc. are just mere
> fancy stories without any substantiality in it.

  

The Chaitanya is real. UpAdhi is illusory. When Ishwara is defined as upahit-chaitanya, it is real.



> Ø You tell me one genuine reason why I should not say your above
> statement itself is another bhrAnti without carrying any substantial
> stuff in it??

  

There may be pramA-mUlakatva-abhAva in a statement owing to it being illusory, but the object of the illusory statement is abAdhita-vishaya. Hence, the statement can be relied upon.

As I understand advaita Bhaskar ji, it is not about logical discussions. It is about truth. The truth is my own Self. Everything else is illusory. So, one can either see it. Or not see it.

  

A movie is playing. I may see it, I may not see it. Analysis of seen is not required. Things are happening. And one sees it. Or one does not see it. There ends the matter.
