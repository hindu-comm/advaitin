Namaste Akilesh ji.

  

> If one *really*believes everything is bhranti, the mind stops, and no
> further questions arise.

  

I have a different take on it. The firmness of conviction regarding seen to be illusion connotes irrelevance for the seen and not necessarily a change in the seen.

  

Mind is seen. Understanding that mind is illusory connotes irrelevance for the happenings of mind.

  

So, whether mind is calm, whether mind stops, or whether mind is super active -- becomes irrelevant.

  

So, I would not gauge the firmness of my understanding by checking whether mind is stopping or not.

  

In fact I would not gauge whether the happenings of mind are bothering me or not.

  

I am not concerned with seen. I am not concerned even with "I have been concerned with the happenings of mind".

  

Understanding illusoriness of seen implies moment-by-monent living. Past and future are irrelevant illusions.

  

There is no comparison with past to find out whether I was concerned with seen.

  

Here is a moment, here is a seen, and here I pay no attention. This is the end.

  

Regards.

Sudhanshu Shekhar.
