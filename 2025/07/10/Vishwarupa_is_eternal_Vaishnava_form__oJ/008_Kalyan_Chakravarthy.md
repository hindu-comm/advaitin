śāśvata  
(H1)\[Printed book page[1068](https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/csl-apidev/servepdf.php?dict=MW&page=1068),3\]  
*śāśvata*mf(*ī*)n.(fr.*śaśvat*) eternal, constant, perpetual, all (*śāśvatībhyaḥ samābhyaḥ*,*śāśvatīḥ samāḥ*, or*śāśvatam*, for evermore, incessantly, eternally),VS.&c. &c.\[ID=216268\]

  

The Vishwarupa form is eternal because its conception exists at all times.

  

There is no comparison with the usage as mentioned in the Bhagavad Gita verse, because the fruits of actions are finite. Whereas, the VishwarUpa is not finite in vyavahAra. At the time of laya, only prakRti becomes avyakta. Bhagavan does not become avyakta. It is stated several times in Mahabharata, that after pralaya, Bhagavan Vishnu lies resting on a serpent, so this too shows that Bhagavan exists during pralaya.
