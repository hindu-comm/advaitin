I wrote something similar on Ishvara, in my "This exists in Brahman" file:

  

"when we look at Brahman, Nirguna Brahman, but we want to associate it with the duality, to say the duality exists in Brahman, in order to explain our seeing it, then we are essentially looking at Brahman as Saguna, as Saguna Brahman or Ishwara. Ishwara is Sat plus Chit, where Chit now denotes the Maya-Shakti of Brahman to bring about this creation. Maya is the cognitive jnana-iccha-kriya shakti of Brahman: The jnana of all possible creation, the Iccha to desire and determine, and the kriya that manifests the Iccha as the particular cognition of that knowledge. So, that is Ishwara for us. Duality exists in Him as His knowledge, is cognized by Him as this Creation.

  

.. \[In\] the Ishvara standpoint, Ishvara is the only Reality but He appears, cognizes, imagines the world of the jiva-standpoint, where He identifies the Self in jivas and jagat and also as their ishvara. The “this” and “that” of jiva-standpoint is nothing but the Chit/Maya of Ishvara. And finally when the imagination is transcended and we align to the Brahman standpoint,the sat and chit duality that defined Ishvara is realized as naught else but the non-dual Sat=Chit=Ananda. The adhishtanam is the Existence non-different from Consciousness.

  

...

  

"If Creation is Ishvara’s manifest cognition of His eternal Knowledge, and this Knowledge is not of His intrinsic reality but of His potential imagination, then all our cognitions, thoughts, experiences are only our present seeing of Him through this mode of imagination. Nothing we experience is new because it is only a cognition that is already His Knowledge. Thus every cognition is a particular projection, Chit-svarupa of Ishvara, an imagination of Sat - the Self - that arises in Ishvara’s Chit. Ishvara is the self of all our cognitions and we see Him everywhere and in everything. The duality of “this and that” is fully sublated in the duality of Sat plus chit. This duality too is surrendered in the final shift of attention to the non-dual Adhishtanam."
