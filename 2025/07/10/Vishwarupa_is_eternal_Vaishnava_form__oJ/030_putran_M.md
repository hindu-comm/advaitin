Namaskaram,

  

I indicated my understanding on this nitya vs anitya question in my Ishvara self-quotations earlier.

  

Whether Brahman has vishwarupa has to be discerned in the right context, again with the view of being consistent or best-pointing to advaita.

  

If we takethe advaitic position that all dualistic namarupa based identifications of Self are adhyasa on Brahman, then such identifications are in the category of imagination. That is, unlike in Visishtadvaita, for an advaitin, this duality or "vishwarupa" is not a real or intrinsicpart of Brahman. It is adhyasa.

  

If you see "snake", then it is not wrong to say "the forked tongue" is real and a part of the snake. But shastra says "There is only Rope". Some will then say "There is onlyrope but it has eternal snake-rupa". This 'eternal' is not untrue within the "i see snake" standpoint. But it is superfluous for someone who realizes that the snake-rupa is in no way a real part of the rope, to begin with: it is adhyasa. If you realize theRope properly, the question of "snake" and its eternality will become moot. That is the thrust of our emphasis.

  

But so long as you see snake and want an explanation, the explanation will amount to Ishvara (Brahman in conjunction with upadhi Maya). But how an advaitin considers Ishvara, Maya and the apparent "vishvarupa" will markedly differ from the other sampradayas, because he knows there is apavada coming up.

  

I have no issues in saying the jnana of Creation is eternal in Ishvara but His manifest-cognition of that knowledge is not so. But even if and when there is creation, it is not real part of Him but in the category of imagination.
