Namaskaram Suresh ji,

  

This is no intrusion at all. So please feel free.

  

\<\<Is it correct to understand that Atma-anAtma distinction is also mithya and needs to be eliminated as Brahman is everywhere. \>\>

  

The belief that there is anatman distinct from atman is mithya and needs to be eliminated. In this sense alone it is accepted that the atman-anatman distinction is mithya.

It is atman alone that even appears as the atman-anatman distinction in vyavahara. Atman cannot be eliminated and no other entity distinct from atman is satya. Even the term 'everywhere' is a misnomer. An entity that transcends space-time-object distinction, it is actually meaningless to label it as 'everywhere' or 'eternal'.

  

\<\<I guess it makes sense as absolute selflessness leads to that only. \>\>

  

Not sure I understand your intent correctly. 'Absolute selflessness', in my understanding, only denotes Buddhist Shunyata. Although in my opinion certain deeper aspects of Mahayana Buddhism is just a restatement of hardcore Advaita, it is not accepted in Advaita that an 'absolute selflessness' can ever lead to Brahman. Self is Brahman; consequently it is an oxymoron to say selflessness leads to Brahman.

  

\<\< Could it be said that anAtma/mithya jagat gets dissolved in the Atman/Brahman in paramArtha? \>\>

  

No, Advaita does not believe in any actual merging / dissolution of anything else with Atman/Brahman in paramartha. There is never ever a point, other than the figure of speech, when a mistaken snake actually ever dissolves into the rope.

In the phrase 'anatma/mithya jagat', the perception that the anatma/jagat is mithya is not paramarthika. If I may repeat, jagat-mithyatva is not paramarthika. This is merelythe drishti of a jnani in vyavahara. The drishti of an ajnani in vyavahara is jagat-satyatva. In paramarthika jagat is only categorized as atyanta asat.

  

\<\<Until that realization, the distinctions continue to exist? \>\>

  

No, the distinctions continue to exist all through vyavaharika. Even a jivanmukta jnani, who has had the full realization, continues to perceive the distinctions around in the jagat. Realization doesn't necessarily erase the jagat for a jivanmukta. It merely renders the perceived jagat as mithya, which was erstwhile falsely considered satya. The non-existence of jagat is only from atman/Brahman/paramarthika perspective, and from which perspective there is never any distinction ever to begin with.

  

Hope this provides some clarification.
