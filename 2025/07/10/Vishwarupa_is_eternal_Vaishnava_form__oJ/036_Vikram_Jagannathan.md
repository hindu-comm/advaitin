Namaskaram Kalyan ji,

  

\<\<

I had written this in an earlier message, which was probably missed.

"In paramArtha, all forms are due to mAyA, and this includes the VishwarUpa."

\>\>

  

Your statement is an incorrect position of Advaita siddhanta. Shri Sudhanshu ji has just corrected and clarified this point in more detail. I too had presented the correction couple of days back. This correction is critical from Advaita understanding perspective.

  

Restating my earlier message: "A quick note - in paramartha there is no maya and its effects. Maya and its effects are in vyavahara alone. A jnani with vyavahara drishti recognizes the play of maya in and through every thing including Bhagavan's vishwarupa, whereas an ajnani fails to recognize the play of the all-powerful maya."

  

\<\< Yes, that's what I meant. There is only nirvisesha brahman in paramArtha. \>\>

  

However, in your latest response, you mentioned "There is only nirvisesha brahman in paramArtha." This is in agreement with Advaita; however this stands in contrast to your earlier statement "In paramArtha, all forms are due to mAyA" , wherein maya is accepted in paramarthika, rendering Brahman as savisesha and not nirvisesha.

  

Regarding the 'eternal' nature of Bhagavan's vishwarupa in vyavaharika, I shared my academic opinionearlier, but it is of no consequence to me whether Bhagavan's vishwarupa is actually eternal in vyavaharika or not. Vyavaharika itself is *not* shashwata / eternal, and from within the vyavaharika perspective Bhagavan is all powerful and I am a lowly individual to have any consequential opinion about His powers. Relative or not, to me Bhagavan is eternal.

  

With this, I will end my participation on the topic of 'eternal nature of Bhagavan's vishwarupa in vyavahara'. However, happy to continue on deeper Advaita siddhanta as appropriate.

  

prostrations,

Vikram
