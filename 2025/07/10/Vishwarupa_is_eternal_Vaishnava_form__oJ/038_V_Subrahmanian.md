Kalyan: the chapter no.187 in the link you gave, is 189 in Nilakantha'scommentary: He says at the beginning of the ch.188 that In Nirguna Brahman itself the Saguna consisting of bhaava and abhaava has been stated in the earlier187the ch. With a view to elucidatethis idea, thatis, in order to **establish Advaita (advaita siddhyarthe**. The earlier ch. acc. to Nilakantha is for Jivanmukti statement), the next two chapters are there: the chapter you mentioned is the second one in this line. This conveys the message that 'Krishna form alone is the Self of all and the Doer of all.' At the beginning of 190 he says: thus Krishnādvaitam has been stated (in 188 and 189 together). Thus, in the commentator's view the chapters on the pralaya vision of Markandeya is to show that Nirguna Brahman is the one in which the saguna (creation, etc.) is superimposed like silver in a shell. Markandeya says: I attained a state of swoon, muurchaa.

  

Since as per the Upanishads and the Gita there can't be anything that can be experienced as this or that since all the particulars have become one indistinguishable whole in pralaya, this darshana of Markandeya is arthavada, stuti, for the Advaita siddhi vidhi. In mimamsa such stuti statements do not have any purport in themselves. There's no requirement for them to have any sensible meaning. The Markandeya darshanam, defying logic and shruti will fall into the category of arthavada and getting mukhyartha. For example in the Mundaka Upanishad there's a statement: There will be no ajnani, abrahmavit, in the family lineage of the Jnani. This can't be literally true. Hence it is treated as stuti of Brahmavidya phalam.
