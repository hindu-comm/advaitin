By saying 'there is no exception', what I meatn is 'there can't be an exception in real terms of a jiva not going into pralaya but continuing with gross body in such a case like the pralaya'. That is the way Vedanta, Advaita Vedanta, sees such things. If there is such a story, well, as I have said already, it is not pramaNa by/in itself; it is conveying soemthing else, 'anyArtha', as Shankara says here:  
  
In the Mandukya, as I stated before, there is the statement: the creation shrutis (with various analogies like clay, gold, etc.) are not to say that a real creation happened, but to drive home the message of the jiva brahma aikya (and hence there is no differentiation due to creation). In this bhashya Shankara says:  
  
मृल्लोहविस्फुलिङ्गाद्यैः सृष्टिर्या चोदितान्यथा।  
उपायः सोऽवताराय नास्ति भेदः कथञ्चन ॥ १५ ॥ 3.15

  

//मृल्लोहविस्फुलिङ्गादिदृष्टान्तोपन्यासैः सृष्टिः या च उदिता प्रकाशिता कल्पिता अन्यथान्यथा च, स सर्वः सृष्टिप्रकारो जीवपरमात्मैकत्वबुद्ध्यवतारायोपायोऽस्माकम् , यथाप्राणसंवादेवागाद्यासुरपाप्मवेधाद्याख्यायिका कल्पिता प्राणवैशिष्ट्यबोधावताराय ;तस्मादुत्पत्त्यादिश्रुतय आत्मैकत्वबुद्ध्यवतारायैव, नान्यार्थाः कल्पयितुं युक्ताः। अतो नास्त्युत्पत्त्यादिकृतो भेदः कथञ्चन ॥//

  

For this concept - the shruti stating something only to convey something else - Shankara gives the analogy of the Upanishads (Prashna, Brihadaranyaka, etc.) presenting a story where there is a conversation between the Prana and the entire sense/motor organs lot, to determine who among them is the greatest. Ultimately it results in all of them agreeing that Prana alone is the greatest. Shankara says this story/conversation is **devised, kalpita,** as a medium to convey the high status of Prana and hence the conversation itself is not their purport. Similarly the creation shrutis are **devised, kalpita,** to convey the jiva-brahma aikyam and non-difference.

  

Naturally, the pralaya related stories are too, kalpita, to convey something else and not to prove that the stories themselves are real.

  

Anandagiri on the above comnversation in the Mandukya bhashyam:

  

प्राणसंवादश्रुतीनांमिथो विरोधान्नास्ति स्वार्थे प्रामाण्यमित्युपसंहरति –

तस्मादिति।

उक्तदृष्टान्तानुरोधादुत्पत्तिवाक्यान्यपि न विवक्षितार्थानि।

क्वचिदाकाशादिक्रमेण सृष्टिः क्वचिदग्न्यादिक्रमेण क्वचित्प्राणादिक्रमेण क्वचिदक्रमेणेत्येवं परस्परपराहतिदर्शनादित्याह –

तथेति।

  

This ना

स्ति स्वार्थे प्रामाण्यम् is a mimamsa concept related to arthavada sentences: they are not pramana by themselves literally but are having some other purport.

  

Shankara has said there: We can't take the Prana-organs conversation at face value since the conversation is different in different Upanishads. It's not the same everywhere. Anandagiri explains: Similarly the creation shruti is different in diff Upanishads and this is another reason not to take them to be real: Somewhere it is in the sequence of Akasha, vayu, etc. Somewhere it is stated to be Agni, etc. sequence. Yet somwhere it is Prana, etc. sequence, somewhere no sequence at all - this way there is mutual contradiction in the creation shrutis.

  

An implication from that, to the Markandeya story would be: the laya also will have to be so widely different across the upanishads. This is because, the involution, laya, is admitted to be of the opposite/reverse sequence of the evolution, srishti. So, even if not explicitly stated, the various Upanishads will have to be admitted to be saying the laya sequence is also so different across the shrutis.

  

I recalled, while reading about Markandeya entering Bhagavan's stomach and seeing so many beings there: **This is exactly the case in Dvaita. Even in mukti (let alone pralaya) the mukta jivas, as per their yogyata, gradation, will rest in various body parts of Bhagavan, some in his stomach too.**

  

It can be easily seen that such ideas are not there in Advaita.

  

Thus the Markandeya episode is Advaita unfriendly and may be finding literal acceptance in other schools. Vishishtadvaita accepts a ksheerabdhi shaayee form of Vishnu.

  

Shankara does cite the Mahabharata in many instances but they are all to convey the Advaitic principles like mithyAtva of a form for Brahman, unreality of creation, the Vedantic teaching, etc.

  

आनन्दगिरिटीका (बृहदारण्यक) In the Brihadaranyaka Bhashyam:

……… यथोक्तस्य**प्राणसंवादस्य काल्पनिकत्वं** दर्शयति — अयं चेति । …

Shankara demonstrates the kAlpanikatva, the concocted nature, of the Prana-organs conversation.

Anandagiri in the Mandukya bhashya: Just as the Prana conversation is devised to convey the excellence of Prana, so too the creation shrutis are devised to convey Ekatva, since the impossibility of a real creation is being taught.

यथा प्राणवैशिष्ट्यदृष्ट्यर्थंप्राणसंवादः श्रुतिषु कल्प्यते तथा सृष्टिरेकत्वप्रतिपत्त्यर्थत्वेन कल्पिता। वास्तव्याः सृष्टेरयोगस्योपदिष्टत्वादित्यर्थः। …

Since you said the Panchatantra is not a scripture, I have given an instance from the Upanishads that Shankara himself cites for the very purpose: a story is to convey something else and not in reiterating the reality of the story.

On similar terms, the Markandeya story which is patently contradicting the Upanishadic concept of Pralaya, is to be seen as conveying something else, as Nilakantha points out, the Advaitic Cause that is Brahman, and not to be seen as a real event.

  

In non-Advaitic schools the idea of Brahman being the Cause is non-negotiable, absolutely real. I recall Sri Mani Dravid Sastrigal saying in a Dvaita-Advaita meet in Bangalore: In this system (Advaita) Ishwara is not someone who is seated in a corner creating the world and managing it etc.

  

Thus, the stories related to Srishti, Pralaya, etc. are viewed totally differently by Advaita compared to non-Advaitic schools.
