Let's break this down:

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 🔱 1.**Nasadiya Sukta (Rig Veda 10.129)**

This hymn poetically describes the origin of the universe, and particularly the state**before creation**(often interpreted as the state during or before*Pralaya*, cosmic dissolution).

Key ideas:

- There was**neither non-existence nor existence**.

- Darkness was hidden by darkness.

- There was**"the One" (tad ekam)**, breathed by its own impulse.

- Even the gods came after creation — meaning even cosmic deities are
  not eternal.

👉**Interpretation**: The hymn expresses radical**ontological monism or agnosticism**— during Pralaya, only an undifferentiated "One" exists (sometimes interpreted as Brahman or a proto-conscious principle). There is**no multiplicity, no individual beings**, no forms.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 📜 2.**Mahabharata & Markandeya during Pralaya**

In the*Vana Parva*(Book of the Forest), the sage**Markandeya**is described as**surviving the Pralaya**. In that narrative:

- He is shown the Pralaya by Lord Vishnu in the form of a child lying on
  a banyan leaf.

- The waters of dissolution have covered everything.

- Markandeya floats through the cosmic waters and sees Vishnu’s cosmic
  form.

- Vishnu grants him a vision of the universe dissolving and
  regenerating.

👉**Interpretation**: This is a**mythic or symbolic depiction**where Markandeya, as an**enlightened sage**or**jivanmukta**, transcends ordinary space and time. His survival during Pralaya is not necessarily literal — it's a narrative device to illustrate**the continuity of consciousness or divine grace**through all cosmic cycles.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 🧘‍♂️ 3.**Reconciling the Two**

Here’s how these can be harmonized:

| Nasadiya Sukta | Mahabharata (Markandeya) |
|----|----|
| Philosophical / mystical | Narrative / devotional |
| Describes**absolute reality** | Describes**relative reality**or**vision** |
| During Pralaya, only**One**exists (no beings) | A sage**experiences**Pralaya through divine grace |

- In*ultimate reality*(paramarthika), only the**One**exists.

  Markandeya can witness divine play, even during Pralaya.

### 📚 Philosophical Echo

This two-tiered view is echoed in Advaita Vedanta:
