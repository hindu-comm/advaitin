SSSS Mandukya Rahasya Vivritti p71  
“Answer: There is nothing wrong in it. Here the being is considered to be in the seed form (due to superimposition). Though in the Shruti the word Praana denotes Brahman, whose nature is pure being, it is taken without giving up the regerminating seed form of the Soul. Hence the being is called here as Praana, what you said is true according to the phrase, “Dear boy, he has come united with being and has attained his own nature”. (Chan 6-8-2). Though in deep sleep the soul is united with the Being, it is said in the later portion that “All these creatures having merged in the Being, do not know ‘We have merged in the Being’. (Chan 6-8-2). As in the case of deep sleep, so also in the case of death and dissolution of the universe the soul will merge in the Being, but they have no awareness that ‘We have become one with the being’. Due to lack of this knowledge they come back again on waking as before (as I am so and so). The same process is applied to re-birth and re-emergence of the world. This is shown in the passage of the Shruti “Whatever these creatures are now, tiger or lion or wolf or boar or worm or flying insect or fire fly or mosquito - that they become again” (Chan 6-9-3). So, in deep sleep all the souls were merged in the Being alone in the seed form and hence Brahman is considered here as a regerminating seed form of the universe as well as of the Souls. Therefore Brahman is denoted here by the name of Praana. It is evident here that Brahman is attributed as having the regerminating seed as explained by us before. Though Brahman (pure Being) is referred to in the Shruti, “In the beginning this was Being alone” etc., it is said so there, accepting the seed form of the universe along with the Brahman. In the later portions also the regerminating seed form along with Brahman is shown as in the phrase, “The deity willed, well let me entering into these three deities in my Jiva-Self (Jeevatman), differentiate the name and form” (Chan 6-3-2). Here in the phrase like “The mind is tied with Praana” and “then he becomes one with the Being” if the Shruti had intended to say that the nature of Brahman is beyond causation and is devoid of the regerminating (seed form), then it would have said: like “Not this, not this” (Brih. 2-3-6). “Failing to reach This (Self), the words turn back along with the mind (Taittiriya: 2-9). “That (Brahman) is surely different from the known: and again, it is above the unknown” (Kena: 1-4) negating all the superimposed qualities. For this reason it is said in Smriti also. “It is said to be neither Sat nor Asat” (BG: 13-2). Therefore we can easily discern that in the context of Chaandogya Upanishadic sentence above, it is not taken as the nature of Brahman devoid of the germinating seed form. Using reason also, this can be confirmed. When the true nature of Brahman (as Absolute, Non-dual) is not cognized, then it is called the Brahman along with the seed form of the universe. This kind of Brahman is called Avyaakritaatma, the cause of the universe.

It is explained in the Karika by Gaudapada (GK: 2-17) as follows: As an unknown rope is the cause for the erroneous notion like ‘This is a snake’ or a ‘Stream of water’ etc. so also the unknown Self is mistaken for the world and its seed form. From this standpoint Atman is called the cause for the universe (though He is by nature non-dual one and absolute).



Suppose as in the Chaandogya Shruti 6-9-2 & 3 we take it for granted that all creatures in deep sleep become one with Brahman, whose nature is devoid of seed form of the universe, then it will be in contradiction with the utterance of the Shruti: “They come back again as what they were before”. In the example cited earlier when the man understands the real nature of the rope, he will have no misunderstanding about the snake or the stream of water etc.,. On the other hand if he does not have a firm conviction about the real nature of the rope, then his misunderstanding will continue. The same principle is applicable in the case of the dissolution of the universe (Swooning, Nirvikalpa Samadhi etc.). But the Shruti here confirms that the creatures come back again to their previous forms from deep sleep as they were before. If you hold the view that even for the souls who have attained oneness with Brahman, who is devoid of the seed form of the universe as in deep sleep, there is a possibility of   re-emergence, then it would mean rebirth for the enlightened Souls also. (To explain: these souls who have attained emancipation by the knowledge of the self-according to the Shruti and who have been established in the true nature of the Self, which is  non-dual, have realized that their true nature is ever free from the mundane life as well as birth and death. To say that for these Jnanis also there is rebirth will be absurd. Because according to the Shruti, these enlightened ones should have no seed form which can cause re-birth. Thus the absence of the seed form will be common to both (the realized soul and the common man who is in deep sleep), according to your view. To remove this ambiguity in deep sleep, the Chaandogya Shruti (6-9-2 and 3) intends to say here, that all creature have become one with Brahman in deep sleep.// SSSS

  

# On June 15 in the Facebook group “Shankara Advaita Vedanta’,

Prasanth Netiji’s commented on a post responding to Karika 1.2. Here is a summary of his discussion, together with a related email from Subbuji and PNji reply. The summary is followed by the full discussion

## The Central Text and Issue

### The discussion revolves around a specific passage in Shankara's commentary where he explains why scripture refers to Existence (Brahman) as Prana and describes it as the cause in all Upanishads. The key Sanskrit passage states that if Brahman were described in its "seedless" (nirbija) state, several philosophical problems would arise:

1.  ### Individuals couldn't re-emerge from deep sleep or cosmic dissolution

2.  ### Liberated souls might be reborn (since there'd be no causal difference)

3.  ### Knowledge of Brahman would become useless (nothing to burn/negate)

### Therefore, Shankara argues, scripture presents Brahman as "with-seed" (sabija) for pedagogical purposes.

## Prasanth Netiji's Interpretation

### Core Thesis: Post-Shankara Vedantins have fundamentally misunderstood this passage by treating the "causal seed" nature of deep sleep as metaphysically real rather than a contextual teaching device.

### Key Arguments:

1.  ### Perspectival Understanding: Deep sleep appears as Prajna (causal consciousness) and bijāvastha (seed-state) only from the ignorant waking perspective. From the standpoint of truth, the same deep sleep is nothing other than Turiya itself.  

2.  ### Adhyāropa-Apavāda Method: The scriptural presentation of deep sleep as causal is adhyāropa (superimposition) - a provisional teaching that gets negated (apavāda) as understanding matures. This is standard Advaitic methodology.  

3.  ### Illusory Numerology: The very designation "fourth" (turīya) is māyā sankhyā (illusory counting), as Shankara states in his opening verse. This eliminates the concern about calling the Self "third" rather than "fourth."  

4.  ### Unified Reality: Brahman is not actually two-fold (with-seed and without-seed). This apparent duality exists only in the context of bondage/liberation discourse. The Self is always unattached (asanga) - not just after liberation.  

## Subbuji's Orthodox Counter-Position

### Core Thesis: Shankara maintains a clear distinction between the causal seed-state of deep sleep and the seed-free Turiya state, and this distinction serves an important philosophical purpose.

### Key Arguments:

1.  ### Textual Fidelity: The bhāshya explicitly states that the seed-free state will be taught "separately" (prithak) as Turiya, indicating these are distinct teachings, not the same reality viewed from different perspectives.  

2.  ### Pedagogical Necessity: Shankara has a specific reason for presenting deep sleep as "with-seed" - to avoid the philosophical problems he outlines (inability to re-emerge, liberated souls returning, etc.).  

3.  ### Scriptural Intent: If the Upanishad intended to present deep sleep as seedless, it would have used the language reserved for Turiya (neti neti, "from which speech returns," etc.), but it doesn't.  

4.  ### Methodological Caution: Just because all three states are ultimately Turiya from the absolute standpoint doesn't mean we should ignore their specific characteristics as taught in scripture.  

## The Deeper Hermeneutical Question

### The debate reveals a fundamental tension in Advaitic interpretation:

### Prasanth's Approach: Scripture's primary purpose (tātparya) in presenting the three states is to point beyond the states themselves to the stateless Self. The causal interpretation of deep sleep is a stepping stone, not the final truth.

### Subbuji's Approach: Scripture methodically presents distinct states with specific characteristics that serve pedagogical functions. Collapsing these distinctions prematurely undermines the systematic teaching.

## Supporting Evidence from Brahmasutra Commentary

### Prasanth strengthens his position by citing Shankara's Brahmasutra commentary (1.3.42), where Shankara explicitly warns against misunderstanding the purpose of state-analysis:

### "If anyone thinks that because there is an exposition of the three states... it follows that the text means to teach the true nature of the transmigrant, he might as well turn his face towards west when setting out to travel east."

### Shankara clarifies that the purpose is to establish "freedom from the states and transmigration," not to solidify the reality of states or transmigration.

## Philosophical Implications

### For Prasanth: This interpretation preserves the radical non-dualism of Advaita by showing that even the most subtle dualities (causal vs. non-causal states) are ultimately pedagogical devices. The Self's nature is always the same - unattached, stateless, non-dual.

### For Subbuji: This approach risks undermining the systematic methodology of Vedantic teaching. The careful distinctions made in scripture and commentary serve important philosophical functions that shouldn't be dissolved prematurely.

## Methodological Significance

### The debate ultimately concerns:

- ### Whether Advaitic teaching is purely deconstructive (systematically negating all positions) or constructive (building understanding through progressive distinctions)

- ### How to balance the absolute perspective (everything is Brahman) with the practical necessity of spiritual pedagogy

- ### Whether traditional post-Shankara interpretations represent authentic development or deviation from Shankara's original insights

Of course talking about sushupti in sabija and nirbija lines, both comes from Sruti alone. If one can show “x” number of references about sushupti being sabija, it is possible to show “y” number of references where that sushupti is also pointed at by Śruti calling it “the highest”.

Here, I ask, which of those two approaches takes one to Truth? That is, we must always ask what is Śruti’s most important approach? - That means, we have to ask what is exact tAtparya of Śruti when it asks us to make avasthatraya vichara?

If you say, it is to present Sushupti as sabija alone and thereby if you say that thus sruti differentiates between moksha (i.e. nirbija) and sabija sushupti (daily 8 hours sleep), then I say, you are putting Śruti to wrong use.

Śruti’s tAtparya in making us do avasthatraya vichara should not be understood in the lines as if Śruti meant to teach us (or support us in our idea that there is) transmigration. As per Siddhanta, we do not accept transmigrating soul at all. That is why assimilating adhyaropa-apavada prakriyā is key.

Bhagavan Bhashyakara make it absolutely clear in Brahmasutra bhāshya 1.3.42 regarding what is exact tAtparya of Sruti when it makes us takeup avasthatraya vichara. He says:

यस्तु मध्ये बुद्धान्ताद्यवस्थोपन्यासात्संसारिस्वरूपविवक्षां मन्यते, स प्राचीमपि दिशं प्रस्थापितः प्रतीचीमपि दिशं प्रतिष्ठेत । यतो न बुद्धान्ताद्यवस्थोपन्यासेनावस्थावत्त्वं संसारित्वं वा विवक्षितं — किं तर्हि ? — अवस्थारहितत्वमसंसारित्वं च । कथमेतदवगम्यते ? यत् ‘अत ऊर्ध्वं विमोक्षायैव ब्रूहि’ (बृ. उ. ४ । ३ । १४) इति पदे पदे पृच्छति । यच्च ‘अनन्वागतस्तेन भवत्यसङ्गो ह्ययं पुरुषः’ (बृ. उ. ४ । ३ । १५) इति पदे पदे प्रतिवक्ति । ‘अनन्वागतं पुण्येनानन्वागतं पापेन तीर्णो हि तदा सर्वाञ्शोकान्हृदयस्य भवति’ (बृ. उ. ४ । ३ । २२) इति च । तस्मादसंसारिस्वरूपप्रतिपादनपरमेवैतद्वाक्यमित्यवगन्तव्यम् ॥

“” But if anyone thinks that because there is an exposition of the three states of waking, dream and dreamless sleep, it follows that the text means to teach the true nature of the transmigrant, he might as well turn his face towards west when setting out to travel east. For the presentation of the states of waking etc., is meant neither to imply the possession of any state nor of transmigration.

What is meant then? What is meant is freedom from the states and transmigration. How is that known? Because at every turn Janaka requests Yajnavalkya: “Please instruct me further about liberation" (Br.U. 4.3.14-16, 33), and because the answer given at every step is: "He is untouched by whatever he sees in that state, for this infinite Being is unattached” (Br.U. 4.3.15-16). Further, it is stated, “(This aspect of his) is untouched by good work and untouched by bad work, for he is then beyond all the woes of his heart (intellect)” (Br.U. 4.3.22). Accordingly, it is to be understood that this text is meant for establishing the nature of the supermundane Self. “”

Personally, by showing sushupti as only that which is sabija, in one shot, ātma’s asangatvam becomes a dogma to be believed. Sage Yajnavalkya did not do that. When Janaka asked again and again to tell about “the highest”, Yajnavalkya talks about waking, dream and deep sleep and he points to deep sleep as “एषा परमागतिः" and pointing that declares purusha is asanga!

I will stop here. I will not accept Sushupti as only sabija. If I do that, I run into what bhashyakara says i.e. स प्राचीमपि दिशं प्रस्थापितः प्रतीचीमपि दिशं प्रतिष्ठेत (he might as well turn his face towards west when setting out to travel east).

Ātma is asanga! If anyone asks, When? The answer is: Always!!Not that it becomes asanga after release.

If anyone further asks, show me when it is asanga? - “Look” into deepsleep i.e. negate waking standpoint explanation of deepsleep. That is the siddhanta and prakriya.

Thus, of course by this explanation the truth of waking and dream is also Brahman. But the trick is that “everything is Brahman alone” happens when enquiry pivots over our understanding of Deepsleep!

Therefore my understanding of karika 1.2 bhāshya is perfectly fine and it is also strengthened when I consider Bhagavan bhashyakara’s Brahmasutra bhāshya 1.3.42 as given above where he explains what is exact purpose of Śruti in making us take up avasthatraya vichara.

Praṇām.
