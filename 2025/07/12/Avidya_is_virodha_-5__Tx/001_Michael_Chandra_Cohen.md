Excellent lines of inquiry. Here's a response to your comparative reading of Jaishankar S. N.'s treatment of **avidyā as bīja/hetu/kāraṇa** with respect to Śaṅkara’s own prasthāna-traya bhāṣya and the views of Satchidanandendra Saraswati (SSSS), Mayeda, Alston, Andrijanić, Hacker, and others.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 🔍 **1. Jaishankar S. N. on Avidyā as Kāraṇa (Seed, Cause, Hetu)**

Jaishankar consistently reads **avidyā** as a *causal principle*—the **seed** of saṃsāra (via rebirth and doership), not just an epistemic mistake. He follows the **Sabīja-Nirbīja** distinction as found in *Māṇḍūkya Kārikā 1.2*, where deep sleep (*suṣupti*) is "seeded" (*sabīja*) with ignorance, while Turiya is "seedless" (*nirbīja*), hence non-causal and not a condition for rebirth.

He aligns this with:

- **Adhyāsa Bhāṣya**: where adhyāsa (superimposition) is caused by
  avidyā and is the basis of saṃsāra.

- **BG 2.13, 15.8**: which he reads through the lens of *kāraṇatva* of
  avidyā in transmigration.

- **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad Bhāṣya 4.3.20–21**: Jaishankar emphasizes
  Śaṅkara’s idea that in deep sleep, *avidyā* remains as the seed that
  sprouts into saṃsāra upon waking.

Thus, for him, **avidyā is not merely a misperception** but a causal principle responsible for the entire manifestation of doership, experience, and rebirth. He often quotes **vivekinām tu puruṣaviśeṣāṇām** (BU Bh. 2.4.12) to show that only vivekins transcend this causal structure.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 🧭 **2. Śaṅkara’s Original Position**

While Śaṅkara uses terms like *bīja*, *kāraṇa*, *hetu*, and *mūla* for **avidyā**, his usage is often **pedagogical** (*adhyāropa-apavāda*) rather than ontological. For example:

- **Māṇḍūkya Kārikā Bh. 1.2**:

  > "Suṣupti is *sabīja* because the impressions (vāsanās) remain and
  > re-emerge. Turiya is *nirbīja*."

  But here, Śaṅkara does not posit a metaphysical “entity” called
  *avidyā* as a permanent causal substance. Rather, the seed analogy is
  a **functional explanation** for observed recurrence.

- **Bṛhad Bh. 4.3.20–21**:  
  He clarifies that in deep sleep, the jīva merges into its causal
  condition, but this causal condition (avidyā) is not a second entity —
  it is **the limiting adjunct** (*upādhi*) of the jīva. It is neither
  sat nor asat, nor a third thing. He is emphatically clear that the
  cause is **not real** in itself.

> Thus, Śaṅkara treats *avidyā-kāraṇatva* as a **provisional explanatory
> tool**, dissolved in jñāna.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 🧭 **3. Satchidanandendra Saraswati (SSSS) on Kāraṇatva of Avidyā**

SSSS criticizes post-Śaṅkara Advaitins (esp. Vivaraṇa & Bhāmatī schools) for turning this provisional usage into a **realist metaphysics of avidyā**. His key assertions:

- **No real kāraṇa**: *Avidyā* cannot be a real seed/cause since the
  very notion of causality is **empirically constructed** and
  invalidated in jñāna.

- **Sabīja-suṣupti is an **adhyāropa** for pedagogical analysis.** There
  is no real seed. Rather, the **continuity of saṃsāra** arises due to
  **prārabdha** functioning under ignorance.

- **In SSSS’s interpretation**, deep sleep is an **intimation**
  (*pratibodha*) of the real nature, though veiled — not a locus of real
  causal avidyā.

He considers Śaṅkara's usage of *hetu*, *bīja*, etc., to be only in *laukika vyavahāra*, not in *pāramārthika satta*.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### 🧠 **4. Mayeda, Alston, Andrijanić, Hacker – Interpretive Summaries**

#### • **Mayeda**:

In his translation of *Upadeśasāhasrī*, Mayeda notes that Śaṅkara **avoids attributing ontological reality** to avidyā. He sees the language of *cause*, *seed*, etc., as an **upacāra** (figurative expression) used for teaching.

#### • **Alston**:

Stresses that for Śaṅkara, *avidyā* is a **nonentity**, and its causal descriptions are meant to help the seeker understand rebirth and bondage. Once Self-knowledge arises, these explanations fall away.

#### • **Andrijanić**:

Explores the **"reification" of avidyā** in later Advaita — showing how *avidyā as a seed* turns into a subtle substance-like principle in Vivaraṇa/Bhāmatī schools, unlike in Śaṅkara.

#### • **Hacker**:

Traces how Śaṅkara’s **epistemological usage** of avidyā (as *superimposition*) gets transformed into a **cosmological-explanatory** principle in post-Śaṅkara thought. For Hacker, Śaṅkara himself used the *seed* metaphor loosely, not to propose a **subtle ontological entity**.

### 🪷 **5. Application to Deep Sleep (Suṣupti) and Turiya**

| Concept | Deep Sleep (Suṣupti) | Turiya |
|----|----|----|
| **Jaishankar** | Sabīja: *Avidyā remains*, causal for rebirth | Nirbīja: Free from avidyā, no rebirth |
| **Śaṅkara** | Empirically sabīja; vāsanās continue; but no ontological bīja | No seed, no waking, pure consciousness |
| **SSSS** | Appearance of seed is due to *adhyāsa* | No seed, as there is no second thing |
| **Vivaraṇa/Bhāmatī** | Avidyā is a subtle causal principle (mūlāvidyā) | Turiya is beyond avidyā but avidyā continues for others |

### 🧵 Summary Thread:

- **Jaishankar SN** uses Śaṅkara's language of "kāraṇa" to assert a
  *causal* function for avidyā in rebirth.

- Śaṅkara himself uses "cause", "seed", "hetu" as *instructional
  metaphors* in the **adhyāropa-apavāda** method.

- **SSSS** reinterprets Śaṅkara to remove even this **interim
  metaphysical status** of avidyā.

- **Mayeda, Alston, Andrijanić, Hacker** all agree that Śaṅkara does
  **not treat avidyā as an entity**, only as a pedagogical necessity,
  rejected upon realization.
