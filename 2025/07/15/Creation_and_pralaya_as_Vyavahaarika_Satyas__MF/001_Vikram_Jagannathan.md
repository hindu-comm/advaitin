There are more constructive ways to convey disagreement. And I am not interested in a comparison that "so and so person said this to me; I said like this to another person".**

  

In the spirit of cordial discussion, I am responding to your statements in an objective manner.

  
  
\<\< quoted Sri Shankara saying that stories of creation should not be taken literally (and hence stories of dissolution too). \>\>  
  

Yes, this aligns to what I mentioned on the other thread. Advaita siddhanta includes various prakriyas on the concept of creation as part of the student's knowledge development. Parinama vada, Vivarta vada (including SDV & DSV), Ajati vada presents a sequence of understanding. They are all explained through the adhyaropa-apavada method. **Ultimately, the reality of creation is completely denied even in vyavaharika!**

  
  
\<\< But does this mean that creation is denied in vyavahArika satya (VS)? Let's say creation is denied as VS. Then pralaya is also denied as VS. \>\>  
  

Yes! **Creation is mithya in vyavaharika!** Vyavaharika-satya is also called mithya or simply just maya! Vyavaharika satya doesn't mean it is actually satya in vyavaharika. **Creation isn't actually satya in vyavaharika and becomes mithya in paramarthika**. Considering creation as satya is ajnani drishti in vyavaharika. Understanding creation as mithya is jnani drishti in vyavaharika. **Similarly pralaya too is only mithya in vyavaharika.**

  
  
\<\< So, if there is no pralaya even as a VS, then Vishnu-loka and Shiva-loka are eternal in VyavahAra - something Sri Subbuji vehemently argues against. \>\>  
  

**There is no actual pralaya in vyavahara either.** An ignorant person believes pralaya is satya, whereas a jnani understands that even the so-called pralaya is only mithya in vyavahara. When all distinctions are included within the scope of maya, even Bhagavan-Vishnu-Loka or Bhavagan-Shiva-Loka are only mithya even in vyavahara. **Again, vyavaharika-satya doesn't mean it is actually satya in vyavaharika. It just means it is mithya, but misunderstood as satya by an ajnani in vyavahara.**Furthermore, all these are ever non-existent in paramarthika.

  
  
\<\< needs to accept that creation is VS and pralaya is VS and therefore, some account of both can also be VS. \>\>  
  

Yes, they are both vyavaharika-satya or in other words mithya, and **that doesn't mean they actually exist even as we speak**! Please note the difference between an ajnani drishti and jnani drishti!  
  
prostrations,  
Vikram
