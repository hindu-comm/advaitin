Interesting that he brings in the word "imagined". That has the mirror question "By whom or what?"

  

There is an Absolute being posited as the adhishtanam of the imagination. You are affirming the dualistic cognitions (things) that in truth do not exist in the Absolute and that do not all vanish from awareness even after realizing the Absolute. So they defy logic - are anirvachaneeya.

  

But who sees/projects them?

  

PSA: so long as you affirm their cognition/imagination in spite of knowledge of the Absolute, then in your standpoint, there is also the imagination of the Absolute as their non-dual Cause. That is, Brahman is seen/imagined as Ishvara so long as namarupa is imagined in the Absolute. The Absolute is imagined as having an adjunct of Maya/avidya that mirrors (or is the causal bimba) for the namarupa projection (the pratibimba).

  

Note: just as the jnani knows namarupa to be imagination on Self, he also knows its mirror Causal shakti in Self is also an imagination. Both are non-existent in Self and yet they appear (i.e. Self is imagined as them) - hence they are mithya.

  

SSS =\> the imagination is in the jiva due to avidya (that goes away post-jnana), it is by ishvara through maya (that does not go away (is real) in this standpoint). That is, the Absolute should not be dragged into the imagination; Leave IT alone. Where namarupa is admitted to be imagination, the causes are self-contained within the dualistic framework of the imagination. No need to superimpose a further imagination of non-dual dual Ishvaratvam onto the Absolute, compromising its Absoluteness.

  

Paramarthika and vyavaharika are two faces of Existence. Vyavaharika is imagination only with reference to paramarthika satya but that does not mean it is to be called ontologically mithya because that wouldamount to inserting a pseudo-duality into the Absolute.  

  

PSA =\> There are no two faces to Existence. There is only the Absolute. Vyavaharika-Paramarthika division is yet another imagination, and so long as we speak of imagination (i.e. acknowledge that Absolute is nirguna), we cannot avoid the mula-imagination of Mula-avidya or Maya. Maya and Vyavaharika (ishvara-jiva-jagat duality) come and go together; you affirm one you affirm the other. The knowledge of their being imaginationon Brahmanis why they have to be classified as "mithya" even while they are perceived; only ajnanis can avoidthis because they have no knowledge of the Absolute as Reality.
