The non-dual Self is alone there. We have to ask what it means to say "pramatr etc" fall along with ignorance.



  

> 

> Afaik PSA agrees to this. Self is nirguna. namarupa is adhyasa. But in
> the standpoint of cognitions, where it stands affirmed even
> post-jnana, they callit mithya due to knowing the reality-standpoint
> of the Self where namarupa has no existence.

> How to define mithya is core! You call it anirvacaniya with a
> quasi-ontic status saying, "something' indeterminable as real nor
> unreal' (sadasadbhyam) while PTB only uses the termtattvanyatvabhyam,
> neither Tat (Brahman) or different from Tat in which case Brahman is
> the only ontic. There is no place for quasi-ontic when Brahman is the
> only Ontology/Sateva

  

I am saying namarupa is adhyasa on orimagination of Brahman. Meaning that Sat does not have namarupa distinctions of any kind intrinsic to it. And yet Sat is known in/via namarupa as if it is possessed of such pratyaksha-based distinctions.

  

So long as you affirm namarupa duality in any kind, it is not asat to you. The shastra says it is asat in Sat - so if you want to include the shastra as pramana, you also must negatethe satyatvamof namarupa. So, "you" and the "duality you see" belong within a plane of imagination superimposed on Sat. Whatever is this imagination is neither sat nor asat (to you who knows also the shastra) - therefore it is called mithya in this standpoint.

If there is namarupa imagination of the Absolute, that involves the seeing of something that is not true in the Absolute. The Seer is only that Absolute for there is no other Self. He alone imagines. "Himself alone". That is vyavaharika satya. So He is the cause for His imagination, He has the knowledge and power (Maya-shakti) to imagine these worlds not real in Him and 'play' within those worlds as jivas and jagat.
