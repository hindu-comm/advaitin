Namaskaram Bhaskar-ji,

  

"SSS": namarupa pratyaksha is of Brahman's svarupas. Svarupa is an upadhi that is an intrinsic structural variation, differentiation or deformation within Brahman. Together the set of svarupa-upadhis is the Upadhi called Maya that is one with Brahman (because the upadhis are constitutionally only Brahman). Pratyaksha reveals Brahman in the context of each svarupa upadhi.

  

This knowledge of Brahman in the realsvarupa-upadhi context is different from the adhyasa that is due to avidya; but the duality of svarupas does not contradict the non-duality of the Atma shining in all of them. Once we realize that Atma in all, we are liberated. This svarupa-duality is different from VA because in VA there is a real differentiation of selves that accompanies the different svarupas(and Narayana "who is special like no other", is embracing all others in Him, but their distinct identities remain). Whereas for SSS, thereis only Self that has multiple svarupas of equal validity - and any other samsara of high-low, good-bad seen in the world of namrupas is due to ignorance - for they all denote only the same Self.  

  

  

imo "PSA" agrees with the basic framework above but the difference is this: Maya is not the repository of (the knowledge of) the real svarupas of Brahman. Brahman being nirguna, nirvisesha has nosvarupa intrinsic/real to It. Maya therefore is the repository of (the knowledge of) the svarupa-imaginations of Brahman. It is the cognitive lens that distorts Consciousness and projects, superimposes the namarupa svarupas.

  

Now it is very important to understand that this position on Maya is valid only where the question is raised about the namarupa duality - thus dividing Brahman to begin with. Only when the premise assumes the dualityof seer vs seen as if real and frames the question based on it. Like "Does the jnani also see namarupa?" If we break the non-duality of Brahman like that, then we are within vyavaharika (the standpoint of imagination) and the explanation will be that Brahman is Ishvara - either that namarupa svarupa is real in Him (SSS) or that Maya (Ishvara) is the upadhi-reality that imagines/projects the namarupa on Sat.

  

But for PSA, neither of these positions is satya because they are founded on (and completions of) the imagination of duality in Brahman. From the standpoint of Truth, such questions have no place. And this higher fact of Reality is emphasized in their categorization of these vyavaharika entities as being "mithya"/adhyasa.

  

As for the question of jnanis seeing the world even after jnana, well again, the whole question is within the imagined dualistic framework. Either we say, jnani is naught-else but Brahman (and shut up talk beyond that), or we say that the jnani cognizes Brahman in namarupa upadhi contexts but knows that such svarupa identification of Sat is mithya (adhyasa) with reference to the standpoint of Reality.

  

Now, all that said from the theoretical comparative standpoint, it may be more useful if people of each camp explain why they think the other party's realization of Brahman cannot truly liberate the jiva from fearlessness and suffering (or whatever practical else jivanmukti constitutes). Some tarka along those lines will be illuminating and complementary to the ontological etc. discussions.

  

thollmelukaalkizhu
