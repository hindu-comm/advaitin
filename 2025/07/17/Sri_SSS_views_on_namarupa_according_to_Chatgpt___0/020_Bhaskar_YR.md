- Very aptly described prabhuji. Yes, rope-snake analogy always reveals
  the fact that for misconceiving the rope as snake, the existing rope
  is nimitta though this nimitta rope does not have any intrinsic
  changes, we the ajnAni-s see the snake in place of rope. We, as
  ajnAni-s, just see the imagined parts of a rope and sAdrushya
  (similarities) between rope-snake, like curved, coiled shape of rope
  give rise to the cognition of serpent, the imagined parts of brahman
  could give rise to the Jagat. Therefore, this imagination is only done
  by us (by avidyAvanta-s) or the jeeva who has the qualified knowledge
  about the rope. Whereas in the pot-clay analogy, the purpose is to
  convey the message of the shruti that though the jagat (pot) has
  emanated from brahman (clay), the latter is still vyavahAraateeta
  (transactionless). Even here, without dropping the idea of creation
  (creation of pot from clay), it is to be reconciled with the
  transaction lessness of brahman (clay) shruti says mruttiketyeva
  satyaM.
