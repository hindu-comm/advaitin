The way I see it: If jagat has Brahman as real upadana, then the vikara that is the basis for identification of jagat (or Brahman as-if jagat), that vikara has to have Brahman as its locus (because there is no other reality), it is Brahman's vikara/knowledge that it brings/cognizes to manifestation.

  

Thus jagat-identification of Brahman and jagat karanatva-association in Brahman come and go together.

  

If we talk about jagat-pratyaksha, then there is a seer (say, jnani or ajnani) for whom that is pratyaksha. The nondual Self is now divided into seer and seen. Division implies a duality of two different entities, identities, selves; one is the seer claiming Self and the other is his not-self seen. The beeja-vikara (Maya=Avidya, for psa) for this division is therefore admitted as a part of or in conjunction with the unitary Self.

  

But when we consider with reference to the paramarthika standpoint (psa: that alone denotes truth of Brahman), both this division and its supposed vikara, both the jagat-identification of Brahman and the jagat upadana/nimitta karanatva-association in Brahman, are realized as adhyasa/imagination on the nirvikara Self. This Self-knowledge negates their satyatvam, and so they can at most be admitted by the jnani as mithya, in any context or standpoint that prima-facie speaks in their dualistic terms (as-if real).

  

  

This is like a dream. I see "Ramu" in the dream. Then I appear as the seer and Ramu as my seen,in the dream. It is (vyavaharika) satya to say "That seen Ramu is My (or my mind's) projection." and "Ramu's beeja-vikara is already and always in Me (or my mind)." Therefore I am the upadana-nimitta karana (Ishvara) for the imagined Ramu.

  

Note that once we take this viewpoint (rooting the entire dream in the Seer-Self), there is no real meaning to "tiny Ramu's imagination". For I am the sole seer and projector of whatever happens in tiny Ramu. It is all My imagination.

  

But let us continue the logical reduction. First Ramu is seen^1, I am seer^1. This seer-seen duality is itself an appearance where the non-duality of Self is inherently contradicted in the context of this division. If we ask, "appears" to whom, where?, the answer again has to be, To ME, In ME, the sole Existence-Consciousness. I am the adhishtanam for the seer-seen duality. But then that would make Me the upadana-nimitta karana (seer^2) for the seer-seen appearance (seen^2). We have ended up in a second order duality, for which again the Self is realized as adhishtanam for an appearance.

  

We went from

  

seer & seen (dream)

  

Seer-\>seen

  

Adhishtanam (seer^2) -\> Seer-seen (seen^2)

  

Either continuing this logic leads to an infinite regress, or we have to simply accept that duality of all kinds (including whatever is called pratyaksha prama) is adhyasa on Self, and hence can at most be regarded as mithyawhenever they are posited.

We have to get over this "tiny jiva" (Ramu) business. (imu) The contention is that Self is nondual, nirvikara, and hence if there is any talk of duality, it is only as an appearance, imagination, cognition due to the coeval causal factor called Maya=Avidya. Brahman is known as Ishvara, and vyavaharika jagat, tiny jiva and its imaginations, big ishvara and his creations, are all His imagination.

  

"Vyavaharika jagat is satya" would imply that jagat-vikara/vritti is an intrinsic part of nirvikara Brahman. That would be a self-contradiction. Therefore vyavaharika jagat is an adhyasa on Brahman, that is considered mithya. It is inconsistent to qualify jagat as "vyavaharika" andseek the distinct real-affirmation of its dualities (as pratyaksha prama), and then to also insist there is no separate identification apart from Self and that it is all only vacharambhana denotations for Sat. To give pratyaksha prama a separate status and consideration requires the re-direction of attention away from the Sat adhishtanam to the realm of dualistic differentiated identifiers/identifications of Sat. Which implies the association of distinct realities (selves) to the distinct namarupa identifications of Sat: the focus is in "their world" now. This compromising of the truth of the nondual, nirvikara Self has to be undone in the very plane of the pratyaksha identifications, by clearly demarking them as mithya and not satya.

  

Again, psa position imu: The realization of the unitary Self (Ishvara) while positing reality to the namarupa dualities (as pratyaksha pramaof satya vyavaharika jagat) is not equivalent to the realization of nirvikara Brahman devoid of all duality, nor are they two true standpoints for knowledge of Brahman. One is satya, the other belongs to mithya, adhyasa.

> and the nAma rUpAtmaka pot does not have any independent existence
> apart from clay.

  

If pot has existence in clay and is dependent on clay, then the nature of this existence and dependence is where there is disagreement. Either we can treat pot as a vacharambhana identifier for nirvikara, non-dual clay (like the word Brahman) and not bring in the question of their existence, meaning, knowledge etc. Or we can consider that Clay is imagined as-if having pot-rupa but in truth the pot has neither existence in nor dependence on clay - hence pot is mithya. Or,we identify clay as actual upadana for the dualistic pot imagination (Its projection, rupa), which means clay has pot-vikara inherent in it and as you put it, "pot does not have any independent existence apart from clay." PSA would not accept the last position as satya; it is also adhyasa.

When we speak of jnani and differentiate from ajnani, we are no longer using such namarupas as if only vacharambhana-identifiers of the Self. They are now distinct individual selves for us that we are comparing and contrasting against each other. Our discussion is based on a standpoint whose"realities" are all based on adhyasa on Self. So, those "realities" that we feel obliged to identify individually and distinguish from each other, compare and contrast, talk about what sees what, what remains for what, etc. - they are all mithya because they are not satya and we continue to talk about them as-if they are.

  

As I see it, your position is conceding vikara (or some beeja-vritti) in Self in order to not consider these identifications of identifiers as mithya, but rather as satya thatis grounded in Sat. That way you can justify "Look. These dualities don't go away for the jnani because they have a differentiated vikara existence in Existence, that is known through pratyaksha; and yet their upadana continues to be Existence; and hence the jnani knows Existence in all of them."

  

PSA refuses to accept vikara in Brahman, therefore the dualities are all imagination and so are the explanations (Maya, prarabdha, prama/vikara) that may make sense (or be an extension) in the standpoint of imagination - because the explanation cannot reachthe non-duality. Because they are all imagination/adhyasa, they have to be considered mithya so long as we continue to speak of them as-if satya. Non-duality of Self is realized not in the clingingofboth sides and seeking a samanvaya between them. That (Ishvara} can be the penultimate step, but ultimately we have to surrender entirely the cogitations on not-Self and settle in the pure Awareness that alone Is.

  

The PSA jnani also realizes "Self in all and all in Self", but when the focus shifts to the "all", then he considers a compromise to Truth has already occurred, therefore it is imperative to emphasize the mithyatva of this dualistic all.
