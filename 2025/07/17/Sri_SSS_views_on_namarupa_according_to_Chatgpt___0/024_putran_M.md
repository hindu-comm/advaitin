That means, the reality (Brahman) in its total sense has both a vikara-less svarupa and vikara-manifest dimension to it. And this two-sided description is held by you to be satyam. Here is the counter-position:

  

"Jnani" is (Self conditioned by) a namarupa. "Jagat" is a namarupa. There can be no identification of individual namarupa that is not also the conditioned adhyasa of the Self with the corresponding upadhi. This is the limiting, individualising, differentiating of undivided Consciousness by the defining attributes or limitations of the superimposed upadhi. The identification of jnani individually is therefore an adhyasa on Brahman, because Brahman is conditioned by that namarupa upadhi and differentiated into individual identities in a mithya-world based on adhyasa.Adhyasa because Brahman is adhishtanam that is devoid of namarupa vikara or vritti. There is no namarupa existence apart from Self, nor in Self: the conditioning/identification of Self with namarupa is adhyasa.

  

Pratyaksha vikara-prama is a mithya-world that coincides with upadhi-adhyasa on Brahman. The jnani of this world knows this truth and abides in the jnana of the unconditioned Self. This jnana translates in (appears in the context of) the mithya standpoint (which is where "jnani sees jagat" has meaning) as the svarupa jnana of Ishvara (the Self conditioned by Maya, the source of vikaras). So Ishvara is vyavaharika satya, or the satya of vyavaharika, and the jnani knows and sees this Ishvara where wesee jagat and jivas.

  

If vyavaharika is mithya in the light of the paramarthika truth of Brahman, then does the jnani have mithya drishti? Vyavaharika itself is mithya, so no question of drishti within it being paramartha satya. But that drishti is now rooted in the knowledge of nondual Self as it translates/projects to the vyavaharika standpoint. The jnani has paramartha satya jnana and vyavaharika satya drishti. That means he realizes the non-dual Self in and through all the pratyaksha-adhyasas alongside the self-adhyasa associated with his own namarupa, which means he "sees" Brahman the All as Ishvara (i.e. unitary Self in conjunction with Maya upadhi, that manifests/imagines/projects vyavaharika namarupa jagat) and yet knows that the adhishtanam is in truth nirvikara, unconditioned Consciousness. Thus the Ishvara drishti too is mithya jnana so long as Existence is understood as having any sort of internal, external, qualifying duality.

  

Does that mean jnani is a prey to adhyasa in spite of paramartha jnana? It is not that the jnani superimposes vyavaharika jagat onto Brahman or that doing so is a sign of ignorance. The word-meaning of jnani in vyavaharika includes this jagat-drishti as part of its manifest definition; it is a part of and linked to the larger mithya world of word-meanings (namarupa) projected by Ishvara. The further specialty of the jnani-namarupa is that it includes the paramartha jnana of Brahman where even Ishvara characterization stands negated. The jnani "sees" Ishvara as the source and antaryami of all vikaras including his own; but *knows* "jnani" "jagat" "Ishvara" are all namarupa adhyasa on nirvikara Brahman. Maya is the real causal "source" Upadhi - vyavaharika satya - so long as one speaks from the standpoint of any individual upadhi-identification - because if Brahman the adhishtanam is to be affirmed from the (adhyasa) standpoint, the ONLY way to do so is as Ishvara.

  

Alternatively, we can simply refuse to superimpose on the jnani the duality of vyavaharika. "The knower of Brahman becomes Brahman". Therefore stop saying things like "jnani sees same-jagat also"; it is meaningless like son of a barren woman. Either this sort of position or we have what I presented above.

Firstly, whether maryada or not, the contention is that Ishvara is not paramarthika satya of Brahman, that such a duality-based knowledge of nirvikara Brahman is mithya. So it is not necessary that jnanis have to abide by a set rule of "How they approach Ishvara" because that is a matter of how much the jnani focuses on pratyaksha-pramathat he realizes is asat in paramarthika. Some may do so more directly and hence see Ishvara (in All forms) in a bhakti mode; others may be considerably detached from the mithya loka with their attention fixed in the paramartha jnana.

  

But secondly: Of course, if you see what I wrote above, Ishvara is vyavaharika satya - so He is accepted in the context of prapancha identification. He is accepted in two ways.

1) As the Lord and karmaphaladataof jiva-jagat; (imm SDV) 

2) and as the adhishtanam+Maya who manifests (imagines) all of vyavaharika, is its upadana-nimitta karana (in entirety). (imm DSV)

  

I think you accept only the first whereas others consider Ishvara in this more general sense (therefore Maya=Avidya for them).  

  

You may ask why some speak of Ishvara explicitly whereas others seem to only refer to Avidya. Are the latter lacking the maryada? I would say the first para above should explain why here as well. Not all will call the adhishtanam seen in second sense as Ishvara; so they restrict that usage to the first sense alone and prioritize the (mithya) causal factor Avidya when approaching in the second sense, to emphasize that (satya) Brahman is untouched by karanatvametc. But then you would note that they say Avidya = Maya; and said or not, maryada is to see Maya as shakti of Brahman, in vyavaharika. Basically, more than the maryada, you have to note that the essential perspective is or aligns to Ishvara in the most general sense. There is the affirmation of shakti or vikara dimension in Brahman (call it together as Ishvara or separately as Avidya), but that it also is an appearance within the plane of adhyasa, so mithya that cannot be absolutely negated so long as you affirm prapancha etc.

So let me just address the perspective behind "His imagination". Once we say there is secondless reality and we claim of pratyaksha prama or satya vikara-jnana, then the unitary locus and source for the vikara is that reality. The reality is now realized as Ishvara and whatever vikara or vritti is identified, He (or His maya) is the source, projector and cognizer of that change or individual identification. We have Ishvara in the general sense now, as the unitary Self and the antaryami of all His vikara appearance.

  

Here are the two approaches:

  

1\. Svarupa and Vikara are two distinct dimensions; put all self-identifications ishvara-jiva-jagat at this vikara level whereas the secondless non-duality is at the svarupa level. This to me is like Sankhya.

  

2\. Adhishtanam + Maya (vikara jnana-iccha-kriya shakti) = Sat + chit =Ishvara, and He is the cause of all creation (ishvara-jiva-jagat). Again, the dream example is perfect for this viewpoint. The dream appears as having its ishvara, jiva and jagat. The Self of them is secondless reality. But so long as both dimensions are affirmed, the Self stands realized as the Dreamer of all the dream.



But keep in mind, those who advocate the latter viewpoint also add that this divided perspective of Brahman (as Sat+ chit) is also adhyasa, and hence its Ishvara is mithya (at least if one thinks the reasoning above shows a "need" for His affirmation in vyavaharika; then He is logical completion to the standpoint and yet not the paramartha satya, therefore He is mithya).

What is vyavaharika pot? It is an object you use to hold water. It has transactional meaning in the world. It involves the conditioning of clay to a particular shape. It is different from the vyavaharika table.

  

This means the vyavaharika pot is an upadhi parichinna drishti of clay.

  

Pot is (only) Clay is true and Clay is all there is (as pots), is also true.

  

In the first statement, the adhyasa of pot (its vyavaharika satta) is negated so that it is only a vacharambhana identifier for Clay. It certainly does not imply pot is not adhyasa on Clay; rather because it is adhyasa, that which is imagined as pot is really only Clay. Likewise nirvikara brahman is imagined as jagat and all that, therefore that which is imagined as jagat is really only Brahman.

  

In the second statement, "Clay is everything" is meant to tell us that that which is superimposed, identified, upadhi-conditioned as vyavaharika pot (the"everything") is really only the unconditioned Clay. The Self is imagined as jnani, ajnani, pot and table. It is not meant to suggest that the namarupa upadhi of pot has paramarthika satta in Clay. However that is how it will *appear* to be when we have upadhi parichinna drishti, and that gives the vyavaharika Ishvara-understanding for these shruti statements.
