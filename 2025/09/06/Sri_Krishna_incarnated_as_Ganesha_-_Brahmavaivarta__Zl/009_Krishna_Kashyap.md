Yes, absolutely! Just like there are specific Agamas for Shaiva, Shakta, and Vaishnava traditions, there is a distinct body of scripture for the **Ganapatya tradition**, where Ganesha (Ganapati) is revered as the Supreme Being.

### Ganapatya Agamas and Related Texts

The Ganapatya tradition primarily draws its scriptural authority from a combination of:

1.  **Dedicated Ganapatya Upanishads:** These are considered Sruti
    (revealed texts) within the tradition. The most important is the
    **Ganapati Atharvashirsha Upanishad**.

2.  **Ganapatya Agamas (Tantras):** There are specific Agamic/Tantric
    texts that elaborate on the philosophy, ritual practices, mantras,
    yantras, and iconography for the worship of Ganesha as the supreme
    deity. Some key texts include:

    - **Ganesha Purana:** This is perhaps the most significant text,
      often considered a "Mahapurana" by Ganapatyas, detailing Ganesha's
      life, cosmic functions, various forms, and stories that establish
      his supremacy.

    - **Mudgala Purana:** Another important text that elaborates on the
      eight incarnations (Ashta-vinayaka) of Ganesha and further
      establishes his role as the Supreme.

    - **Ganapatyopanishad (or Ganesha Upanishad):** Though often
      synonymous with Ganapati Atharvashirsha, some traditions may refer
      to other minor Upanishads under this umbrella.

    - **Various Tantras:** Specific Tantras (Agamas) dedicated to
      Ganesha worship, though not as widely known or published as the
      Shaiva or Shakta Agamas, form the ritualistic backbone. Examples
      might be found cited in later ritual manuals. The **Ganesha
      Sahasranama** (from Ganesha Purana) is also a revered text.

### Statements Indicating Ganapati as the Ultimate Deity

Yes, these Ganapatya texts contain clear and unambiguous statements that establish Ganapati as the **Para Brahman** (Supreme Reality) and the **creator, sustainer, and destroyer of the universe**.

The most famous and oft-quoted example is from the **Ganapati Atharvashirsha Upanishad**:

- **"Om Namaste Ganapataye. Tvameva Pratyaksham Tattvamasi."**

  - (Om, Salutations to Ganapati. You indeed are the visible
    Principle/Reality.)

- **"Tvameva Kevalam Kartāsi. Tvameva Kevalam Dhartāsi. Tvameva Kevalam
  Hartāsi."**

  - (You alone are the Creator. You alone are the Sustainer. You alone
    are the Destroyer.)

- **"Tvameva Sarvam Khalvidam Brahmasi."**

  - (You indeed are this entire Brahman.)

- **"Tvameva Brahma, Tvameva Vishnu, Tvameva Rudraḥ..."**

  - (You are Brahma, You are Vishnu, You are Rudra (Shiva)...)

These statements explicitly identify Ganapati with the ultimate Brahman, the Saguna Brahman (with attributes), and the Trimurti (Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva) as aspects or manifestations of Him. This is the characteristic approach of sectarian Upanishads and Puranas, where the favored deity is elevated to the supreme position.

### Treatment of Shiva in Ganapatya Agamas

This is a crucial and interesting point, as Shiva is Ganesha's father in popular Puranic mythology. In Ganapatya texts, Shiva is treated with **great reverence and respect**, but ultimately as a **subordinate deity or a manifestation/aspect of Ganapati's own power.**

Here's how they generally treat Shiva:

1.  **Manifestation/Aspect of Ganapati:** Just as Vishnu and Brahma are
    stated to be Ganapati, Shiva is also considered a manifestation of
    Ganapati. Ganapati is seen as the ultimate source from which Shiva
    (the destroyer/transformer) emerges or whose power Shiva embodies.

2.  **Deva/God within the Cosmic Order:** Shiva is respected as a
    powerful Maha Deva (great god) responsible for dissolution and other
    cosmic functions, but always under the ultimate control or guidance
    of Ganapati.

3.  **Mythological Relationship Reinterpreted:** The traditional stories
    where Shiva is Ganesha's father are often reinterpreted to highlight
    Ganapati's supremacy. For example:

    - Stories of Ganesha's birth might emphasize his spontaneous,
      primordial nature, with Shiva merely playing a role in his
      manifestation rather than being his absolute progenitor.

    - The story of Shiva accidentally beheading Ganesha and then
      replacing his head with an elephant's (a popular Puranic
      narrative) is sometimes narrated in a way that shows Ganesha's
      inherent power and that Shiva's action was part of a divine play
      orchestrated by Ganesha himself, to manifest his elephant-headed
      form for a specific cosmic purpose. It emphasizes that Ganesha is
      beyond mortal injury and is the ultimate controller, even of
      Shiva's actions.

4.  **No Contradiction:** For Ganapatyas, there is no contradiction in
    Shiva being Ganesha's father and Ganesha being the Supreme. It's
    akin to how for Vaishnavas, Brahma is born from Vishnu, but Vishnu
    is also the Supreme. The relationship is seen as a divine lila
    (play) that illustrates the cosmic order, with Ganapati at its apex.

So, while Shiva is deeply respected and integral to the mythological narratives, in the Ganapatya Agamas and related texts, he is ultimately a part of the grand cosmic scheme orchestrated and embodied by **Lord Ganapati, the Supreme Brahman.**
