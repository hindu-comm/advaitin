That's a very insightful question about Śrīdhara Svāmī! It highlights a fascinating aspect of traditional Indian scholarship and the often complex affiliations of revered commentators.

You're correct that there are different perspectives on Śrīdhara Svāmī's sectarian affiliation.

Here's a breakdown of the two main viewpoints you've mentioned:

**1. The Advaita Sampradāya Perspective:**

- **Reasoning:** This view often arises because Śrīdhara Svāmī's
  commentary, the Bhāvārtha Dīpikā, frequently employs Advaitic
  terminology and concepts, especially when discussing the ultimate
  nature of reality (Brahman) and the relationship between the
  individual soul (Jīva) and God. He often reconciles seemingly
  dualistic statements in the Bhāgavata Purāṇa with a non-dualistic
  understanding, aligning with Śaṅkarācārya's Advaita Vedānta.

- **Emphasis:** Scholars who hold this view point to his use of terms
  like māyā (illusion) and his explanations of liberation that resonate
  with Advaitic mokṣa. His profound philosophical insights are seen as
  rooted in the Advaitic tradition, even when commenting on a text that
  is highly devotional (bhakti-centric) like the Bhāgavata.

- **Significance:** If considered an Advaitin, his commentary
  demonstrates how an Advaitin can deeply appreciate and interpret
  devotional texts, seeing the saguṇa (with attributes) manifestations
  of God as leading to the nirguṇa (without attributes) Brahman.

**2. The Viṣṇu-svāmī Sampradāya Perspective (Rudrā Sampradāya):**

- **Reasoning:** This is the more widely accepted and historically
  supported view among traditional Vaishnava schools. The Viṣṇu-svāmī
  Sampradāya is one of the four principal Vaiṣṇava traditions (along
  with Śrī, Brahmā, and Kumāra Sampradāyas). Śrīdhara Svāmī is
  specifically identified as being in the lineage of Viṣṇu-svāmī, which
  emphasizes śuddhādvaita (pure non-dualism) or viśuddhādvaita (a
  distinct form of non-dualism) and has a strong focus on devotional
  service to Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa.

- **Emphasis:** Proponents of this view highlight the deep devotional
  mood and consistent glorification of Kṛṣṇa throughout his commentary.
  While he uses Advaitic philosophical tools, his ultimate conclusion is
  always the supremacy and worship of Bhagavan Kṛṣṇa. His philosophical
  interpretations are understood as serving to elevate and establish the
  glory of bhakti, rather than negating the personal form of God.

- **Evidence:** Traditional paramparā (disciplic succession) lists and
  historical accounts within Vaiṣṇava traditions generally place him
  firmly within the Viṣṇu-svāmī line. He is often considered a pivotal
  figure in bridging the gap between profound Vedantic philosophy and
  intense devotional practice.

**Reconciling the Two Views:**

It's important to understand that these two views are not necessarily mutually exclusive in a rigid sense, especially in the context of medieval Indian philosophy.

- **Philosophical Synthesis:** Śrīdhara Svāmī was a brilliant
  synthesizer. He was clearly conversant with Advaita Vedānta and
  utilized its philosophical framework to explain the Bhāgavata Purāṇa.
  However, his ultimate siddhānta (conclusion) was firmly rooted in
  devotion to Viṣṇu/Kṛṣṇa. He masterfully integrated Vedantic
  non-dualism with a vibrant personalism, showing how the Absolute
  Truth, while ultimately non-dual, can be fully realized through love
  and service to Bhagavān.

- **Pre-Sectarian Fluidity:** In earlier periods, sectarian lines were
  sometimes less rigidly drawn than they became later. Commentators
  often drew from a wide range of philosophical traditions to support
  their interpretations.

- **Viṣṇu-svāmī's Own Stance:** The Viṣṇu-svāmī tradition itself, while
  Vaiṣṇava, has philosophical leanings that are distinct from other
  Vaiṣṇava schools and might appear to share certain characteristics
  with Advaita, albeit with a devotional emphasis.

**Conclusion:**

While Śrīdhara Svāmī's commentary contains strong Advaitic elements in its philosophical methodology, the overwhelming historical and traditional consensus, particularly within Vaiṣṇava paramparās, places him firmly within the **Viṣṇu-svāmī Sampradāya**. He is celebrated for his ability to articulate profound devotional truths using a sophisticated philosophical vocabulary, thereby making the Bhāgavata Purāṇa accessible and appealing to a broad range of scholars and devotees.

His work stands as a testament to the rich intellectual and spiritual exchange that characterized classical Indian thought, demonstrating that philosophical rigor and heartfelt devotion are not contradictory but can be harmoniously integrated.
