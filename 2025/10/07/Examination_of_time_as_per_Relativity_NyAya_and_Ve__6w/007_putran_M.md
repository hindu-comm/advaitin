> I think horse-with-hare and unicorn are both tuccha. This is because
> there is no upādhi (locus) in which these are perceived as existing.
> क्वचिदपि उपाधौ सत्त्वेन प्रतीत्यनर्हत्वम्।

  

Why I thought as I did is: A unicorn has a definable svarupa whereas the sought-after definition of horse-with-horn cannot be corresponded to a rupa/meaning because of contradiction in the terms. There is no horse-with-horn even in imagination in a world where horses and horns have their well-defined meanings.

  

Whereas the namarupa unicorn is well-defined as an object of thought, having locus in mind at least and therefore superimposable onto some upadhi-based object "outside". Like snake on rope, unicorns and big-foots are seen (imagined) onto distant unclear figures.

  



>   
>
> One can try to frame one's intuitive understanding. However, nāma-rūpa
> are avidyā-kārya.
>
>   
>
> While khaṇḍa-kāla is avidyā-kārya and is hence nāma-rūpa, the mahākāla
> is either avidyā (sābhāsa-avidyā) or avidyā-cit-sambandha. The latter
> may not fall in the category of nāma-rūpa. Rather, it is avidyā or
> avidyā-prayukta.

  

Ok, I probably hold to a variant viewpoint on this. That avidya, avidya-chit-sambandha, Ishvara, avidya-karya/Ishvara-kalpita are all in the same mithya-boat, each pointing to the other "in front". This accommodates for multiple framing for vyavaharika.
