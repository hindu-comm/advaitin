Namaskaram Bhaskar-ji,

  

You see that that is what the Valmiki Ramayana tells of his emotions etc. during these episodes. It is pretty straightforward; other Ramayanas could give alternate interpretations that make Ishvaratvam explicit during these incidents but the original kavya does not. You need to bring in logic and other assumptions if you wish to claim he did not experience grief etc. "because we know He is Vishnu avatara, therefore He cannot experience grief, dvesha etc." That is logic. And I too brought in an alternate logic (with the movie analogy) that one can have a semblance of grief (that is valid at a mind-level) and participate as-if, even while knowing the higher truth and being free in truth of the Play.

  

The whole universe is Vishnu avatara; raga-dvesha is a part of that projection. What the bhakta needs to know with regard to Rama etc. is only that He is Vishnu'savataraspecially (not a particular jiva-manifestation) for the sake of loka and dharma rakshanam and then he will interpret (as per his own needs for fixing mind in Ishvara through that avatara, as shown by different Ramayanas) accordingly His actions, His exhibitions of joy, grief, dvesha etc. in alignment to the higher purpose of the avatara. The bhakta sees Bhagavan's iccha/sankalpa in all of His avatara-leela. We need not negate Rama's joy upon seeing Bharatha saying Ishvara cannot have such raga, nor Narasimha's dvesha towards Hiranyakashipu. Some may need to do such negation whereas others can accept such expressions manifest in the avatara without losing sight of Him. He is still the same Bhagavan participating as avatara in His play.

  

thollmelukaalkizhu
