- You are absolutely right, going by just mUla rAmAyana, reading about
  extra ordinary suffering and reactions of rAma in those demanding
  situations, one can easily conclude he is just an ordinary king who
  meticulously attached to his wife but the same rAma when asked to go
  to forest for 14 years by his father just before the coronation,
  maintained exemplary tranquility and handled the situation as it
  comes. Since we have already have the firm conviction that rAma is
  the sAkshAt vishNu, we give all sorts of justifications /
  clarifications to maintain his noble stature. And one of those
  justification is poet’s license / rights to compose the kAvya in which
  he can manipulate the situation to ‘add more spice’ to the actual
  situation. Since I consider rAma as sthita prajna, I would imagine
  that rAma after knowing his wife is missing, would have responded to
  the situation, ‘OK she is missing, let us search for her with all
  sincerity as it is the duty of the husband to protect his wife’, this
  is with purely in karma yOga bhAva without any attachment to it. But
  if it is said like that it does not add any masala to that situation.
  It is because of this reason one can infer that sometimes, this
  artistic liberty allows the poets like Valmeki to exaggerate the
  situations to convey the mood of rAma with all sorts of eccentric
  emotions. And the fact remains as you rightly observed rAmAyana is a
  kAvya and kavi will have every right to bend or change the factual
  details at that particular situation for the sake of conveying the
  gravity/seriousness of that situation.
