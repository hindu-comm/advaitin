> \>Sir, tell me - how can you logically hold -- non-mention of x by
> Shankara in his bhAshya implies non-acceptance of x by Shankara.
>
>   
>
> I do not hold that above position. I only say this - If someone claims
> Sri Shankara accepted Buddha as an incarnation of Vishnu, the evidence
> needs to be presented directly from his bhAshyas.

  

Sir, you are not appreciating the basic principles of logical deduction.  
  
It is a basic tenet of logical analysisthat --- "p =\> q" is identical to "~q =\> ~p". \[For reference, please check the book Symbolic Logic by I. M. Copi which is taught in the first year of Undergraduate studies.\]

  

Now, let us suppose,

- "Non-mention of x by Shankara in his bhAshya" = p,
- "Mention of x by Shankara in his bhAshya" = ~p,
- "Non-acceptanceof x by Shankara" = q,
- "Acceptance of x by Shankara" = ~q.

I asked you a question as to how can a person with common sense hold that p =\> q. How can mere non-mention of x by Shankara in his bhAshya amount to non-acceptance of x by Shankara. And you reply -- No, I don't hold that p =\> q, instead I hold ~q =\> ~p.

  

Sir, p =\> q and ~q =\> ~p are identical statements. Just as it is absurd to propose that - if Shankara has not mentioned BuddhAvatAra in his bhAshya, then it implies that he did not accept BuddhAvatAra. Similarly, it is equally absurd to propose that - if Shankara accepted BuddhAvatAra, then it must be mentioned in his bhAshya. Both are the same statement.
