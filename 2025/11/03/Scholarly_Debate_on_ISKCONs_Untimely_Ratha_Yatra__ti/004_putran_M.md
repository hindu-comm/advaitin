5.10 Saguṇa Brahman versus Nirguṇa Brahman  
The revered scholars of the SJTA, due to their fidelity to the Advaita-siddhānta of  
Śrīpād Ādiśaṅkarācārya, may not accept our argument. The reason is that,  
according to Advaita, the form of Kṛṣṇa is still considered māyā, not transcendental.  
However, Prabhupāda’s followers in ISKCON do not accept any difference between  
Kṛṣṇa’s form and His inner self; they are absolute. Consequently, if Kṛṣṇa’s form is  
māyā, it lacks intrinsic purifying power, just as a wall-painting of a lamp cannot give  
light—it may resemble illumination, but it cannot dispel darkness. And because it  
lacks that power, then there is a corresponding overemphasis on observing vidhis  
instead of an emphasis on absorbing the mind in Kṛṣṇa, as Prabhupāda had done.  
Kṛṣṇa makes a similar point in Gītā 12.5, when he compares the impersonal and the  
personal modes of meditation. Meditation on the impersonal is inferior to meditation  
on the personal form of the Lord. This seems to be an underlying, unspoken reason  
for the disagreement regarding ISKCON’s policy on Rathayātrā dates. In other  
words, śāstric injunctions that support the saguṇa conception of Brahman give rise to  
a vikalpa, or option, that the nirguṇa conception of Brahman cannot accommodate.  
And Prabhupāda exercised that option.  
A reading of Śrīla Prabhupāda’s commentary on the Bhagavad-gītā, particularly to  
ślokas 2.12, 12.5, and especially 18.66 (sarva-dharmān parityajya), confirms that  
Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava siddhānta rejects the Advaitin conception of bhakti, considering it  
pratikūla (unfavorable) and thus to be abandoned in order for one to reach the  
transcendental platform. Śrīla Prabhupāda writes in the Preface to Bhagavad-gītā As  
It Is:  
When Kṛṣṇa says, man-manā bhava mad-bhakto mad-yājī māṁ  
namaskuru, etc., we, unlike the so-called scholars, do not say that  
Kṛṣṇa and His inner spirit are different. Kṛṣṇa is absolute, and there is  
no difference between Kṛṣṇa’s name, Kṛṣṇa’s form, Kṛṣṇa’s qualities,  
Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes, etc. . . . Such unauthorized commentary upon  
Bhagavad-gītā is known as māyāvāda-bhāṣya, and Lord Caitanya has  
warned us about these unauthorized men. Lord Caitanya clearly says  
that anyone who tries to understand Bhagavad-gītā from the Māyāvādī  
point of view will commit a great blunder. The result of such a blunder  
will be that the misguided student of Bhagavad-gītā will certainly be  
bewildered on the path of spiritual guidance and will not be able to go  
back to home, back to Godhead.  
On account of such a fundamental difference with the siddhānta of Śrī  
Adiśaṅkarācārya, whose Advaitavedānta the some difference at the level of practice  
is unavoidable. But just because there is difference does not mean that the  
differences themselves are not grounded in śāstra.
