I am grateful for the insights from the respected members. Thank you Chittaranjan ji, Putranji, Dennis ji, Ram Chandran ji and Khaaksaar ji.  
  
I have carefully gone through those verbose responses.   
Thankful to Dennis ji, for explaining in a simple way, and calling out that you do not actually have to do anything at all to ‘become’ Brahman.  
Thankful to Chittaranjan ji for explaining naiskarmya-siddhi. And the significance of shravana, manana and nidhidhyasana which constitutes the path of nivritti dharma as distinct from the path of pravritti-dharma.  
  
With due respect, may I request a closing clarification on the point. This should be my final question on this topic. Hopefully.  
  
If someone realises the truth entirely outside the formal framework of Vedantic study, would the traditional Advaitins refrain from acknowledging such a person as a jivanmukta solely because he has not studied the scriptures?  
Is it appropriate to gently question the idea that one must formally follow Vedanta in order to become a jivanmukta?  

Are there any verses outlined in Vedanta itself to caution seekers against clinging rigidly to terminology or intellectual constructs of vedanta?  
Would you agree if one attempts tomake this statement - *Vedanta primarily aids in recognizing enlightenment by offering conceptual clarity and language, and thereby the continuous practices of shravana, manana and nidhidhyasanato achieve the realization. While realization may also arise through alternative or undocumented paths - one's life’s experiences and sufferings that has naturally dissolved every trace of desire, and every thought that arises in him without ego, motive, fear, or expectation, and thereby he who knows that there is no bondage and hence no liberation is needed.*  
Does Advaita itself encourage us to remain open to such possibilities?  
  
Thank you again for your guidance.  
  
Hari Om. Namaskarams.  
Harish
