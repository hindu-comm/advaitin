ANY teaching or method that brings you to the certain knowledge of non-duality must be fine. You have to drop everything in the end anyway, since all teaching is necessarily in duality. The reason why traditional Advaita is so valuable is that it has been proven to do this in a systematic and reproducible manner since the ‘consolidation’ by Śaṅkara. The problem for today’s seeker is finding a modern traditional source. Since Śaṅkara’s time, there has been a tendency to make the teaching more ‘academic’, arguing over meanings and complicating the message, or (with satsang and neo-Advaita) attempting to shortcut and jump to the bottom line so as to (theoretically) make it possible for a seeker to make the journey more quickly. (Of course that doesn’t work!) What is really needed is teaching that derives from prasthāna traya and Śaṅkara’s bhāṣya but which is cognizant of modern society and science and does not attempt to convey material in such a way that is likely to be rejected by an intelligent mind.



I address all these issues and more in the book recently published – ‘Self Seeking: Finding a Modern Teacher of Advaita’. (See [https://www.advaita-vision.org/finding-a-teacher/](https://www.advaita-vision.org/finding-a-teacher/) for contents.)
