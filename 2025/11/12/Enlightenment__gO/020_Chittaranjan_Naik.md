Namaste Sri Harish-ji,

A seeker needs scriptures only to the extent that he/she still has doubts in his mind about the Vedantic truth. If after hearing the Vedantic truth, the seeker has no doubts in the mind, then all those scriptures - and all the arguments contained in them including the terminologies - are entirely superfluous for him. So, yes, the 'formal framework' as you call it, may not be required at all.

But strictly speaking, there is no such thing as 'outside the framework' because what the Vedantic texts deal with is the great multitude of doubts that are prone to arise in the mind along with the reasoning required to clear those doubts. The framework is not something unnatural; it constitutes a natural path that leads from a condition wherein the seeker has doubts to a condition in which the seeker's doubts are dissolved. A seeker may resort to the scriptures only to the extent that it helps him to remove his doubts and ignore the rest.

There have been many great jnanis who have attained mukti without having undertaken any formal study of the scriptures, for example, Sri Ramana Maharshi. But one must be cautious before concluding that they hadn't undertaken study of the scriptures because the journey on the path to jivanmukti is not limited to just this one life. It must be understood, in their case, that they had undertaken sadhana in many previous lives. But if your question pertains to what is observed in this current life only, then yes, depending on the ripeness that the jiva has attained in its long journey through countless lives of sadhana, it is possible that the jiva may appear, in its current life, to have reached the goal from 'outside the framework'.

As to the question whether there are any verses outlined in Vedanta itself to caution seekers against clinging rigidly to the scriptures, etc, yes there are. Here is one instance: "This Self cannot be attained through much study of the scriptures, nor through the intellect, nor through much hearing of sacred texts. It is attained by him alone whom the Self chooses. To such a one, the Self reveals Its own form". (Katha Upanishad, I,ii,23).

In the end, one must follow one’s own calling or inner voice. At the same time, one needs to avoid falling into the trap of thinking that it can be achieved by oneself because one cannot know the impurities in one’s own mind and potential to be led astray by it. External help in the form of the guidance of a guru is therefore held to be indispensible.



Warm regards,

Chittaranjan
