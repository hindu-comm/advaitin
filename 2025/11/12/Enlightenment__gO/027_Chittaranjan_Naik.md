Namaste Sri Harish-ji,

  

*Kindly let me clarify - my observation was not directed at the Vedanta texts themselves, but at the inferences, interpretations, and conclusions that have been drawn from them through various refined pedagogical traditions over the past few centuries.*

  
Could you please be a bit more specific by providing some examples of the kind of inferences, interpretations and conclusions that your observations are directed at?

  

*For instance, I strongly feel that the way Sanatana Dharma or Hinduism is often practised today by the majority, does gross injustice to the teachings of Advaita Vedanta.*

  

Yes, the way Sanathana Dharma is **practiced today by the majority** does gross injustice to (not only to the teachings of Advaita Vedanta) but to the teachings of the entirety of Sanathana Dharma itself. This is because the **majority practices have deviated from the shastras** and not because the inferences, interpretations and conclusions of the traditional pedagogical based on them have deviated from (the practices that lead to the goal of) Advaita Vedanta.

  

*I feel, these evolved pedagogies instead could have, and should have, served as a foundation for meaningful social reform in this country long ago.*

  

What you call evolved pedagogies are the pedagogies based on the eternal principles of Sanathana Dharma as they manifest in various ways that are suitable for the particular places (desha-s) and times (kala-s) that occur in the cycles of creations. The social fabric that forms around these pedagogies are aimed at **fulfilling the four purusharthas namely kama, artha, dharma and moksha** in such a way that the **pursuit of kama and artha does not become a hindrance to the attainment of moksha (the Vedantic ideal) and the pursuit of dharma provides a positive impetus towards its attainment.**

  

So, what is it that you mean when you say these pedagogies do not serve meaningful social reform? If by social reform you have in mind a reform of society that is aimed **solely at the betterment of the material condition of man** while ignoring the two-fold dharma of pravritti and nivritti (which are aligned with the purusharthas of dharma and moksha) and which form the **ground of a social order for facilitatingthe majority in society to evolve towards the attainment of the goal of Vedanta,** then such social reform, which ignores the four purusharthas, is likely to lead to a dystopian world.

  

Warm regards,

Chittaranjan
