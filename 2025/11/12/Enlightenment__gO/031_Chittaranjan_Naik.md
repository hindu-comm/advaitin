Namaste Sri Harish-ji,

  

*If Advaita could be treated as a set of independent principles - carefully and respectfully separated from the wider historical evolution of Smriti traditions and Puranas - then I have no further questions or arguments to make. I’m good to close this email thread, and am grateful for all the guidance and responses.*

  

The teaching of Advaita consists of two things: (i) The articulation of the Truth of Advaita, and (ii) the sadhana required to attain knowledge of that Truth as an '**ImmediatelyExperienced Truth*',***referred to as**aparoksha-jnana**.  

  

With regards to (i), the entire teaching is encapsulated in a few statements, known as the Mahavakhyas, such as "***Prajnanam Brahma", "Aham Brahmasimi", "Tat Tvam Asi"***, etc.

  

With regards to (ii), a sadhaka who has the four qualifications (known as sadhana chatushtayas) enumerated as **viveka, vairagya, mumukshatva and the six sampattis**is regarded as possessing the necessary qualifications for the attainment of the knowledge of Brahman irrespective of what his/her varna, jati, gender, race, status, scholarship may be. There is nothing that stands in his/her way from attaining the goal and there is no social rule or regulation that can take away his/her adhikara to attain the supreme goal of obtaining knowledge of Brahman. This is an Advaitic teaching which is accepted by the Advaita tradition and it remains free of any subsequent historical development.  

  

If you are satisfied with this answer, we can stop here.

  

If however, your question pertains to the means by which chitta-shuddhi, and the four qualifications which are consequent to the attainment of chitta-shuddhi, may be obtained, then the discussion opens up into a wider sphere.
