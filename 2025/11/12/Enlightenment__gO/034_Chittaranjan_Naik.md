Namaste Sri Dennis-ji,

  

I agree with you that the ***language of experience*** is not appropriate for describing the Advaita sadhana; it is more suited to the path of Yoga which tends to describe its attainments in terms of spiritual experiences such as the attainment of various siddhis and samadhis (such as savikalpa samadhi, nirvikalpa samadhi, etc).

  

While I agree with you also that 'Enlightenment' means *certainty in the knowledge of the truth of the Mahavakhyas*, I believe that a certain nuance in the meaning of the word 'knowledge' has to be taken into consideration whereby it may mean either **immediate knowledge,**as in the case of knowledge obtained by pratyaksha, or **mediate knowledge,** as in the case of knowledge of fire obtained by the immediate perception of smoke.

  

In the case of *mediate knowledge* there is always some obstruction that prevents the knowledge from being immediate such as, in the instance of the obtainment of inferential knowledge of fire, one's spatial location which prevents the fire from being directly perceived. When the obstruction to the perception of the fire is removed by changing one's spatial location, one obtains *immediate knowledge* of the fire. Likewise, I would say that in the case of *certain knowledge of Brahman*, when the obstruction that constrains the knowledge of Brahman to being a kind of mediate knowledge is removed, it results in the rise of spontaneous immediate knowledge of Brahman. If it should be asked what this obstruction is, I would say it is mula-avidya, and its dissolution results in knowledge of Brahman in which the distinction between all experiences is subsumed in that One Knowledge which is neither savikalpa nor nirvikalpa but what Sri Ramana Maharshi described as 'sahaja'. This is my understanding so far and I would like to hear your views on it.

  

Warm regards,

Chittaranjan
