*But you may find it interesting to note that knowledge has been defined in Nyaya as two-fold, consisting of smriti and anubhava. Anubhava is subsumed under knowledge as being one kind of knowledge. This goes against the popular view held by most people that anubhava is something separate from knowledge having the capacity, especially in the spiritual domain, to lend validity to our knowledge. According to Nyaya, anubhava is a kind of knowledge and it consists of both prama and bhrama, so it is correct knowledge, prama, that makes spiritual experience valid rather than anubhava that makes spiritual knowledge valid.*

  

And this perhaps explains why the words of a Shrotriya are held to be more reliable than the words of a Brahmanishta. For all said and done, the words of a (mere) Brahmanishta amount to little more than claims based on personal experience in contrast to those of a Shrotriya which are based on the infallible Shruti-vakhyas and arguments grounded in them.
