But you may find it interesting to note that knowledge has been defined in Nyaya as two-fold, consisting of smriti and anubhava. Anubhava is subsumed under knowledge as being one kind of knowledge. This goes against the popular view held by most people that anubhava is something separate from knowledge having the capacity, especially in the spiritual domain, to lend validity to our knowledge.



- Yes that is the view point of some schools of thought within Advaita
  tradition 😊 As per them, shruti and shruti vAkya janita jnAna is JUST
  theoretical knowledge and you have to ‘experience’ that practically in
  the state of samAdhi!! As per them, shruti is just an ‘instruction
  manual’ and it has to be put in practice in the yOga lab to get the
  literal experience of ekatvam in the state of samAdhi. Hence some
  schools equated nidhidhyAsana to dhyAna and resultant jnAna (atma
  jnAna) as samAdhi experience. Some prakaraNa grantha-s like VC, JMV,
  PD etc. vociferously endorsing and insisting this mysterious state of
  experience (anubhava) to evaluate the shruti vAkya janita jnAna. 





According to Nyaya, anubhava is a kind of knowledge and it consists of both prama and bhrama, so it is correct knowledge, prama, that makes spiritual experience valid rather than anubhava that makes spiritual knowledge valid.



Ø Individual anubhava is purusha tantra and knowledge of svarUpa is vastu tantra very clearly differentiated by bhAshyakAra in tattu samanvayaadhikaraNa.
