Dear Chitta-ji,



I think the distinction has to be made between the ‘process’ of reaching an understanding of what Advaita is saying and the actual realization that its conclusion is true.



All of what you say is reasonable from the standpoint of the teaching. And no one can deny that some sort of teaching is needed for (nearly?) all seekers. The potential problem with all teaching, however, is that the seeker can be derailed or diverted by some terms/concepts. At the very least, I think that most seekers spend a lot of time trying to understand, and/or argue about, some of the words that are used to convey the teaching. All of the arguments and ‘explanations’ have to be dropped in the end, anyway. They only have value to the extent that they help one to reach the understanding.



I believe that the idea of an entity called ‘ignorance’ is a particularly diverting one that has led to enormous amounts of material being written. Having read much of this over the past 2 - 3 years in order to write my book on ‘Confusions’ about the topic, my conclusion has to be that it is best not to think of it as a positive entity.



The reason that we do not initially understand that ‘I am Brahman’ is lack of knowledge. No one has explained this to us and used reason and arguments from PTB to convince us. That lack is remedied by the ‘correct’ teaching of Advaita. If we sometimes refer to all of this as the ‘removal of ignorance’, it should be understood only as a convenient way of talking about it, and not literally. Such an understanding would have avoided much of the wasted time and arguments of post-Śaṅkara authors, lots of it virtually incomprehensible!



Of course, I know that there has been lots of discussion on this, and there are many Sanskrit terms addressing the various nuances. I will not even attempt to consider any of this here. Nor can I be stimulated into indulging in discussion. I have noticed before that certain words trigger l-o-n-g exchanges on the group that rarely seem to reach any consensus! There are over 115,000 words on all of this in the book if anyone is interested, where I do address most of those aspects. The book is due to be published by Indica Books hopefully in the next couple of months, or it is available now in electronic form, converted from PDF, on Kindle from Amazon.



Best wishes,

Dennis
