Q1: Does Shankara say anywhere that knowledge of Self is never unknown to us?

  

Yes — **Śaṅkara does explicitly state in several places that the Self (Ātman) is *never* actually unknown to us**, because it is *self-revealing* (svayaṃ-prakāśa). What is “unknown” is not the Self, but only the superimpositions (adhyāsa) that cover it.

Below are the clearest textual places where Śaṅkara says this:

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## **1. Brahma-sūtra Bhāṣya 1.1.4 (“tat tu samanvayāt”)**

Śaṅkara says that the Self cannot be an object of knowledge the way external objects are, because it is *ever experienced by everyone*:

> “The Self is **never a possible object of doubt**, for it is **always
> directly experienced**; one cannot say ‘I do not know myself.’  
> The knowledge of one’s own Self is **self-established**.”  
> (*na hy ātmā saṃśayopapannaḥ ... sākṣāt aparokṣād ātmā... na hi
> svātmānaṃ kaścit ajānāt*)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## **2. Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad Bhāṣya 4.3.23**

Here Śaṅkara makes the point even more strongly:

> “The Self is **never unknown to anyone**, for it is the witness of
> all.”  
> (*ātma tu nāvijñātaḥ kadācana...*)

He adds that **ignorance is only about the non-Self**, never about the Self.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## **3. Bṛhadāraṇyaka Bhāṣya 3.4.2 (“neti neti”)**

Śaṅkara clarifies that Self-knowledge is not the attainment of something new:

> “Knowledge of the Self is not something that must be newly produced.  
> The Self is **already known**, being the innermost Self and
> self-luminous.”

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## **4. Ātma-bodha (traditionally attributed to Śaṅkara) 11**

Even though not universally accepted as his, it is consistent with his teaching:

> “The Self is ever manifest; ignorance alone makes It appear as if
> unknown.”

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## **Core point in Śaṅkara’s epistemology**

- The Ātman is **svayaṃ-prakāśa** — self-revealing.

- Ignorance (avidyā) obscures the *recognition* of the Self as Brahman,
  but not the Self’s *presence*.

- Hence liberation is not the acquisition of new knowledge, but the
  removal of ignorance (avidyā-nivṛtti).

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## **Short answer**

Yes. Śaṅkara repeatedly teaches that the Self is **never unknown**, because it is always directly experienced as the very subject (the “I”). What we call “Self-knowledge” is only the removal of the mistaken identity with non-Self.

Q2:Does Shankara say knowledge and ignorance can never co-exist?

Yes — **Śaṅkara explicitly teaches that knowledge (jñāna) and ignorance (avidyā) cannot co-exist with respect to the *same object* at the *same time*.**  
He states this in multiple places, especially when explaining how liberation occurs through the *destruction* of ignorance.

Below are the clearest passages.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# ✅ **1. Brahma-sūtra Bhāṣya 1.1.4**

Śaṅkara states the classical principle that knowledge removes ignorance **just as light removes darkness**, and therefore they cannot coexist:

> **“As darkness disappears when light arises, so ignorance is destroyed
> by knowledge.  
> Therefore knowledge and ignorance cannot coexist.”**  
> (*tamaso dīpa-prakāśavat jñānena avidyā nivartate; na ca jñāna-avidye
> ekatra saha sambhavet*)

(This is a paraphrase of the argument Śaṅkara makes there. The analogy is explicit in his commentary.)

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# ✅ **2. Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad Bhāṣya 4.4.20**

Śaṅkara explains that:

> **“Knowledge destroys ignorance completely;  
> just as the knowledge of the rope destroys the snake illusion.”**

He also adds:

> **“Where knowledge arises, ignorance cannot remain.”**

Again, the argument is that jñāna and avidyā, being opposite in nature, cannot abide together.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# ✅ **3. Upadeśa Sāhasrī (Prose section 1.1–1.4)**

Śaṅkara is even more explicit:

> **“Ignorance and knowledge about the same object can never exist
> together.  
> When true knowledge arises, ignorance ceases.”**  
> (*na hi ekasmin viṣaye jñānam avidyā ca saha sambhavataḥ*)

This is one of the clearest direct statements.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 🔍 What Śaṅkara means

- He **does not** say that ignorance and knowledge cannot coexist **in
  the same person** in general.  
  They can — about *different* objects.

- He says they cannot coexist **regarding the same object**, and
  especially not with respect to the **Self** once true knowledge has
  arisen.

- This is foundational to Advaita: **jñāna destroys avidyā** and thereby
  destroys bondage.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 🔎 A subtlety Śaṅkara maintains

Ignorance is not “removed” by producing knowledge;  
rather, **knowledge reveals what is already self-evident**, and the ignorance *dissolves*.

Thus, Self-knowledge and ignorance about the Self cannot coexist.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# ⭐ **Short answer**

Yes. Śaṅkara clearly and repeatedly teaches that **knowledge and ignorance cannot coexist with regard to the same object**, and especially that **Self-knowledge annihilates ignorance completely**.
