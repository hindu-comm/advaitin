Namaskaram Dennis-ji,

  

I too don't want to get into another round of big discussion over whether avidya is mithya, or Maya=avidya, etc. So evidently we are both alluding to the topics, as if tangentially. And in my case, some genuine "lack of knowledge" as well unless I find myself in the middle of a debate and have to pay attention to detail.

  

But without the technicals, I do think the phrase "lack of knowledge" is misleading, since it suggests there is no Self-knowledge in the Self and it is attained by recourse to external shastra. Whereas the better approach imo is to affirm upfront that there is nothing apart from Self, that all knowledge (Self or otherwise) is in the Self, and cognition/manifestation is a matter of removing avidya obstructing the knowledge. Avidya here is a positive entity in the sense that it typically is a different knowledge that obstructs the knowledge we are referring to. For example, I don't see the sun because of the clouds. The clouds are then the positive obstruction (that I do see) to my vision of the sun. Therefore remove the clouds.

  

In the case of Self-knowledge, a better analogy would be the all-pervading Light that illumines all objects. We are mistaken when we attribute the Light dually to the objects themselves. But such attribution I argue is also a specific knowledge, a positive removable imagination added to the generally-speaking "irremovable" world knowledge. One can know this world and still realize it as nothing but (mithya) appearance in (adhyasa on) "all-pervading" nondual Light (Sun). Or one can know it as apart from Sun, or as a duality of suns, etc. This latter knowledge is a positive ignorance that doesn't negate the Light but distorts/obstructs its proper knowledge. Remove this superimposed imagination, and realize the Sun as is - with or without the world adhyasa/cognition.
