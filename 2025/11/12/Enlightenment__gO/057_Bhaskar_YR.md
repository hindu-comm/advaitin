With regard to the rest of your post, I see that making any reply to it is likely to draw me once again into that endless debate about whether a jnani has a body and experiences prarabda karma, whether a trace of avidya in the form of avidya-lesha still afflicts him and whether he attains complete mukti only on the fall of the body, termed videhamukti, etc. As you know I had actively participated in that debate in the years 2009-2010 and I don't want to enter it again. My views on the topic remain unchanged except that I would like to clarify that by 'jnani' I had always meant a jnani who had attained perfection in naiskarmya siddhi and had attained immediate mukti here and now, i.e., a jnani who had attained sadyo mukti. I put forth this clarification since the word 'jnani' can refer to a wide spectrum that includes paroksha jnanis, asthitha jnanis as well as sthitha-prajna jnanis. For a jnani who has attained sadyo-mukti and to whom there remains neither vasanakshaya nor manonasha to be attained, there is neither prarabda nor avidya-lesha nor any body that remains to fall, all these being attributes in the eyes of others' only, as they assign to the jnani. Nor does the world disappear since there never was a world that was not Brahman in the first place, so the world remains what it always was, that is, as Brahman, entirely subsumed in Brahman. No more discussion on this topic please.
