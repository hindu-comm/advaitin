Dear Sri Dennis-ji,

  

*But I do not accept that so called ‘merely intellectual’ knowledge has to be followed by some sort of ‘experience’ to validate it.*

  

I did not say that the intellectual knowledge of Brahman has to be followed by an experience to validate it. What I said was that the intellectual knowledge of Brahman, being a kind of mediate knowledge which has not been grounded in an immediate cognition of the Truth, cannot be considered to be complete knowledge of Brahman and that it needed a direct cognition of the Truth to make it complete. That direct cognition of the Truth is not a cognition that validates the truth of the sruti vakhya but a cognition that brings the pursuit of the knowledge of Brahman to its natural completion.

  

*I already AM the Self. I have always been Brahman; I just did not appreciate this before. Once Advaita has explained what this means and I have understood, cleared any doubts and consolidated that knowledge, why should I need to do anything else?*

  

Because I find myself in the world instead of finding the world in my Self. Because I think I am standing on the earth when it is my Self that supports not only the earth but also the heavens and the starry firmament. To know Brahman, in the full import of the sense of the word 'know', is not just to appreciate what Brahman means intellectually but to BE IN IMMEDIATE COGNITION OF IT.

  

*Indeed, what could I possibly do to ‘make it real’. There is nothing else to ‘taste’.*

  
In my understanding, there is something 'to be done'. For Brahman gets to be tasted through the akhandakaravritti. As to what is to be done to bring it about, if the intellectual understanding is correct, it is nidhidhyasana. But nidhidhyasana is strictly not some thing *to be done* for it is not a kind of meditation or contemplation; it is more a kind of renunciation in which one abides as Brahman and renounces everything else so that everything extraneous to It drops off just water falls off the petals of a lotus flower. This is my understanding.

  

Warm regards,

Chittaranjan
