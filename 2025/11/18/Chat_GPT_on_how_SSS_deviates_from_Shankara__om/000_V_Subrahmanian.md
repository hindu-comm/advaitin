# **I. FOUNDATIONAL DEVIATION: THE “Ātmaikatva-vāda only” ADVOCACY**

### **SSS’s Position**

SSS repeatedly asserts that the *only* doctrine that truly belongs to Śaṅkara is **adhyāsa** and its resolution through **śuddha-śravaṇa** (direct recognition of one’s identity with Brahman), and that almost everything else found in post-Śaṅkara Advaita (pañcīkaraṇa, nirguṇa–saguṇa distinction as later understood, māyā as a “positive” entity, anirvacanīya-khyāti, superimposed causal chains, etc.) is a *misreading*.

He insists:

- **All** of Śaṅkara’s teaching is only the *removal of ignorance about
  the meaning of the mahāvākya*.

- **No** real metaphysical system exists in Śaṅkara beyond
  *adhyāsa-apavāda*.

This already marks a strong divergence, because:

### **Śaṅkara’s Own Position**

Śaṅkara’s bhāṣyas contain numerous **doctrinal structures**, not reducible to “only adhyāsa,” e.g.:

1.  **Isvara-jagat-jīva sambandha explained through māyā**

    - Brahma-sūtra bhāṣya (BSB):  
      *māyāmātram idaṃ dvaitam* (2.1.14)

    - *īśvarasya māyā-śaktiḥ* is explicitly recognised (2.1.18).

2.  **A detailed cosmology** (sṛṣṭi-prakriyā):

    - ChUp Bh. 6.2.1 begins with a clear acceptance of the cosmological
      teaching as **pramāṇa for a qualified upāsaka**.

3.  **Three levels of reality—pāramārthika, vyāvahārika, prātibhāsika**

    - Explicit in Māṇḍūkya-kārikā-bhāṣya, e.g. 2.32 commentary.

    - SSS rejects the three-tier doctrine as a “post-Śaṅkara
      development.”

Thus **SSS deviates** by *drastically minimizing* the metaphysical/theoretical components of Śaṅkara which Śaṅkara himself actually elaborated.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# **II. SSS’s REJECTION OF ŚAṄKARA’S ANIRVACANĪYA-KHYĀTI THEORY**

### **SSS’s Position**

SSS argues that Śaṅkara **never taught** “anirvacanīya-khyāti”; that this is a later invention of Vācaspati Miśra (Bhamati tradition).  
He insists that the Advaita theory is **only** “adhyāsa of the non-self on the self,” not a theory of cognition.

### **Śaṅkara’s Textual Position**

However, Śaṅkara uses the exact conceptual structure that later authors call “anirvacanīyatā”:

1.  **BSB 2.1.14**:

    - Śaṅkara says the world has **no real origination**:  
      *nāmarūpavyākaraṇam māyāmātram*

    - The perceived duality is *sad-asad-vilakṣaṇa* (neither real nor
      unreal).  
      SSS calls this a later misinterpretation, but the language is
      Śaṅkara’s.

2.  **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Bhāṣya 2.1.20**:

    - Śaṅkara states that objects in a dream are  
      *“sadasad-vilakṣaṇāḥ”* — exactly the technical concept used later
      to define **anirvacanīyatā**.

3.  **Māṇḍūkya-kārikā-bhāṣya 3.29**:

    - Śaṅkara explicitly says the world cannot be classified as either
      *sat* or *asat*.

**Evidence of deviation:**  
SSS’s insistence that Śaṅkara had *no* anirvacanīya concept contradicts multiple passages where Śaṅkara unmistakably uses “neither real nor unreal” framing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# **III. REJECTION OF ŚAṄKARA'S COSMOLOGY AS “PRĀMANIKA”**

### **SSS’s Position**

- Sṛṣṭi-śruti-s (creation passages) are **only upāsana-oriented**, never
  metaphysical.

- Śaṅkara’s bhāṣya references to sṛṣṭi-prakriyā are “mere concessions,”
  not actual doctrines.

### **Śaṅkara’s Position**

Śaṅkara explicitly holds that:

1.  Creation teaching is valid **vyāvahārika** knowledge.

    - *ChUp* 6.2.1 Bhāṣya:  
      Śaṅkara says the sṛṣṭi passage has a **real purpose** in
      establishing Brahman as the *cause* of the universe.

2.  In BSB 2.3.6, Śaṅkara argues **against Sāṅkhya** and establishes
    Brahman as the **intelligent cause**.  
    This is not merely concession—this is active argumentation.

Thus, SSS diverges: he insists that Śaṅkara had no doctrine of Brahman as *upādāna-niyamaka-kāraṇa* (material + intelligent cause), whereas Śaṅkara defends that very point elaborately.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# **IV. SSS's ASSERTION THAT ŚAṄKARA NEVER TAUGHT MĀYĀ AS A POSITIVE POWER**

### **SSS’s Position**

- According to him, *māyā* is not a śakti or capacity of Brahman.

- All references to māyā-śakti are “prakriyā-s only,” not metaphysics.

### **Śaṅkara’s Position**

Śaṅkara says:

1.  **Īśvara possesses māyā-śakti**

    - BSB 2.1.18: *īśvarasya māyā-śaktiḥ*.

2.  **Māyā is kāraṇa** for the superimposition of names and forms (ChUp
    Bh. 6.3.2).

These statements describe māyā as a **functional power**, not a mere pedagogical fiction.

SSS departs by insisting it is purely linguistic or instructional.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# **V. REDUCTION OF ŚAṄKARA’S METHOD TO “PURE ŚRAVAṆA” ALONE**

### **SSS’s Position**

- **Manana** and **nididhyāsana** are unnecessary if śravaṇa is correct.

- He repeatedly writes that nididhyāsana is only “repeated recognition,”
  not a separate discipline.

- Many sādhana-s listed in Śaṅkara are dismissed as post-Śaṅkara.

### **Śaṅkara’s Position**

Śaṅkara says explicitly:

1.  **Manana is required** for removing doubts

    - Bṛhadāraṇyaka Bhāṣya 2.4.5: *saṃśaya-bhrānti-nivṛttaye mananam
      avaśyam*.

2.  **Nididhyāsana is required** for removing habitual contrary
    tendencies

    - BSB 1.1.4: *nididhyāsanam tu nityānitya-vastu-viveka…*

3.  He prescribes **śama, dama, titikṣā** etc. as **mandatory** (BSB
    1.1.1).

SSS’s minimalism contradicts Śaṅkara’s repeated threefold instruction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# **VI. SSS’s RADICAL READING OF “ADHYĀSA BHĀṢYA” AS THE *ONLY* PHILOSOPHY**

### **SSS’s Position**

- The *Adhyāsa Bhāṣya* is the **sole** philosophical foundation of
  Advaita.

- Everything else is an application or repetition.

- He denies a metaphysical system beyond “I am not the not-self.”

### **Śaṅkara’s Position**

Śaṅkara writes numerous **metaphysical arguments** across the bhāṣyas, e.g.:

1.  **Refutation of Sāṅkhya metaphysics** (BSB 2.2.1).

2.  **Nature of jīva** (BSB 1.1.31; ChUp Bh. 6.8.7).

3.  **Brahman as the material cause** (BSB 1.4.23).

4.  **Gradation of realities** (GK Bh. 2.32; 3.18).

These are not simply “applications of the adhyāsa idea”; they are metaphysical constructions.

SSS diverges by collapsing all of Śaṅkara into one methodological introduction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# **VII. SSS’s REJECTION OF PRAJÑĀNAGHANA AS COSMIC INTELLIGENCE**

### **SSS’s Position**

- The word *prajñānaghana* denotes only the pure consciousness in the
  individual.

- He denies that Śaṅkara uses it to speak of **Īśvara as omniscient
  consciousness**.

### **Śaṅkara’s Position**

Śaṅkara repeatedly interprets *prajñānaghana* as **sarvajña-brahman**:

- BSB 1.1.1 commentary: Brahman is *prajñānaghana*, meaning all-knowing,
  all-pervading consciousness.

Thus SSS diverges by limiting the scope of this term.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# **VIII. A GENERALIZED DEVIATION: “BACK TO ŚAṄKARA” BY *REMOVING* HALF OF ŚAṄKARA**

SSS’s program is essentially:

> “Return to Śaṅkara by rejecting 80% of what Advaitins attribute to
> him.”

But many of the things he rejects (māyā-śakti, sṛṣṭi-prakriyā, three levels of reality, nature of īśvara, Brahman as material cause, necessity of nididhyāsana, etc.) are actually **directly present in Śaṅkara’s own bhāṣyas**.

Thus the *core deviation* is not that SSS adds something new, but that he *subtracts* much of Śaṅkara.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# **CONCLUSION (CLEAR STATEMENT)**

**Sri Sacchidānandendra Saraswati’s deviations from Śaṅkara primarily consist of:**

1.  **Radical minimalism**—reducing Advaita only to *adhyāsa-apavāda*
    and the mahāvākya.

2.  **Denial** of central Advaita doctrines **explicitly taught by
    Śaṅkara**:

    - anirvacanīya status of the world

    - māyā as a power of Brahman

    - Brahman as material + intelligent cause

    - three levels of reality

    - mandatory role of manana and nididhyāsana

3.  **Reinterpretation** of many Śaṅkara passages as “mere concessions”
    to upāsakas.

4.  **Exclusion** of cosmology and theology despite Śaṅkara’s careful
    use of them.

SSS’s work is brilliant, syntactically elegant, and has brought clarity to advaita pedagogy.  
**But textually, it is demonstrably narrower than Śaṅkara’s actual bhāṣya content.**

# 2) Traditional scholastic critiques of SSS — a concise (evidence-based) summary

Below I summarise the **main lines of criticism** coming from the conservative / scholastic Advaita tradition (the kind that Swami Gambhirananda represents in his translations/editions, and that Madhusūdana-centred traditions as edited by Anantakrishna Śāstri represent). I cite representative sources.

**A. “SSS discounts necessary ontological technicalities”**

- **Critique:** Traditional scholars say SSS’s reduction of Śaṅkara to
  *only* adhyāsa undervalues Śaṅkara’s explicit ontological statements.
  The traditional defense points to explicit bhāṣya statements where
  Śaṅkara uses technical categories (mithyā, avarāṇa/vikṣepa,
  māyā-śakti, līlā/upādāna roles of īśvara). See Śaṅkara’s bhāṣyas and
  the standard translations/editions by traditional publishers.
  ([Internet
  Archive](https://archive.org/stream/BrahmaSutraSankaraBhashyaEnglishTranslationVasudeoMahadeoApte1960/Brahma%2BSutra%2BSankara%2BBhashya%2BEnglish%2BTranslation%2B-%2BVasudeo%2BMahadeo%2BApte%2B1960_djvu.txt?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Full text of "Brahma Sutra Sankara Bhashya English ...")).

**B. “On avidyā: SSS collapses centuries of careful refinement”**

- **Critique:** Traditionalists (and classical commentarial lines, e.g.,
  Mādhva/Prakarana commentaries and the Advaita Siddhi tradition) argue
  that the later technical refinements about *mūla-avidyā* and
  *anirvacanīya* were developed for good doctrinal reasons (to answer
  objections re: logical consistency, causality, and scriptural
  statements). The *Advaitasiddhi* tradition (Madhusūdana) as edited by
  Ananta-Kṛṣṇa Śāstri defends these classical refinements; critics say
  SSS’s sweeping rejection leaves several objections unanswered that the
  scholastic positions were created to solve. See *Advaitasiddhi*
  critical editions (Anantakrishna Śāstri’s editions). ([Internet
  Archive](https://archive.org/details/Advaita.Siddhi.by.Madhusudana.Sarasvati?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Advaita Siddhi by Madhusudana Sarasvati with Various ...")).

**C. “Selective use of Śaṅkara passages”**

# 3) Point-by-point refutation using *only* Prasthāna-traya bhāṣya passages

Below I give the **most important SSS claims** (as you asked earlier) followed by a *direct* Prasthāna-traya bhāṣya citation / passage that contradicts that claim or shows Śaṅkara’s contrary position. For each I cite sources so you can read the original lines in context.

> position that “Śaṅkara did not teach X”.

### Claim A — *“Śaṅkara never taught anirvacanīya-khyāti (neither real nor unreal)”*

**Refutation (Prasthāna-traya evidence):**

- **Brahma-Sūtra 2.1.14 (Śaṅkara bhāṣya):** Śaṅkara explicitly treats
  the world as *mithyā* — the locus of apparent effecthood while not
  being absolutely real; see his discussion on cause/effect identity and
  the *sadasad-vilakṣaṇa* status of the world. ([Wisdom
  Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/brahma-sutras-thibaut/d/doc63822.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Brahma-sutra: II, 1, 14 [Shankara Bhashya]")).

- **Māṇḍūkya-kārikā bhāṣya (Śaṅkara):** At multiple places (e.g., 3.29
  context) Śaṅkara uses the same characterization — the world as
  apparent duality caused by māyā; he explicates the
  ‘neither-real-nor-unreal’ formula. ([Wisdom
  Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/mandukya-upanishad-karika-bhashya/d/doc143698.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Mandukya Karika, verse 3.29")).

**Bottom line:** The formal language Śaṅkara uses is the basis for the later *anirvacanīya* technicalization; to deny Śaṅkara used the concept is not supported by the bhāṣya text.

### Claim B — *“Śaṅkara does not treat māyā as any kind of power of Īśvara.”*

**Refutation (Prasthāna-traya evidence):**

- **Brahma-Sūtra bhāṣya 2.1.18 (Śaṅkara):** Śaṅkara uses the expression
  **īśvarasya māyā-śaktiḥ** and repeatedly explains māyā in terms of
  concealment and projection (avarana/viksepa) operated by Īśvara — this
  is explicit in the BSB bhāṣya and in the Upaniṣad bhāṣyas (e.g.,
  Chāndogya, Māṇḍūkya).
  ([Sandeepa](https://sandeepa.in/2022/07/11/brahma-sutras-adhyasa-or-superimposition-chapter-2-section-1-slokas-1-slokas-37/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Brahma Sutras – Chapter 2, Section 1 – Slokas 1 – Slokas 37")).

**Bottom line:** Śaṅkara’s bhāṣyas do attribute a functional power to Īśvara called māyā; SSS’s pedagogical reading is interpretive, but not the only textual reading.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Claim C — *“Avidyā is not a substantive or root-entity in Śaṅkara; Śaṅkara never posits mūla-avidyā.”*

**Refutation (Prasthāna-traya evidence):**

- **Bṛhadāraṇyaka and other Upaniṣad bhāṣyas:** Śaṅkara’s commentary
  treats avidyā/nescience as causally operative in producing
  superimposition; while Śaṅkara uses language that makes avidyā
  paradoxical (beginningless, terminable by jñāna), he nonetheless uses
  the notion in ways that later scholastics refined into a *mūla-avidyā*
  technical apparatus to solve philosophical puzzles. See Śaṅkara’s
  Bṛhadāraṇyaka bhāṣya passages on avidyā and the courses of the three
  states.
  ([Archive.org](https://ia903409.us.archive.org/19/items/MandukyaUpanishadKarikaWithShankaraBhashya-SwamiNikhilananda/MandukyaUpanishadKarikaWithBhashya-SwamiNikhilananda_text.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Mandukya Upanishad & Karika with Shankara Bhashya")).

**Bottom line:** Śaṅkara’s own language supports the later technical problems that scholastics solved; SSS’s non-substantive reading is one defensible interpretation but not the only textual one.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Claim D — *“Śaṅkara prescribes only śravaṇa; manana and nididhyāsana are optional/redundant.”*

**Refutation (Prasthāna-traya evidence):**

- **Brahma-Sūtra introduction / Bṛhadāraṇyaka passages / Gītā bhāṣya:**
  Śaṅkara explicitly expounds the śravaṇa–manana–nididhyāsana triad and
  prescribes mananam to remove doubts and nididhyāsana for steadying
  realization (see BSB 1.1.1 and related bhāṣya material). He also
  mentions the need for *śama, dama, titikṣā* (ethical disciplines) as
  prerequisites. ([Internet
  Archive](https://archive.org/stream/BrahmaSutraSankaraBhashyaEnglishTranslationVasudeoMahadeoApte1960/Brahma%2BSutra%2BSankara%2BBhashya%2BEnglish%2BTranslation%2B-%2BVasudeo%2BMahadeo%2BApte%2B1960_djvu.txt?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Full text of "Brahma Sutra Sankara Bhashya English ...")).

**Bottom line:** The threefold method is definitely present and prescribed in Śaṅkara’s bhāṣyas.

### Claim E — *“Śaṅkara had no metaphysical cosmology; any talk of Īśvara as cause is upāsana-only.”*

**Refutation (Prasthāna-traya evidence):**

- **Brahma-Sūtra (various adhikāra) and Śaṅkara’s refutation of Sāṅkhya
  / arguments for Brahman as intelligent cause:** Śaṅkara engages
  Sāṅkhya and other schools with positive metaphysical arguments (not
  mere concessions), arguing for Brahman/Īśvara as the intelligent cause
  and using sṛṣṭi passages as evidence for a theistic causal account at
  the vyāvahārika level. See BSB 2.3.\* and related bhāṣyas. ([RK
  Math](https://rkmath.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/Brahma-Sutra-Bhasya-Of-Sri-Sankaracarya_reduce.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Brahma-Sutra-Bhasya-Of-Sri-Sankaracarya_reduce.pdf")).

**Bottom line:** Śaṅkara’s bhāṣyas contain substantive cosmological/metaphysical argumentation.

# 5) Short, practical conclusion (what this all means)

- **SSS’s achievement:** He did a painstaking, methodical re-reading of
  the Advaita tradition and produced a coherent *methodological* reading
  of Śaṅkara focused on adhyāsa/apavāda and the Upaniṣadic mahāvākyas.
  His work clarified pedagogical issues and removed confusions
  introduced by later scholasticism — and it is enormously valuable for
  pedagogy and method. ([Internet
  Archive](https://archive.org/stream/the-method-of-the-vedanta-a-critical-account-of-the-advaita-tradition-swami-satchidanandendra/The%20Method%20of%20the%20Vedanta%20A%20Critical%20Account%20of%20the%20Advaita%20Tradition%20-%20Swami%20Satchidanandendra_djvu.txt?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Full text of "The Method Of The Vedanta A Critical Account ...")).

- **Where SSS departs textually from Śaṅkara:** On multiple explicit
  points (anirvacanīyatā of the world, māyā-śakti and Īśvara’s role, the
  practical necessity of manana/nididhyāsana, the status of avidyā),
  Śaṅkara’s bhāṣyas themselves contain language and structured arguments
  that SSS either reinterprets strongly or rejects. The Prasthāna-traya
  passages cited above show those disputes are textual (not merely
  speculative). ([Wisdom
  Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/brahma-sutras-thibaut/d/doc63822.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Brahma-sutra: II, 1, 14 [Shankara Bhashya]")).

- **Scholarly stance:** Many modern scholars accept that SSS’s method is
  an important corrective and re-ordering, but also note that SSS does
  not strictly *recover* a single “pure” Śaṅkara text — rather he offers
  a re-interpretation that is philosophically powerful but selective.
  (See academic articles).
