\# Analysis of Bhāmatī and Vivaraṇa Deviations from Śaṅkara's Core Philosophy

  

\## Introduction

  

The Bhāmatī and Vivaraṇa schools represent the two major post-Śaṅkara interpretative traditions of Advaita Vedānta. While both claim fidelity to Śaṅkara's Prasthāna Traya Bhāṣya (commentaries on the Upaniṣads, Brahmasūtras, and Bhagavad Gītā), they introduced distinctive philosophical formulations that arguably deviated from certain aspects of his original teachings. Understanding these deviations requires examining key doctrinal differences.

  

\## Core Areas of Deviation

  

\### 1. \*\*The Locus of Avidyā (Ignorance)\*\*

  

\*\*Śaṅkara's Position:\*\*

In the Prasthāna Traya Bhāṣya, Śaṅkara maintains deliberate ambiguity about avidyā's locus. He treats it as anirvacanīya (indefinable) and avoids definitively locating it in either Brahman or jīva. His focus is soteriological rather than systematic—avidyā is what veils reality and must be removed through knowledge.

  

\*\*Bhāmatī Deviation (Vācaspati Miśra):\*\*

\- Locates avidyā in the \*\*individual jīva\*\*

\- Each jīva has its own separate avidyā

\- This creates a problematic plurality and raises the question: how does the jīva exist prior to avidyā to serve as its locus?

\- \*\*Issue\*\*: This appears to compromise the absolute non-duality by introducing multiple loci of ignorance before the illusory distinction between jīvas is established

  

\*\*Vivaraṇa Deviation (Prakāśātman):\*\*

\- Locates avidyā in \*\*Brahman itself\*\*

\- Brahman is both the locus (āśraya) and object (viṣaya) of avidyā

\- Uses the concept of limiting adjuncts (upādhi) to explain how the limitless can appear limited

\- \*\*Issue\*\*: This appears to compromise Brahman's absolute purity and knowledge-nature by making it the substrate of ignorance, even if only from an empirical standpoint

  

\*\*Śaṅkara's Approach:\*\*

Śaṅkara deliberately avoids this scholastic question, treating it as vyāvahārika (empirical) speculation that doesn't aid liberation. His non-committal stance preserves the paradoxical nature of avidyā—it is neither real nor unreal.

  

\### 2. \*\*The Nature and Ontology of Avidyā\*\*

  

\*\*Śaṅkara's Position:\*\*

\- Avidyā is primarily \*\*epistemological\*\*—a wrong cognition (mithyājñāna)

\- It is destroyed by knowledge alone

\- Emphasis on the cognitive error aspect rather than material causation

\- Uses terms like adhyāsa (superimposition) and bhrama (error)

  

\*\*Bhāmatī Approach:\*\*

\- Develops avidyā as a \*\*positive entity\*\* (bhāvarūpa)

\- Introduces sophisticated analysis of error-cognition

\- Makes distinctions that go beyond Śaṅkara's simpler treatment

\- \*\*Deviation\*\*: Over-reification of what Śaṅkara treated more as absence of knowledge

  

\*\*Vivaraṇa Approach:\*\*

\- Treats avidyā as \*\*both positive entity and material cause\*\* (upādāna kāraṇa) of the world

\- Avidyā has two powers: āvaraṇa (veiling) and vikṣepa (projecting)

\- The entire empirical universe is a transformation (pariṇāma) of avidyā

\- \*\*Deviation\*\*: This gives avidyā a cosmic, creative role that goes beyond Śaṅkara's primarily epistemic treatment, approaching a more elaborate cosmogonic theory

  

\### 3. \*\*Doctrine of Pratibimbavāda (Reflection Theory)\*\*

  

\*\*Śaṅkara's Position:\*\*

\- Uses reflection (pratibimba) as a \*\*pedagogical metaphor\*\* occasionally

\- Not developed as a systematic doctrine

\- Employs various metaphors (space in pot, rope-snake, etc.) without privileging one

\- Flexible use of analogies based on context

  

\*\*Bhāmatī Innovation:\*\*

\- Systematizes \*\*pratibimbavāda\*\* as the primary explanation

\- The jīva is a reflection (pratibimba) of Brahman in avidyā

\- Develops elaborate theory of how reflection works

\- \*\*Deviation\*\*: Elevates a casual metaphor to doctrinal status, creating new philosophical problems about the relationship between original and reflection

  

\*\*Vivaraṇa Alternative:\*\*

\- Prefers \*\*avacchedavāda\*\* (limitation theory)

\- Brahman appears limited by adjuncts (upādhi) like space in a pot

\- Less emphasis on reflection imagery

\- \*\*Deviation\*\*: While closer to some of Śaṅkara's usage, still systematizes what was meant to be provisional teaching

  

\### 4. \*\*Liberation and Avidyā Nirvtti (Cessation of Ignorance)\*\*

  

\*\*Śaṅkara's Position:\*\*

\- Liberation (mokṣa) is the immediate result of Self-knowledge

\- Knowledge and ignorance cannot coexist (like light and darkness)

\- Emphasis on direct, immediate realization

\- Minimal discussion of post-knowledge residues

  

\*\*Bhāmatī Modification:\*\*

\- Since avidyā is located in individual jīvas, each jīva's liberation affects only that jīva

\- Introduces complications about the relationship between individual liberation and cosmic ignorance

\- \*\*Deviation\*\*: Creates logical puzzles about sequential liberation that Śaṅkara avoided

  

\*\*Vivaraṇa Modification:\*\*

\- Since avidyā is in Brahman, liberation involves the cessation of the limiting adjunct

\- Introduces the concept of \*\*jīvanmukti\*\* with greater elaboration

\- Discusses prārabdha karma (fructifying karma) continuing after knowledge

\- \*\*Deviation\*\*: Opens space for more dualistic conceptions of a "liberated person" with residual karma, which sits uneasily with pure non-duality

  

\### 5. \*\*Epistemology and Means of Knowledge\*\*

  

\*\*Śaṅkara's Position:\*\*

\- Śruti (scripture) is the only pramāṇa for Brahman

\- Direct realization follows from śravaṇa, manana, nididhyāsana

\- Simple, direct approach to valid knowledge

\- Minimal engagement with logical intricacies

  

\*\*Bhāmatī Development:\*\*

\- Extensive engagement with Nyāya logic and epistemology

\- Sophisticated analysis of cognition, error theory, and pramāṇas

\- Detailed treatment of anumāna (inference) in relation to Brahman-knowledge

\- \*\*Deviation\*\*: Over-scholasticization that may obscure the direct, intuitive realization Śaṅkara emphasized

  

\*\*Vivaraṇa Development:\*\*

\- Also develops elaborate epistemological frameworks

\- Detailed analysis of how knowledge removes ignorance

\- Technical discussions of vṛtti-vyāpti and phala-vyāpti

\- \*\*Deviation\*\*: Similar scholastic tendency that moves away from Śaṅkara's more experiential emphasis

  

\### 6. \*\*Īśvara (Personal God) and Creation\*\*

  

\*\*Śaṅkara's Position:\*\*

\- Īśvara is Brahman associated with māyā from the vyāvahārika standpoint

\- Creation is vivarta (apparent transformation), not pariṇāma (real transformation)

\- Minimal elaborate cosmology

\- Focus on the ultimate unreality of creation

  

\*\*Vivaraṇa Elaboration:\*\*

\- Since avidyā is in Brahman, develops more detailed theory of how Īśvara relates to avidyā

\- Brahman + avidyā = Īśvara

\- More systematic cosmology with avidyā as material cause

\- \*\*Deviation\*\*: Risks making creation more "real" by giving it a systematic causal account

  

\*\*Bhāmatī Position:\*\*

\- Less systematic about Īśvara's relationship to cosmic avidyā

\- Focus on individual jīva's relationship to ignorance

\- \*\*Deviation\*\*: Less problematic here but creates asymmetries in the system

  

\## Methodological Deviations

  

\### 1. \*\*Systematization vs. Apophatic Approach\*\*

  

\*\*Śaṅkara\*\*: Maintains apophatic elements—what cannot be said, what must remain paradoxical. His method is often dialectical, showing what Brahman is NOT.

  

\*\*Both Schools\*\*: Attempt comprehensive, systematic philosophical systems that answer every question. This systematization tendency itself represents a deviation from Śaṅkara's more open, pedagogically flexible approach.

  

\### 2. \*\*Scholasticism and Technical Elaboration\*\*

  

\*\*Śaṅkara\*\*: Relatively straightforward language aimed at spiritual aspirants. Technical terms used minimally and contextually.

  

\*\*Both Schools\*\*: Highly technical Sanskrit philosophical terminology, engagement with rival schools (especially Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā), elaborate logical arguments. This creates accessibility barriers and risks intellectualization of what Śaṅkara presented as experiential wisdom.

  

\### 3. \*\*Response to Objections\*\*

  

\*\*Śaṅkara\*\*: Addresses objections but maintains focus on practical liberation. Some logical puzzles left unresolved.

  

\*\*Both Schools\*\*: Feel compelled to answer every pūrvapakṣa (objection), leading to increasingly complex formulations that may obscure rather than clarify.

  

\## Fidelity and Innovation

  

\### Where They Remain Faithful:

  

1\. \*\*Core non-dualism\*\*: Both maintain Brahman as the sole reality

2\. \*\*Illusory world\*\*: Both uphold māyāvāda—the world's appearance is ultimately unreal

3\. \*\*Knowledge as liberator\*\*: Both agree knowledge alone liberates

4\. \*\*Scriptural authority\*\*: Both recognize śruti as the primary pramāṇa for Brahman

5\. \*\*Ethical teachings\*\*: Both maintain the importance of sādhana-catuṣṭaya (fourfold qualifications)

  

\### Where Innovation Becomes Deviation:

  

The crucial question is whether philosophical precision aids or hinders liberation. Śaṅkara's genius lay in his pedagogical flexibility—using whatever teaching tool suited the student's capacity. Both later schools, in their quest for systematic completeness, may have:

  

1\. \*\*Reified what was meant to be provisional\*\*: Avidyā, māyā, and jīva receive systematic treatment beyond Śaṅkara's intention

2\. \*\*Answered unanswerable questions\*\*: The locus of avidyā being a prime example

3\. \*\*Made metaphors into doctrines\*\*: Reflection and limitation theories

4\. \*\*Intellectualized immediate experience\*\*: Over-emphasis on logical coherence at the expense of direct realization

5\. \*\*Created new problems\*\*: Each solution generates new philosophical puzzles

  

\## Specific Textual Tensions

  

\### Brahmasūtra Bhāṣya Tensions:

  

\*\*On Avidyā's Locus\*\* (BSB 2.3.50): Śaṅkara's text suggests the question itself is misguided, yet both schools force an answer.

  

\*\*On the Nature of Jīva\*\* (BSB 1.3.19): Śaṅkara's teaching that jīva is Brahman with upādhi is interpreted differently—Bhāmatī emphasizes the reflection aspect, Vivaraṇa the limitation aspect, but Śaṅkara seems to use both provisionally.

  

\### Upaniṣad Bhāṣya Tensions:

  

\*\*Taittirīya Upaniṣad\*\* (on ānandamaya kośa): Both schools develop elaborate theories about the relationship between Brahman and the sheaths, but Śaṅkara's commentary is more direct about transcending all conceptualization.

  

\*\*Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad\*\* (on "neti neti"): Śaṅkara emphasizes the negative path, while later schools risk positive assertions about Brahman's nature through their systematic frameworks.

  

\## Philosophical Assessment

  

\### Arguments That These Are Genuine Deviations:

  

1\. \*\*Śaṅkara's intentional ambiguity is lost\*\*: His strategic vagueness on certain points served soteriological purposes

2\. \*\*Over-rationalization\*\*: Attempting to make Advaita "logically bulletproof" contradicts its core insight that reason has limits

3\. \*\*Reification of the vyāvahārika\*\*: Both schools give empirical categories more solidity than Śaṅkara intended

4\. \*\*Distraction from praxis\*\*: Elaborate theories may distract from the actual path of śravaṇa-manana-nididhyāsana

  

\### Arguments That These Are Legitimate Developments:

  

1\. \*\*Clarification not deviation\*\*: They clarify what was implicit in Śaṅkara

2\. \*\*Defense against rivals\*\*: Sophisticated arguments were needed to defend Advaita against Dvaita, Viśiṣṭādvaita, Buddhist, and Naiyāyika critiques

3\. \*\*Pedagogical innovation\*\*: Different students need different approaches; multiple interpretations serve diverse spiritual temperaments

4\. \*\*Natural philosophical evolution\*\*: Every living tradition develops; complete stasis would indicate death

  

\## Conclusion

  

The Bhāmatī and Vivaraṇa schools represent sophisticated philosophical developments that both preserve and transform Śaṅkara's teachings. Their primary deviations include:

  

1\. Forcing answers to questions Śaṅkara deliberately left open

2\. Systematizing what was pedagogically flexible

3\. Reifying conceptual categories that Śaṅkara treated more lightly

4\. Over-intellectualizing what was meant to point toward direct experience

  

However, these "deviations" must be understood in context. Both schools arose to defend Advaita against increasingly sophisticated philosophical attacks and to provide comprehensive frameworks for serious students. The question is whether their scholastic rigor serves or obscures the original liberative intent.

  

From a traditional Advaita standpoint, one might say: These elaborations are themselves within māyā—useful perhaps at certain levels of understanding, but ultimately to be transcended. Śaṅkara's genius was pointing directly to the moon; the later schools risk making us study the finger in ever-greater detail.

  

The ultimate test is pragmatic: Does a particular formulation aid the destruction of avidyā and the realization of one's identity with Brahman? Where the schools serve this purpose, they fulfill Śaṅkara's legacy. Where they become ends in themselves, they deviate from his core vision.
