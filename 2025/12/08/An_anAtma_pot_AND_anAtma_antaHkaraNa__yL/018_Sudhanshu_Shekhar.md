Hare Krishna Bhaskar prabhu ji.



> It becomes quite evident from the above mentioned sentences that all
> these matters like avidyA the object for avidyA and the substratum for
> avidyA are, in truth, conjured up or projected by avidyA itself.

  

This is fine. The avidyA-Ashrayatva and avidyA-vishayatva are mithyA-dharma and hence avidyA-krita. However, pot-ness, despite being mithyA-dharma, is not stated to reside in a cloth. That would be illogical. Similarly, avidyA-Ashrayatva and avidyA-vishayatva, despite being mithyA-dharma, cannot logically be stated to pertain to any anAtmA as anAtmA is ajnAna-prasUta. So, pArisheshyAt, only AtmA remains to which these mithyA-dharma can be attributed. Just as world-causehood is superposed in shuddha-chaitanya, similarly ajnAna-Ashrayatva-vishayatva is superposed in shuddha-chaitanya.



> If we observe properly although it is the absolute truth that there is
> no Ashraya whatsoever for avidyA, for the purpose of carrying out
> AtmAnAtmaviveka (intuitive deliberation between Atman and anatman)Shri
> shankara has expressed that : Because it is a pratyaya (mental
> concept) AVIDYA IS ANTAHKARANA DHARMA – A QUALITY, ATTRIBUTE OF THE
> INNER INSTRUMENT OF MIND (emphasis is mine) This statement should be
> understood to have been made adopting the view point of deliberation,
> discrimination of the work-aday transactions.

  

As explained earlier, antah-karaNa is anAtmA and is hence ajnAna-prasUta and hence cannot be avidyA-Ashraya. One needs to understand the gravity of arguments presented in NS. See for e.g. as to why anAtmA cannot be ajnAna-Ashraya "तस्य हिस्वरूपमेवाज्ञानं न हि स्वतोऽज्ञानस्याज्ञानं घटते". The very swarUpa of anAtmA is ajnAna. How can there be ajnAna of ajnAna. (There can be ajnAna of adjectives-of-ajnAna, but not of ajnAna per se.)

  

Since BhAshyakAra here talks of that ajnAna which is pratyayAtmaka, it is clear that adhyAsa is being talked about. And hence, the Ashraya of adhyAsa is correctly stated to be buddhi. But ajnAna, the cause of anAtmA (अनात्मनश्चाज्ञानप्रसूतत्त्वात्), cannot justifiably belong to any anAtmA.

**Further**

  

It was stated by you that jIva-AtmA should be taken to be the meaning of AtmA when NS (and SSSS ji) state that anAtmA cannot be Ashraya of ajnAna and only AtmA can be Ashraya of ajnAna. I had asked the counter question that by the very same logic, jIva-AtmA should be the vishaya of ajnAna also as per you, because NS states same AtmA to be Ashraya and vishaya.

  

So, as per you then, jIva is the vishaya of ajnAna. This is unknown to advaita. It is only the Brahman which is vishaya of ajnAna because it is only Brahman, knowing which, ajnAna is removed.

  

Further, if you carefully read the objection of pUrvapakshI to the claim by NS that AtmA is Ashraya/vishaya of ajnAna, then you will appreciate that shuddha-chaitanya is being talked about by the word "AtmA". I am reproducing from Klesha-apahAriNI - "आत्मनोऽपि नैवाज्ञानम् आश्रयित्वेन विषयित्वेन वा घटते । कुतः ? तस्य ( १ ) ज्ञानस्वरूपत्वात् । ज्ञानाज्ञानयोस्तमः प्रकाशयोरिव विरुद्धस्वभावयोः संसर्गानुपपत्तेरिति भावः । (२) अनन्यत्वाच्च । नह्यद्वितीयकूटस्थचैतन्यस्वभावात् अन्यत् संभवत्यज्ञानं नाम, यस्या- श्रयो विषयो वा भवेदात्मा इत्यर्थः । इतश्च । (३) ज्ञानप्रकृतित्वात् । अज्ञाननिवर्तकप्रमाणज्ञानकारणत्वात् इत्यर्थः । न हि तमोनिवर्तक- प्रकृष्टप्रकाशस्य भानोस्तमआश्रयत्वं विषयत्वं वा घटत इत्यभिप्रायः । (४) 'ज्ञानप्रकृतित्वादिभ्यश्च' इति 'आदि' शब्देन असंसर्गस्वाभाव्यादाकाशवत्, अज्ञानादिसर्वसाक्षित्वेन, स्वसाक्ष्यभूताज्ञानाश्रयत्वविषयत्वयोरनवक्लृप्तेः, कूटस्थनित्यस्वभावस्य च ज्ञानाज्ञानकृतविशेषानुपपत्तेः, अज्ञानादिसर्वानात्मव्यापकस्य चिद्रूपस्यात्मनः स्वव्याप्याज्ञानकृतावरणासंभवाच्च इत्यादयो हेतवो ग्रहीतव्याः।

अथ समाधत्ते घटत एवेति ।"

  

If you see the objection, the AtmA is described as jnAna-swarUpa, advitIya-kUTastha-chaitanya, ajnana-sAkshI, chid-rUpa. These are the descriptions of shuddha-AtmA and not jIva-AtmA. The rebuttal by SSSS ji does not say that - no, no, it is jIva-AtmA which is Ashraya (vishaya??) of ajnAna through buddhi (as you propose). So, your claim that jIva-AtmA is the meaning of the term "AtmA" in NS 3rd chapter introduction is devoid of merit.
