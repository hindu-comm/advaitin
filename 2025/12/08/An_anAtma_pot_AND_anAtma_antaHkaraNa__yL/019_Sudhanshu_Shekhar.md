> Since BhAshyakAra here talks of that ajnAna which is pratyayAtmaka, it
> is clear that adhyAsa is being talked about. And hence, the Ashraya of
> adhyAsa is correctly stated to be buddhi. But ajnAna, the cause of
> anAtmA (अनात्मनश्चाज्ञानप्रसूतत्त्वात्), cannot justifiably belong to any
> anAtmA.

  

If however one insists that it is ajnAna per se, and not adhyAsa, about which buddhi is stated as the Ashraya, then that is explained as under:

  

There is tAdAtmya-adhyAsa of buddhi (ahamkAra) with Shuddha-AtmA. ajnAna has Shuddha-AtmA as Ashraya. However, since buddhi (ahamkAra) has tAdAtmya-adhyAsa with shuddha-chaitanya, the ignorant one confuses and feels that ahamkAra (buddhi) itself is the Ashraya of ajnAna (He thinks that ahamkAra is AtmA and confuses Atma-dharma to be ahamkAra/buddhi-dharma). Doing the anuvAda of such an understanding of ignorant, it can be said that ajnAna has ahamkAra (buddhi) as Ashraya.

  

It is like this. Burning-hood belongs to fire. However, when iron ball is kept in fire, there arises tAdAtmya-adhyAsa of iron ball with fire. Then, even though fire only burns, one says -- iron ball burns. Because due to tAdAtmya-adhyAsa of iron ball with fire, the attribute of fire is stated to belong to iron ball. Similarly, due to tAdAtmya-adhyAsa of buddhi (ahmakAra) with Shuddha-AtmA, ajnAna-Ashrayatva/vishayatva appear belonging to buddhi.
