**Subject:** Re: Loka Samgraha and the Jnani (Gita 3.20)

Namaste Sudhanshu Ji,

Reflecting on what Akhilesh Ji mentioned, if we look closely at the Gita, Loka Samgraha (holding the world together/setting an example) remains relevant even for a Jnani or Sri Krishna himself.

Please consider Gita Shloka 3.20 along with Shankara Bhashya:

> **कर्मणैव हि संसिद्धिमास्थिता जनकादयः ।**  
> **लोकसङ्ग्रहमेवापि सम्पश्यन्कर्तुमर्हसि ॥ २० ॥**

Shankara provides two alternative interpretations here, covering both the realized and the seekers:

**1. If we assume Janaka etc. were realized souls (Jnanis):**  
Shankara writes: “यदि ते प्राप्तसम्यग्दर्शनाः, ततः लोकसङ्ग्रहार्थं प्रारब्धकर्मत्वात् कर्मणा सहैव असंन्यस्यैव कर्म संसिद्धिमास्थिता इत्यर्थः”  
**Meaning:** Even if they possessed right vision (samyag darshana), they remained active without renouncing action because of Prarabdha karma, purely for the sake of Loka Samgraha.

- Application: If we view Rama or Vasistha as Jnanis, they performed
  karma to set an example.

**2. If we assume they were not yet fully realized:**  
Shankara writes: “अथ अप्राप्तसम्यग्दर्शनाः जनकादयः, तदा कर्मणा सत्त्वशुद्धिसाधनभूतेन क्रमेण संसिद्धिमास्थिता इति व्याख्येयः श्लोकः”  
**Meaning:** In this view, they acted as a means for sattva shuddhi (purification of the mind) to eventually attain realization.

**Why a Jnani must act:**  
Shankara further addresses the objection: "If previous kings acted out of ignorance, why should a realized soul like me act?"  
He answers: “तथापि प्रारब्धकर्मायत्तः त्वं लोकसङ्ग्रहम् एव अपि लोकस्य उन्मार्गप्रवृत्तिनिवारणं लोकसङ्ग्रहः तमेवापि प्रयोजनं सम्पश्यन् कर्तुम् अर्हसि”  
**Meaning:** Even as a knower of Truth, one is under the momentum of Prarabdha. Furthermore, one must act to prevent the masses from going astray (unmarga pravrutti nivaranam). If a Jnani sets a wrong example by abandoning duty, it could lead society to ruin.

**The Exception (Samadhi vs. Vyavahara):**  
Of course, there is a valid nuance: When a Jnani is in Nirvikalpa Samadhi—absorbed entirely in Nirguna Brahman—there is no perception of the world, and thus no question of duty. However, once they descend from Samadhi into Vyavahara (worldly transaction), the rules of Loka Samgraha and the momentum of Prarabdha apply.
