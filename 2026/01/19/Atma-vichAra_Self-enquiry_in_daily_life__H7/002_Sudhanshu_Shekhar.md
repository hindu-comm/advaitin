Yes. In this regard, the opening lines of Hastamalakiya bhAshya are very important.

  

इह हि सर्वस्य जन्तोः सुखं मे भूयाद्दुःखं मे मा भूयात् इति स्वरसत एव सुखोपादित्सादुःखजिहासे भवतः । तत्र यः कश्चित् पुण्यातिशयशाली अवश्यंभाविदुःखाविनाभूतत्वादनित्यत्वाच्च विषयजं सुखं दुःखमेवेति ज्ञात्वा यत्नेन ससाधनात्संसारात्त्यक्तासक्तिरत्यन्तं विरज्यते । विरक्तश्च संसारहानौ यतते । संसारस्य च आत्मस्वरूपापरिज्ञानकृतत्वात् आत्मज्ञानान्निवृत्तिरिति तं प्रत्यात्मज्ञानमाचार्य उपदिशति

  

I am giving translation done by me:

  

In this world, all living beings have naturally the following desires:

  

1\. Let there be happiness for me.

2\. Let there never be misery for me.

  

Naturally everyone desires for acquisition of happiness and removal of sorrow.

  

However, some extremely virtuous person understand that happiness-generated-from-vishaya is misery only. This is so on account of the following:

  

1\. Happiness cannot arise without mandatory sorrow.

2\. Happiness is transitory.

  

Understanding sukha-born-from-vishaya also as dukha alone, he deliberately, with effort, detaches himself from samsAra and its means.

  

Detached from samsAra, he strives for removal of samsAra.

  

Since samsAra is caused by ignorance of Atma-swarUpa, samsAra cannot be removed without Atma-jnAna.

  

Hence, the teacher gives upadesha for pratyak-Atma-jnAna (knowledge of inner Self).
