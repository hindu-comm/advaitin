Namaskars,

I think our commitment in Vedanta has to be two-fold, for knowledge and for inner purification and not so much for any math. We can respect all maths, subject to our discretion {about any indiscrete acts of a guru such as Deepak Chopra who appeared in the Epstein files) because all traditional maths follow some sampradaya/achara. Achara is important because it instills discipline in a person. Vedas do not purify a person who is devoid of Vedas, it is said. Even the non-traditional maths follow some achara.

There is no need to follow one teacher only. The word bahu-shruta implies that one can hear from many. Each teacher has a unique way of presentation or clarification of concepts.

The traditional guru is compared to a gardener. Just as a gardener protects the plant growing into a tree and later when it gives fruits, he takes care to see that the fruit gets properly ripened, similarly the teacher is expected to watch the inner purification of the student. He has to watch thekama,krodha etc., levels just as a doctor watches the LDL levels and prescribes medicines. The student used to approach such a guru and stay as ante-vaasi.We do not have such students now, staying with gurus in the ashrams. Some ashrams may be teaching some selected students in this manner but most of us do not stay so. We wish to be Ekalayva type disciples. Like Ekalavya attained some perfection, we too may attain some perfection, and stay without fear of any teacher asking for our thumb.

Aravinda Rao
