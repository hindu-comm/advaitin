In creation, human birth is superior. It is only with this human  
body that one can engage in dharmic activities. Therefore, the more  
meaningfully man engages this human birth, the more beneficial it  
will be for him. For this, man must often strive to purify his mind;  
because it is only with a pure mind that man can engage in any  
noble activity. Our subsequent births are dependent on our karma.  
  
In the Bhagavad Gita, the Lord has well described the three gunas of  
the mind. It is through these gunas that man performs his karma.  
Having well understood the nature of these gunas, if the wise man  
establishes his mind in sattva guna, then action performed by him  
will not be erroneous. Results will be favourable and his well-being  
will be ensured.  
  
However, at times, results may unfold contrary to his ideals. In  
such situations, he must not be swayed by his tamo guna and react  
impulsively. With due deliberation and forethought, man must veer  
back to sattva guna and begin his work -- this is what our great  
sages advise.  
  
रजस्तमोभ्यामाक्षिप्तं विमूढं मन आत्मनः ।  
यच्छेद्धारणया धीरो हन्ति या तत्कृतं मलम्‌ ॥  
  
It means that if the wise man is pulled towards rajo or tamo guna  
(owing to situations), he must control his deluded mind by means of  
dharana (concentration). That dharana will rid man of the  
transgressions committed.  
  
Having understood this well, man must strive to keep his mind pure  
under all circumstances. This will bestow on him immeasurable  
fortune.
