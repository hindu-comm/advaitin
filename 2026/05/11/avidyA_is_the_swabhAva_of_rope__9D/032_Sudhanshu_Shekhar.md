Hare Kr̥ṣṇa Bhaskar prabhu ji.

  

Let me respond to some of the queries you posted. Prior thereto, let us revise some key statements from bhāṣya:

  

यथा रज्ज्वां **प्राक्सर्पोत्पत्तेः** रज्ज्वात्मना सर्पः सन्नेवासीत्.. (Māṇḍūkya Kārikā 1.6)

  

न हि **रज्ज्वादीनामविद्यास्वभावव्यतिरेकेण** सर्पाद्याभासत्वे कारणं शक्यं वक्तुम् ॥
(Māṇḍūkya Kārikā 1.9)

  

While the 1.6 clearly states the utpatti/birth of (illusory) snake in the rope, the 1.9 states that avidyā is the swabhāva of rope. 1.9 goes on to state that if avidyā is not accepted as swabhāva of rope, then one cannot explain the cause of appearance of snake.

  

These are statements by bhāṣya and not of later ācāryās.

Sir, this svabhāva of Brahman namely avidyā is not feature of nirupādhika-śuddha-Brahman. It is an ādhyāsika relationship. One needs to understand the ambit of the word svabhāva and to what extent bhāṣya uses it. It will be really foolish for anyone to say that avidyā is sat-chit-ānanda. So, one should appreciate that when avidyā is stated as swabhāva of Brahman, it should be understood as swa-niṣṭha-bhāva and this swa-niṣṭha is ādhyāsika i.e. avidyā-prayukta.

  

So, the crux is this -- swa-bhāva is swa-niṣṭha-bhāva and this residence of avidyā in Brahman is avidyā-prayukta.

  

As far as the word swarūpa is concerned, there are three ways in which one can appreciate it. One is, swa-niṣṭha-rūpa, other is pariṇāmī-upādāna-kāraṇa and last is vivarta-upādana-kāraṇa.

  

So, pr̥thu-budhnodara-ādi-ākāra (swa-niṣṭha-dharma) is the swarūpa of pot, clay /ajñāna ( pariṇāmī-upādāna-kāraṇa)is also swarūpa of pot and Brahman (vivarta-upādana-kāraṇa) is also swarūpa of pot.

  

In case of avidyā, there is no pariṇāmī-upādāna-kāraṇa. So, either the first or third explanation can work. So, triguṇatmakatā is swarūpa of avidyā being swa-niṣṭha-dharma. And, if one were to use the word swarūpa in the sense of vivarta-upādāna-kāraṇa, then since avidyā is adhyasta in śuddha Brahman, it can be called as the swarūpa of avidyā. I don't see any harm.

  

So, the crux is this -- how one is defining swa-bhava or swa-rūpa. Accordingly, one can understand.
