In the same text, I randomly opened to page 347. Note from this passage:

  

"Just as in a painted canvas,

four states may be discerned as it were, the bleached state, the paste-stiffened, sketched with lines and finally coloured, even so four states in the Supreme Self~-Pure Consciousness by Itself, Antaramn as in association with  
Maya, Sutratma with the adjunct of the subtle universe, and Vrat with the adjunct of the manifested gross universe All living beings from Brahma to the tiniest plant and even the inanimate things are in Vrat like the painted figures on the canvas "

  

Why should the shastra teach us of Virat, Hiranyagarbha, Ishvara? Why not just posit the nondual adhishtanam and point out the ishvara-jiva-jagat duality that we experience as nothing but adhyasa? The latter is true of course but the shastra allows for an added dualistic dimension linking the dual of our experience to the non-dual of Brahman, resulting in the singularization of Brahman as the One who appears as the All. Whether we consider this as adhyaropa, or as a necessary logical extension to our adhyasa once we learn of Brahman, we are beckoned to singularize our vision, to see the all in the One. To center all material awareness as ("the body of") Virat; or if we sublate from gross to the subtle (mind-level), then also not to let the duality of thoughts and minds remain but see it all as (projection of) the singular Hiranyagarbha; finally to sublate the subtle in the causal Maya that also is to be understood not ambiguously but as the adjunct-shakti of Ishvara. It is He who projects, appears and plays this Play - however we posit the duality, it reduces to Him. The non-duality of Self is being emphasized.

  

This singularization step is not obvious nor perhaps natural to us; and may not even be necessary for everyone to acknowledge. (And let's admit: quitedifficult to practice consistently.) However the viewpoint also cannot be negated as "unsampradayic". Apart from the duality of ishvara-jiva-jagat and the pure non-duality of Nirguna Brahmana, the shastra allows or guides us to envision the All in the One (Ishvara) and that One (antaryamin) in All as an intermediary step to realizing Brahman.
