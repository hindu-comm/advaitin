Namskaram Putran Ji,

Reg // Why not just posit the nondual adhishtanam and point out the ishvara-jiva-jagat duality that we experience as nothing but adhyasa? //,

And

// This singularization step is not obvious nor perhaps natural to us; and may not even be necessary for everyone to acknowledge. (And let's admit: quitedifficult to practice consistently.) However the viewpoint also cannot be negated as "unsampradayic" //.

I am not sure if your observations are meant to be understood as rhetorical or as as questions needing response. I do realize that my response herein will invite the usual quota of debate. But just to clarify my own understanding, and to revive some sort of debate which seems to be missing these days, I am responding.

\*\*as nothing but adhyasa\*\* seems to be very simplistic and missing the essential idea intended by Sri Bhagavatpada. It seems to imply that the two entities involved in adhyAsa are not related but \*just\* two entities, one mistaken for the other. That, if correct, I think is a very serious deviation from what Sri Bhagavatpada intended. Just so that I may not be understood as just presenting a view without any backup from the Bhashya, I am presenting just one from BUB 3-5-1

BUB 3-5-1 // शोकं मोहम् — शोक इति कामः ; इष्टं वस्तु उद्दिश्य चिन्तयतो यत् अरमणम् , तत् तृष्णाभिभूतस्य कामबीजम् ; तेन हि कामो दीप्यते ; मोहस्तु विपरीतप्रत्ययप्रभवोऽविवेकः भ्रमः ; स च अविद्या सर्वस्यानर्थस्य प्रसवबीजम् ;//

// shokaM moham — shoka iti kAmaH ; iShTaM vastu uddishya chintayato yat aramaNam , tat tRRiShNAbhibhUtasya kAmabIjam ; tena hi kAmo dIpyate ; mohastu viparItapratyayaprabhavo.avivekaH bhramaH ; sa cha avidyA sarvasyAnarthasya prasavabIjam ; //.

Reg // However the viewpoint also cannot be negated as "unsampradayic" //, There are I am afraid such views quite prevalent, but in my view they are \*unsampradayic\* because it represents a fundamental deviation from what is intended by Sri Bhagavatpada and strikes at the very basis of His interpretation of the Shrutis.

I leave it at this because I am not sure if I am understanding your observation correctly. Maybe further comments and views from others could clarify.

Regards
