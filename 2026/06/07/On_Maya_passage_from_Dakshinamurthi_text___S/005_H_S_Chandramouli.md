Namaskaram Putran Ji,

Reg // But seeing Brahman in the relational lens (as the singular Knower, Ishvara, etc.) is part of sampradaya imo //,

Certainly yes. That is in the sAdhana part. But to see it so requires admission of some reality/existentiality to the adhyAropa which leads to the universal,intuitive,instinctive sense of absolute Reality to all our experiences. This I am afraid does not seem to be the case with many interpretations of the Siddhanta doing the rounds. They imply there is no creation at all in the first place. Even if it appears , it is just \*imagination\*. That I think is unsampradayic. Where does this sense of reality/existentiality for Creation derived from. It is derived through the adyAsika tAdAtmya with Atman which is part of the definition of adhyAsa as per Sri Bhagavatpada.

Reg // there is the valid "connecting" step shown by shastra that singularizes all the dualistic cognitions in the nondual Self, i.e. we reduce to Ishvara (who yet is not paramarthika nirguna satya) //,

What in your view is the difference between \*nondual Self\* and \* paramarthika nirguna satya \* which the shastra shows up through a \*connecting\* step?. Singularizing all the dualistic cognitions in the nondual Self (who yet is not paramarthika nirguna satya) is parOksha jnAna. This is the state of the particular jIva which has reached that stage. But the jIva at that stage has still not Realized , Anubhava, in my view, that it is nondifferent from \* paramarthika nirguna satya\*. That is the state of aparOksha jnAna. And this is the ultimate status because at this stage the AdhyAsika tAdAtmya referred above is destroyed. This AdhyAsika tAdatmya between viparItapratyaya and Atman is termed mOha (Bhranti) in the Bhashya BUB 3-5-1 which I had cited earlier. Also termed avidyA therein. I am tempted to understand your terminology for mOha \* **non-discrimination (aviveka)\* as AdhyAsika tAdAtmya.**

My post may appear to be rambling. My apologies. The topic is so. Yet if I have conveyed some sense, I am happy. The primary intention is to convey that adhyAsa as defined by Sri Bhagavatpada in the AdhyAsa BhAshya involves AdhyAsika tAdAtmya between the adhishthAna and adhyAropa.

Regards
