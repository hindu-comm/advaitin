---

  

The question of whether the knowledge of Ishvara is paroksha or not, I think, is tied to the question of whether He is vyavaharika sathya or simply adhyaropa. In my view, Ishvara may be adhyaropa for us and our sadhana may involve His singular contemplation in all cognitions. However, for the jnani, He/I is thesathya of vyavaharika, the correct way of knowing Brahman when also referring to the duality of cognitions. It is an if-and-only-if, in mathematicalterms. So, vyavaharika context means Ishvara is the corresponding knowledge of Brahman. The jnani therefore when referring to Brahman in this context will speak of Ishvara, not as a concession for the ignorant but simply because Brahman is known and to be known as Ishvara in vyavaharika. So, imv, aparoksha jnana of nirguna Brahman translates in the vyavaharika standpoint as aparoksha jnana of Ishvara.

  

---

  

When I see multiple cognitions of the chair in my room, I realize the "same chair" in all the cognitions of that chair. That is an analogy, for similarly the jnani realizes Ishvara ("his own Self") in all cognitions (table, chair, everything). We have to say "Ishvara" here because the discussion is juxtaposing a duality of cognitions against the knowledge of their nondual Self. Such a 2-dimensional knowledge (Knower-known) of Self is Ishvara - the singular Knower/antaryamin who knows/projects Himself as the known.

  

And therein is the relation between creation and Creator.

  

MK 2.12: "Ātman,*the self-luminous*,*through the power of his own*Māyā,*imagines in himself by himself (all the objects that the subject experiences within or without*).*He alone is the cognizer of the objects (so created*).*This is the decision of the Vedānta.*"

  

This is aparoksha jnana of Self that Gaudapada is expressing in vyavaharika context. Since sampradaya accepts MK, I assume your understanding of adhyasika tadatmya and the idea of "imagination" that you consider "unsampradayic" are not in contradiction with MK 2.12 and its reference to "imagine". In my case, I am pretty much trying to say what this verse says but imo this Vedantic conclusion is with regard to the (final) vyavaharika sathya, the singular realization of the Atman in the context of a duality of cognitions "of Him, by Him".

  

Because Ishvara is a 2-dimensional knowledge of Self, juxtaposing atma and anatma, Brahman and maya, Knower and known (or the Knower and His variegated knowledge of Himself), Ishvara is at best the right-pointing knowledge of Self that is "decision of Vedanta" *for the vyavaharika standpoint*. It points out the non-duality in the sense that Ishvara alone is the Self for all dualistic cognitions, and it points out the nirgunatvambecause all guna-based duality is His imagination,i.e. the reality is neti, neti. (That is the extent of what I meant by "connecting") But Ishvara (the Self as knowable in vyavaharika) is neither absolutely nondual nor nirguna: He (Knower) is associated with the known through the adjunct of His own shakti (knowing). So ultimately Ishvara (along with all creation) is adhyasa on the Reality (nirguna) Brahman. (Technical analysis here leads to mithya etc.)
