Namaskaram Putran Ji,

TAdAtmya in general means \*identity\*. Instead of concentrating on formal definitions, we can concentrate on understanding it, especially in the context of the Bhashya. When we see a red hot iron ball, we cannot distinguish between the object iron sphere and the Fire Principle as two distinct separate categories or entities. The two can only be perceived or experienced together as a single entity. The relationship between the two is one of tAdAtmya. As per advaita siddhanta, there is tAdAtmya relationship between an object and its quality. For example the relationship between a flower (vastu) and its red colour (quality) is one of tAdAtmya. The two can never be perceived independent of each other. AdhyAsika means the two entities involved are of different levels of Reality. Indeed there are different types of adhyAsa as relevant to different situations.

Reg // So, imv, aparoksha jnana of nirguna Brahman translates in the vyavaharika standpoint as aparoksha jnana of Ishvara //,

I am not sure if I have understood you correctly. In Advaita Siddhanta, knowledge of the nature \*\* This is Iswara\*\* is parOksha jnAna. Knowledge of the nature \*\* I am Iswara/Brahman/Atman\*\* is aparOksha jnAna. So paroksha or aparOkasha jnAna a jnAni is referring to when he speaks of Brahman or Iswara depends upon how he is addressing the issue.

Reg // The question of whether the knowledge of Ishvara is paroksha or not, I think, is tied to the question of whether He is vyavaharika sathya or simply adhyaropa //,

I am not sure again how you differentiate between vyavaharika sathya and adhyaropa. Whatever you are talking of as vyavaharika sathya is adhyaropa only. There is no difference between the two.
