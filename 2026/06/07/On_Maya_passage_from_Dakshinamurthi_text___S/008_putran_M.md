Namaskaram Chandramouli-ji,

  

Yes, your explanation of tadatmya is very good to understand. The adhyasika and adhyasika-tadatmya components are not as clear because you only briefly touch on the former at the end. But from your earlier post, it is clear that adhyasika-tadatmya is a case of ignorance that gets destroyed with aparoksha jnana of Self.

  

Also I understand the paroksha jnana of Ishvara that you are referring to is the knowledge of ishvara in my ishvara-jiva-jagat framework, where "This (not me) is ishvara." And you have stated the aparoksha jnana is of the nature "I am Ishvara." - the Self *of all*. ayam atma brahma.

  

"Seeing the universe contained within Himself, like a city seen in a mirror ..."

  

This Self as known or expressed here is Ishvara because He/I is realized as the Self *of all*. The duality of cognitions is posited and sublated in Him, of Him, by Him - hence He is the sathya of all. And the standpoint where this duality remains a point of reference is vyavaharika - hence He is the vyavaharika sathya.
