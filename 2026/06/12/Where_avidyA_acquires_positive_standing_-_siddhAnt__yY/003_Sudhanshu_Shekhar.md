Namaste Michael ji.

> 

  

> What §55 establishes by pramāṇa is the viśeṣaṇa-set: bhāvarūpatva,
> anāditva, jñāna-nivartyatva.

  

Pramāṇa establishes avidyā-delimited-by-bhāvarūpatva/anāditva/jñāna-nivartyatva. This is the precise statement.

  

Sākṣī establishes avidyā-delimited-by-saviṣayakatva/avidyā-tva/jñāna-virodhitva.

  

> That avidyā must carry these positive qualifiers, and that a
> dharmi-grāhaka-vyāpāra is required to establish them, is what I am
> pointing at?

  

> The real question is why establish qualifiers at all?

  

See. There can be no difference of opinion on something which is anubhava-siddha. That is why both you and I have unanimity of opinion on our experience of avidyā.

  

But something which is pramāṇa-siddha, there can be a difference of opinion thereupon. For e.g. you may say that hill has fire because it has smoke. But I can say that there is no fire on hill because it is not smoke but dust. This is because fire of bill is pramāṇa-siddha.

  

So, when you say -- I am ignorant -- I accept. Because it is sākṣi-siddha. But when you say that this avidyā is abhāva, then I dispute. Because abhāva-tva of avidyā is not sākṣi-siddha.

  

That is why the qualifiers are required to be investigated.

  

Please note. Bhāvarūpa doesn't mean positive. It means neither bhāva nor abhāva. This I have said many times and showed evidence therefor. But it has been ignored in your responses, which is strange.

  

> My thesis is that on SSS's reading of the Adhyāsa-Bhāṣya, avidyā is
> simply adhyāsa — the lived self/not-self confusion, evident to
> everyone — and nothing more is said of it.

  

One cannot run away like this. SSSS ji has claimed that this avidyā is abhāva. So, he has to adduce pramāṇa for the abhāva-tva of avidyā. He cannot repeat anubhava-siddha, because abhāva-tva of avidyā is not anubhava-siddha.

  

> Two things force the qualifiers here. First, the word is *siddhānta*,
> not *prakriyā* — SB owns DSV/EJV as its settled conclusion, so
> whatever that conclusion requires is doctrinal, not a dispensable
> teaching-device. Second, and decisively: *jīva … svājñāna-vaśāj
> jagad-upādānam* — the jīva's own ignorance is made the *material
> cause* of the world.

Jīva is defined variously in eka-jīva-vāda. One such explanation is given in SB. There are several nuances to it, which is not the subject of this discussion.

> That single assignment forces the set, because a material cause cannot
> be an absence — nothing is made of an abhāva. SB says exactly this at
> §57:

This is true. "Ignorance is material cause" is the settled conclusion of Advaita. And due to adhyāropa, the property of ignorance is superimposed on inactive Brahman and Brahman is taughut as material cause by Śāstra. Inactive Brahman just cannot be material cause. This is where adhyāropa comes in picture as a method of teaching which is later negated by apavāda.

  

> Read §57 as the direct answer to "why": *bhāvarūpatva* is established
> *from* upādānatva. Because avidyā has to be the upādāna, it cannot be
> abhāva;

  

This is fine.

  

> therefore it is positive;

  

*This is your plain error. And this is also the error by SSSS ji.* How on earth you hold non-abhāva as bhāva sir?

  

That avidyā is material cause only proves that it is non-abhāva. Period. You cannot jump to say that it is bhāva.

  

It is neither bhāva nor abhāva.

  

> and being positive is not given in the witnessed *na jānāmi*, so it
> has to be argued — that is the pramāṇa-vyāpāra.

  

Useless.

  

> The other qualifiers ride the same logic: *jñāna-nivartyatva*, because
> the material cause of bondage-appearance must be the sort of thing
> knowledge can end, else no mokṣa (the *anirmokṣa* worry §54 already
> raises); *anāditva*, because the substrate of beginningless saṃsāra
> cannot itself have an origin without regress. Each qualifier is what
> avidyā must satisfy to hold the upādāna-role the siddhānta hands it.

  

Sir, upādāna-tva only proves non-abhāva-tva of avidyā.

  

The anumāna "vināśī bhāvaḥ sādiḥ, ghaṭavat", proves that avidyā has to be non-bhāva as well.

  

So, avidyā is neither bhāva nor abhāva. This being neither bhāva nor abhāva is called bhāvarūpa.

  

> For AB as SSS reads it, this error called adhyasa is the sole avidya
> and it requires no causal explanation as it is anadi and naisargaika.

  

Sir, what is error? One needs to explain that. Is it a thought? If yes, then it is a product of mind. If so, mind cannot be adhyāsa because mind itself cannot be its own product. However, mind is admitted in Advaita to be adhyāsa. 🙂

  

SSSS ji held avidyā to be abhāva. And equated it to adhyāsa, which is bhāva. 🙂

  

Does SSSS Ji's avidyā have naisargika-tva or not? If yes, then tell me, is naisargika-tva bhāva or abhāva? If naisargika-tva is bhāva, then how it can be in your avidyā which is abhāva. And if naisargika-tva is abhāva, then to proclaim avidyā as naisargika is akin to saying barren woman's son has horns of hare in his hands. Plain useless stuff.

  

SSSS Ji's understanding stands no chance against any logical analysis.
