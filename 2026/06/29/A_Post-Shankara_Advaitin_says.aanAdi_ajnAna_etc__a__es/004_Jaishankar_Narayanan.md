Namaste,

  

You claim

  

Śaṅkara's avidyā is neither a bare abhāva nor a bhāva-padārtha; it is atasmiṃs tad-buddhiḥ

  

That is the definition of bhAvarupa which you have given, which is the same for moolAvidya as for viparyaya jnAna.

  

Further you are talking about bijAnkura nyAya which is appropriate only if the bIja is moolAvidya. The bIja cannot be abhAva in which case you are closer to Buddhists than Shankara who clearly talks about a bIja which is bhAvarupa and which needs to be burned by the fire of knowledge.

  

I think enough has been said about these topics.

  

With love and prayers,

Jaishankar
