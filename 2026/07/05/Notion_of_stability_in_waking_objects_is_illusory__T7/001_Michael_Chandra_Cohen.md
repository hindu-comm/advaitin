Śaṅkara grounds unreality in *neither* horn as you've posed them.

Objects are not unreal *because* they are momentary — Śaṅkara is the tradition's most relentless opponent of *kṣaṇikatva* — nor does the false ascription of permanence *constitute* their unreality. The mark of the unreal in the prasthānatraya-bhāṣya is *bādhyatva*, and more precisely *vyabhicāra*: deviation from the form in which a thing was ascertained (Taittirīya-bhāṣya 2.1: *yad-rūpeṇa yan niścitaṃ tad-rūpaṃ na vyabhicarati tat satyam … vyabhicarati tad anṛtam*). The real is the *non-deviating*; the object is *nāma-rūpa-vikāra* superimposed on *sat* (Chāndogya-bhāṣya 6.1.4: the *vikāra* is *vācārambhaṇa*, mere speech-handle — *mṛttikety eva satyam*). Unreality is *anātmatva / adhyastatva*, not brevity.

So where does transience sit? As a *liṅga*, not the *definiens*. In the Gītā-bhāṣya Śaṅkara does run the come-and-go argument — the *dvandvas* are *āgamāpāyino 'nityāḥ* (2.14), and *nāsato vidyate bhāvaḥ* (2.16) is unpacked through the constancy of the *"asti, asti"* cognition that abides while the objects it illumines lapse. But note what does the work: not that objects flicker, but that *sattā* is the one term that never deviates while forms deviate through it. Transience is symptomatic of *vikāratva*, and *vikāratva* — becoming-other — is the disqualification. This is emphatically not the Sautrāntika point-instant, which Śaṅkara dismantles at BSB 2.2.28–31 precisely because *pratyabhijñā* refutes it: a momentary object cannot even be *known* to be momentary.

Now the more interesting half, and where I'd resist the Vicāra Sāgara framing. The *perceived permanence* is not the falsity's locus; it is the *satya-aṃśa* mislocated. What lends an object its felt "is-ness" and continuity is the borrowed *sattā* of Brahman — *adhyāsa* being *atasmiṃs tad-buddhiḥ*, the mutual transfer of *ātmānātma-dharmas*. The permanence you detect in the dream-mountain is the *nityatva* of the *draṣṭṛ* — the very *avināśitva* of BU 4.3.23 you were just reading — misattributed to the *dṛśya*. The error is not that permanence is *perceived* but that it is *mislocated*: read onto the perishable name-form instead of recognized as the abiding *sat*. Strip the mislocation and you do not arrive at flux; you arrive at *kūṭastha* being.

That is the caveat against Niścaladāsa's move, congenial as it is. Making the object *tat-kṣaṇotpanna* and *cirantanatva* a *separate anirvacanīya* superimposition (note the doubled *anirvacanīyatayotpādyate*) foregrounds *cirantanatva-adhyāsa* as the salient falsity and leans on a dream-waking *momentariness*-parity harder than Śaṅkara's own hand allows. Śaṅkara guards the asymmetry: *vaidharmyāc ca na svapnādivat* (BSB 2.2.29) — waking is *not* dream, because the dream is sublated *within* vyavahāra by waking, whereas waking yields only to *jñāna*. And Ramana's river-and-flame, taken at the letter, makes *flux* the fact and *stability* the error — which quietly inverts the *mukhya siddhānta*, where *nitya-sat* is the truth and the deviating forms are the unreal. As *upāya* it is unimpeachable; as ontology it drifts toward the very *kṣaṇikavāda* Śaṅkara spent BSB 2.2 refuting. The felt permanence is not an error to be dissolved into flux; it is Brahman's own *avināśitva* awaiting relocation.

Sharpest form: for Śaṅkara, objects are *mithyā* as *bādhya nāma-rūpa* upon *sat*. Transitoriness is a *symptom* of that (Gītā-bhāṣya register), and perceived permanence is a *misplaced trace of the real*, not the ground of unreality. The VS/Ramana emphasis — permanence-as-the-locus-of-falsity — is a later, DSV-anirvacanīya recentring: not false to Śaṅkara, but not his ordering, and it carries the standing risk of terminating in flux rather than in the *kūṭastha* the *draṣṭṛ*-vākyas were framed to secure.
