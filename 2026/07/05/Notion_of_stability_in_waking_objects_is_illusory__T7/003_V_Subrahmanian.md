Dear Bhaskar ji,

  

What you say about Ishwara's unique power is not denied and Shankara's great regard for that power. However, Shankara does not differentiate the Ishwara-created world from the jiva-created dream:

  

BSB 3/2/4 सूचकश्च हि श्रुतेराचक्षते च तद्विदः ॥ ४ ॥ In the swapna adhikarana: where the earlier sutra is: मायामात्रं तु कार्त्स्न्येनानभिव्यक्तस्वरूपत्वात् ॥ ३ ॥

  
  
पारमार्थिकस्तु नायं सन्ध्याश्रयः सर्गःवियदादिसर्गवत्— इत्येतावत्प्रतिपाद्यते। न चवियदादिसर्गस्याप्यात्यन्तिकंसत्यत्वमस्ति। प्रतिपादितं हि[‘तदनन्यत्वमारम्भणशब्दादिभ्यः’ (ब्र. सू. २। १। १४)](https://advaitasharada.sringeri.net/display/bhashya/BS/devanagari?page=2&id=BS_C02_S01_V14&hl=%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%A6%E0%A4%A8%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B5%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AD%E0%A4%A3%E0%A4%B6%E0%A4%AC%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A6%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%A6%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%AD%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%83)इत्यत्र **समस्तस्य प्रपञ्चस्य मायामात्रत्वम्।** प्राक् तु ब्रह्मात्मत्वदर्शनात्वियदादिप्रपञ्चोव्यवस्थितरूपो भवति। सन्ध्याश्रयस्तु प्रपञ्चः प्रतिदिनं बाध्यते — इत्यतो वैशेषिकमिदं सन्ध्यस्य मायामात्रत्वमुदितम् ॥ ४ ॥

  

**(On the unreality of the dream-creation)**

"But this creation that has the dream-state (sandhyā) as its locus is not, in the ultimate (pāramārthika) sense, a real creation like the creation beginning with space (ākāśa)." This alone is what is intended to be established here.

**Nor, indeed, does even the creation beginning with space possess absolute reality**. For it has already been established in the aphorism **'tad-ananyatvam ārambhaṇa-śabdādibhyaḥ'** (Brahma Sūtra 2.1.14) **that the entire phenomenal universe is nothing but māyā.**

However, prior to the realization that Brahman is the Self, the empirical universe beginning with space appears as a well-established and orderly reality. The dream-world, on the other hand, is sublated every single day upon waking. Therefore, it is only in this special sense that the dream has been declared to be merely an appearance produced by māyā.

And Shankara says in the BGB 2.16:

**(Verse 16 Commentary)**

The intention is this: You too, Arjuna, **adopting the standpoint of the knowers of Truth (tattva-darśins),** should abandon grief and delusion. Recognizing firmly in your mind that **the pairs of opposites**—such as heat and cold—which are of fixed or occasional occurrence, are merely modifications, t**hat do not really exist, but falsely appear like the water seen in a mirage (*marīci-jala*), should cultivate forbearance (*titikṣā*).**

So, Shankara calls the Ishwara-created world māyāmātram. And says, 'It does not really exist but appears to exist.'

These are just two examples of many where Shankara holds the Ishwara-created world to be a mere appearance like a dream or a rope-snake.
