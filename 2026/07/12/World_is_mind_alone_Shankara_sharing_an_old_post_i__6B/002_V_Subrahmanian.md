Dear Michael ji,

  

I don't see Claude here making any significant point. The Mundaka Bhashya is very emphatic: dvaita is a product of the mind. Also, even though the mantra concerns the cosmic person, Shankara's invocation of waking and sleep to explain the appearance and disappearance of dvaita—thereby conveying that dvaita exists only when the mind is active and not when the mind is inactive in sleep, is very important. This is because the cosmic person does not experience waking or sleep as we do.We are not familiar with it. On theother hand, Shankara is talking about the individual jiva's experience. This is a common Advaita method: the waking-sleep anvaya vyatireka logic is used to deny the reality of dvaita.

  

In the Mandukya, Shankara, following Gaudapada, denies reality to the objects experienced in the waking state by drawing a similarity with dreams. This is too well known.
