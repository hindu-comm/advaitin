Namaste Subbuji,  
  
***Claude's and my response***:

> Namaste, and thank you — the point about the vyatireka being the
> jīva's is well taken, and on reflection it points somewhere we may
> both find congenial. Notice the register of the whole prakaraṇa.
> Muṇḍaka 2.1 is unfolding the viśvarūpa: fire as the head, sun and moon
> as eyes, the quarters as ears, the Vedas as speech, earth from the
> feet — the cosmic person with the three lokas for body, held up for
> sarvātma-upāsana. *Hṛdayaṃ viśvam asya* stands in that series, and
> Śaṅkara's gloss on it does not end where the quotation is usually cut.
> The sentence runs on: this deva, all-pervading, the antarātman of all
> beings, is the one who in every being is *draṣṭā śrotā mantā vijñātā*,
> and who is *sarva-kāraṇam*. The subject the bhāṣya is driving toward
> is the antaryāmin, not the manas.
>
> Read whole, then, the antaḥkaraṇa-vikāra clause is a subordinate step:
> it explains *why* the mantra can call the cosmic person's heart the
> universe — the seen has no differentiated standing apart from the
> inner field — on the way to identifying that whole with the ātman who
> is its seer and its cause. The mind here is spoken of much as the
> "ears" and "feet" are: within the mantra's own body-imagery, gauṇatas.
> To lift this one clause out of a saguṇa upāsana and read it as a
> nirguṇa siddhānta — the mind as jagad-upādāna — is, I'd suggest, to
> change its genre. And your own observation supports this: precisely
> because the cosmic person does not wake and sleep, the waking–sleep
> vyatireka must be the meditating jīva's — which is just what an
> upāsana borrows, the familiar to lead toward sarvātma-bhāva. That the
> seen subsides in suṣupti is invoked to turn the upāsaka toward the
> antarātman, not to make the manas the being of the world; and the
> passage names that antarātman, not the mind, as *sarva-kāraṇam*.
>
> On the Māṇḍūkya: there too the burden is *na nirodho na cotpattiḥ*,
> and the dream-parallel is drawn to deny the waking world's reality,
> not to lodge its being in the mind — *na ca manasi
> rajjusarpasyotpattiḥ pralayo vā*.

Pranams, Michael🙏🙏🙏
