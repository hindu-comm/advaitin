Dear Michael Ji,

  

I find Claude's replies only funny. Claude is not clear what it is refuting. Clearly, for Shankara, this Mundaka mantra is not a sarvātma upasana. It is Jnana context. Shankara says the entire world is only a transformation/vikāra of the mind. The Mundaka 21.2 has clearly said:
अप्राणोह्यमनाश्चेति प्राणादिवायुभेदाः कर्मेन्द्रियाणि तद्विषयाश्च तथा बुद्धिमनसी बुद्धीन्द्रियाणि तद्विषयाश्च प्रतिषिद्धा वेदितव्याः Brahman has no prāṇa-, no mind/manas/buddhi. When Shankara says 'the whole world is mind's transformation,' this statement cannot apply to the Brahman since Brahman has no mind. So, essentially it refers to the jiva's mind. I had also cited the Mandukya3.31मनोदृश्यमिदं द्वैतं यत्किञ्चित्सचराचरम्।

मनसो ह्यमनीभावे द्वैतं नैवोपलभ्यते ॥ ३१ ॥ where Shankara/Gaudapada say that all this duality is a concoction of the mind. In Mandukya karika 3.43 Shankara again says सर्वं द्वैतमविद्याविजृम्भितंदुःखमेव All this dvaita is avidya-projection. In 3.31 he said that duality (dvaita) is a concoction of the mind. Therefore, Shankara's remark in the Mundaka Upanishad can't be attributed to Brahman.

  

Claude can't understand these points; therefore, it is better we rely on the Bhashya's global picture.
