Namaste Sudhanshuji, Subbuji, Bhaskarji, **Rajaji**[et.al](http://et.al).,

Both limbs granted, exactly as stated — GK-Bh 4.66's *tac-cittāvyatiriktāḥ* gives the first, GK-Bh 4.67's *draṣṭṛ-dṛśyatvāt* the second, and the conclusion, *cittam api naivāsti paramārthataḥ*, is textually undeniable. No side of this exchange has ever disputed it. What I'd still ask you to look at is the elephant line itself, since it's carrying the whole argument and it says something more specific than "neither exists."

*Na hi svapne hastī hasti-cittaṃ vā vidyate* — in dream, neither elephant nor elephant-citta exists. True of the dream elephant, and true, on your reading, of the waking elephant too: co-negated with its citta. But notice what is *not* co-negated in either case: the dream itself, and the *citta* whose *pariṇāma* the dream-elephant is standardly held to be — the dreamer's own state, real enough as a state of the dreamer to be recollected on waking as "I dreamed of an elephant." GK 4.67's own conclusion is not "citta is unreal absolutely"; it is *citta-dṛśyavac cittam api naivāsti paramārthataḥ* — non-existent *like its object*, i.e. at the object's level, the level at which elephant and elephant-citta stood as a seen pair. What survives is one level up: *draṣṭur ekatvam* — the oneness of the seer — which is precisely why the chapter's own summary word is *draṣṭā*, not *cittam*, and why 4.67's close is *jāgrat-svapnayor ubhayor api dṛṣṭr-aikatvaṃ tu vaitathye pratipāditam* — sameness of the seer *established through* vaitathya, the negation being the route, not the terminus.

That is the whole of my point, restated once more since it seems not to have landed: dvaita including citta is *mithyā* — appearance without being — I have granted this from the first reply in this thread. What I have not granted, because the texts you are citing do not say it, is that this makes citta the *upādāna* of the seen world at any level, even a level later negated. The rope-snake needs no rope-substance called "mind" to be silver from; it needs a rope. Here the rope is the draṣṭā throughout, and citta — like the silver — is what is *negated*, not what does the *appearing*.

Since you've said the case is closed and the literature unnecessary — I'll leave it there and let the list judge the texts on their own. I'm grateful for the exchange regardless; it's sharpened both papers.

praṇāms,  
Michael
