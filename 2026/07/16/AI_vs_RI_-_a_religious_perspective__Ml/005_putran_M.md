This depends on which Hindu philosophical school you're asking about, but**the mainstream classical answer is also "no."**The mind (*manas*) is generally not regarded as something that emerges from the arrangement of gross physical elements.

Here's how the major traditions approach it.

### Sāṃkhya and Yoga

In Sāṃkhya Kārikā and the Yoga tradition, the mind (*manas*), intellect (*buddhi*), and ego (*ahaṃkāra*) are all products of**prakṛti**(primordial nature), but they are**subtle**evolutes—not gross physical objects.

The hierarchy is roughly:

- Prakṛti

- Buddhi

- Ahaṃkāra

- Manas

- Sense faculties

- Subtle elements (tanmātras)

- Gross elements (mahābhūtas)

So the mind is actually*prior*to the gross elements in the cosmological sequence. On that view, gross matter cannot generate mind because mind belongs to a subtler level of manifestation.

### Advaita Vedānta

In Advaita, the mind is part of the subtle body (*sūkṣma śarīra*). It is an instrument through which consciousness (Ātman/Brahman) is reflected.

The brain is viewed as a physical instrument that enables the functioning of the mind in embodied life, but the mind itself is not identical with the brain.

A common analogy is:

- Brain = hardware

- Mind = subtle operating system

- Consciousness = the illuminator that makes experience possible

This analogy is modern rather than scriptural, but it captures the traditional distinction.

### Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika

These schools do regard*manas*as a kind of substance, but not one composed of gross matter. It is described as an extremely subtle, atomic entity that mediates between the self and the senses. It is not something produced by combining physical elements.

### Can engineering create a mind?

From the standpoint of these classical systems:

- Building a physical brain from gross elements would create a physical
  organ.

- That does**not**, by itself, create*manas*.

- For cognition to occur, a self (*ātman*), a subtle mind (*manas*), the
  senses, and the physical body all have to be connected.

So classical Hindu philosophy does not support the idea that sufficiently complex arrangements of gross matter automatically produce a mind.

### Are there dissenting views within Indian philosophy?

Yes. The Cārvāka (Lokāyata) school argued that consciousness arises from material elements, much as intoxication arises from fermenting ingredients. This is the closest classical Indian analogue to modern materialism.

However, Cārvāka was explicitly rejected by most other Hindu philosophical schools, which argued that consciousness and mental functions cannot be fully explained as products of gross matter alone.

### A modern perspective

If you ask the question from the standpoint of contemporary neuroscience or AI rather than scripture, the answer is unsettled. Some scientists and philosophers argue that consciousness and mind emerge from sufficiently complex physical systems, while others disagree. Hindu scriptures themselves do not resolve that scientific debate; they present a different metaphysical framework in which mind is a subtle principle rather than an emergent property of gross matter.

So,**within the framework of classical Hindu scriptures and most orthodox schools**, the answer is that*manas*cannot be engineered solely from gross elements because it is not considered to be made of gross elements in the first place.
