Namaste Sundar ji.

  

An enjoyable read. I will comment in detail some time later. Just couple of things I wanted to share. Regarding mention of samadhi in Upanishads, the following may also be noted:

  

यदा पञ्चावतिष्ठन्ते ज्ञानानि मनसा सह ।  
बुद्धिश्च न विचेष्टति *तामाहुः परमां गतिम्* ॥ १० ॥

  

The bhasya thereupon and on the next verse may be seen. The upanishad and bhashya clearly state what is the supreme state. This is what is samdhi.

  

Regards.

Sudhanshu Shekhar.

> I gave the “Weekend with Wisdom (INDICA Moksha / Advaita
>
> Academy)” Talk on the role of dhyana and samadhi in Sankara's
> Advaita.
>
>   
>
> Since this question surfaces here periodically, I am sharing a summary
> of the argument, with the citations, for those who may wish to examine
> or
>
> contest it.
>
>   
>
> THE STARTING POINT I CONCEDE FULLY
>
>   
>
> Suffering is due to avidya; avidya is removed only by jnana; sabda is
>
> the pramana that generates that jnana. Meditation is not an
> independent
>
> pramana. No trance constitutes knowledge, and nirvikalpa samadhi is
> not
>
> a mandatory gate that sruti anywhere enjoins as such. On all of this
>
> there is no dispute.
>
>   
>
> The question I raised is narrower: granting all of the above, is the
>
> contemplative discipline something Sankara treats as enjoined - or as
>
> dispensable once the mind is purified? Five familiar claims say the
>
> latter. I examined each against the mula bhashyas.
>
>   
>
> 1\. "YOGA IS UPAKURVANTU - USE IT AND DISCARD IT"
>
>   
>
> The claim requires that dhyana be merely instrumental. But two things
>
> occur in ekagrata that instrumental scaffolding does not produce:
>
> sattvika sukham (BG 18.37, atma-buddhi-prasada-jam; BG 6.21, sukham
>
> atyantikam ... buddhi-grahyam atindriyam), and the loosening of
>
> deha-buddhi in real time - which, in Sankara's own vocabulary, is
>
> adhyasa-viyoga occurring as a temporary avastha. Dhyana is not merely
>
> preparation for the removal of superimposition; it is that removal, in
>
> miniature.
>
>   
>
> 2\. "ONLY THE FIRST FIVE LIMBS ARE NEEDED"
>
>   
>
> Patanjali himself calls the last three antaranga relative to the first
>
> five (YS 3.7, trayam antarangam purvebhyah). The five-limb position
>
> keeps what he called outer and discards what he called inner. More to
>
> the point, Sankara opens his commentary on Gita chapter 6 by calling
>
> yoga antarangam moksha-sadhanam - the inner means to moksha - and then
>
> comments on forty-seven verses covering seat, place, diet, moderation
>
> and na kincid api cintayet. That is considerable ink for something
>
> merely preparatory, from the most economical commentator in our
>
> tradition.
>
>   
>
> (In the talk I put this as a cricketing analogy: telling a batsman in
>
> form to stop hitting boundaries and push singles. Dharana and dhyana
>
> are the boundaries; samadhi the sixes.)
>
>   
>
> 3\. "SAMADHI IS NOT IN THE UPANISHADS"
>
>   
>
> A word-search may not return samadhi; that is not the same as absence.
>
> Brhadaranyaka 4.4.23 has samahito bhutva - the past participle of the
>
> very root sam-a-dha. Badarayana himself uses the term at BS 2.3.39
>
> (samadhy-abhavac ca). And decisively, Sankara's gloss there reads
>
> samadhividhanat - "because samadhi is enjoined" - citing as the
>
> enjoining texts drastavyah srotavyo mantavyo nididhyasitavyah (BU
>
> 2.4.5), so 'nvestavyah sa vijijnasitavyah (CU 8.7.1), and om ity eva
>
> dhyayatha atmanam (MuU 2.2.6).
>
>   
>
> 4\. "ADVAITIC DHYANA IS ENTIRELY DIFFERENT FROM YOGIC DHYANA"
>
>   
>
> Half true, and the true half helps. The metaphysics differ profoundly
> -
>
> kaivalya isolates one purusa among many with a real prakrti standing;
>
> Sankara's moksha is sarvatma-bhava. And Sankara does refute the Yoga
>
> school (etena yogah pratyuktah, BSBh 2.1.3). But read what is refuted:
>
> the pradhana and the dualism, not the discipline. In that same passage
>
> he acknowledges that sruti itself recognises yogic practice, citing
>
> Katha and Svetasvatara. He rejected the metaphysics and kept the
>
> discipline; we have somehow reversed him.
>
>   
>
> On this point I would commend Vidyasankar Sundaresan's paper
>
> "Sankara-granthah katham otah protas ca?" (17th International Congress
>
> of Vedanta, 2007), which argues that Sankara's corpus must be read as
> a
>
> hyperlinked set of cross-citations rather than as standalone books.
>
> Following his own links: the same BU 2.4.5 sentence that glosses
>
> samadhi at BSBh 2.3.39 is cited at BSBh 2.1.3 as an instance of sruti
>
> enjoining yoga. The identical sentence, under "samadhi" in one
>
> adhikarana and under "yoga" in another.
>
>   
>
> I should add the precision that Sundaresan himself observes, and which
>
> I accept: this is not an apurva-vidhi. Sankara denies an original
>
> injunction for Self-knowledge. What he accepts (BUBh 1.4.7) is a
>
> niyama-vidhi for atma-vijnana-smrti-santana - the steady recollection
> of
>
> Self-knowledge - which he then calls the ananya-sadhana for
>
> citta-vrtti-nirodha.
>
>   
>
> 5\. "SAMADHI IS TEMPORARY, HENCE IRRELEVANT TO THE TIMELESS"
>
>   
>
> The session ends; its effect on the mind does not. This is precisely
> why
>
> BS 4.1.1-2 enjoins repetition (avrttir asakrd upadesat) - realisation
>
> does not always dawn from a single hearing. The pramana is sabda; the
>
> assimilation is contemplative.
>
>   
>
> A related claim, that nididhyasana is effortless because sravana and
>
> manana have removed all doubt, meets two difficulties. An automatic
>
> consequence requires no injunction; yet sruti enjoins nididhyasana,
> and
>
> Sankara glosses it niscayena dhyatavyah. And at Mundaka Bhashya 3.1.8
> he
>
> describes this dhyana as prayatna-visesah - a special sustained
> effort.
>
> Effortlessness is the destination of nididhyasana, not its definition.
>
>   
>
> THE POSITIVE EVIDENCE
>
>   
>
> Where does Gita 6 - the chapter Sankara labels antarangam
>
> moksha-sadhanam - culminate? Not in kaivalya, but at 6.29:
>
> sarvabhutastham atmanam sarvabhutani catmani. And Sankara tells us in
>
> the Brhadaranyaka Bhashya (4.3.20) what that summit means: so long as
>
> one sees anything as other than oneself even by a hair's breadth, that
>
> is still ignorance; when ignorance is destroyed, sarvatma-bhava -
> which
>
> is another name for liberation - is attained.
>
>   
>
> Gaudapada, for his part, names the Atman itself samadhi (Karika 3.37),
>
> and Sankara's bhashya explains why the name fits rather than
> explaining
>
> it away. Karikas 3.40-46 are a meditation manual, ending at nispannam
>
> brahma tat tada.
>
>   
>
> IN SUM
>
>   
>
> Samadhi is not the liberating knowledge and is not a pramana. It is
> the
>
> highest abidance of the mind in that knowledge - and on Sankara's own
>
> word, enjoined. The talk closes with a Q&A on akhandakara-vrtti versus
>
> Patanjali's vrtti-nirodha, on anubhava and sruti, and on whether
> samadhi
>
> is attainable without a guru.
>
