Namaskaram Subbu-ji,

  

Between pages 7 and 8, you have introduced an argument for why dvaita's notion of swatantra vs paratantra is essentially the same as paramarthika vs vyavaharika. I tried to understand thelogic you mentioned.

  

They say jiva and Brahman are two distinct existences but jiva is dependent on Brahman. This would imply that jiva's reality itself is not dependent on Brahman because it is true irrespective of whatever Brahman does. At most we can say the jiva depends on Brahman in other ways like growth, energy, manifestation etc. But Brahman has no say/power over the jiva's existence as a separate reality. 

  

This either amounts to saying that the jiva's reality is independent of Brahman, or that Brahman is not independent of sustaining the jiva's existence. Two realities means there is something "real" in each that stands independently and on par with the other, and neither is fully independent either - jiva certainly not but Brahman also is conditioned/bound by law/swabhava/guna/"karunai" to play master to the jiva's samsara.

  

This may be called a cynical and skewed perspective on Ishvara'sgoodness and grace. But that is not the point. We can consider it good but cannot call it true independence when there is a dvaita of realities and consequently a world of mutual dependence and obligation. Whereas when the Reality is realized as nirguna/advaita and the world of duality mithya/kalpita/adhyasa, there is no conflict of the paratantra apparent dependence afflicting in any real way the swantantraof Brahman. For the dream-snake cannot afflict the rope in any true sense.
