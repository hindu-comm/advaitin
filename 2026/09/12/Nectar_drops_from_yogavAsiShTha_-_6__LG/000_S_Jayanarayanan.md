The Arrival of Viśvāmitra:  
  
He remains alone in silence saying: “Of what avail is prosperity, adversity, house or intentions? Everything is only unreal?” He does not appear  
among merriments and does not sink into enjoyments. He does not perform his duties and resorts only to silence. Women with disheveled and  
creeper-like curly locks of hair, with their eyes moving around in amorous sport, do not delight him as female deer (do not delight) a forest-tree.  
  
He attains satisfaction in solitary and remote places, banks of rivers, groves and forests as if sold away among animals. King! By that aversion  
from taking clothes, drinks and food, he follows the ascetic obeying the law of a wandering mendicant. He remains alone with a vacant mind, firmly  
rooted in the lotus-pose (of yoga), placing the surface of his cheek on the palm of his left hand. We do not know what he goes to, what he does,  
what he desires, what he thinks of, what he arrives at and now and what he pursues. He becomes emaciated and pale day by day and becomes detached  
every day as a tree (is transformed) at the end of autumn.  
  
Even if questioned again and again by servants, princes and mothers, he remains silent and desireless, stating that there is nothing whatever  
(to be told). He advises the worthy and friendly people nearby, thus: “May you not fasten your mind to enjoyments which are pleasant only at the  
current moment. Do you long for wealth which is the sole abode of calamities?” — Thus discriminating, he gives away everything to one who asks for it.  
Powerful One with eyes resembling lotus petals! We do not know what we should do to him of such a mind. You alone are our refuge in this matter.  
The sages ordered the Rama’s men: “If it be so, please bring him, of great wisdom, quickly here”. “This delusion of Raghupati is neither due to  
calamities nor due to passion. It is only the dawn of wisdom in one possessed of discrimination and dispassion. If this delusion is wiped out by  
proper reasoning he will certainly attain peace.”  
  
In the meanwhile Rama having come there to worship the feet of his father, bowed down to his father and the two sages and sat down on a carpet  
spread on the ground by the attendants. Then, Rama, questioned about the cause of his delusion by the king and the two sages, told these words duly  
and in great detail.
