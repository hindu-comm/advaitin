The survey **admits the contention, with one qualification**: it admits *adhyāsika tādātmya* as a description of the **misidentification produced by adhyāsa**, but it does not thereby admit that this “identity” is a separate positive structure or entity underlying the error.

Indeed, the *Adhyāsa Bhāṣya* itself is even more direct than BUB 3.5.1. Śaṅkara says that, despite the radical difference of Self and non-Self, there is:

> *anyonyasmin anyonyātmakatām anyonyadharmāṃś cādhyasya …
> itaretarāvivekena*

That is: **superimposing upon each the nature of the other and the properties of the other, through non-discrimination of the two**. So if *adhyāsika tādātmya* means the erroneous appearance “I am this \[body/mind\]” and conversely the attribution of consciousness/Selfhood to the non-Self, then yes: that is plainly contained in Śaṅkara’s account of *adhyāsa*. The important phrase is *anyonyātmakatām adhyasya*—literally, superimposing “the being-the-other” or “the nature of the other” upon each.

Your BUB 3.5.1 passage reinforces this very strongly:

> *mohas tu viparīta-pratyaya-prabhavo ’vivekaḥ bhramaḥ; sa cāvidyā
> sarvasyānarthasya prasava-bījam*

Here *moha* is defined as **aviveka/bhrama arising from contrary cognition**, and that very thing is called *avidyā* and the “generative seed” of all evil. Thus *aviveka* is not mere absence in the sense of an inert blank. It is implicated in *viparīta-pratyaya*—cognition in which something is taken otherwise than it is.

But I would distinguish two propositions:

**(A)** *Adhyāsa involves an experienced or cognized identity/non-difference between what is actually distinct.*  
→ **Yes. The survey should unequivocally admit this.**

**(B)** *Therefore the causal avidyā responsible for that identity must itself be a positive, insentient entity possessing a distinct nature and powers.*  
→ **No. Neither this BUB passage nor the Adhyāsa Bhāṣya establishes that further claim.**

In fact, BUB 3.5.1 is quite congenial to our second finding. When Śaṅkara actually tells us what this *avidyā* is, he gives us **aviveka, bhrama, and viparīta-pratyaya**, not an independently characterized positive substance or principle with differentiated *āvaraṇa* and *vikṣepa* powers.

So I would **not change the three-point conclusion**, but I think the objection exposes something worth making explicit in the appendix: our conclusion does **not** reduce *avidyā* to passive “mere absence,” nor does it deny the very robust misidentification involved in adhyāsa. What it denies is the inference from that robust cognitive misidentification to a separately articulated positive causal principle.

One terminological caution: I would say that *adhyāsika tādātmya* is an excellent **analytic description** of Śaṅkara's *anyonyātmatā*-superimposition, but unless we establish the historical usage, I would not say Śaṅkara himself presents “adhyāsika tādātmya” as a formally named category. That distinction will keep the response very strong.
