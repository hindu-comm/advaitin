namaste Sudhanshu ji,

I think you may have taken the title as attributing to positive-*avidyā* theory the claim that non-recognition is the material cause. That is not intended. The title points instead to Sureśvara’s own language, which requires explanation:

> *dvaitasya ca … sarvasyāpi
> svataḥsiddhādvitīyātmānavabodhamātropādānatvāt*  
> “The entire dual order has mere non-recognition of the
> self-established nondual Self as its *upādāna*.”  
> —*Naiṣkarmyasiddhi*, opening prose

> *yasmād ātmānavabodhamātropādānāḥ pramātrādayas tasmāt*  
> “The knower and the rest have mere non-recognition of the Self as
> their *upādāna*.”  
> —NS II, prose following II.105

> *ātmāgrahātirekeṇa tasyā rūpaṃ na vidyate*  
> “Avidyā has no form apart from non-apprehension of the Self.”  
> —*Taittirīyavārttika* II.179

The paper asks whether this language requires a positive *avidyā* underlying non-recognition, or whether Sureśvara locates the causal ground in the Self itself as unknown.
