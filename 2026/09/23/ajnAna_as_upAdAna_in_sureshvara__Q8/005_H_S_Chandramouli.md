Namaste Michael Ji,

Reg // The paper asks whether this language requires a positive *avidyā* underlying non-recognition, or whether Sureśvara locates the causal ground in the Self itself as unknown //,

Reference is to the three verses you have cited from Sureswaracharya immediately prior to the above statement. My understanding is in line with the latter as explained below which is also consistent with the Bhashya. Actually, the language in all the three citations clearly maintains that whatever is responsible for the non-recognition of the Self/Chaitanya , through tAdAtmya with that very same Self/Chaitanya, constitutes what is called positive avidyA. Swami Sureswaracharya , in your terminology, locates the causal ground as the tAdAtmya between the Self and whatever makes the Self unknown.

The word \*positive\* in this context refers to the Reality/existential status of avidyA, as defined above, in relation to the Self itself. If the Self is considered as \*positive\*, then avidyA also is \*positive\* though the level or order of that status could be different. If the \*rope\* in the rope-serpent illustration is considered \*real/existent/positive\*, then the rope-serpent is also of the same status, though to a lesser level/order. It differentiates from what the rope-serpent is called as \*negative\* concept or \*nonexistent\* order or just \*imaginary\*.

If you have promoted the survey or the author is known to you, you may like to check if his understanding is the same while arriving at his conclusions. Please post his views also here for information.
