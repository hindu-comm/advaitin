Namaste ALL, Yes, Bhavarupa avidya needs definition agreeable to all.  
  

The safest formulation is: bhāvarūpa avidyā means ignorance that is not a mere absence of knowledge. It has enough “presence” or efficacy to function as an explanatory principle: it can veil, project, persist causally, generate appearances, and be removed by knowledge. But it is not *sat* in the way Brahman is sat. Later Advaita therefore also calls it *anirvacanīya*—not determinable simply as existent or nonexistent. Citsukha is reported as making the point quite sharply: avidyā is called *bhāva* only in the sense that it is different from mere non-being, not because it is an ordinary positive existent.

So your darkness analogy is good if we qualify it. We can say: darkness is treated here not simply as “no light,” but as something phenomenally and explanatorily present. Likewise, bhāvarūpa avidyā is not a chair-like substance, yet neither is it the sheer nonoccurrence of knowledge. Traditional summaries explicitly describe it as beginningless, removable by knowledge, capable of *āvaraṇa* and *vikṣepa*, and in the Vivaraṇa line as functioning as a material cause of the world.  
  
 A dream is a thought modification—but what exactly is *bhāvarūpa avidyā*?  
 A thought (*vṛtti*) is a particular manifestation: “this is a snake,” “I am a man,” a dream elephant, a perception, a memory. Mūlāvidyā/bhāvarūpa avidyā is the underlying explanatory condition that makes such erroneous differentiation possible. In some later systems, particular errors are treated as modes or derivative forms (*tūlāvidyā*, *avidyā-vṛtti*) of the underlying ignorance. Prakāśātman’s Vivaraṇa line, for example, accepts one root ignorance but innumerable particular modes; the shell-silver error is one such derivative manifestation, while root ignorance remains until brahmajñāna.   
  
the real question for the Sureśvara paper: **How much of that structure is already present in Sureśvara** when he calls ignorance an upādāna, and how much belongs to later theoretical articulation?
