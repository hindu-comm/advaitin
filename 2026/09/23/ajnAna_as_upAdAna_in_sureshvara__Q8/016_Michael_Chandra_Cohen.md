Namastre Sudhanshuji,

Fine response -we agree at the paramārthika level that causation is ultimately unreal and “merely appears.”

But that immediately raises the question: what is it that merely appears?

Is there an appearing ignorance-principle that functions as a causal factor, or is the Self itself misapprehended under superimposed distinctions? This is a comprehensive study of Śaṅkara and Sureśvara's vast corpus adn it clearly favors the latter:That is why simply saying “causation is unreal” does not yet answer the question posed by Sureśvara’s use of *upādāna*. We still have to ask what explanatory work that term is doing. If the apparent cause is nothing over and above misapprehension of the Self, then Sureśvara’s causal language has a very different force from a later theory in which avidyā is given a more articulated causal status.

I would also still qualify “when causation is given up, we at once situate as the seer.” The seer-position remains relational, since seer implies seen. Advaita finally rescinds even that distinction.

Best, MIchael
