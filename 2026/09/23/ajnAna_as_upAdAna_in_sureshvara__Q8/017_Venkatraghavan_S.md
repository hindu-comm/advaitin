Your statements above reminded me of the kAraNatva adhikaraNa (1.4.14) of the brahmasUtra. Here, there is a discussion on why there is a difference between various Upanishads in the specific order in which creation is said to have occurred.

  

Shankaracharya states the problem first.

  

कार्यविषयं तु विगानं दृश्यते —

With respect to effects, though, there is difference within the Upanishads themselves in the stated order of creation.

  

क्वचिदाकाशादिका सृष्टिः क्वचित्तेजआदिकेत्येवंजातीयकम् ।

In some places, the Upanishads speak of space as the first element created, and some others speak of fire as the first created etc.

  

न च कार्यविषयेण विगानेन कारणमपि ब्रह्म सर्ववेदान्तेष्वविगीतमधिगम्यमानमविवक्षितं भवितुमर्हतीति शक्यते वक्तुम् , अतिप्रसङ्गात् ।

Just because there is difference in the order in which effects have been created, it does not follow that there must be difference in the cause also, and therefore it cannot be said that Brahman is not the central message of the Upanishads, for that would be an overextension.

  

समाधास्यति चाचार्यः कार्यविषयमपि विगानम् ‘न वियदश्रुतेः’ (ब्र. सू. २ । ३ । १) इत्यारभ्य ।

The contradiction in the teaching of the effects will also be remedied by the teacher starting from the sUtra "Space is not created, as that is not said in the Upanishads" (in the third and fourth pAdas of the second chapter).

  

Then he explains that it is possible for there to be a difference in the specific sequence by which effects come into being.

  

भवेदपि कार्यस्य विगीतत्वमप्रतिपाद्यत्वात् ।

It is possible for there to be difference in effects, for they are not meant to be the teaching.

  

And here is the important point -

न ह्ययं सृष्ट्यादिप्रपञ्चः प्रतिपिपादयिषितः ।

The intent of the shruti is not to talk of creation at all.

  

न हि तत्प्रतिबद्धः कश्चित्पुरुषार्थो दृश्यते श्रूयते वा ।

Nor is there any benefit in knowing about creation - for such a benefit is neither seen (experienced) or heard (stated in the shruti).

  

न च कल्पयितुं शक्यते, उपक्रमोपसंहाराभ्यां तत्र तत्र ब्रह्मविषयैर्वाक्यैः साकमेकवाक्यताया गम्यमानत्वात् ।

Nor can one imagine that there would be a benefit in knowing about creation, for (sentences about creation) wherever they are present in the beginning or end of passages, have ekavAkyatA with sentences that have come to reveal about Brahman. (ie their purpose is to reveal Brahman, not talk about creation per se).

  

दर्शयति च सृष्ट्यादिप्रपञ्चस्य ब्रह्मप्रतिपत्त्यर्थताम् — ‘अन्नेन सोम्य शुङ्गेनापो मूलमन्विच्छाद्भिः सोम्य शुङ्गेन तेजो मूलमन्विच्छ तेजसा सोम्य शुङ्गेन सन्मूलमन्विच्छ’ (छा. उ. ६ । ८ । ४) इति ।

It is directly revealed in the Chandogya upaniShad that sentences about creation are mentioned for the purpose of revealing Brahman - "Oh dear one, from the effect that is earth, discover its cause, water. From the effect that is water, discover its cause, fire. From the effect that is fire, discover its cause, Brahman".

  

मृदादिदृष्टान्तैश्च कार्यस्य कारणेनाभेदं वदितुं सृष्ट्यादिप्रपञ्चः श्राव्यत इति गम्यते ।

It follows that with the help of examples of clay etc, sentences about creation say that the effects are non-different from the cause.

  

तथा च सम्प्रदायविदो वदन्ति — ‘मृल्लोहविस्फुलिङ्गाद्यैः सृष्टिर्या चोदितान्यथा । उपायः सोऽवताराय नास्ति भेदः कथञ्चन’ (मा. का. ३ । १५) इति ।

So say the knowers of tradition (Gaudapadacharya) - "The creation that is spoken of in different ways, through the examples of clay, iron, sparks of fire etc, their purpose is in revealing Brahman - therefore, there is no contradiction whatsoever between various sentences of the Upanishads."

  

ब्रह्मप्रतिपत्तिप्रतिबद्धं तु फलं श्रूयते — ‘ब्रह्मविदाप्नोति परम्’ (तै. उ. २ । १ । १) ‘तरति शोकमात्मवित्’ (छा. उ. ७ । १ । ३) ‘तमेव विदित्वाति मृत्युमेति’ (श्वे. उ. ३ । ८) इति ।

Whereas, the benefit of knowing Brahman is heard - "The knower of Brahman attains the supreme.", "The knower of the Atma crosses sorrow", "By knowing that alone, one transcends death".

  

प्रत्यक्षावगमं चेदं फलम् , ‘तत्त्वमसि’ इत्यसंसार्यात्मत्वप्रतिपत्तौ सत्यां संसार्यात्मत्वव्यावृत्तेः ॥ १४ ॥

This result is directly experienced when one is freed from the notion of being trapped in samsAra, because one's nature is as an asamsAri, as known through sentences such as "You are that".

  

From the above, Shankara makes the important point that the purpose of the revelation of creation is not to talk of creation per se, rather it is to reveal the creator. He gives two reasons for this

  

1\) unanimity in what the kAraNa is, vigAna (difference) in the kArya - If the revelation of creation was the intent of the shruti there would have been unanimity on how that creation had occurred.

  

2\) phala - The knowledge of Brahman has a stated phala, benefit (moksha) whereas the knowledge of creation has no benefit stated. phala is a very important tAtparya linga (indicator of the central teaching of a passage). If the shruti states a particular benefit from knowing one thing (Brahman) but stays silent on the benefit of knowing something else (creation) it follows that the former is what the shruti intends to reveal, not the latter. Further, such a benefit is a matter of practical experience, for the knowledge of Brahman leads to the cessation of samsAra.

  

Therefore, it behooves the serious seeker to use the statements of creation as intended - an instrument through which one knows Brahman
-सन्मूलमन्विच्छ - see their cause, the Sat.

  

Kind regards,

Venkatraghavan
