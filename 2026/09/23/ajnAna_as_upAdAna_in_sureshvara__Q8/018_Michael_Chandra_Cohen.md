Namaste Venkatraghavan,  

Though not addressed to me specifically, I thought my reply might provide insight on the issue of causation and Sankara's call of it as a teaching device. Yes, I agree with your central point that Śaṅkara does not present creation as an independently important truth. In BSBh 1.4.14 he says plainly that the *sṛṣṭyādi-prapañca* is not what śruti intends to teach; creation passages are subordinate to the disclosure of Brahman and to showing the non-difference of effect from cause. At the final level, causation is withdrawn.

The historical issue becomes more serious with Madhusūdana. By his time the causal account is not merely fuller in detail. Avidyā has acquired a coordinated explanatory structure: it can serve as *upādāna*, possess covering and projecting functions, stand in determinate relation to sākṣin, have locus and object, persist causally, and even possess state-specific *vṛttis*. In deep sleep, for example, the theory no longer stops with “differentiation is absent and the causal conditions for waking remain.” It gives an internal account of what is present in the state and how the later waking report is possible

That changes what bondage itself means. In Śaṅkara and, I would argue, fundamentally in Sureśvara, the central problem is the present misapprehension of the self-established Self—the superimposition of Self and not-Self. In the mature Madhusūdana structure, that misapprehension is increasingly explained through a persisting causal principle with its own powers and modes. The error is no longer merely described and rescinded; it is theoretically generated.  

*māyāvidyānirvācyam anṛtaṃ tattva-jñāna-nivartyaṃ cājñānam eva sva-parādhyāse kāraṇam.*

“Ignorance itself—indescribable as māyā/avidyā, unreal, and removable by knowledge of Reality—is the cause of the superimposition of itself and of what is other than itself.” Siddhantabindu

And that inevitably changes the account of liberation as well. If avidyā is principally misapprehension, knowledge corrects the mistake about what is already the case. If avidyā is a structured causal veil, then one must explain precisely how knowledge opposes, removes, or terminates that structure, while accounting for what remains at the empirical level. Hence the need for distinctions involving *āvaraṇa*, *vikṣepa*, *avidyā-vṛtti*, *avidyā-leśa*, and so forth.

So I would no longer describe the later development as merely “more explanation.” The paramārthika conclusion remains the same: no real creation, no real bondage, no second thing apart from Brahman. But the vyāvahārika theory through which bondage, world-appearance, sleep, and liberation are understood has changed substantially.

That is why saying “causation is ultimately unreal” does not settle the issue. A theory may be ultimately sublated and still reshape the entire understanding of what the problem is, how appearance occurs, what persists in ignorance, and what knowledge is said to remove.
