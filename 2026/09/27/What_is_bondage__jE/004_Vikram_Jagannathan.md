Namaskaram Sudhanshu ji,

  

I define bondage as the apparent lack of abidance in one’s true nature - the Self.  
  
Now, who needs to abide in the true nature of the Self? Obviously, the Self is ever abiding in Its own nature as the Self. It is svataḥ-siddha, svayaṃ-prakāśa, pūrṇa.  
  
Yet I currently “believe” myself not to be abiding in my own true nature, even though the fact is that I am, here and now, and always, abiding in my own true nature.  
  
This paradox - that even while one is actually abiding in one’s true nature, one somehow remains oblivious of it - is the mysterious nature of bondage.  
  
Abidance in the true nature of the Self can be indicated by the complete absence of fear. As long as I “have” fears, I have not yet realized my own true nature; consequently, I remain in bondage.  
  
To overcome bondage, all that is required is to remove the root cause of fear. I will leave it to the Upaniṣads to show us the way.  

  

prostrations,

Vikram
