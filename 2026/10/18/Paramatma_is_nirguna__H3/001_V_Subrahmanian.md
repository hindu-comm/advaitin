Namaste Putran ji,

  

In the Gita Bhashya for the word nirguna, Shankara has given the meaning that which is free of the three gunas of prakriti: sattva, etc.

  

The position of Advaita is all gunas, auspicious or otherwise, in Brahman is possible by default only through the combinations of the three gunas. Hence since Ishwara is saguna, with Maya three gunas association, all auspicious gunas or accepted in Advaita. Only in nirguna Brahman these are not there.

> Yes. In the Mahabharata, when Bhima meets Hanuman, Bhima explicitly
> says that the Paramatma is nirguna and can be known through jnana. The
> passage occurs in the Vana Parva, in the episode of Bhima and
> Hanuman's encounter.
>
>   
>
> The relevant Sanskrit verse is:
>
>   
>
> \> निर्गुणः परमात्मा तु देहं व्याप्यावतिष्ठते।तमहं ज्ञानविज्ञेयं नावमन्ये न लङ्घये॥
>
>   
>
> Transliteration:
>
>   
>
> Nirguṇaḥ paramātmā tu dehaṃ vyāpya avatiṣṭhate,
>
>   
>
> Tam ahaṃ jñāna-vijñeyaṃ nāvamanye na laṅghaye.
>
>   
>
> Meaning:
>
>   
>
> “The Supreme Self, devoid of qualities, pervades the body and abides
> within it. I cannot disrespect Him, who is to be known through
> knowledge; therefore, I will not step over you.”
