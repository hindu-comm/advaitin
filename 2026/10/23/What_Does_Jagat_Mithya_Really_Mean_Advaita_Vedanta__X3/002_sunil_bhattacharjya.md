Dear Subujiand other friends,

  

In the Mahabharata, Sri Vedavyasa clearly says that all the upanishads are cows and Lord Krishna was the milkman and Lord Krishna tells usabout this eternal reality or truth in the very second chapter of the original Bhagavad Gita that Brahman alone is the permanent reality
inthe world and everything else isimpermanent in the world. Lord Krishna says thesame thing in the Chapter on Jnana in the Uddhava Gita also..

  

Adi Shankara requested his paramguru Sri Gaudapada (i.e., Adi Shankara's guru Sri Govindapada'sguru Gaudapada) to allow him to write a commentary on the Bhagavad Gita, but unfortunately Sri Gaudapada did not agree to that. Gaudapada wanted Adi Shankara to spend his last years on the worship of Mother Kamakshi (also called mother Shodashi), in Kanchipuram.

  

About one thousand and three hundred years (i.e., about 1300 years) after Adi Shankara, one Sri Abhinava Shankara of the Kanchi Kamakota Math, tried to write a commentary on the incomplete Bhagavad Gita of 700 verses), but this book was not complete. He endedup his life in a cave in Badari in the Himalaya. It is quite possible that he came to know that the original Bhagavad Gita of 745 veses.

  

Similarly,Gnanadeva also knownasSant Dnyaneshwar or referred to as Jñāneśvara, Jñānadeva, Dnyandev or Mauli or Dnyandev Vitthal Kulkarni, was born in 1275 CE,wrote at the age of 15 years, a commentary of the Bhagavad Gita of 700 verses.Sri Jnaneshwar took pilgrimage to Ujjain, Prayag, Kashi, Ayodhya, Gaya, Gokul-Vrindavan, Girnar other with famed tirthas along with Sant Namadev, who enjoyed special grace of Lord Vitthal. At the age of 21 years, 3 months and 5 days on Margashirsha Krishna Trayodashi day, he took Sanjeevan Samadhi at Alandi. Within a year his brothers and sister left their mortal bodies.
