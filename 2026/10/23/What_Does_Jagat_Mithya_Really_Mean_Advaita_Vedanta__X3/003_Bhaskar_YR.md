“Does it mean that the world does not exist, or is there a deeper philosophical meaning behind this statement?”

Shatavadhani Lalithaditya’s presentation explains the logical perspective on one of the most fundamental teachings of Advaita Vedanta, exploring what it means to call the world \*mithya\*.

- Yes to say or to realize anything is mithya we need the adhishtAnaM
  and that adhishtAnaM is brahman, to see the mithyA sarpa we need the
  rope, to see the mithyA silver we need the nacre, to see the mithyA
  jagat we need the adhishtAnam brahman. Simply we cannot imagine on
  our own out of thin air without the ‘existence’ of something out there
  as substratum. And brahman is the adhishtAnam for the mithyA jagat,
  brahman which is the material and efficient cause of the rope (jagat)
  is the adhishtAnam to ‘see’ the mithyA snake / jeeva’s avidyA Kalpita
  jagat.



- For example : Though in reality we are looking at the rope, sometimes
  we see a snake instead. In such a scenario what is being seen by us is
  only the imagined parts of the rope as snake that means, the imagined
  parts of the rope have given rise to the shape of the snake due to its
  sAdrushya. Similarly, the imagined parts in the nirvikAri brahman
  could give rise to the shape of the Jagat. This example given by
  bhAshyakAra himself in chAndOgya and further clarified and warns us
  that this jagat IS NOT like imagined snake on the rope!!
