Namaste Sudhanshuji,  
  

From Karika 2.7 - an objection:

स्वप्नदृश्यवज्जागरितदृश्यानामप्यसत्त्वमिति यदुक्तम् तदयुक्तम् ।

“The assertion that objects perceived in waking are unreal, like objects perceived in dreams, is untenable.”

The objector argues that waking objects serve practical purposes: food satisfies hunger, drink quenches thirst, and vehicles provide transportation. Dream objects do not have this efficacy in waking life.

Śaṅkara's response:

अतो मन्यामहे तेषामप्यसत्त्वं स्वप्नदृश्यवदनाशङ्कनीयमिति ।

“Therefore, we consider the unreality of waking objects, like that of dream objects, to be beyond reasonable doubt.”

His argument is that practical efficacy is relative to the state. Eating in waking does not satisfy hunger experienced in a dream; eating in a dream does not satisfy hunger upon awakening.

He concludes:

तस्मादाद्यन्तवत्त्वमुभयत्र समानमिति मिथ्यैव खलु ते स्मृताः ।

“Therefore, because having a beginning and an end is common to both, they are indeed regarded as mithyā.”

###  

This is strong evidence for the unreality of waking appearances, not merely their resemblance to dreams. Śaṅkara explicitly denies that ordinary practical usefulness establishes ultimate reality.

But observe what is not asserted:

**There is no claim that the waking object's existence begins with its perception, no assertion that the individual jīva creates the object, and no formulation of ekajīva-vāda.**The conclusion concerns asattva or mithyātva, not the mechanism of creation.The distinction is simple: Śaṅkara establishes the unreality of waking objects through the dream analogy; he does not thereby establish their production by perception.
