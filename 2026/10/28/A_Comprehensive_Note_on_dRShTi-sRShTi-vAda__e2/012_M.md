It's nothing too complex.

  

What sss means is, from the point of view of the waking ahamkara the difference between waking & dream is evident. However, those who have studied Vedanta for some time and understand the concept of Sakshi see dream & waking as exactly the same, because both are seen by the Sakshi, & from its point of view, they have no difference at all. Example :- Two films are playing on a wall ; one is logical, and the other is illogical. For the character present within the logical film there is vaidharmya between the logical film & the illogical film but for the wall, they are the same irrespective of content.

  

So, that's all he's stating here.

  

But, the difference between SSS & DSV is that for DSV the seer & seen are utterly different. Seen being a product of MulaVidya, is the same as the seer because both are avidya karya & Sakshi bhāsya. For SSS, all contingent beings are Brahman in Swaroopa. The Waking dream similarity is pointed by him to put the attention of the seeker from ego (ahamkara) to sakshi.
