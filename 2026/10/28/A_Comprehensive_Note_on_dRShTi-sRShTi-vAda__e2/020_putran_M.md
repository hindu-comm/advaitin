> Likewise when we say all beings are Brahman in svaroopa, what is
> meant? Does Brahman have a duality-embedded svaroopa in a real sense?
> That is probably held by VA. For Advaita, is it not the case that the
> rupa constituting the contingent beings is not really "**sva**-roopa"
> of Brahman but rather a vivarta appearance or maayic kalpitham of
> Brahman? If we claim vivarta, then sagunatvam or svaroopam should be
> in a lower standpoint of ignorance?

  

I think I initially misunderstood the statement "all contingent beings are Brahman in svaroopa". Now: It is not the beings constitute the svarupa of Brahman but that Brahman is the svarupa (i.e. the reality) of all beings - like gold is the "svarupa" of rings and bangles.

  

Now it is a question of whether there is a conflict between the gold-ring and the rope-snake analogies, and how they are used in the teaching of AV, by these sub-schools. For example, it seems less to the point to say the rope is svarupa of snake (as if the snake-identification is valid) but rather that the rope is imagined falsely (adhyasa) as snake.
