A comprehensive note on Drishti-Srishti-Vada (the doctrine of "creation through perception" or "perception is creation" also known as DSV) was posted by one of our advaita scholar, Sudhanshu. The note is compiled by organizing the available literature and we are thankful to him. Most in this group are aware that the phrase **Prasthana-Trayi-Bhashya (PTB)** refers to the authoritative commentaries written by Adi Shankaracharya on the three foundational pillars of Vedanta: the Principal Upanishads, the Brahma Sutras, and the Bhagavad Gita.

We also know that the presence, validity, and backing of DSV within Shankaracharya’s original commentaries are one of the most debated intellectually by Vedantic scholars. Scholars and traditional acharyas debate whether Shankara openly propounded DSV in his PTB, or if it was a later refinement by post-Shankara thinkers like Prakashatman or Madhusudana Sarasvati. The consensus falls into two main views:

Most scholars argue that in the PTB, Shankara primarily operates from Srishti-Drishti-Vada (**SDD**) as a pragmatic tool (*vyavahara*) to teach morality, devotion, and structural cosmic creation. From this viewpoint, DSV is a higher-level pedagogical stepping stone meant to dissolve the reality of the external world entirely, prepping the seeker for absolute non-duality (*Ajata-vada*). Several proponents of DSV argue that the seeds of this doctrine are directly embedded across dozens of places in Shankara's commentaries. They look for textual evidence where Shankara collapses the waking state into the dream state, or dissolves the world during deep sleep.



While the **Mandukya Upanishad** and its **Karika** (often studied alongside the PTB) are widely considered the ultimate scriptural bastions of DSV, specific references can be traced inside the main Prasthana-Trayi-Bhashya: In **Chhandogya Upanishad Bhashya,** Shankara directly equates the external objects of the waking state to internal mental projections, noting that waking experiences are generated out of mental impressions much like dreams.

In the foundational introduction of **Brahma Sutra Bhashya (Adhyasa Bhashyam),** Shankara remarks that an object does not strictly need to be physically present right in front of the senses to be perceived incorrectly. He uses the example of a child looking at the sky and perceiving it as a blue surface, pointing out that projection happens from within the individual consciousness.



Also in his commentary of **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad Bhashya,** Shankara details the deep sleep state (*sushupti*). He explains how the entire universe—including the life forces (*prana*) and cosmic entities—completely merges back into the self. DSV advocates use this to show that the universe has no objective reality independent of the conscious witness.



The current discussions focus on the philosophical views of **Sri S. S. Satchidanandendra Saraswati** (**SSS**), a prominent 20th-century scholar known for his interpretations of Adi Shankara’s advaita *vedantic* tradition. The ongoing discussions got diverted into the right or wrong debates with respect to the documented views of SSS and Shankara. When the subject matter of discussion focuses on relative merits or demerits of Vedantic philosophies then such discussions are likely to develop an Intellectual Crisis. Intellectual Crisis always brings chaos and utter confusion. Instead of focusing on the philosophical issues, such debates got diverted on the abilities of the debaters. One of the themes of Advaita is to curtail Ego and debates kindle Ego! At some threshold point it is necessary for the intellect to bow down to faith and intuition. It is intellectually arrogant for anyone to believe that through arguments,we cancome to a conclusive position and there will be no disagreement whatsoever. None of us can ever claim that we have completely understood our scriptures and the philosophical and religious dogmas that have been postulated in the past. If anyone dares to make such claims, no one will ever believe! 
  

I hope that the debate that took place long time backbetween Sankaracharya and Mandana Misra will open our eyes and ears. Mandana Misra's wife Bharathi (considered reincarnation of Goddess Saraswati) was appointed as the referee. The debate was not decided by intellectual abilities of these two masters. Instead, the debaters were garlanded with two identical flower garlands. These two great intellects had the humility to bow down to faith in the miracle of whether their respective garlands withered away or not. The debate continued for several weeks Sankaracharya was declared the winner by the adjudicator, the wife of his opponent! This episode once again illustrates the superiority of faith and intuition over intellectual ability. Faith and Intuition are parts of Hindu tradition and Advaita is no exception.
