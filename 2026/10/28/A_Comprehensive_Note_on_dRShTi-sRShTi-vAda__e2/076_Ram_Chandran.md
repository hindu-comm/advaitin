Namaste Sudhanshu:

First, I do not want to start a ‘debate’ on the questions that you have raised.

If I say that I have full faith (without an iota of doubt) on Vedas and accept them as the Truth. Now if start questioning the validity of Vedas, I am not bow down to my faith and I am intellectually arrogant!

These two types of Truth be relevant in this context:

Divine Truth which is Eternal truth and Intellect will not be used to find fault with it! It can’t be changed through intellectual argument!

Human Truth is what I perceived as the ‘Truth’ which is conditioned by location and time! I can claim that the tree has green leaves but it may not be true during the autumn season and the color may change! Human Truth is perceived by different humans differently and that explains the debate! There is a famous story that explains how blinds persons who touches the different parts of the body of an elephant perceive differently.

The minds of the intellect of the debaters are not PURE and polluted which causes different perceptions of a subject and they try to maintain what they perceive.

In Vedanta, the spiritual intuitive faculty is known as Divya Chakshu (दिव्य चक्षु) or Prajñā Chakshu (प्रज्ञा चक्षु). A Sage or Rishi who perceive the deeper, unseen reality beyond ordinary logic and senses possesses this Divine Eye or the Eye of Wisdom.

Swami Chinmayanandaji used to give the Pole-vault example where the pole (intellect) is used to jump over the pole, that is go beyond intellect.
The person has to drop the pole in order to go over! In other words, at certain point, we have drop our intellect to go beyond intellect to find the truth!

This is my quick reply from what I know. I am not a Sage possessing the Eye of Wisdom and I could be wrong!

With warm regards,

Ram Chandran

Note: Our wise moderator, Dennisji (author of many books on advaita Vedanta) brought to the attention of one of my post at the forum::

“All discussants in forums like the advaitin list should also become familiar with two key terms – kutarka and vitanda. Here is my understanding of these terms: Kutarka is the technique generally applied by ego centered scholars to use their knowledge and skills of communication to discredit the viewpoints expressed by others. Mostly the kutarkis (those who apply the kutarka) will not hesitate to change their logic quite often because their only goal is to discredit others. From the Vedic time peirod, discussions are classified by Samvada, Vada, Jalpa and Vitanda. Vitanda is the least preferred and recognized among the above four employed with the sole purpose to defeat the opponent. Those who employ Vitanda do not have any conviction and only purpose of vitanda is to invalidate any established position. It is important for all of us to know the pitfalls of kutarka and vitanda so that we avoid employing those ill- suited techniques for discussions focusing on \`Seeking the Truth.' “
