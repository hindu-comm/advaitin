You would appreciate that the reconciliation is not in vibhAShA like vRhibhiryajeta, yavairvA, but in asAdhAraNa analysis. pramANa can never be more than one mukhya. The others if appear as pramANa are sahakArI. In Naishkarmyasiddhikara refutes the sA~NkhyapakSha in the 3rd chapter opening that shruti vAkya leads to same j~nAna that they reach by anvayavyatireka by stating that only vAkyarthabodha leads to akhaNDArthabodha also called as avAkhyArtha. Siddhanti concludes by establishing that anvayavyatirekarUpa tarka is undoubtedly needed for padArthashodhana, but it is insufficient, vAkyArtha being more than that; it is more than sambandha of the words too to land in akhaNDArtha because lakShaNA has to be used. Only the following possibilities exist then:  
1) vAkya leads to j~nAna.  
2) tarka+vAkya leads to j~nAna  
3) tarka independently doesn't lead to j~nAna.

asAdharaNa being vAkya, bhAShya means the case 2 when it says that tarka is also a pramANa. Not only so, Naishkarmyasiddhi shows the avAntaravAkyas that show the anvayavyatireka tarka to be used as well! So 2 collapses in 1 if you used the source of tarka as shruti as well. Further...

  

> What exactly is Shruti-anugriheeta-tarka. It is that tarka which is
> blessed by Shruti. Which has anugraha of Shruti. That means - that
> tarka which leads to same truth which Shruti teaches.
>
>   
>
> So, truth is the sole determinant. Whatever conforms to truth is
> acceptable and is capable.

The question then is how will anyone using only tarka know that they have landed on the truth? If you say one will know when one knows, then it is no different from intuition-pakSha. (Swami Paramarthananda used to joke that AtmA is not known by intuition, but in tuition, learning from the Guru in sampradAya). Yogis would claim this intuition independently. Else you have to accept some dependency. The advaita tattva revealed by the shruti as the conclusion has to be already known in shravaNa with pramANanishchaya and that is what the tarka must conclude with prameyanishchaya for it to be called as shruti-anugRhIta tarka. Else tArkikas would also call their tarka concluding in nAnA AtmA as also shruti-anugRhIta.  
  
Ergo, it must be accepted that shravaNa of the shruti vAkya must necessarily precede this tarka, which is to say that shruti is, again, asAdhAraNa kAraNa here, leading to j~nAna. Without the vAkya, no tarka can independently land one into advaita/aikya jnAna. brahma is known through shruti, aikya is known through shruti and tarka is also based on analysis of shrutivAkyas to have the exact meaning using ShaD tAtparyali~Ngas. tarka as an independent pramANa is refuted in Sankhya's use of tarka and extending it to tArkika darshana underfew ending sutras ofनविलक्षणत्वाधिकरणम्sUtras and the nextशिष्टापरिग्रहाधिकरणम्, especiallyतर्काप्रतिष्ठानादप्यन्यथानुमेयमिति चेदेवमप्यविमोक्षप्रसङ्गः andएतेन शिष्टापरिग्रहा अपि व्याख्याताः। It is worthy to note that Bhagavan Bhashyakara says there that never will there be a time when all tArkikas of the past, present and future could sit together and come to a common conclusion.

Bhagavan Bhamatikara gives the meaning ofश्रुत्यनुगृहीत as \*श्रुत्याः श्रवणस्य\* पश्चादितिकर्तव्यतात्वेन गृहीतः। Even if you say shravaNa is vichArAtmaka, that vichAra is of shruti vAkyas only as pramANa. shabda is the only pramAna for brahmAtmAikya:अतीन्द्रियविषयेषु शास्त्रमेकमेव प्रमाणम्। Everthing else is sahakArI. tarka used is only itikartavyatA, meaning the steps to be used, how to.  
  
Bhagavan Bhashyakara says यथासम्भवम् in Adhyasa Bhashya so:न धर्मजिज्ञासायामिव श्रुत्यादय एव प्रमाणं ब्रह्मजिज्ञासायाम् । किन्तु श्रुत्यादयोऽनुभवादयश्च यथासम्भवमिह प्रमाणम् , अनुभवावसानत्वाद्भूतवस्तुविषयत्वाच्च ब्रह्मज्ञानस्य ।  
Bhamatikara says:श्रुत्यादय इति ।श्रुतीतिहासपुराणस्मृतयः प्रमाणम् । अनुभवोऽन्तःकरणवृत्तिभेदो ब्रह्मसाक्षात्कारस्तस्याविद्यानिवृत्तिद्वारेण ब्रह्मस्वरूपाविर्भावः प्रमाणफलम् । The latter is because anubhava is not pramANa but pramA. However, the usage here is as karaNa to the phalIbhUtabrahmasAkShAtkAra, the akhaNDAkAravRtti itself is called as anubhava. There is a reason that tarka is in neither group, because it doesn't fit the example dharma and exemplified brahman both.
