Namaste Sudhanshu ji,

  

In addition to the passages quoted by Swami Ananta Chaitanya ji, I would direct you to the following janmAdyadhikaraNa bhAShya passage:

  

वाक्यार्थविचारणाध्यवसाननिर्वृत्ता हि ब्रह्मावगतिः, नानुमानादिप्रमाणान्तरनिर्वृत्ता ।

The direct cognition of Brahman (which you as a mumukshu seek) is only possible through an enquiry into the meaning of vedAntic sentences (which is only possible if we understand the tAtparya of vedAntic sentences), it is not possible for it to arise through reasoning etc.

  

This does not mean that reasoning does not serve any purpose. Rather, it is to help firm up the meaning conveyed by vedAntic sentences.

  

सत्सु तु वेदान्तवाक्येषु जगतो जन्मादिकारणवादिषु , तदर्थग्रहणदार्ढ्याय अनुमानमपि वेदान्तवाक्याविरोधि प्रमाणं भवत् , न निवार्यते, श्रुत्यैव च सहायत्वेन तर्कस्याभ्युपेतत्वात् ।

There being vedantic sentences that support the view of those that hold the creation of the world, reasoning that does not contradict vedAntic sentences also can be valid in the firm understanding conveyed by those sentences. That is not being denied - because the shruti itself takes the support of reasoning in some places.

  

The role of anumAna as a pramANa in the case of Brahman is only as a support to the shruti (hence the use of the phrase यथासम्भवम् later in the janmAdi bhAShya) - and it is in that context that the Agama prakaraNa bhAShya statement you quote (अद्वैतं किमागममात्रेण प्रतिपत्तव्यम्... शक्यते तर्केणापि ज्ञातुम्) needs to be understood.

  

In fact, commenting on this Agama prakaraNa bhAShya sentence, the TIkAkAra, Anandagiri AchArya beautifully contextualises the words of the bhAShya -

  

स्वतन्त्रतर्काप्रवेशेऽपि तस्मिन्नागमिकतर्कस्य सहकारितया सम्भावनाहेतुत्वात् तर्केणापि ज्ञातुं शक्यमिति व्यवहारोपपत्तिरिति मत्वाऽऽह –अद्वैतेति ।

  

In the case of Brahman, tarka can only be a *sambhAvanA hetu* - it can establish that the existence of Brahman is a possibility. And in that aspect, there is prAmANya vyavahAra of tarka. However, it is the shruti alone that establishes the existence of Brahman as fact (not merely a possibility).

  

With the backing of shruti though, the jnAna revealed by anumAna can be relied on as absolutely valid.

  

Why cannot the tarka be relied as absolutely valid? ie why is there *svatantra tarka apravesha* with respect to Brahman? The reason is given elsewhere (the तर्काप्रतिष्ठानात् sUtra bhAshya quoted by Sri Ananta Chaitanya ji) - because of the risk that there can always arise an anumAna at a later time, from a more intelligent person, that falsifies the first anumAna revealed knowledge.

  

Whereas when the shruti says that Brahman exists, there is nothing else that can falsify shruti.
