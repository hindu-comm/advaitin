Namaste Venkat ji.

  

> Why and from what standpoint are you questioning? If you can accept
> svapna shruti, you must have no qualms accepting svapna apauruSheyatva
> also.

  

The swapna-nyAya is applied within a state which appears like non-swapna, but is proved through swapna-nyAya to be swapna.

  

In this standpoint only, where the state appears like non-swapna, but is proved to be swapna - one concludes that Shruti is not apaurusheya, but anishchita-advaita-kalpakena-kalpita.

  

Accepting that entire seen is prAtibhAsika and yet claiming Shruti (which is one such seen) to be apaurusheya does not appear to be coherent.



> For a dreamer who is convinced of the reality of the dream, it follows
> that with respect with to the dreamer the svApna shAstrayonitva of
> Brahman is valid. Its only upon waking up that we realise it is
> svApna. But when in that frame of reference, there is internal
> capacity of shruti to reveal BrahmAtma aikya independently and for
> svApna tarka to depend on svApna shruti for the same.

  

Of course, the dreamer who is convinced of reality of dream - for him, the pre-eminent status of Shruti is valid. But what about a dreamer who knows, through swapna-anumAna, that it is a swapna -- no matter how non-swapna it appears to be. Can he still claim that Shruti is apaurusheya etc?

  

> Outside of svapna (ie waking up to the absolute reality) , there is no
> pramANa prameya vyavahAra anyway. So there is no scope to question
> apauruSheyatva of a non-existent shruti. Inside svapna for the eka
> jIva, that apauruSheyatva is equally unquestionable.

  

That is fine. That is not the standpoint whence the apaurusheyatva is questioned.
