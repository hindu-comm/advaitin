> Namaste Venkat ji.
>
>   
>
> > Why and from what standpoint are you questioning? If you can accept
> > svapna shruti, you must have no qualms accepting svapna
> > apauruSheyatva also.
>
>   
>
> The swapna-nyAya is applied within a state which appears like
> non-swapna, but is proved through swapna-nyAya to be swapna.

  

You seem to now be arguing that svapna "nyAya" - ie tarka - is insufficient to prove that something is svapna. Indeed, agreed. That is why you need the shruti and guru to wake you up. Until you wake up, no amount of tarka is going to be sufficient, because there is always the doubt that the anumAna is falsifiable at some future date!

  

>   
>
> Accepting that entire seen is prAtibhAsika and yet claiming Shruti
> (which is one such seen) to be apaurusheya does not appear to be
> coherent.

Why? Is there a restriction on what can appear in a dream and what cannot? If so, then the framework of DSV itself is shaky, because there are many other things than the apauruSheyatva of shruti which may be restricted from appearing in a dream.

  

> 
>
> Of course, the dreamer who is convinced of reality of dream - for him,
> the pre-eminent status of Shruti is valid. But what about a dreamer
> who knows, through swapna-anumAna, that it is a swapna -- no matter
> how non-swapna it appears to be. Can he still claim that Shruti is
> apaurusheya etc?

  

Well either he is convinced he is in a svapna or he is not. If he is convinced, he has already woken up and is not in a svapna anymore and the discussion is moot. If he is not convinced, the tarka really hasnt helped him.

  

We dont get to pick and choose what aspect of the world is a dream and what is not. Either everything is, or DSV is invalid.

  

It is the same argument that the opponents to DSV make - i.e the argument "it is incoherent to suppose that creation can be this orderly if it is simply a dream" is logically equivalent to your argument "it is incoherent to postulate shruti's apauruSheyatva if it is simply a dream".

  

Kind regards,

Venkatraghavan
