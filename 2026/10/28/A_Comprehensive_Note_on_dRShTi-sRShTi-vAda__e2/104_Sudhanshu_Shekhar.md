> You seem to now be arguing that svapna "nyAya" - ie tarka - is

No, I am not making that argument. I am saying that svapna-nyAya is sufficient.



> That is why you need the shruti and guru to wake you up. Until you

Why? If tarka has led one to truth, why should the doubt remain? Does truth needShruti to validate it?

  

> > Accepting that entire seen is prAtibhAsika and yet claiming Shruti
> > (which is one such seen) to be apaurusheya does not appear to be
> > coherent.
>
> Why? Is there a restriction on what can appear in a dream and what
> cannot? If so, then the framework of DSV itself is shaky, because
> there are many other things than the apauruSheyatva of shruti which

Anything can appear in the dream. But, he who knows that seen is prAtibhAsika cannot claim that Shruti "is" apaurusheya. He would say that entire seen including Shruti is anishchita-advaita-kalakena-kalpita that he himself is the kalpaka. Shruti is not apaurusheya will be his coherent understanding.



> Well either he is convinced he is in a svapna or he is not. If he is
> convinced, he has already woken up and is not in a svapna anymore and
> the discussion is moot. If he is not convinced, the tarka really hasnt
> helped him.

  

He is convinced that he is seeing a swapna. He is "in" swapna - is not what I am saying. He is seeing a swapna.

  

This is not exactly "woken up" wherein there has to be no seeing-swapna.



> We dont get to pick and choose what aspect of the world is a dream and
> what is not. Either everything is, or DSV is invalid.

  

I don't understand where is pick-and-choose in my statement.

  

> It is the same argument that the opponents to DSV make - i.e the
