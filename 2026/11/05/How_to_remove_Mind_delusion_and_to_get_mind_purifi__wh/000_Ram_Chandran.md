Namaskar:

How to remove Mind delusion and to get mind purification.

The entire Bhagavad Gita contains the dialog between the warrior intellect Arjun and Lord Krishna and the one of the focus is on the importance of “Anthakarana Suddhi.” Arjuna was very fortunate to get his delusion removed by the Lord through the discussions from chapters 2 to chapter 18.

In Bhagavad Gita Chapter 18 Verse 72, Bhagwan Sri Krishna asks Arjuna:

कच्चिदेतच्छ्रुतं पार्थ त्वयैकाग्रेण चेतसा \|

कच्चिदृज्ञानसम्मोहः प्रनष्टस्ते धनञ्जय \|\|

EnglihTranslation: Lord Krishna asks: "O Partha (Arjuna), did you hear these teachings with a concentrated, attentive mind? O Dhananjaya, has your illusion and ignorance of mind (mind delusion) now been fully destroyed?" \[

Here, Lord Krishna steps out of the role of a philosophical guide and checks in as a teacher. He wants to make sure that Arjuna did not merely listen passively, but actually absorbed the spiritual core. The spiritual core is one's true nature is the eternal soul (Atman) rather than the temporary material body, mind, intellect and anxieties.

In Bhavavad Gita Chapter 18 Verse 73, Arjuna provides the answer:

अर्जुन उवाच \| नष्टो मोहः स्मृतिर्लब्धा त्वत्प्रसादान्मयाच्युत \|

स्थितोऽस्मि गतसन्देहः करिष्ये वचनं तव \|\|

English Translation: Arjuna says: "My dear Krishna, O infallible one, my illusion is now gone. I have regained my memory of my True Self by Your grace. I am now firm, free from all doubts, and prepared to act according to Your instructions."

Here Arjuna resolves his crisis of duty on the battlefield. He also acknowledges Krishna’s grace. His internal confusion was dissolved, and he was able to shift from paralyzing emotional grief to clear, decisive action and more correctly *dharmic* duty.

The entire Bhagavad Gita may be considered as a means to get the mind purified provided we follow the teachings of Bhagwan Sri Krishna with total devotion and dedication.
