Namaste Lokesh ji.

  

> From the standpoint of ultimate reality, I assume both world and maya
> are unreal. If that's the case what so special about that we call the
> world unreal but maya as neither real nor unreal? Or is it that even
> the world from the standpoint of logic is neither real nor unreal?

  

This is an issue of defintion and words. We have terms like sat, asat, bhāva, abhāva, tuccha, mithyā etc.

  

Jagat and Māyā are both mithyā.

  

Jagat has both bhāva-entities and abhāva-entities. But Māyā is bhāva-abhāva-vilakśhaṇa.

  

Jagat and Māyā are both sat-asat-vilakśhaṇa.

  

These all descriptions are from standpoint of logic, which is within the frame of reference of Māyā.

  

From the frame of reference of Brahman, both Māyā and world are tuccha, like horns of hare.

> I understand that maya and world are assumed as entities because they
> appear true in transactional reality. However what's the utility of
> assuming the controller of maya ie Isvara.Isvara appear to us neither
> in ultimate reality nor in transaction reality.

  

Māyā being an illusion needs a substratum. Being Māyā, it needs a Māyāvī. The point is -- that who is termed as Māyāvī is termed so only from the frame of reference of Māyā. From the frame of reference of non-Māyā, Māyā is tuccha. Hence, there is no Māyāi-tva there.

  

The adhyāropa of controller-hood is done only as a part of adhyāropa, to be negated later to arrive at desert per se. It is not with the intention of perpetuating an imagination of controller-hood. It is with an intention of negating it subsequently.
