Namaskar:

Advaita Vedanta is simple if we fully understand the implied concepts in clear terms. But unfortunately, that is not always the case because Sattva (foundational concept of Advaita Vedanta) is complex!

As most of us know that in Advaita Vedanta, “Reality” is categorized into three levels:

Pāramārthika (Absolute Reality): Brahman alone, which is never contradicted by any experience across past, present, or future.

Vyāvahārika (Empirical Reality): The world we experience daily. It is functional and real for all practical purposes, but only until one realizes Brahman.

Prātibhāsika (Illusory/Subjective Reality): Dreams or temporary illusions, like mistaking a rope for a snake or seeing water by a traveler while going through the desert.

Māyā and Jagat belong to the Vyāvahārika level. They are called *mithyā* because they occupy a category called *Anirvacanīya* (indescribable as either completely real or completely unreal. They are absolutely real because they change and they, are dependent on Brahman, and disappear upon spiritual awakening (*jñāna*). It should be understood that they are not absolutely non-existent like a rabbit's horns because they are experienced by us right now. Just as a clay pot (*mithyā*) cannot exist without clay (*satyam*), both Jagat and Māyā exist and function only because they borrow their reality from Brahman.

For greater understanding, specific Advaita Vedanta texts such as like the Vivekachudamani or Tattva Bodha explores how Brahman relates to these two concepts.
