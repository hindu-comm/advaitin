Namaste Lokesh ji,

  

> I'm still not convinced as to why do we need mayavi (a controller of
> maya when maya ultimately is unreal). You said
>
>   
>
> \>Māyā being an illusion needs a substratum.
>
>   
>
> Are you saying that needing a substratum amounts to needing a
> controller? How?

  

Māyā is illusion. Illusion needs substratum. That substratum has to be non-illusion. So, the substratum is non-Māyā.

  

So, Māyā needs non-Māyā.

  

Point is this -- how does non-Māyā appear from the frame of reference of Māyā? In what form?

  

Māyā (M) sees non-Māyā (NM) as completely pervading M.

  

NM illuminates M.

  

NM enlivens M.

  

The very appearance of M depends on NM.

  

M says that NM is her enlivener, illuminator, support and controller.

  

M says that she is dependent on NM.

  

Just as the magic of magician is dependent on magician, similarly M is dependent on NM. Hence, the word Māyāvi is used. I checked in Advaita Sharada and one useful reference came up - अविद्येत्यादिना ।

मायामयी प्रसिद्धमायोपमिता । लोके मायाविनो मायावत्परतन्त्रेत्यर्थः ।

  

So, the word Māyāvi may not be taken to mean that there is one magician, who is creating these universes by utilising Māyā etc. That is another imaginagion. Nothing is happening.

  

Māyāvi may just mean the description of NM from the frame of reference of M as the one on which the very appearance of M depends, like in usual worldly transactions.

  

  

> A person may not know what a gyroscope is, however it doesn't imply
> that someone has induced that ignorance upon him. The ignorance is
> natural in this case and is anadi.

  

None has induced the ignorance. It is indeed natural. Similarly its dependence is natural. Being illusion, ignorance needs a substratum.

  

That substratum is called "substratum" only from point of view of ignorance. Substratum-hood, being a seen, is also illusion.

  

Ignorance brings in division of controller-controlled.

  

Like people living on earth say that Sun is their controller. But Sun has no concern with earth. Our existence may depend on Sun, but Sun has no interest in that. (Please try to take the essence of the analogy).

  

"Him" etc also part of ignorance.

  

> Also, coming to think of the substratum of illusion, ain't I the
> substratum of the illusion given that it is my illusion?

  

No, I is also an illusion. I is a seen, and hence it is also an illusion.

  

The substartum has no trace of I.

  

It is another matter that substartum is indicated (lakśhya-artha) by the word I.

  

> I am the one in ignorance, not Īśvara. Why is Isvara required to
> account for my illusion?

  

See, this jīva-Iśvara-vibhāga is another imaginagion. You can have hundreds of theories around it. That will not convince you.

  

It depends on how you define jīva. How you distinguish Māyā and ignorance.

  

If you equate Māyā and ignorance, and hold singular ignorance, and define jīva as avidyā-upahita-caitanya, then there is no difference between vācya-artha of jīva and Iśvara.

  

But if you define jīva as avidyā-pratibimbita-caitanya or antah-karaṇa-upahita-caitanya, then there will arise a distinction between jīva and Iśvara.

  

It is all a matter of defintions.

  

If we can keep the discussion to M and NM, it solves all problems.
