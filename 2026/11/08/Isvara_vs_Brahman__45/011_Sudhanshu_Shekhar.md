Namaste Lokesh ji,

  

> Thanks for explaining in detail. However I still have doubt as to why
> we need Isvara to explain the Maya rather than using Brahman to
> explain Maya. To me these two are different paths. One is to reject
> Maya and arrive at Brahman and other is to accept it and arrive at
> Isvara. I don't see how can one after realising Isvara, realize
> Brahman.

  

M is an illusion. It needs, therefore, NM.

  

NM viewed from M is called Ishwara.

  

NM viewed from the frame of reference of NM is called Brahman.

  

While being in M, the NM appears as Ishwara.

  

One can say that Brahman is the substratum of M. There is nothing wrong in it. One can definitely use Brahman to explain M. However, one needs to immediately qualify that substratum-hood of Brahman is part and parcel of M.

  

NM is the substratum of M. But it is so only while viewing it from M. So, from avidya-drishti, NM is the substratum of M. From Brahma-drishti, M itself does not appear. So, NM exists as it is, without the attribution of substratum-hood.

  

The moment you superimpose substratum-hood to NM, you arrive at Ishwara.

  

> 

>   
>
> \> Māyā is illusion. Illusion needs substratum. That substratum has to
> be non-illusion. So, the substratum is non-Māyā.
>
>   

> Why can't the substratum be Brahman?

  

Brahman is the substratum. But it has no substratum-hood. Shastra superimposes substratum-hood to Brahman. We arrive at Ishwara then.

  

> Also I remember reading somewhere that it is impossible to know NM
> from point of view of ignorance. Just as if we take mirage to be real
> we wouldn't be looking at its substratum ie desert. Similarly for
> rope-snake analogy. If I'm convinced of the snake why would I look for
> the rope? Only after taking the snake as an illusion, and coming out
> of it I can realise rope. Fir example without waking up from sleep,
> its impossible to realize the substratum of a dream.

  

I feel it is correct. Knowing NM as NM is impossible while being in M.

  

Only when M is completely rejected, one can situate as NM.

  

Again, every possible facet of M has to be rejected to situate as NM.

  

So, substratum-hood, controller-hood, witness-hood -- which are all part of M - need to be rejected to situate as NM.

  

Situating as NM is identical to knowing NM.

  

> Yes, the Sun has no concern, however, it unknowingly causes life on
> Earth. How can we deny that?

  

The analogy fails in this aspect. Illusion, by definition, implies that nothing is happening.

  

> Desert may be the ultimate truth however how can we deny that mirage
> as a phenomenon is nothappening in desert?

  

There is no mirage-water. The analogy is in respect of mirage-water. Not in respect of sun-rays getting refracted etc.



> Similarly if Isvara is willingly causing the existence of world and
> sustaining it, why do we then need Brahman?

  

Again, Ishwara doing all these things is merely an adhyaropa done by Shruti to negate it subsequently.



> You didn't get what I want to say. Yes the feeling of I is an illusion
> but the subject of that feeling (saksi) is not.

  

"I" is also an illusion and sAkshi-hood is also an illusion. SAkshI is not an illusion but its sAkshi-tva is an illusion.कारणत्ववत्साक्षित्वमपि स्वाज्ञानकृतमित्युक्तमेव प्रसङ्गात्प्रकटयति.

  

> I am the one in ignorance. The ignorance is mine. I am the one who is
> ignorant. So I'm the sustainer of it.

  

It is not the case. I and ignorance are both superimposed in NM. Ignorance, which is a delimitor of shuddha chaitanya, appears to act as an avachchhedaka of I, on account of abheda-adhyasa of I with shuddha chaitanya. Therefore, this experience occurs - I am ignorant. One should not derive thence that I is sustainer of ignorance.

  

There is a detailed analysis of "aham ajnah" in Advaita Siddhi. It is a must read if you want to go deep in this.

  

> Why do I need an Isvara and say that he created this universe? Why
> delude myself further? This doesn't seem like adhyaropa but like
> putting myself in utter illusion by now believing in entities that I
> don't even perceive in the practical world.

  

Depends on your adhikaaritvam. If you can set aside entire seen world, this adhyaropa is not needed.
