Namaste Lokesh ji.

  

> So, essentially, my understanding of what you are pointing towards is
> that when illusion is recognized there is Isvara, when even the
> illusion isn't recognized it is Brahman.

  

When illusion is perceived, Brahman is termed as Ishwara. When illusion is not perceived, Brahman is termed as (shuddha, NirguNa) Brahman.



> My question is can we get away with not recognizing the illusion, and
> even if we do not recognize the illusion does it become non-existent
> by virtue of our non-recognition of it?

  

The illusion-hood of illusion is also an illusion. The illusion and the content thereof are all non-existent. Even when illusion is being perceived, it is non-existent. It just appears to exist, while it does not exist.

  

So, my non-recognition does not make it non-existent. It is non-existent in itself. The attribution of existence therein ceases when one situates in the substratum.



> Ofcourse we can get away with saying that snake that appears on the
> rope is false, or that water that appears in the mirage is false,
> however, can we get away with saying that the phenomenon of mirage or
> seeing a rope in a snake is non-existent? I don't think so. Not
> recognizing something doesn't mean it becomes false.

  

That is the limitation of these analogies. The teaching is this -- from the frame of reference of Brahman, there is no perception of MAyA. That is ajAti-vada.

  

  

> Further if you claim that from Brahman POV, illusion does not exist
> while admissing the appearance of the illusion, then in my opinion
> Brahman cease being the absolute reality because in absolute reality,
> appearance of illusion do exist.

  

No. From the frame of reference of rope, snake does not appear. From the frame of reference of non-rope, snake appears.

  

From the frame of reference of Brahman, MAyA does not appear/ From the frame of reference of non-Brahman, MAyA appears.

  

The non-existence of MAyA is common, be it appearance or non-appearance.



> Another question I have is that every adhyaropa gets rids of some form
> of ignorance (while inducing another one), whats the ignorance we are
> getting rid of while admissing existance of Isvara?

  

Negation of adhyArOpa through apavAda removes ignorance. Admitting Ishwara does not get rid of ignorance. Removal of Ishwara-hood, and our jIva-hood, and equating them removes ignorance.
