## Manjushree Hegde — “Question of Two Brahmans”

Technical summary · Based on Chapter 4, pp. 116–144, of her 2025 doctoral thesis

Central thesis: Following Saccidānandendra Sarasvatī (SSS), Hegde argues that Śaṅkara's distinction between para and apara brahman is fundamentally hermeneutical and soteriological, not ontological. It differentiates Brahman as an object of knowledge (jñeya) from Brahman as an object of meditation (upāsya).

### 1. The criterion is function, not attributes

The customary identification of aparabrahman with Īśvara or saguṇa brahman is misleading. Śaṅkara attributes causality, omniscience, omnipotence, and lordship to Brahman in both contexts.

The decisive question is what these attributes accomplish.

|  |  |
|----|----|
| Parabrahman | Aparabrahman |
| Jñeyabrahman — to be known | Upāsyabrahman — to be meditated upon |
| Jñāna / inquiry | Upāsanā / prescribed mental activity |
| Attributes ultimately rescinded | Attributes retained for meditation |
| Adhyāropāpavāda operative | No corresponding retraction required |
| Immediate liberation (sadyo-mukti) | Particular meditative fruits, including krama-mukti |

### 2. Īśvara is not necessarily aparabrahman

Hegde's strongest contention is that Īśvara can designate parabrahman even when characterized as creator of the universe.

In the jñeya-brahma-prakaraṇas, the attribution of jagatkāraṇatva is pedagogical rather than a definitive metaphysical assertion.

Thus, in BSB 1.1.2 (janmādy asya yataḥ), the causal designation identifies the Brahman under inquiry, not a separate lower Brahman.

### 3. The kārya-kāraṇa-prakriyā

Hegde analyzes the Bhṛgu–Varuṇa dialogue (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 3.1–6) as a systematic application of anvaya-vyatireka.

The causal definition of Brahman functions through three stages:

1.  Adhyāropa: Brahman is provisionally characterized as the cause of
    origination, sustenance, and dissolution.

2.  Discrimination: Entities subject to origination and dissolution are
    excluded from Brahman's nature.

3.  Apavāda: Causality itself is finally rescinded, leaving Brahman free
    of causal determination.

The important point is that causality is not introduced to explain the mechanics of creation. It serves the inquiry culminating in the negation of causal relations.

### 4. Upāsanā is a distinct textual domain

Her contrasting examination of Chāndogya Upaniṣad 8.1–6 (dahara-vidyā) establishes that not every attribution to Brahman is intended for subsequent negation.

In upāsanā-prakaraṇas, qualifications are deliberately retained because they constitute the content of meditation.

Hence, adhyāropāpavāda is not indiscriminately applicable to every Upaniṣadic passage. Its distinctive soteriological operation belongs to the texts teaching jñeyabrahman.

### 5. The criticism of post-Śaṅkara Advaita

Hegde challenges the tendency to assign Īśvara and causality to a relatively valid vyāvahārika domain while reserving nirguṇa brahman for the pāramārthika.

On SSS's interpretation, the retraction takes place within the teaching itself, not merely from an imagined post-realization standpoint.

She does acknowledge that post-Śaṅkara authors do not ordinarily assign Īśvara an independent, ultimately real existence. Her criticism concerns their explanatory use of Īśvara and māyā, rather than an alleged doctrine of two independently real Brahmans.

## Assessment

The paper's most consequential claim is that the para/apara distinction is determined by the intended epistemic operation of a passage, not simply by the presence or absence of upādhis.

This makes tātparya and prakriyā decisive for interpretation. One cannot infer from the occurrence of īśvaratva or kāraṇatva that a passage concerns aparabrahman.

A question worth pressing is whether Hegde's proposed criteria always reliably distinguish jñāna from upāsanā, especially in passages where meditation, inquiry, and causal language overlap.

Reference: Hegde, doctoral thesis — Chapter 4, pp. 116–144.
