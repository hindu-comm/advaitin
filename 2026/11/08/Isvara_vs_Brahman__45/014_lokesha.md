\> So, my non-recognition does not make it non-existent. It is non-existent in itself. The attribution of existence therein ceases when one situates in the substratum.  
  

Could you explain how is the illusion non-existent in itself? When I  
wake up from the dream or get to know that it is not a snake but a  
rope, I don't say that the illusion I saw was non-existent.  

  
\> No. From the frame of reference of rope, snake does not appear. From the frame of reference of non-rope, snake appears.  
  

I do not agree with this point. I do not understand what you mean by  
"frame of reference of rope". Rope is basically dead matter, so it's  
frame of reference or point of view doesn't make sense. I hope by  
"frame of reference of rope" you mean the reality that there is only  
rope and no snake. In that case, in that frame of reference, there's  
no denying that rope appears as snake because it's properties match to  
that of snake in certain conditions such as darkness, etc.  
  
You say that the illusion-hood of illusion is also an illusion. How  
so? Don't we all know that the water seen in a mirage is not real, yet  
we do not deny this illusion?  

  
\> Negation of adhyArOpa through apavAda removes ignorance.  
  

True. However, adhyarope too removes ignorance. That's the whole idea  
of having an adhyaropa. The apavada is there to remove the new  
ignorance that is left out by the adhyarope step. This is my  
understanding.
