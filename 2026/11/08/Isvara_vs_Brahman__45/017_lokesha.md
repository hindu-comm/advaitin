Dear Sudhanshu Ji  
  
The original debate was around Isvara and Brahman and we came to the  
understanding that when there's no acknowledgment of illusion, that NM  
is Brahman and where there is acknowledgment of illusion, that NM is  
Isvara. I agree with you so far.  
  
The place where we diverge is when you make the extraordinary claim  
that even the illusion is non-existent.  

  
\> However, when entire seen is treated as illusion -- then illusion also being a seen is discarded as illusion. So, illusion itself becomes non-existent like illusory-snake.  
  

So, lets take the example of a dream2 inside a dream1. Even when we  
realize that dream1 was unreal, we still can't deny the fact that we  
did see dream2 instead dream1. Or lets take story within a story. Both  
the stories are unreal because they never happened, however you can  
still argue about the facts of both the stories. The fact that inner  
story happened inside the outer story, (meaning the entire illusion  
seen was itself an illusion) still doesn't grant us the liberty to  
claim that inner story is non-existent. It is still existent not as  
per its contents but as a phenomenon.  
  
Using the dream-within-a-dream or story-within-a-story analogy, I  
don't think it makes sense that even when entire illusion is proved to  
be an illusion, the inner illusion can be said to be non-existent. The  
phenomenon of illusion still says and cannot be denied.  
  
This brings me back to the original argument: How can there be no  
trace of illusion as a phenomenon in Brahman?  

  
\> Imagine that you are "in" rope. You cannot see snake. That is what I mean by frame of reference. The terms used in VedAnta are like avidyA-drishTi, Brahma-drishTi etc.  
  

I think we need to elaborate on "in" a bit more to make things  
clearer. Even when I'm "in" the snake or when I'm "in" the desert if  
I'm a knowledgable person I cannot deny the fact that because of the  
similarity of rope and snake, and mirage and water, the phenomenon of  
illusion is false. I do acknowledge the possibility of illusion even  
when I'm "in" the rope. I can not deny it.  

  
\> All seen is illusion. Illusion-hood of illusion is also a seen. So, illusion-hood is also an illusion. This may sound simple. But it is profound.  
  

This is where we disagree. The object seen in an illusion could be  
unreal but the phenomenon itself cannot be denied.  

  
\> I don't know what is new ignorance.  
  

For example, श्रीकृष्ण says in भगवद्गीता that this आत्मा changes  
bodies. This is an adhyaropa that strikes down the old ignorance that  
Atman is अनित्य, however it creates another ignorance which is then  
retracted with the अपवाद that आत्मा is निश्चल.
