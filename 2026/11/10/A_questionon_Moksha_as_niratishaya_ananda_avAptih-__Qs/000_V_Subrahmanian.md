In Advaita we say mukti is'niratishaya ananda avāptih'. A question arises as to how this is an absolute definition of mukti. This is because as long as the Jnani is alive he can experience the Atma Ananda. However once death occurs, there will be no \*antahkarana\* medium to experience the Atma Ananda. In that scenario the definition does not work. Hence it would be best to say that mukti is 'remaining as the Advitiya Sat Brahman.' There will be no contradiction as even while alive, on the lines of the BG, 'na asatah vidyate bhāvah, na abhāvah vidyate satah,' is experienced, which will not be contradicted even when there is no individual entity to experience that. Since Sat cannot disappear, saying that 'remaining over as that Sat is mukti' would be the most appropriate way to define moksha. There will not be the contingency of the absence of the \*antahkarana\* medium to experience Sat, since Sat remains without being destroyed, whether experienced or not. Non-Advaitins question / object that in Advaita there will be no jiva who can experience his mukti after death.
