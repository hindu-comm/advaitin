Namaste Subbuji,

On Tue, Nov 10, 2026 at 12:56 PM V Subrahmanian \<[v.subra...@gmail.com]()\> wrote:  

> In Advaita we say mukti is'niratishaya ananda avāptih'. A question
> arises as to how this is an absolute definition of mukti. This is
> because as long as the Jnani is alive he can experience the Atma
> Ananda.

You already know that definition of mukti is twofold AtyantikaduHkhanivRttiH niratashayAnandAvAptishca. I am sure that there is no issue with the former but only with the latter. However, experience the AtmAnanda is incorrect interpretation of muktyAnanda. One can say that the jIvanmuktyAnanda is definitely the pratibimba of the svarUpAnanda, anUbhUta, because of no viShaya dragging the antaHkaraNa outward from svarUpa that would have otherwise brought duHkha. After the drop of the body, however, there are two ways to look at it: one, from the perspective of the others, the jnAnI has merged with IshvarasvarUpa who is non-different from AnandasvarUpa, because all the prayers towards are for Ananda itself and without Ishvara Himself being Ananda, what would He grant the others. Hopefully, dvaitis won't have issue here.

> However once death occurs, there will be no \*antahkarana\* medium to
> experience the Atma Ananda. In that scenario the definition does not
> work. Hence it would be best to say that mukti is 'remaining as the
> Advitiya Sat Brahman.' There will be no contradiction as even while
> alive, on the lines of the BG, 'na asatah vidyate bhāvah, na abhāvah
> vidyate satah,' is experienced, which will not be contradicted even
> when there is no individual entity to experience that.

  

Second, merging with satsvarUpa, which is merging with chaitanya and Ananda also since they are one and the same. This need not be anubhUtAnanda through antaHkaraNa, but as shruti says pashyatyachakShuH, it can be said to be anubhavatyanantakaraNaH. I hope Vedantis won't have issue with that. Else they need to explain pashyatyachakShuH etc which applies to brahman, which jnAnI is.



> Since Sat cannot disappear, saying that 'remaining over as that Sat is
> mukti' would be the most appropriate way to define moksha.

The same rule of sat can be stated about sadAnanda, that is nAbhAvaH vidyate sadrUpasyAnandasya, with the hetu of not having gradations. Else, there is aprAmANyApattiH of AnandamImAMsA of Taittiriya. Also, brahmasvarUpalakShaNavirodhaH etc.



> There will not be the contingency of the absence of the
> \*antahkarana\* medium to experience Sat, since Sat remains without
> being destroyed, whether experienced or not.

There is no mention of Ananda being experienced as an object in mukti anywhere. The experiencer is separated from the experienced, but one who is beyond the experiencer, this doesn't apply to that svarUpataH sacchidAnandaH.

  

Non-Advaitins question / object that in Advaita there will be no jiva who can experience his mukti after death.

If you mean dvaitIs, they should question the shruti aboutpashyatyachakShuHthen, not us. If you mean nAstikas, in a rare possibility, they deserve no answer!
